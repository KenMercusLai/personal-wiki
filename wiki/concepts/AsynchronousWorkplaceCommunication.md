## Definition
[[AsynchronousWorkplaceCommunication]] is the exchange of work information without requiring participants to be available at the same time, using durable writing, explicit context, predictable cadences, and shared records so people can respond with considered attention.

## Current Synthesis
The 37signals guide treats asynchronous communication as an organizational default rather than a remote-work convenience. Consequential matters begin and end with complete written thoughts because writing persists, reaches people outside the room, and gives future employees a record. Immediate response is reserved for true emergencies; otherwise, time is allowed to improve judgment and reduce the pressure created when a sender's urgency becomes a recipient's interruption.

The model depends on information architecture as much as prose. Communication should contain factual context and live beside the object it concerns, while one centralized workspace serves as the shared record. Predictable prompts create loose accountability and ambient awareness at different horizons: daily reflection, weekly projection, optional social contact, six-week retrospective Heartbeats, and prospective Kickoffs. This distributes context without requiring everyone to follow every project or synchronize calendars.

## Key Claims
- Asynchronous writing should be the default for substantial communication, while synchronous exchange remains available for genuine emergencies or discussions that need it.
- Durable complete thoughts preserve rationale, broaden access beyond the room, and create organizational memory.
- Response-time expectations are part of system design: routine immediacy creates interruption pressure and can degrade judgment.
- Communication needs both factual context and spatial context, with discussion attached to the relevant task, document, policy, or project.
- Predictable check-ins and multi-horizon summaries can maintain awareness without universal monitoring.
- Meetings should be evaluated by total participant time, interruption cost, and whether writing could accomplish the purpose.

## Evidence
- Default and pace: [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] recommends real-time communication sometimes and asynchronous communication most of the time, with meaningful time for discussions to develop.
- Writing and memory: [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] says important decisions belong in complete written exchanges that remain useful to absent and future colleagues.
- Attention protection: [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] rejects immediate-response expectations outside true emergencies and warns that rushed approaches can compel rushed replies.
- Context placement: [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] says task, document, policy, and project discussions should remain attached to those objects.
- Operating cadence: [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] describes daily and weekly prompts, optional social questions, and six-week Heartbeats and Kickoffs.
- Meeting cost: [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] counts a meeting's cost across all participants and treats meetings as a last resort.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one company's first-party operating guide, not a comparative study. Writing can be slow, effortful, inaccessible to some participants, or poorly suited to ambiguity, conflict repair, emergencies, sensitive personnel matters, rapid collaborative exploration, and situations where tone or embodied cues matter. A centralized written record can improve discoverability while also creating surveillance concerns, information overload, or false confidence that publication equals understanding. Small-team norms may not transfer to regulated, multilingual, safety-critical, frontline, or highly interdependent work.
