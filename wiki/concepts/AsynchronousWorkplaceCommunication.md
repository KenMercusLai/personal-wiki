## Definition
[[AsynchronousWorkplaceCommunication]] is the exchange of work information without requiring participants to be available at the same time, using durable writing, explicit context, shared records, and bounded response expectations so people can coordinate without routine schedule synchronization.

## Current Synthesis
The 37signals and Gumroad accounts treat asynchronous communication as organizational infrastructure rather than a remote-work convenience. Both make writing the default, preserve discussion in shared tools, and reject routine immediate-response pressure. 37signals emphasizes complete thoughts, project-local context, one durable workspace, and recurring written cadences; Gumroad adds a 24-hour response expectation, a shared task queue, self-selection of work, and product delivery without routine meetings.

The combined model separates availability from progress. People can protect focus and choose working hours when the record contains enough context for another person to act later. Accountability comes from visible work, predictable prompts or queues, a measurable direction, and eventual response rather than simultaneous presence. Gumroad's year-long Memberships project suggests that large work can be decomposed and coordinated this way, but one successful launch does not show that every dependency or conflict is better handled asynchronously.

## Key Claims
- Asynchronous writing can be the default for substantial communication while synchronous exchange remains available for emergencies or discussions that genuinely need it.
- Durable complete thoughts and work-local records preserve rationale, broaden access beyond the room, and create organizational memory.
- Response-time expectations are part of system design: a bounded window can preserve accountability without making every message an interruption.
- Shared queues, recurring written prompts, and measurable direction can coordinate work without universal monitoring or routine meetings.
- Asynchronous work requires unusually clear writing because delayed clarification makes ambiguous or incomplete messages more expensive.
- Large projects can be decomposed and shipped through asynchronous coordination, but the evidence does not establish that this model suits every team or dependency.

## Evidence
Default, pace, and attention:
- [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] recommends real-time communication sometimes and asynchronous communication most of the time, with meaningful time for discussions to develop.
- [[no-meetings-no-deadlines-no-full-time-employees]] reports that Gumroad used GitHub, Notion, and occasional Slack with a normal 24-hour response expectation and no routine meetings.

Writing, memory, and context:
- [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] says important decisions belong in complete written exchanges attached to the relevant task, document, policy, or project.
- [[no-meetings-no-deadlines-no-full-time-employees]] says the absence of standups and synchronous clarification required contributors to write clearly and extensively.

Coordination mechanisms:
- [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] describes daily and weekly prompts, optional social questions, and six-week Heartbeats and Kickoffs inside a shared system of record.
- [[no-meetings-no-deadlines-no-full-time-employees]] describes a shared queue, contributor-selected tasks, creator earnings as a north star, and incremental release when work improved production.

Project-scale example:
- [[no-meetings-no-deadlines-no-full-time-employees]] presents Gumroad Memberships as a roughly year-long project shipped without a meeting or video call and shows completed and pending roadmap items.

Meeting-cost rationale:
- [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] counts a meeting's cost across all participants and treats meetings as a last resort.
- [[no-meetings-no-deadlines-no-full-time-employees]] links a no-meeting norm to founder burnout, focus, flexibility, and reported contributor productivity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Both sources are first-party descriptions from small software companies, not comparative studies. Gumroad's revenue, growth, and launch outcomes do not isolate communication mode from product-market fit, worker selection, staffing cost, leadership, or market conditions. Writing can be slow, effortful, inaccessible, or poorly suited to ambiguity, conflict repair, emergencies, sensitive personnel matters, rapid collaborative exploration, multilingual teams, and work requiring tight physical coordination. A 24-hour expectation is still a deadline of sorts, and shared records can create information overload, surveillance concerns, or false confidence that publication equals understanding.

## What Changed
- Added bounded response expectations as a mechanism that joins contributor autonomy with accountability.
- Added shared task queues, self-selection, and one measurable direction as alternatives to meeting-led coordination.
- Added Gumroad Memberships as a qualified example of a large asynchronously coordinated launch.
- Strengthened the selection and causality limits on claims that meeting-free work produces superior results.
