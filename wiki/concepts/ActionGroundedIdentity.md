## Definition
[[ActionGroundedIdentity]] is the principle that a present-tense role or character title should remain answerable to present behavior rather than rest indefinitely on an earlier achievement or self-declaration.

## Current Synthesis
[[DerekSivers]] treats identity language as a claim that can expire. Calling oneself an entrepreneur, athlete, leader, risk-taker, success, or good friend may be accurate only while current conduct continues to support the title; an earlier accomplishment remains real but belongs in the past tense. The practical mechanism is motivational: a flattering label can deliver premature satisfaction, encourage the belief that future performance follows automatically from who one is, and displace the work that would make the label true. Voluntarily retiring an outdated title restores descriptive honesty and creates a choice between accepting the changed identity and resuming the relevant practice.

## Key Claims
- Present-tense role titles should be supported by present conduct.
- Past achievement remains legitimate even when it no longer justifies a current identity claim.
- Self-labels can create premature satisfaction that substitutes for the work they imply.
- Retiring an outdated title can improve self-knowledge by separating past accomplishment from current activity.
- Discomfort at losing a valued title can motivate renewed practice, but the renewed action—not the declaration—earns the title.

## Evidence
- Current conduct: [[keep-earning-your-title-or-it-expires-derek-sivers]] says Sivers stopped calling himself an entrepreneur because years had passed since he started a company and compares this with an inactive former athlete.
- Satisfaction without action: [[keep-earning-your-title-or-it-expires-derek-sivers]] argues that an old title can provide a premature sense of success and make future success seem assured.
- Temporal honesty: [[keep-earning-your-title-or-it-expires-derek-sivers]] recommends speaking in the past tense about completed accomplishments and in the present tense only about current activity.
- Renewed practice: [[keep-earning-your-title-or-it-expires-derek-sivers]] says that someone unwilling to lose a title can respond by doing the work needed to earn it again.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a short first-person motivational essay, not evidence that titles causally reduce effort or that removing them improves performance. Some identities describe durable commitments, qualifications, relationships, communities, or constraints rather than continuous visible output, and periods of illness, caregiving, unemployment, recovery, or incubation do not necessarily erase them. A title can also function prospectively as a goal or as social permission to practice. The useful boundary is therefore accountability between identity and conduct, not compulsory denial of every role during inactivity or dismissal of genuine past achievement.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish identity grounded in current practice from identity resting only on past accomplishment or declaration.

## Related Concepts
- [[SelfDiscipline]] - repeated action supplies the behavioral evidence that keeps a chosen identity current.
- [[GoalSetting]] - an aspirational identity becomes credible when translated into a concrete outcome, path, and practice.
- [[WorkHabits]] - recurring behavior is the observable substrate of an ongoing role.
- [[SelfEfficacy]] - belief in one's capability can support action, while a status label alone does not demonstrate capability or progress.
- [[PerformativeSelfPresentation]] - public identity display can diverge from lived behavior, although Sivers focuses primarily on self-deception rather than audience performance.
