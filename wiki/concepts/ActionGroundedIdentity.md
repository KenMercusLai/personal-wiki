## Definition
[[ActionGroundedIdentity]] is the principle that a present-tense role or character title should remain answerable to present behavior rather than rest indefinitely on an earlier achievement or self-declaration.

## Current Synthesis
[[DerekSivers]] treats both identity language and declared priorities as claims that behavior can test. Calling oneself an entrepreneur, athlete, leader, risk-taker, success, or good friend may be accurate only while current conduct supports the title; likewise, years of not pursuing a stated goal may show that another way of living has greater operative priority. An earlier accomplishment and a weaker unrealized desire can both remain real, but neither proves a current commitment. The practical mechanism is motivational: a flattering label or repeated declaration can deliver premature satisfaction and displace the work that would make it true. Recognizing the mismatch creates a choice between accepting the priority expressed by current conduct and beginning the relevant practice.

## Key Claims
- Present-tense role titles should be supported by present conduct.
- Past achievement remains legitimate even when it no longer justifies a current identity claim.
- Self-labels can create premature satisfaction that substitutes for the work they imply.
- Persistent inaction can expose that a professed goal ranks below another wanted condition.
- Retiring an outdated title can improve self-knowledge by separating past accomplishment from current activity.
- A mismatch between declaration and conduct invites either honest acceptance or renewed practice; action, not repetition of the claim, tests the commitment.

## Evidence
- Current conduct: [[keep-earning-your-title-or-it-expires-derek-sivers]] says Sivers stopped calling himself an entrepreneur because years had passed since he started a company and compares this with an inactive former athlete.
- Satisfaction without action: [[keep-earning-your-title-or-it-expires-derek-sivers]] argues that an old title can provide a premature sense of success and make future success seem assured.
- Temporal honesty: [[keep-earning-your-title-or-it-expires-derek-sivers]] recommends speaking in the past tense about completed accomplishments and in the present tense only about current activity.
- Renewed practice: [[keep-earning-your-title-or-it-expires-derek-sivers]] says that someone unwilling to lose a title can respond by doing the work needed to earn it again.
- Competing priorities: [[actions-not-words-reveal-our-real-values]] interprets Sivers's unlaunched company as evidence that he wanted his simpler life of learning, writing, and parenting more.
- Behavioral test: [[actions-not-words-reveal-our-real-values]] proposes either admitting the priority expressed by inaction or beginning the professed goal to test it in practice.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Both sources are short first-person motivational essays, not evidence that titles causally reduce effort, that removing them improves performance, or that inaction perfectly reveals desire. Some identities describe durable commitments, qualifications, relationships, or communities rather than continuous visible output. Illness, disability, poverty, coercion, caregiving, unemployment, recovery, discrimination, addiction, fear, missing knowledge, and incubation can all separate sincere values from visible conduct. A title can also function prospectively as a goal or as social permission to practice. The useful boundary is accountability between claims and behavior under current conditions, not compulsory denial of every inactive role or moral judgment of every blocked goal.

## What Changed
- Broadened the concept from role titles to stated priorities that repeated inaction can test.
- Qualified behavioral evidence as condition-dependent rather than conclusive proof of desire or moral value.

## Related Concepts
