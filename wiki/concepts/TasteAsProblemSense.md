## Definition
[[TasteAsProblemSense]] is the ability to notice a real, personally compelling question, care enough to investigate it deeply, and judge which materials, evidence, and expressions are worth using.

## Current Synthesis
RORIRI defines taste less as aesthetic polish and more as problem sense. In a world where AI can execute many tasks, the distinct human contribution is finding a real question before execution begins. This problem sense emerges from accumulated model thinking, repeated inquiry, and metacognitive feedback rather than from innate mystery alone.

Hanyang adds a production and material layer: when generation becomes cheap, taste also means knowing what is worth writing, which evidence is strong, which narrative carries force, and when real-world material matters more than the newest model. In this view, taste is not only the start of inquiry but also the editor's judgment over archives, interviews, photographs, examples, and generated variants.

## Key Claims
- AI increases the value gap between problem awareness and execution.
- A real question is one whose answer the learner does not already know and genuinely wants to know.
- Machines can process inputs and outputs, but they do not supply concern, direction, or stance.
- Taste can be cultivated through model thinking, inquiry cycles, and visible growth.
- AI-era taste includes material judgment: knowing which evidence is hard, what deserves attention, and which version is worth signing.
- Real-world materials can outweigh model novelty when the work depends on cultural depth, field evidence, or original archives.

## Evidence
- Value gap: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] argues that execution can be amplified or outsourced while caring about a problem cannot.
- Real question: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] defines human uniqueness around proposing a question one truly wants to understand.
- Machine boundary: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] emphasizes that AI lacks stance, direction, and drive.
- Cultivation path: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] links taste to model thinking that makes people gently suspicious of complete-looking answers.
- Material judgment: [[gei-ren-wen-gong-zuo-zhe-de-ai-shi-yong-zhi-nan]] says work quality in the AI era increasingly resembles "materials x taste," because generation is cheap but worthwhile evidence and judgment are scarce.
- Field grounding: [[gei-ren-wen-gong-zuo-zhe-de-ai-shi-yong-zhi-nan]] argues that complete archives, oral histories, field interviews, and photographs may produce stronger work than newer models using only generic online material.
- Signature test: [[gei-ren-wen-gong-zuo-zhe-de-ai-shi-yong-zhi-nan]] treats willingness to sign the final work as the checkpoint that taste and process have remained human-owned.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources' accounts of taste are philosophical and practitioner-oriented rather than empirical theories of creativity. They also depend on knowledge: RORIRI explicitly notes that higher-order abilities still require factual, conceptual, and procedural knowledge, while Hanyang's account assumes the worker can obtain and judge real materials. Taste language can hide unequal access to archives, fieldwork, time, mentors, and editorial feedback.

## What Changed
- Created this concept to preserve the source's final claim about human value in AI-era education.
- Added material judgment and signature-level editorial responsibility as AI-era dimensions of taste.
