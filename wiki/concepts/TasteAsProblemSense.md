## Definition
[[TasteAsProblemSense]] is the ability to notice a real, personally compelling question and care enough to investigate it deeply.

## Current Synthesis
RORIRI defines taste less as aesthetic polish and more as problem sense. In a world where AI can execute many tasks, the distinct human contribution is finding a real question before execution begins. This problem sense emerges from accumulated model thinking, repeated inquiry, and metacognitive feedback rather than from innate mystery alone.

## Key Claims
- AI increases the value gap between problem awareness and execution.
- A real question is one whose answer the learner does not already know and genuinely wants to know.
- Machines can process inputs and outputs, but they do not supply concern, direction, or stance.
- Taste can be cultivated through model thinking, inquiry cycles, and visible growth.

## Evidence
- Value gap: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] argues that execution can be amplified or outsourced while caring about a problem cannot.
- Real question: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] defines human uniqueness around proposing a question one truly wants to understand.
- Machine boundary: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] emphasizes that AI lacks stance, direction, and drive.
- Cultivation path: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] links taste to model thinking that makes people gently suspicious of complete-looking answers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's account of taste is philosophical and educational rather than an empirical theory of creativity. It also depends on knowledge: the essay explicitly notes that higher-order abilities still require factual, conceptual, and procedural knowledge.

## What Changed
- Created this concept to preserve the source's final claim about human value in AI-era education.
