## Definition
[[StartupFocus]] is the discipline of choosing one strategic path strongly enough that scarce founder time, team attention, and resources are not split across incompatible business models.

## Current Synthesis
The Maderight source frames focus as the courage to choose when both options have evidence and emotional pull. Maderight began as a service company to get paying customers and understand their problems, then saw software adoption on its fourth iteration while existing clients still wanted high-touch service. The team chose to do both, preserving revenue but splitting attention so both the service and product paths suffered. The Groove source adds a simpler filter for day-to-day advice pressure: a business needs to create value and earn profit; every other claimed necessity should be treated as a negotiable tactic until evidence shows it matters for the chosen strategy.

## Key Claims
- "Do both" decisions can hide a refusal to choose between incompatible strategies.
- Existing revenue can make it emotionally hard to abandon a service path.
- Early customer service can be useful for discovery, but it should not automatically remain the business.
- New product adoption may require dropping or deprioritizing old customers if their needs reinforce the wrong model.
- Scarce founder time makes strategic ambiguity operationally expensive.
- Startup advice that does not directly support value creation or profit can become tactic-chasing distraction.

## Evidence
- Service origin: [[4-lessons-from-a-failed-startup-from-and-for-first-time-founders]] says Maderight began as a service company to get customers from day one and learn their problems.
- Product adoption conflict: [[4-lessons-from-a-failed-startup-from-and-for-first-time-founders]] says the fourth software iteration gained adoption while existing clients still wanted the high-touch service.
- Split-cost lesson: [[4-lessons-from-a-failed-startup-from-and-for-first-time-founders]] says choosing both split time and resources and made both projects suffer.
- Value/profit filter: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] says the two true business needs are delivering value and making a profit, while other "must-do" advice should not be accepted as gospel.
- Tactic distraction: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] warns founders against chasing every tactic they are told they need.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not prove that hybrid service/software models always fail or that all non-core tactics are useless. Their shared warning applies when parallel paths, copied advice, or guilt-driven tactics consume scarce founder attention before the team knows whether its current strategy is working.

## What Changed
- Added a value/profit filter for rejecting tactic-chasing and false "must-do" advice.
