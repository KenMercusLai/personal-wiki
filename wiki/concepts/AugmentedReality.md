## Definition
[[AugmentedReality]] is the real-time computational transformation of a camera-mediated view, including both overlays placed into a scene and edits to the captured people or environment.

## Current Synthesis
The source broadens AR beyond the late-1990s vision of information and virtual objects superimposed on external space through glasses. [[Snapchat]] Lenses instead made face tracking, beautification, and playful self-transformation a consumer-scale interaction: the camera became an input method, the captured image became editable, and the result became a social message. This supports a plural model of AR rather than replacing spatial overlays with selfie effects.

## Key Claims
- AR can edit the camera's perceived base layer, not only place graphics above an otherwise unchanged scene.
- The front-facing camera makes the self, not only the surrounding environment, a primary augmentation target.
- Real-time processing turns an image sensor into an interaction input whose output can carry mood, identity, and narrative.
- Rotating effects and social transmission can make AR a recurring product experience rather than a specialist display technology.

## Evidence
- Historical contrast: [[forget-drones-and-spaceships-the-snapchat-dogface-filter-is-the-future]] compares older data-glasses and environmental-annotation forecasts with face-centered mobile use.
- Base-layer transformation: [[forget-drones-and-spaceships-the-snapchat-dogface-filter-is-the-future]] describes Lenses smoothing skin, enlarging eyes, and changing faces into animals or effects in real time.
- Interface role: [[forget-drones-and-spaceships-the-snapchat-dogface-filter-is-the-future]] uses the image-sensor-as-input framing to connect capture, processing, messaging, and social interaction.
- Product recurrence: [[forget-drones-and-spaceships-the-snapchat-dogface-filter-is-the-future]] reports a changing daily set of roughly 15-20 permanent, rotating, and sponsored Lenses.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
One 2016 essay cannot establish the dominant or durable form of AR. Face filters, spatial overlays, industrial guidance, games, and head-worn interfaces can coexist, and the source supplies no comparative use, retention, revenue, technical-performance, or wellbeing data. Its account also blurs beautification, image filtering, face tracking, and augmentation without defining a strict technical boundary among them.

## What Changed
- Established AR as both overlay and real-time transformation of the camera-mediated view.
- Added self-presentation and social messaging as interaction contexts alongside annotation of external space.
- Preserved spatial and glasses-based AR as complementary rather than obsolete.
