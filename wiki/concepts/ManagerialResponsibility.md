## Definition
[[ManagerialResponsibility]] is the view that greater organizational authority increases a leader's obligation to create conditions for others to succeed, absorb difficult work, diagnose failures, develop judgment, and remain accountable for consequential personnel decisions.

## Current Synthesis
[[HenryWard]] presents management as responsibility without privilege. The manager should reinforce demonstrated strengths, approach errors as diagnostic questions, and first test whether communication or training failed. Delegation is not a mechanism for pushing undesirable tasks downward: managers should give away attractive work that develops others and move themselves toward the problems no one wants. The progression from employee to manager to executive is consequently framed as a shift from solving problems to preventing them, not an escape from difficult work.

The same principle changes how authority operates. Senior leaders depend on capable lieutenants who retain their own judgment, relationships, and outside options, so influence and trust become more important than command. Employees should learn to evaluate their own work and ask for advice rather than wait for a grade or an order. When role fit ultimately fails, Ward asks the manager to act quickly, explain the decision, apologize for failures in hiring or support, listen, and offer an honest account of where the person's strengths may fit better.

The useful core is an accountability direction, not a complete personnel system. Managerial ownership should encourage diagnosis and humane action, but it cannot erase employee agency, the need to correct harmful behavior, procedural fairness, evidence, legal duties, or uncertainty in a manager's judgment.

## Key Claims
- Management should amplify strong work while still correcting errors whose impact, recurrence, safety, or ethics make them material.
- Curiosity is a better starting point than blame because mistakes may expose unclear communication, insufficient training, or a mistaken managerial assumption.
- Delegation should distribute desirable, developmental work while responsibility for neglected and difficult problems moves upward.
- Managers develop judgment by teaching self-evaluation and offering advice, not by making employees permanently dependent on ratings or commands.
- Larger scope reduces the practical reach of command because senior leaders must influence capable people with their own expertise, loyalties, and alternatives.
- Humane accountability includes timely role-fit decisions and honest reflection on management's contribution, but also requires fair process and grounded evidence.

## Evidence
- Strengths and diagnosis: [[a-managers-faq-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] argues for reinforcing good work and beginning negative feedback with curiosity about the employee's reasoning.
- Communication and training ownership: [[a-managers-faq-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] says managers should test whether an error followed from an unclear instruction or missing capability support.
- Delegation and hierarchy: [[a-managers-faq-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] recommends delegating work the manager wants and retaining undesirable work, treating promotion as increased responsibility rather than privilege.
- Problem progression: [[a-managers-faq-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] distinguishes managers who resolve present problems from executives who prevent larger ones.
- Judgment development: [[a-managers-faq-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] favors employee self-evaluation and voluntary advice-seeking over institutional grading and direct command.
- Role exits: [[a-managers-faq-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] recommends prompt, candid dismissal when success is judged impossible, paired with apology, listening, and a sincere reference.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one founder's normative 2016 essay about eShares, with no employee accounts, outcome measures, comparison group, or analysis of whether the practices were applied consistently. Its maximization frame can become a false choice: managers need both strength development and timely correction, especially for safety, ethics, harassment, reliability, or repeated high-impact errors. Assuming good intent helps inquiry but should not pre-judge misconduct or remove employee accountability.

The essay's rejection of performance-improvement plans and its claim that dismissal needs no evidentiary case are too categorical. Documentation and a meaningful opportunity to respond can constrain bias, clarify expectations, support accommodation, satisfy policy or law, and sometimes enable recovery. Conversely, a performative plan used only to manufacture a firing record can indeed be deceptive and harmful. Managerial apology is valuable only when paired with concrete accountability; it must not recast every exit as proof that the manager alone caused the outcome.

- Added fair-process, evidence, safety, employee-agency, and legal boundaries to the source's categorical personnel advice.
