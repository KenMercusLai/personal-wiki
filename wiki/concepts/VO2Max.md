## Definition
[[VO2Max]] is a measure of maximum oxygen uptake during exertion, used in the wiki as a marker of cardiorespiratory capacity and as an intensity reference for exercise protocols.

## Current Synthesis
The current evidence gives VO2 max two linked roles. An inspected chart uses it to connect present aerobic capacity with later-life functional reserve: high-, average-, and low-fitness curves decline with age, but higher starting capacity stays above demanding activity thresholds longer. The HICT article uses percentages of VO2 max to distinguish moderate, vigorous, and supramaximal work and summarizes studies in which limited-rest high-intensity protocols produced similar or sometimes greater VO2 max gains than steady-state exercise at lower volume. Together, the sources make VO2 max both a long-term capacity marker and a training outcome, while leaving individual prescription and the exact efficacy of the illustrated seven-minute circuit unresolved.

## Key Claims
- VO2 max is used as a quantified bridge between cardiovascular fitness and functional tasks.
- Age-related decline affects all displayed fitness percentiles, but the curves begin from very different capacity levels.
- Higher VO2 max creates more future capacity reserve because demanding activities have fixed approximate metabolic thresholds.
- High-intensity protocols may improve VO2 max with less exercise volume than steady-state work, but results cannot be transferred automatically across different protocols.
- Very short protocols in the HICT review often require supramaximal intensity; the sample circuit is therefore repeatable rather than a universal seven-minute dose.

## Evidence
- Metric and axes: [[andrew-chen-fun-graph-from-peter-attias-book-outlive]] includes an inspected chart whose y-axis is VO2 max in ml/kg/min and whose x-axis is age in years.
- Percentile comparison: [[andrew-chen-fun-graph-from-peter-attias-book-outlive]] shows high, average, and low fitness curves, labeled as 95th, 50th, and 5th percentiles.
- Activity thresholds: [[andrew-chen-fun-graph-from-peter-attias-book-outlive]] overlays task lines for sleep, walking on flat ground, incline walking, brisk stair climbing, jogging, hill jogging, carrying weight upstairs, and running on flat ground.
- Behavior change: [[andrew-chen-fun-graph-from-peter-attias-book-outlive]] says the graph led Chen to resume running and add cycling-oriented workouts.
- Training response: [[high-intensity-circuit-training-using-body-weight]] summarizes laboratory comparisons reporting similar or sometimes greater VO2 max gains from HICT despite lower exercise volume.
- Intensity and dose: [[high-intensity-circuit-training-using-body-weight]] distinguishes four-minute protocols performed above 100% of VO2 max from more attainable protocols and recommends repeated circuits for most people.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The age-and-capacity source is a brief article centered on one chart and provides no clinical interpretation, sex-specific norms, or later measurement data from Chen. The HICT source is a practitioner review rather than a trial of its exact illustrated routine; it summarizes heterogeneous protocols, populations, and intensities and therefore cannot guarantee that one seven-minute circuit improves VO2 max. Neither source provides an individualized medical or coaching prescription.

## What Changed
- Added VO2 max as both an exercise-intensity reference and a potentially trainable outcome.
- Qualified the seven-minute framing by separating supramaximal research protocols from the sample circuit's repeatable dose.
