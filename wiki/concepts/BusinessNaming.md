## Definition
[[BusinessNaming]] is the process of framing, generating, screening, and testing a company name so it expresses the intended brand while remaining understandable, pronounceable, findable, and operationally usable.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded source treats naming as a design problem with divergent and convergent phases. Define begins with mission and values because a name should connect to the visual and behavioral brand around it. Discover gathers customer and industry language, competitor patterns, and common naming forms so category fit or departure is deliberate rather than accidental.

Develop then favors many ideas over premature selection, using collaborators, sketches, word tools, repeated sessions, and varied settings to widen the candidate set. Deliver groups and removes duplicates, rejects semantic or spelling friction, tests pronunciation and first impressions with unfamiliar people, checks domains and social handles, and compares finalists through behavior or stated preference. The practical judgment is not that one naming style is universally best, but that creative distinctiveness must survive comprehension, availability, and brand-alignment constraints.

## Key Claims
- Mission, values, category, and customer language should supply criteria before candidate names are generated.
- Competitor and naming-pattern research helps a team recognize whether it is following or deliberately breaking category conventions.
- Divergent ideation works best when quantity, collaboration, visual prompts, deferred judgment, and repeated sessions precede selection.
- Convergent screening should test meaning, spelling, typing, pronunciation, emotional association, domain availability, and relevant social handles.
- Landing-page comparisons or audience polls can inform a final choice, but their result is conditional on representative traffic, comparable execution, and an appropriate outcome measure.

## Evidence
- Strategic framing and context: [[need-help-with-business-naming-apply-these-design-thinking-exercises]] starts from company purpose, values, customer language, competitors, and naming conventions.
- Divergent generation: [[need-help-with-business-naming-apply-these-design-thinking-exercises]] recommends high-volume, collaborative, visual, game-like, tool-assisted, and iterative brainstorming.
- Usability and operational filtering: [[need-help-with-business-naming-apply-these-design-thinking-exercises]] screens for semantic simplicity, spelling, pronunciation, impressions, domains, and social availability.
- Final testing: [[need-help-with-business-naming-apply-these-design-thinking-exercises]] proposes matched landing-page tests or social polling for the remaining candidates.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework comes from one 2017 practitioner article, not a comparison of naming methods or later business outcomes. Competitor examples and domain conventions are historically bounded, and category norms may differ by language, geography, customer, and channel. Social polls measure stated preference in a self-selected network; landing-page tests can confound the name with copy, creative, targeting, novelty, and short-run click behavior. Legal clearance, trademark conflict, linguistic review, accessibility, international interpretation, and post-launch learning are not developed, so availability checks are necessary but insufficient.

## What Changed
- Established naming as a staged brand-design process that separates context, divergent generation, screening, and testing.
- Added an explicit tradeoff between memorability or distinctiveness and comprehension, pronunciation, spelling, and online availability.
- Qualified polls and landing-page comparisons as partial evidence rather than automatic decision rules.
