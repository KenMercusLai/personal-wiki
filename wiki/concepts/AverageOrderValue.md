## Definition
[[AverageOrderValue]] is the mean revenue recorded per order over a defined period, commonly calculated as total order revenue divided by order count.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats AOV as a revenue lever distinct from order volume and the listed price of an individual product. Its basic arithmetic shows that doubling average basket value at the same unit count doubles sales, then argues that retailers can pursue that change by making additional purchases easier or more attractive. [[ProductBundling]] does this through complementary products, additional variants, or gift value; Sumo's promoted alternative uses progressive spend-and-discount thresholds after a shopper has added an item to the cart.

The metric must remain subordinate to economics and customer outcomes. A displayed bundle total is only potential basket value, not observed AOV; discounts can raise gross order revenue while reducing contribution margin; and substitution, returns, order frequency, conversion, and customer quality can reverse the apparent gain.

## Key Claims
- AOV separates revenue per completed order from raw order or unit volume.
- Bundles can raise AOV without changing the advertised price of the anchor product.
- Add-to-cart behavior can trigger threshold incentives because it signals stronger purchase intent than a page view alone.
- Potential bundle totals do not establish realized AOV lift or profit.
- AOV should be interpreted with conversion, margin, returns, purchase frequency, and retained customer value.

## Evidence
- Metric arithmetic: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] contrasts 1,000 units at $50 AOV with 1,000 at $100 AOV.
- Complementary basket: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] calculates a Beats bundle rising from $349.99 to $540.97.
- Multi-variant basket: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] calculates $44.97 of additional potential value from four Fingerlings variants.
- Threshold intervention: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] describes escalating percentage discounts tied to additional cart spend.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source provides examples and offer arithmetic rather than transaction data. It does not define the measurement period or treatment of discounts, taxes, shipping, refunds, and cancellations; report offer exposure or take rates; or measure conversion, margin, order frequency, cannibalization, returns, satisfaction, or lifetime value. Its recommendations are hypotheses for merchant testing rather than demonstrated causal effects.

## What Changed
- Established AOV as a distinct ecommerce metric whose apparent lift must be reconciled with conversion, margin, and customer value.
