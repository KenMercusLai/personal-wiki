## Definition
[[FounderSuccessDefinition]] is the founder's explicit, personal description of what business and life success should fund, protect, or make possible.

## Current Synthesis
The source pushes against generic hustle mythology by asking founders to define their own "it." The article's practical move is to describe the desired life concretely, estimate the cost of funding it, and build the business backward from that target. This can reduce unnecessary overwork when a founder's real goal is not a maximal-status company but a satisfying work life, family support, location freedom, and enough financial security.

Pigford adds a psychological reason for doing this work: without a personal definition of “made it,” business growth may move the comparison target rather than end self-doubt. More revenue and visible success can coexist with greater stress, public scrutiny, and [[ImposterSyndrome]], leaving “fake it till you make it” without a meaningful stopping rule.

## Key Claims
- Founders should not inherit someone else's definition of success.
- Hustle requirements depend on the goal being pursued, not on a universal founder identity.
- A concrete desired lifestyle can be translated into a financial target.
- Working backward from that target may reveal that less extreme growth or overwork is required.
- An undefined success threshold can recede as a company grows, allowing external achievement and internal insufficiency to coexist.

## Evidence
- Personal definition: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] contrasts Gary Vaynerchuk's ambition with the author's simpler picture of meaningful work, family support, and living near the water.
- Financial translation: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] recommends calculating what the ideal lifestyle costs and building backward from it.
- Anti-hustle qualification: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] says founders may need to hustle less than they thought if their real target is different.
- Psychological endpoint: [[confronting-imposter-syndrome-as-a-startup-founder-baremetrics]] asks when a founder has “made it” after reporting that more money brought more mental pressure rather than less stress.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not deny that unusually large ambitions may require unusually intense work. Their claim is that founders should make that tradeoff consciously instead of absorbing a default culture of constant hustle or assuming a financial milestone will automatically produce confidence. A personal success definition may improve judgment, but it does not by itself resolve clinical distress or structural business pressure.

## What Changed
- Added the psychological stopping-rule function: defining “made it” can keep growth from becoming an indefinitely receding confidence threshold.
