## Definition
[[UserTrustCapital]] is accumulated goodwill from reliable, considerate product experiences and communication that increases the chance users will interpret failures or major changes charitably.

## Current Synthesis
[[SarahTavel]] describes user trust as a bank balance: products make deposits through useful everyday experiences, careful error copy, responsive support, and candid communication, then make withdrawals when the service fails or a product change disrupts expectations. A surplus does not erase harm, but it can give teams room to recover from outages and attempt bold changes without every ambiguity being interpreted negatively.

At the shorter time horizon described by [[HierarchyOfTrust]], a new visitor needs enough evidence of relevance, preference, and control for the site's immediate ask before goodwill can accumulate. A forced login, federated-account connection, or address request can overdraw a trust balance that has not yet been established. Clear positioning, representative content, credible proof, free exploration, and reversibility can act as early deposits because they let the visitor evaluate the product before surrendering information.

Together, the sources connect first-use request calibration with longer-run organizational resilience. Trust is produced across product, support, interaction design, and communication rather than by brand messaging alone. Both accounts remain practitioner models, so teams still need direct evidence about affected segments, abandonment, trust transfer across contexts, severity, repair, and whether goodwill actually changes behavior.

## Key Claims
- Trust accumulates through repeated product and service experiences rather than one campaign.
- Core product quality, error messages, support, and change communication all contribute to the balance.
- Negative experiences can consume goodwill faster than routine positive experiences build it.
- A trust surplus can make users more forgiving during outages and more open to unfamiliar product changes.
- Early requests should not assume goodwill that a new visitor has had no chance to accumulate.
- Trust capital is not permission to ignore harm, accountability, or the needs of users affected by a change.

## Evidence
- Deposit channels: [[five-lessons-from-scaling-pinterest-sarah-tavel-medium]] names product delight, error copy, help-desk support, and update communication as trust-building work.
- Outage response: [[five-lessons-from-scaling-pinterest-sarah-tavel-medium]] says Pinterest users sometimes thanked the team after candid social updates during outages.
- Product-risk interpretation: [[five-lessons-from-scaling-pinterest-sarah-tavel-medium]] argues that accumulated goodwill shifts users toward a more positive default interpretation of bold changes.
- First-use calibration: [[hierarchy-of-trust-the-5-experiential-levels-of-website-commitment]] maps escalating asks from relevance through personal and financial information to an ongoing relationship.
- Premature withdrawals: [[hierarchy-of-trust-the-5-experiential-levels-of-website-commitment]] shows ThredUp and Nextdoor asking for registration or address data before visitors could adequately inspect the offer.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The Pinterest source's claim that one negative experience requires five positive experiences is hearsay without a cited study and should not be treated as a calibrated exchange rate. The hierarchy source likewise offers no causal evidence that every visitor traverses five discrete levels in order. Public thanks during an outage may overrepresent vocal supporters, while historical gate screenshots do not measure abandonment. Trust is context-specific: goodwill in one area may not transfer to privacy, safety, billing, accessibility, or repeated reliability failures, and some harms require remedy rather than communication.

## What Changed
- Added first-use trust as a prerequisite to accumulating longer-run goodwill.
- Distinguished an escalating request from a justified one: registration, linked accounts, or address data can overdraw trust before relevance is established.
