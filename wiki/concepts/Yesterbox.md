## Definition
[[Yesterbox]] is an email-processing method that makes yesterday's inbox the primary bounded queue for today's work while allowing genuinely quick new messages to be answered or delegated immediately.

## Current Synthesis
The method tries to turn an open-ended stream into a finishable daily list. [[TonyHsieh]] reportedly devoted substantial scheduled time to the previous day's messages, used short acknowledgments to keep communication moving, and delegated work that did not require him. The article also makes clear that the personal routine was supported by a chief-of-staff team: at the reported volume, an individual executive could not process every message alone. Calendar blocks then carried proactive or longer work that email alone could not represent well.

## Key Claims
- Delaying the main queue by one day creates a stable daily processing boundary.
- Very short replies and obvious delegation can be handled immediately without reopening the whole current-day stream.
- Acknowledgment can reduce uncertainty for senders even when a complete answer must wait.
- High-volume executive email requires triage, delegation, and ownership rules as well as individual discipline.
- A calendar complements the inbox by representing duration, capacity, travel, meetings, and proactive work.

## Evidence
- Bounded queue: [[i-studied-the-zappos-ceos-schedule-for-a-year-heres-what-i-learned]] describes yesterday's inbox as today's to-do list.
- Quick handling: [[i-studied-the-zappos-ceos-schedule-for-a-year-heres-what-i-learned]] recommends immediate short replies or delegation when little work is required.
- Capacity boundary: [[i-studied-the-zappos-ceos-schedule-for-a-year-heres-what-i-learned]] reports at least 379 incoming messages per day and says a chief-of-staff team triaged and handled much of the remainder.
- Calendar complement: [[i-studied-the-zappos-ceos-schedule-for-a-year-heres-what-i-learned]] uses calendar blocks for proactive, long-term, and more intensive tasks.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is an observational article about one executive and the author's adaptation, not a controlled comparison. Its throughput figures are internally inconsistent, and the underlying schedule dataset is not included in the saved source. A one-day delay may be unsuitable for emergencies, customer support, incident response, regulated deadlines, or other time-sensitive work. Quick replies can also create interruption, shallow decisions, and more email unless teams define escalation, delegation, and response expectations.

## What Changed
- Created Yesterbox as a bounded-queue variant of email task management.
- Made team triage and calendar capacity explicit rather than presenting the method as personal willpower alone.
