## Definition
[[ObjectOrientedOntology]] is the philosophical framework, associated here with [[GrahamHarman]], that treats objects as more than their relations, uses, appearances, or effects.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents object-oriented ontology as a way to resist reducing objects to what humans or other objects encounter. Objects have dimensions that withdraw from both practical use and theoretical description. Harman's vocabulary of real objects, sensual objects, real qualities, and sensual qualities gives the essay a grammar for discussing art and metaphor without claiming that perception or language fully reveals the thing itself.

## Key Claims
- Objects are not exhausted by their practical function, perceptual appearance, or conceptual description.
- Relations between objects are translations rather than full disclosures of essence.
- Real objects remain withdrawn, while sensual objects are the appearances or projections available in experience.
- Real qualities belong to withdrawn objects and require intellectual exploration, while sensual qualities appear in experience.
- The framework can be extended from ontology into aesthetics by asking how art and metaphor stage contact with withdrawn objects.

## Evidence
- Anti-reduction claim: [[cong-hai-de-ge-er-de-chui-zi-dao-yin-yu-de-xi-ju-xing]] says objects cannot be exhausted by relations with humans or other objects.
- Translation model: [[cong-hai-de-ge-er-de-chui-zi-dao-yin-yu-de-xi-ju-xing]] describes each object relation as a translation rather than a total presentation.
- Object and quality schema: [[cong-hai-de-ge-er-de-chui-zi-dao-yin-yu-de-xi-ju-xing]] summarizes Harman's real/sensual objects and real/sensual qualities.
- Aesthetic extension: [[cong-hai-de-ge-er-de-chui-zi-dao-yin-yu-de-xi-ju-xing]] applies the schema to art and metaphor.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is an expository philosophical essay rather than a systematic defense of object-oriented ontology. It explains the framework through literary and artistic examples, so the page should treat its claims as source-scoped unless later sources add broader philosophical debate or criticism.
