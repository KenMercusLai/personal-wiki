## Definition
[[SEOConsultantSelection]] is the evaluation and governance of a search-engine-optimization provider through relevant experience, transparent methods, realistic outcomes, staged access, independent corroboration, and explicit owner accountability.

## Current Synthesis
Hiring an SEO is not merely a purchase of ranking improvement. The consultant may influence site structure, content, redirects, JavaScript, campaigns, keywords, and reputation, so the engagement combines marketing judgment, technical change, delegated access, and platform-policy risk. The useful decision frame starts by deciding whether outside help is needed at all: a small business may perform much of the work itself, while a redesign or launch can justify early professional input because foundational structure is still changeable.

Selection should test both competence and conduct. Ask for relevant work, guideline adherence, industry and geographic experience, techniques, expected timeframes, success measures, communication practices, and an explanation of the client's customers and economics. Then verify references and commission a technical and search audit with read-only access before granting greater authority. The client should require clear reasoning, corroborate recommendations, track every change, and distinguish organic work from advertising spend.

The negative signals are unusually concrete: guaranteed first place, secret methods, claims of privileged submission, unsolicited pitches, shadow domains controlled by the consultant, keyword-stuffed doorway pages, hidden links, paid link schemes, fake search traffic, falsified identities, or fees disguised as organic inclusion. These practices can transfer control, reputation, and search-policy risk to the provider while leaving the site owner responsible for the consequences.

## Key Claims
- SEO engagements combine technical, content, marketing, access-control, and reputation risk rather than offering a simple purchasable ranking outcome.
- Basic owner literacy improves selection because it makes discouraged techniques and implausible promises easier to recognize.
- Relevant experience, method transparency, references, realistic timelines, and explicit measurement are core diligence evidence.
- A paid audit with read-only Search Console access creates a safer evidence stage before implementation or broader permissions.
- Organic search work must be separated from paid advertising in both expected outcomes and fee allocation.
- Site owners retain responsibility for consultant-made changes and need visibility, corroboration, and control over domains, content, links, and access.
- Ranking guarantees, secrecy, shadow domains, doorway pages, link schemes, hidden cross-client links, and false platform relationships are strong rejection signals.

## Evidence
- Scope and timing: [[do-you-need-an-seo-search-console-help]] lists content review, technical advice, development, campaigns, keyword research, training, and market expertise, and says launch or redesign planning is an advantageous hiring point.
- Diligence and measurement: [[do-you-need-an-seo-search-console-help]] recommends checking past work, references, methods, relevant experience, expected results, timeframes, measures, communication, and understanding of the client's business.
- Staged authority: [[do-you-need-an-seo-search-console-help]] recommends paying for an audit and initially granting read-only rather than write access to Search Console.
- Economic separation: [[do-you-need-an-seo-search-console-help]] says Google advertising does not improve organic ranking and warns clients to distinguish permanent inclusion claims from temporary advertising fees.
- Abuse patterns: [[do-you-need-an-seo-search-console-help]] identifies shadow domains, doorway pages, hidden links, link schemes, fake engines, aliases, secrecy, and guaranteed rankings as warning signs.
- Accountability: [[do-you-need-an-seo-search-console-help]] says owners remain responsible for hired companies' actions and should require explanations and trusted corroboration.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one platform-authored guidance page, not a comparative study of consultant selection or SEO outcomes. Its four-month-to-one-year result window is a broad heuristic whose applicability depends on the site, competition, market, implementation, and search-system changes. The saved page also contains legacy terminology and broken local-form links despite a 2026 update field, so current operational rules need confirmation in live documentation. Following the checklist cannot guarantee competence, ethical conduct, ranking improvement, or freedom from search penalties; a technically valid recommendation can still be commercially poor, and an ethical provider cannot control platform rankings.

## What Changed
- Created a staged diligence model that joins consultant competence, transparent methods, access control, and owner accountability.
- Separated organic search outcomes from paid placement and treated fee clarity as part of selection.
- Added concrete rejection signals for control-transfer, manipulation, and false-guarantee risks.
