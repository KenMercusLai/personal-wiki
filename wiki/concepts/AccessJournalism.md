## Definition
[[AccessJournalism]] is reporting shaped by dependence on continued proximity to influential subjects, creating a tradeoff between information gained through access and scrutiny that might endanger it.

## Current Synthesis
The Code Conference case shows access as a social and institutional incentive rather than only an explicit bargain. Exclusive invitations, mentorship, friendship, informal conversation, hospitality, and repeated contact can make sources easier to understand and approach while normalizing their assumptions or raising the cost of confrontation. The risk is diffuse: coverage may become softer without any direct censorship or agreement.

## Key Claims
- Access can improve information gathering while simultaneously weakening adversarial distance.
- The expectation of future invitations or interviews can shape questions and coverage before any source applies explicit pressure.
- Shared elite settings can normalize subjects' premises and status, turning extraordinary claims into agenda-setting statements rather than claims to test.
- Transparency about relationships and incentives helps readers evaluate coverage but does not remove their effects.

## Evidence
- Proximity tradeoff: [[ground-control-to-silicon-valley]] argues that getting closer can mean knowing more and saying less to preserve access.
- Conference mechanism: [[ground-control-to-silicon-valley]] describes curated attendance, luxury hospitality, mentoring and friendship ties, and informal access to executives and investors.
- Coverage effect: [[ground-control-to-silicon-valley]] contrasts intensive coverage of Musk's speculative remarks with limited scrutiny of antitrust, diversity, platform governance, and political influence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a critical interpretation of one 2016 conference, not a comparative study of interview toughness or published coverage. Access can also enable informed questioning, disclosures do not prove compromised reporting, and particular journalists may resist social incentives. The mechanism should therefore be evaluated through question quality, omitted context, correction, sourcing, and independence rather than inferred from proximity alone.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from the Code Conference case and bounded it as an incentive risk rather than an automatic failure of independence.
