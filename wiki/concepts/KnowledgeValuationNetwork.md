## Definition
[[KnowledgeValuationNetwork]] is the mental and neural valuation framework that assigns value to knowledge, making some information feel meaningful, coherent, useful, desirable, or worth pursuing.

## Current Synthesis
The wiki now treats knowledge valuation at three linked levels. At the cognitive level, [[PiotrWozniak]] describes the valuation network as a fast, personalized system that judges knowledge by coherence, consistency, and especially applicability to personal goals. Semantic links let value propagate from goals to details, which is why expert choices can feel immediate even when the person cannot fully explain the decision tree behind them.

At the educational level, learners need a way to value knowledge before they can reliably enjoy learning. Goals can act as organizing centers in that valuation network, helping otherwise scattered facts connect to dreams, role models, missions, or big questions. [[FreeLearning]], play, broad behavioral space, and low chronic stress are presented as conditions that let [[LearnDrive]] follow high-value information, while [[CoerciveLearning]] and [[ReverseLearning]] can attach negative value to study contexts and suppress memory consolidation.

At the existential level, [[PiotrWozniak]] argues that ordinary value judgments form chains of instrumental value: one thing matters because it helps produce another valued thing. Since current science does not establish a final intrinsic value, Wozniak treats knowledge growth and reality modeling as the rational response to uncertainty. In that sense, valuation is not only a motivational tool for learning but also the open problem that learning is trying to resolve.

## Key Claims
- Knowledge valuation depends heavily on applicability to personal goals, not only novelty or probability.
- Goals, role models, missions, and emotionally salient cases can serve as centers around which knowledge receives value.
- Strong semantic networks can transmit value quickly from goals to details, supporting fast expert judgment.
- [[LearnDrive]] is strongest when available information matches the learner's current semantic knowledge and valuation signals.
- [[FreeLearning]] and healthy developmental conditions strengthen valuation networks, while coercive schooling and reverse learning can damage them.
- Wozniak links knowledge valuation to OFC-centered neural valuation and the [[CommonCurrencyModel]], while acknowledging unresolved neuroscience debates.
- Ordinary value judgments often derive from later outcomes, so continued knowledge growth is framed as the defensible response when ultimate value remains scientifically uncertain.

## Evidence
- Valuation need: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] says that without goals it is hard to value knowledge, and without valuation it is hard to develop learning pleasure.
- Goal center: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] describes goals as valuation centers that can support rich mental connections.
- Emotional seeds: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] suggests inspiring practitioners, missions, and dramatic cases as ways to awaken passion.
- Enjoyment and efficiency: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] says interest seeds can make required learning more pleasant and effective.
- General principles: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] argues that law students should seek big-picture theories and golden rules instead of memorizing all details.
- Applicability: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] says knowledge valuation mainly depends on how applicable knowledge is to personal goals.
- Fast semantic valuation: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] compares the network to a decision tree where goals and emotions set root values and semantic links pass value to details in milliseconds.
- Image evidence: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] includes a semantic-link graph with knowledge as a hub connected to learning, philosophy, science, memory, spaced repetition, SuperMemo, sleep, and incremental reading.
- Educational conditions: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] argues that low chronic stress, play, broad behavioral space, and [[FreeLearning]] help build healthy valuation networks.
- Neural model: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] discusses OFC-centered valuation and [[CommonCurrencyModel]] as possible mechanisms for integrating knowledge and reward signals.
- Negative learning: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] says [[CoerciveLearning]] and [[ReverseLearning]] can lower valuation and block durable memory consolidation.
- Instrumental chains: [[nei-zai-jia-zhi-zhuang-tai]] models ordinary value as derived from the probability of reaching some further valued outcome.
- Ultimate uncertainty: [[nei-zai-jia-zhi-zhuang-tai]] says science cannot currently determine intrinsic value, so inquiry into reality becomes the rational shared purpose.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept remains theoretical and partly source-scoped. The educational sources do not provide independent empirical tests for the network metaphor, the neuroscience discussion acknowledges debate over common valuation signals, and the intrinsically valuable state source does not prove that objective intrinsic value exists. The wiki therefore treats knowledge valuation as Wozniak's motivational, educational, neural, and philosophical framework rather than a settled scientific model.

## What Changed
- Added the source's fuller model of knowledge valuation as fast, goal-rooted, semantic, and partly neural.
- Added the education-development contrast between [[FreeLearning]] and [[CoerciveLearning]].
- Added reverse learning and common-currency valuation as qualifications to the earlier motivational account.
- Preserved the existing philosophical layer about whether objective intrinsic value grounds value chains.
