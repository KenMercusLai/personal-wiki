## Definition
[[DeveloperLedTechnicalCulture]] is an organizational pattern in which leaders retain hands-on technical work, earn authority through demonstrated craft, use visible work as selection evidence, and give capable contributors substantial autonomy.

## Current Synthesis
The MySQL story presents [[MontyWidenius]] as an extreme version of this pattern. He reportedly remained primarily a programmer as CTO, rejected calendar-driven management, earned developer respect by doing the same work at a high level, and hired self-directed people. Open-source participation made code quality, questions, and community conduct observable before employment, giving [[MySQLAB]] a work-sample-rich recruiting channel.

This model can preserve technical depth and reduce coordination overhead, but the source does not show that direct technical excellence substitutes for management. A 550-person company necessarily depended on other leaders and systems that the profile barely describes. The useful synthesis is therefore leadership proximity plus delegated autonomy, not heroic individual coding as a complete operating model.

## Key Claims
- Hands-on technical leaders can earn practitioner trust through visible competence and shared work.
- Public contribution supplies richer evidence of code quality and collaboration than a short interview alone.
- Selecting self-directed employees can reduce the need for close supervision and preserve leader maker-time.
- Technical authority and organizational scale can coexist when non-coding responsibilities are genuinely distributed.
- Exceptional individual skill is not a general staffing system and should not erase collective contribution or management work.

## Evidence
- Craft authority: [[founder-stories-a-hackers-hacker-openocean]] quotes Widenius saying developers respect a leader who does their work better.
- Continued coding: [[founder-stories-a-hackers-hacker-openocean]] says Widenius spent most of his time coding even while serving as CTO.
- Hiring evidence: [[founder-stories-a-hackers-hacker-openocean]] says open-source code, questions, and community interaction revealed candidate quality more clearly than interviews.
- Autonomy: [[founder-stories-a-hackers-hacker-openocean]] attributes the low-supervision model to hiring self-driving people.
- Distributed roles: [[founder-stories-a-hackers-hacker-openocean]] says David Axmark and Allan Larsson handled work outside Widenius's coding focus, though it does not detail later leadership structure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a celebratory profile centered on an unusually skilled founder and provides no delivery, reliability, retention, employee, meeting-load, or comparative management evidence. “Self-driving” can conceal missing support or coordination, public contribution can exclude capable people without spare time or open-source access, and coding superiority does not establish skill in hiring, feedback, conflict, strategy, inclusion, or organizational design.

## What Changed
- Created the concept while narrowing the source's heroic-programmer story into a qualified model of visible craft, work-sample hiring, delegation, and autonomy.
