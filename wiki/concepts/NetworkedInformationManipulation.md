## Definition
[[NetworkedInformationManipulation]] is the coordinated exploitation of platform distribution, social participation, and media incentives to alter what becomes visible, how events are framed, whom audiences trust, or whether targets feel safe speaking.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents manipulation as a system-level practice rather than a synonym for fake content. Participants can design material for spread, coordinate reposting, game rankings or trending systems, trigger journalistic coverage, encode in-group meanings, harass visible targets, and distribute mutually reinforcing claims across channels. Old and new media then interact: platforms supply reach and measurable signals, while publishers amplify material that appears newsworthy, popular, controversial, or balanced.

These mechanics do not determine motive. Similar knowledge can support marketing, activism, jokes, propaganda, political organization, or harassment. The source's historical judgment is that scale, coordination, and strategic purpose transformed a familiar repertoire: tactics used for transgressive play could be redirected toward reputational, psychological, economic, social, or physical harm and toward weakening confidence in institutions.

## Key Claims
- Manipulation operates through coupled technical and social systems: platform rules, algorithms, participant coordination, journalistic incentives, and audience interpretation.
- Visibility can be engineered without centralized command when many participants understand how to create, repeat, modify, rank, and amplify material.
- The same distribution techniques can serve playful, commercial, civic, ideological, or abusive ends, so intent and impact require separate evaluation.
- Harassment, doxing, swatting, gaslighting, dog whistles, and propaganda can suppress speech or manufacture uncertainty without relying on one plainly false claim.
- Networked tools increase the attainable scale, coordination, ambiguity, and strategic power of manipulation, even when individual acts remain legal or policy-adjacent.
- Platforms, journalism, and law struggle when harmful networks adapt faster than institution-specific rules and exploit the boundaries among abuse categories.

## Evidence
- Distributed visibility: [[hacking-the-attention-economy-data-society-points]] describes meme mutation, Oprah trolling, TIME 100 voting, rickrolling, trending campaigns, and Kony 2012 as varied efforts to shape attention.
- Transferable mechanics: [[hacking-the-attention-economy-data-society-points]] places teenage trolls, marketers, activists, political consultants, law enforcement, and hate groups in the same evolving media ecosystem without equating their goals.
- Escalating harm: [[hacking-the-attention-economy-data-society-points]] connects Gamergate-era doxing and swatting with reputational, psychological, economic, social, and physical risk.
- Epistemic disruption: [[hacking-the-attention-economy-data-society-points]] treats gaslighting, distributed ambiguity, suggestive framing, and encoded dog whistles as ways to induce doubt and evade simple content rules.
- Institutional mismatch: [[hacking-the-attention-economy-data-society-points]] argues that communication companies, legal instruments, and abuse controls were poorly adapted to coordinated networks operating near policy and legal boundaries.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is synthesized from one 2017 interpretive essay, not a representative campaign dataset or causal evaluation. Selected successful or notorious cases can overstate how reliably actors control public attention; the source does not examine failed campaigns, counter-mobilization, platform variation, audience resistance, or later enforcement changes. It also compresses distinct phenomena—marketing, activism, trolling, harassment, propaganda, misinformation, and institutional distrust—whose ethics and mechanisms overlap without being identical. Decentralization does not imply absence of influential organizers, and encoded or ambiguous speech is not necessarily manipulative.

## What Changed
- Created a concept that joins platform gaming, decentralized coordination, media amplification, harassment, and epistemic doubt without reducing manipulation to false news.
- Made motive, legality, social acceptability, and impact separate analytical dimensions.
