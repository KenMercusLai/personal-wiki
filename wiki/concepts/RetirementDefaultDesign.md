## Definition
[[RetirementDefaultDesign]] is the institutional choice architecture that preconfigures participation, contribution, matching, investment allocation, rebalancing, age-based risk changes, and withdrawal friction in long-term retirement saving.

## Current Synthesis
The source contrasts voluntary tax-advantaged accounts that require repeated active decisions with employer-linked plans that make saving recur through payroll and supply prefilled answers. Tax relief appeals to calculation, employer matching makes non-participation feel like a loss, and automatic enrollment works through inertia. Qualified default investments then direct participants toward diversified funds that rebalance and become more conservative with age, while portability and withdrawal rules help preserve the long horizon.

The value does not come from defaulting alone. A default is a high-leverage routing rule and can create harm if it channels passive money into a recent winner, sector fund, expensive product, or overly conservative cash holding. Defensible retirement defaults therefore need product standards, diversification, low cost, age and liquidity fit, portability, reversibility where appropriate, and governance against conflicted distribution. They complement contribution capacity and access; they cannot solve low wages, unequal plan coverage, or the transfer of investment risk to households.

## Key Claims
- Automatic payroll contribution changes saving from a repeated discretionary decision into a recurring flow.
- Employer matching combines an immediate financial return with a salient loss from failing to participate.
- Automatic enrollment can raise participation because inertia now preserves saving rather than postponement.
- Qualified diversified defaults can complete asset allocation and age-based rebalancing for participants who never make an active selection.
- Portability and early-withdrawal friction help preserve the intended long-term horizon across job changes and short-term temptation.
- Default power requires fiduciary product standards because inertia can magnify harmful as easily as beneficial allocation.
- Retirement defaults cannot substitute for plan access, spare household income, transparent costs, or protection against conflicted fund menus.

## Evidence
Three behavioral levers:
- [[da-shui-yu-rong-qi]] contrasts tax incentives with employer matching and automatic enrollment, including a reported participation change from 37% to 86% after enrollment became opt-out.

Qualified investment default:
- [[da-shui-yu-rong-qi]] describes U.S. rules protecting employers when default money enters target-date, balanced, or professionally managed diversified products rather than individual stocks or single-sector funds.

Recurring and stabilizing structure:
- [[da-shui-yu-rong-qi]] describes payroll-timed contributions, low participant trading, automatic rebalancing, and limited crisis-era contribution stopping as mechanisms that reduce procyclical behavior.

Access and transfer limits:
- [[da-shui-yu-rong-qi]] notes incomplete employer-plan coverage, weaker benefits for people with little room to save, commercially shaped fund menus, and the movement of investment risk from employers to individuals.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is an unsourced cross-country synthesis rather than a causal evaluation supplied with underlying studies. Reported participation, asset, trading, and contribution figures come from different years and populations. The article explicitly warns against copying 401(k) wholesale because Chinese pension pillars, employer costs, labor conditions, product markets, and regulatory capacity differ. Automatic equity exposure can still lose money, age alone is not a complete risk model, and withdrawal penalties can harm households facing emergencies. Default effectiveness should be evaluated alongside coverage, adequacy, fees, liquidity, fiduciary governance, and distributional effects.

## What Changed
- Established retirement saving as a sequence of participation, contribution, allocation, rebalancing, portability, and withdrawal defaults.
- Added qualified-product governance as the condition that turns inertia from a risk into a long-term saving aid.
- Preserved access, affordability, liquidity, and cross-country transfer limits.
