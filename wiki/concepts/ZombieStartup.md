## Definition
[[ZombieStartup]] is a company that preserves the external appearance and continued funding of a startup while showing too little product, customer, or execution momentum to offer the growth trajectory prospective employees expect.

## Current Synthesis
[[MathiasPastor]] uses the label as a candidate-side risk category, not merely as a description of a small company. The danger is a mismatch between venture-style promise and operating reality: fundraising, prizes, press, and recruiting can keep the organization visible while weak product progress, selling, team incentives, or information flow make meaningful growth unlikely. Because an employee cannot diversify their working time like a venture portfolio, staying has a concentrated [[OpportunityCost]].

The practical response is evidence-based selection. Candidates should inspect who funded the company and why, whether it has shipped and sold, whether the broader team shares in the upside, whether a role delegates proven work or searches for a rescuer, and whether employees can see enough of the business to judge its condition.

## Key Claims
- Startup appearance, continued financing, and survival do not by themselves demonstrate momentum or venture-scale prospects.
- Product launches, sales, customer value, and execution are stronger signals than awards, publicity, or an unlabeled funding round.
- Weak team incentives, desperation hiring, and secrecy can prolong the appearance of progress while transferring risk to employees.
- Employees bear concentrated timing risk because years spent waiting cannot be diversified across several companies at once.
- Avoidance depends on combining company evidence with conversations with current employees, not on any single red flag.

## Evidence
- Appearance versus momentum: [[avoiding-zombie-startups]] defines zombie startups as companies that look like startups but move slowly and are likely to remain small while weakly discriminating funding keeps them alive.
- Output over prestige: [[avoiding-zombie-startups]] contrasts building and selling with prizes, pitching, publicity, and fundraising headlines.
- Organizational signals: [[avoiding-zombie-startups]] recommends testing team equity incentives, the real reason for a hire, and founders' internal transparency.
- Concentrated career cost: [[avoiding-zombie-startups]] argues that an employee may need several startup attempts to find valuable equity and therefore pays heavily for staying too long in a stagnant one.
- Triangulation: [[avoiding-zombie-startups]] recommends investigating backers and speaking with multiple employees rather than relying on founder recruiting claims.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept comes from one 2019 practitioner essay and is not backed by a prospective study showing which signals predict company outcomes. "Zombie" is a polemical label that can confuse deliberate small-business scale, patient research, regulated product development, public-interest work, or capital-efficient survival with failure. Public funding, inexperienced investors, awards, press, slow growth, or founder uncertainty may be weak evidence individually; the useful judgment concerns the pattern and the mismatch between claims and observable progress.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish continued startup appearance from evidence of product, customer, and execution momentum.
- Made employee time concentration and opportunity cost central to the risk.
- Preserved the distinction between a stagnant venture-style company and a healthy business designed to remain small.
