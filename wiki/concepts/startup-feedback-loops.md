Startup feedback loops are the ordinary mechanisms that keep a growing team from guessing how it is doing. In Anand Sanwal's CB Insights reflection, several mistakes are failures of feedback design: not doing enough one-on-ones, lacking 360 feedback, giving unclear instructions, weak onboarding, poor post-demo follow-up, and insufficient communication about what different teams were working on.

The concept is broader than performance feedback. The source shows loops between leadership and employees, product and business development, sales and prospects, customer success and users, and founder priorities and company direction. When these loops are absent, people may still be working hard, but each function learns too late what the others already know.

Feedback loops also need cadence. Sanwal's proposed repairs are not heroic interventions; they are recurring practices such as scheduled one-on-ones, systematic peer feedback, better onboarding, clearer requirements, more customer meetings, and regular team updates. Their value is that they expose confusion while it is still small enough to correct.

The Groove source adds two outside-company loops. Regular relationships with smart founders, mentors, and advisors give founders a place to take hard decisions before isolation distorts judgment. Frequent customer conversations create a second loop by revealing customer personas, usage patterns, frustrations, and hidden churn risk before those signals appear in dashboards.

The How-do-you-feel dinners source adds a founder-level loop. Instead of waiting for role or strategy arguments to expose drift, founders periodically discuss personal life, work, and desired changes in a setting where others ask clarifying questions before debating fixes. That makes emotional load, family constraints, envy, runway anxiety, and ownership fears visible before they harden into grudges.

## Failure Signals

- People discover company direction, product changes, or role expectations by accident.
- Feedback arrives only during annual reviews or after a sales trial has already gone cold.
- New hires are expected to infer how the company works without a real onboarding path.
- Customer learning stays inside sales or customer success instead of reaching product decisions.
- Founders rely on generic online advice while lacking trusted people and customers who can react to the actual business context.
- Founders argue about titles, ownership, or strategy while personal motivations and constraints stay unstated.
