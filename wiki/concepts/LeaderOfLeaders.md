## Definition
[[LeaderOfLeaders]] is a scaling leadership model in which a founder or executive stops serving as the universal operating hub and instead develops durable senior owners, teaches decision principles, audits written reasoning and outcomes, and reserves direct attention for selected high-leverage or long-horizon work.

## Current Synthesis
Fortune presents [[JeffBezos]] as a concrete transition from founder-centered control to leadership through other leaders. Amazon's size and Bezos's simultaneous commitments to [[WashingtonPost]] and [[BlueOrigin]] made universal involvement impossible. Long-tenured lieutenants therefore owned large operating domains while annual planning, six-page narratives, recurring reviews, customer-focused questions, and selective deep dives gave Bezos visibility and influence. The shift was not withdrawal: Post product leaders call him pushy, Blue Origin devoted substantial recurring time to reviews, and Amazon executives say he still audited plans. The change was from prescribing most work to teaching and refining how other leaders reasoned.

## Key Claims
- Organizational scale turns a founder who remains the decision hub into a bottleneck.
- Durable senior leaders make selective founder attention possible because they preserve operating context over time.
- Written narratives and recurring reviews can provide oversight without reclaiming every decision.
- Delegation works best with explicit authority boundaries, such as the Post's separation of editorial judgment from owner-led product pressure.
- Patient funding and tolerance for failed experiments expand the option set available to delegated leaders.
- Selective depth remains part of the model: the leader chooses future-facing or high-leverage areas rather than becoming uniformly hands-off.

## Evidence
- Leadership transition: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] quotes Amazon director Patty Stonesifer saying Bezos moved from being the center of a hub-and-spoke system to a leader of leaders.
- Teaching rather than prescription: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] quotes [[JeffWilke]] describing Bezos's later style as teaching and refining.
- Operating mechanisms: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] describes Amazon annual plans, six-page narratives, Post management calls, and Blue Origin operating and vehicle reviews.
- Authority boundaries: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] reports that Bezos pressed Post technology and customer-experience work while leaving coverage to [[MartyBaron]].
- Experiment tolerance: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] says Post leaders felt able to take risks without being punished for near-term failure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept rests here on one admiring executive profile and testimony from Bezos and senior colleagues. It does not measure decision quality, employee experience, succession resilience, or whether oversight felt empowering below the executive layer. Amazon's contested workplace culture also warns that leader leverage at the top does not by itself establish healthy delegation or humane conditions throughout an organization.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish scalable leadership through senior owners and operating mechanisms from simple withdrawal or hands-off delegation.
