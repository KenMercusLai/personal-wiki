## Definition
[[VanityMetrics]] are attention or activity numbers that look impressive but provide weak evidence of durable customer demand, revenue, retention, or business progress.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents vanity metrics as a startup diligence risk. When founders answer a milestone question with press coverage or website visits, candidates should treat that as weaker evidence than recurring revenue, paid customers, or explicitly defined active users. The concept is not that attention is worthless, but that attention should not be mistaken for validated business traction.

## Key Claims
- Vanity metrics can make a startup appear more validated than it is.
- Press mentions and website traffic are weak milestone answers unless connected to business outcomes.
- Stronger milestone evidence includes revenue, paid customers, and carefully defined active use.
- Candidate diligence should examine which metrics founders choose because those choices reveal management orientation.

## Evidence
- Milestone contrast: [[7-questions-to-ask-founders-before-joining-their-startup]] treats recurring monthly revenue, paid customers, and defined weekly active users as strong milestone answers.
- Vanity warning: [[7-questions-to-ask-founders-before-joining-their-startup]] warns that TechCrunch coverage or raw website visitors may forecast vanity-oriented management.
- Diligence use: [[7-questions-to-ask-founders-before-joining-their-startup]] recommends asking about milestones partly to learn what founders value.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not say press, traffic, or awareness are always irrelevant. In some consumer, marketplace, or media businesses, top-of-funnel demand can be useful evidence when paired with retention, conversion, monetization, cohort quality, or customer behavior. The warning is about substituting surface attention for business proof.
