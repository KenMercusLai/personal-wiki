## Definition
[[RevealedPriorities]] are the relative weights expressed by how a person allocates action, attention, time, and sacrifice when multiple wants compete, as distinct from the priorities they verbally profess.

## Current Synthesis
[[DerekSivers]] uses his failure to launch a long-discussed company as a personal audit. He still wanted the company, but his continued life of learning, writing, and parenting showed that he wanted that arrangement more. This makes behavior useful evidence of ranking rather than a binary test of whether any desire exists. The practical response to a persistent words-actions gap is either to accept the priority currently expressed or begin the claimed goal and see whether action continues. The evidence must remain conditional, however: observed conduct reflects available options, capability, obligations, habits, fear, and structural constraints as well as values. It reveals an effective priority in a situation, not necessarily a person's deepest moral value or unconstrained preference.

## Key Claims
- Behavior can expose the relative ranking among competing wants more reliably than repeated declarations alone.
- Inaction need not mean zero desire; it can mean that another benefit, duty, comfort, or avoided cost currently wins.
- A stable mismatch between professed and revealed priorities is information that can support acceptance or deliberate change.
- Beginning the professed goal turns a verbal claim into a behavioral test, but sustained practice provides stronger evidence than one symbolic act.
- Revealed priorities are condition-dependent because opportunity, power, capability, habit, and constraint shape what action is possible.

## Evidence
- Personal allocation: [[actions-not-words-reveal-our-real-values]] says Sivers's years without launching a company were better explained by his stronger preference for a simple life centered on learning, writing, and his child.
- Competing wants: [[actions-not-words-reveal-our-real-values]] explicitly reframes the gap as wanting the company somewhat while wanting something else more.
- Corrective choice: [[actions-not-words-reveal-our-real-values]] proposes admitting the operative priority or starting the claimed goal to see whether conduct supports it.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a compact anecdote about one voluntary project, not a validated general model of motivation. Its coach's claim that people would already have acted if they truly wanted an outcome is too absolute. Poverty, coercion, discrimination, illness, disability, caregiving, addiction, trauma, fear, missing knowledge, conflicting duties, and delayed opportunity can all block action without erasing sincere desire. Habits can also preserve behavior that a person reflectively rejects. Actions therefore support an inference about effective priorities under observed conditions; they do not by themselves establish moral character, intrinsic values, or what someone would choose with materially different options.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish declared wants from the relative ranking expressed through conduct.
- Bounded the inference to present conditions rather than treating action as infallible evidence of unconstrained desire.
