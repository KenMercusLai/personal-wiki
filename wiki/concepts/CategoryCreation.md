## Definition
[[CategoryCreation]] is a positioning strategy that frames a new market category, problem, or buying logic so a company becomes associated with the category rather than merely compared inside an existing one.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats category creation as a stronger move than feature-level positioning in crowded markets. A company does not always need to invent the first product in a technical sense; it can teach the market to see a new problem or solution category, then position itself as the obvious answer. The approach is demanding because it requires product or service innovation, business-model innovation, and education around future demand, but it can give the creator the advantages normally associated with market leadership.

## Key Claims
- Category creation helps a company escape direct comparison with similar products.
- The category creator often sells the problem before it sells the product.
- Being associated with a category can create leadership and mental-availability advantages.
- Category creation requires stronger innovation and education than ordinary messaging.
- If a company cannot become first or second in an existing category, creating a narrower or reframed category may be strategically attractive.

## Evidence
- Escape comparison: [[cha-yi-hua-zhan-lue-zhi-nan-what-why-yu-how-36kr]] says [[Drift]] avoided competing as another live-chat tool by framing "conversational marketing."
- Selling the problem: [[cha-yi-hua-zhan-lue-zhi-nan-what-why-yu-how-36kr]] argues that category creation educates the market about a problem and makes the company's innovation the best solution.
- Leadership advantage: [[cha-yi-hua-zhan-lue-zhi-nan-what-why-yu-how-36kr]] links category creation to the economic benefits of becoming the market leader.
- Innovation demand: [[cha-yi-hua-zhan-lue-zhi-nan-what-why-yu-how-36kr]] says the strategy requires radical product or service innovation plus business-model innovation.
- Strategic fallback: [[cha-yi-hua-zhan-lue-zhi-nan-what-why-yu-how-36kr]] cites Jack Welch's first-or-second category discipline to motivate creating a more ownable category when direct leadership is unrealistic.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source presents category creation as powerful but expensive and risky. A new category may begin with little demand, so the company must create understanding and urgency rather than simply capture existing search or comparison traffic.
