## Definition
[[StartupDealDocumentation]] is the practice of recording a startup's agreements, contributions, counterparties, intellectual-property boundaries, and disclosures in contemporaneous, mutually acknowledged form so later decisions do not depend on memory or continued goodwill.

## Current Synthesis
The available source treats documentation as relationship infrastructure rather than paperwork added after trust fails. Its three cases show different versions of the same exposure: a license without sufficiently enforceable royalty and ownership controls, labor exchanged for an ambiguous equity promise across shifting legal entities, and an invention disclosed more fully than the author's confidentiality and bargaining position could support. The shared remedy is to make obligations, value transfers, identity, and access legible while cooperation is intact.

The source also proposes a practical hierarchy. Professional legal drafting and review are preferable when available, but scarce cash is not a reason to leave a deal entirely oral: confirmed emails, invoices that state full value and the reason for any discount, time and expense records, and signed disclosure logs can create a contemporaneous trail. These records are not substitutes for valid terms or legal advice. They preserve facts and expectations that would otherwise be harder to establish.

For intellectual property, documentation must be paired with disclosure control. A focused NDA and signed access record can define confidentiality, while a demonstration that reveals function without revealing implementation can reduce what the counterparty learns. Royalty licenses need their own operating design: a calculation base, reporting or audit cadence, ownership language, anti-circumvention protection, performance thresholds, and reversible exclusivity.

## Key Claims
- Material agreements should be written, tied to named parties and legal entities, and acknowledged while incentives remain aligned.
- Non-cash contributions need a ledger of hours, expenses, nominal value, invoices, discounts, and the consideration expected in return.
- Royalty arrangements require measurable calculation, verification, ownership, performance, circumvention, and exclusivity rules rather than only a promised percentage.
- Confidentiality and patent strategy should govern who sees an invention, what they see, when they see it, and how that access is recorded.
- Documentation preserves evidence and expectations but cannot guarantee enforceability, affordable remedies, or equal bargaining power.

## Evidence
- Written fallback: [[do-not-make-the-mistakes-i-have-document-your-business-dealings-from-day-0-startups]] argues for documenting agreements from day zero and says a confirmed email is better than an undocumented handshake when legal drafting is unaffordable.
- Contribution accounting: [[do-not-make-the-mistakes-i-have-document-your-business-dealings-from-day-0-startups]] recommends recording every hour and expense, invoicing work at full price, and showing any discount and its reason explicitly; the author reports using time activities and a 100% discount to measure free work by customer.
- Counterparty identity: [[do-not-make-the-mistakes-i-have-document-your-business-dealings-from-day-0-startups]] describes an equity dispute complicated by multiple LLCs and the claim that a promise applied to a different company.
- License governance: [[do-not-make-the-mistakes-i-have-document-your-business-dealings-from-day-0-startups]] calls for an explicit royalty basis, periodic sales audits, retained design ownership, anti-circumvention language, volume obligations, and automatic loss of exclusivity after underperformance.
- Disclosure control: [[do-not-make-the-mistakes-i-have-document-your-business-dealings-from-day-0-startups]] recommends a technology-specific NDA, signed access records, attention to provisional-application timing, and functional demonstrations that conceal unnecessary implementation detail.
- Remedy boundary: [[do-not-make-the-mistakes-i-have-document-your-business-dealings-from-day-0-startups]] reports that written records still left the author facing litigation cost and a better-funded counterparty.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is a single anonymous founder's retrospective account, not a court record, contract study, or comparison of documented and undocumented ventures. The disputes, financial estimates, ownership claims, and expected legal outcomes are unverified. Contract formation, copyright ownership, patent rights, confidentiality, amendments, audit rights, and remedies vary by jurisdiction and fact pattern, so the source's clauses are issue-spotting prompts rather than legal templates. Documentation can be incomplete, invalid, ambiguous, fabricated, or too costly to enforce; an NDA can also be overbroad enough that a reasonable counterparty refuses it. The source file is contaminated after its titled discussion by unrelated posts, which were excluded from this synthesis.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from the source's common pattern across royalty, sweat-equity, and invention-disclosure disputes.
- Distinguished low-cost evidence capture from professional legal drafting and from actual enforcement power.
