## Definition
[[IndexFundStrategy]] is the use of low-cost broad-market ETFs as a simpler investing approach that relies on diversified market exposure rather than frequent stock selection.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents low-cost S&P 500 exposure as the simpler strategy and contrasts it with higher-risk thematic or single-stock bets. The inspected slides show VOO's long rise from 2011 to 2025 and an example mix of 50% S&P 500 ETF, 40% Nasdaq 100 ETF, and 10% ARK innovation ETF, with ETF screenshots exposing how much top holdings overlap across Microsoft, NVIDIA, Apple, Amazon, Meta, Broadcom, Google, and Tesla. The strategy is not treated as risk-free, but as easier to understand and maintain than chasing individual winners.

## Key Claims
- Low-cost S&P 500 funds are presented as a simple default that can outperform many high-fee active funds.
- Broad-market ETFs reduce reliance on selecting individual companies at the right time.
- Nasdaq and innovation ETFs add growth exposure but also increase concentration in technology and volatile themes.
- ETF holdings should be inspected because broad labels can hide overlap in the same mega-cap companies.

## Evidence
- Simple default: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] says low-cost S&P 500 index funds can beat most high-fee active managers.
- Long-term example: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] shows a Vanguard S&P 500 ETF chart from 2011 to 2025 with large cumulative appreciation.
- Portfolio mix: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] presents 50% S&P 500, 40% Nasdaq 100, and 10% ARK innovation as a simple example allocation.
- Holding overlap: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] lists VOO and QQQ top holdings with repeated mega-cap technology names.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's ETF examples are date-specific screenshots, not a universal allocation recommendation. Broad-market funds can still draw down, and adding Nasdaq or ARK exposure increases concentration and valuation risk.
