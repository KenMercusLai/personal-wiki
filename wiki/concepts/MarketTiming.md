## Definition
[[MarketTiming]] is the attempt to improve returns by predicting short-term or cycle-level market entry and exit points.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats market timing as a trap because being directionally right is not enough. Missing a small number of unusually strong trading days can hurt returns, and even accurate cycle views can fail if capital, temperament, or clients cannot survive the wait. Michael Burry's early subprime short and Julian Robertson's Tiger Fund technology short are used as examples of how correct judgments can become nearly impossible to hold through wrong timing. For ordinary investors, the practical alternative is regular broad exposure and sell decisions grounded in thesis change rather than recent price movement alone.

## Key Claims
- Short-term market timing is hard because the best trading days are unevenly distributed and difficult to identify in advance.
- Cycle timing is also hard because investors can predict a bubble or crash too early.
- Professional investors face additional timing pressure because underperformance can trigger redemptions or career damage.
- Selling only because price fell or rose is usually a weak reason unless the underlying thesis changed.
- A better sell reason is that the buy case no longer holds, valuation is no longer attractive, the purchase was wrong, or capital has a superior destination.

## Evidence
- Trading-day problem: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-1-shi-chang-gui-lu]] says the best stock-market days are unevenly distributed and missing them can damage long-term return.
- Crisis-timing problem: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-1-shi-chang-gui-lu]] notes that predicting the 2008 crisis in advance still left the problem of whether it would arrive in 2007, 2008, or 2009.
- Professional pressure: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-1-shi-chang-gui-lu]] uses technology-bubble examples where managers could be punished before their bearish view paid off.
- Bad sell reasons: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-1-shi-chang-gui-lu]] rejects selling merely because an asset fell or because it quickly rose 10-15%.
- Thesis-based selling: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-1-shi-chang-gui-lu]] says the central sell reason is that the original buy reason no longer stands.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not claim valuation, rebalancing, or disciplined opportunity switching are useless. It specifically warns against relying on precise timing confidence and emotionally triggered selling.
