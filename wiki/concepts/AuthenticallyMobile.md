## Definition
[[AuthenticallyMobile]] describes a product whose central experience depends on the distinctive capabilities and context of an always-carried, networked mobile device, rather than an existing desktop interaction merely being resized or made portable.

## Current Synthesis
The sources distinguish adaptation from dependence. A mobile-first service may improve an older activity through portability and a touch interface; an authentically mobile service combines capabilities such as cameras, sensors, location, continuous connectivity, notifications, and co-located capture and consumption in a way that would be impossible or largely meaningless on a desktop. Uber is the first source's clear location-and-marketplace example, while Snapchat adds a social case built around opening to the camera, producing vertical media, sharing immediately, and consuming on the same class of device.

Evans sharpens the category by separating products that merely work better on phones from those whose interaction model only makes sense there. Treating the image sensor as a programmable input rather than a camera lets the sensor, screen, and app operate as one mechanism in Lenses, Stories, and other camera-mediated products. The distinction remains architectural and behavioral, not a guarantee of durable advantage: rivals can copy individual features, and the category is most useful when it identifies which coupled mobile affordances create the experience and what breaks when they are removed.

## Key Claims
- Mobile-first adapts an experience to smartphones; authentically mobile makes mobile capabilities constitutive of the experience.
- The strongest cases depend on several coupled affordances rather than on screen size alone.
- Always-carried location and connectivity can coordinate two-sided services that would otherwise be impractical.
- Camera-first social products can unify collection, editing, distribution, and consumption on one device.
- Default interface state can reveal the product’s native action: Snapchat’s camera privileges capture over feed browsing.
- A mobile-native form can arise when sensor, screen, software, and network function as one interaction mechanism rather than as separate tools.
- Dependence on a mobile substrate does not by itself prove adoption, defensibility, good outcomes, or readiness for a post-phone platform.

## Evidence
- Category distinction: [[ghost-in-the-machine-snapchat-isnt-mobile-first-its-something-else-entirely]] contrasts adapted desktop possibilities with services that require a networked pocket computer.
- Location coordination: [[ghost-in-the-machine-snapchat-isnt-mobile-first-its-something-else-entirely]] uses Uber’s always-present location devices on both sides of a marketplace as the classic example.
- Camera-centered collection: [[ghost-in-the-machine-snapchat-isnt-mobile-first-its-something-else-entirely]] uses Snapchat’s camera default, vertical media, and shared capture-consumption device to extend the category into social communication.
- Native interaction model: [[imaging-snapchat-and-mobile-benedict-evans]] distinguishes phone-portable services from products that only work or make sense when mobile sensors, display, and software are unified.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The category is supplied through two 2016 strategy essays, one of which paraphrases an earlier venture-capital article; the archived comparison table is unreadable, and neither source provides an independent taxonomy or empirical test. “Could not exist without smartphones” can be a fuzzy threshold because many services have partial web, tablet, wearable, or desktop analogues. Device dependence can also create platform exposure, safety risks, battery and bandwidth constraints, accessibility problems, and rapid imitation. Claims that native mobile forms point toward head-worn AR or another post-phone platform are forecasts, not demonstrated transition mechanisms.

## What Changed
- Added sensor-screen unification as a mechanism that can make an experience mobile-native rather than merely mobile-first.
- Added Evans's distinction between products improved by mobile and products that only make sense on mobile.
