## Definition
[[AuthenticallyMobile]] describes a product whose central experience depends on the distinctive capabilities and context of an always-carried, networked mobile device, rather than an existing desktop interaction merely being resized or made portable.

## Current Synthesis
The source attributes the term to Peter Wagner and Martin Giles and applies it to [[Snapchat]]. A mobile-first service may improve an older activity through portability and a touch interface; an authentically mobile service combines capabilities such as cameras, sensors, location, continuous connectivity, notifications, and co-located capture and consumption in a way that would be impossible or largely meaningless on a desktop. Uber is the source’s clear location-and-marketplace example, while Snapchat adds a social case built around opening to the camera, producing vertical media, sharing immediately, and consuming on the same class of device.

The distinction is architectural and behavioral, not a guarantee of durable advantage. A product can depend on smartphones yet still reproduce old feed, identity, or advertising patterns, and rivals can copy individual features. The category is most useful when it identifies which coupled mobile affordances create the experience and what breaks when those affordances are removed.

## Key Claims
- Mobile-first adapts an experience to smartphones; authentically mobile makes mobile capabilities constitutive of the experience.
- The strongest cases depend on several coupled affordances rather than on screen size alone.
- Always-carried location and connectivity can coordinate two-sided services that would otherwise be impractical.
- Camera-first social products can unify collection, editing, distribution, and consumption on one device.
- Default interface state can reveal the product’s native action: Snapchat’s camera privileges capture over feed browsing.
- Dependence on a mobile substrate does not by itself prove adoption, defensibility, good outcomes, or readiness for a post-phone platform.

## Evidence
- Category distinction: [[ghost-in-the-machine-snapchat-isnt-mobile-first-its-something-else-entirely]] contrasts adapted desktop possibilities with services that require a networked pocket computer.
- Location coordination: [[ghost-in-the-machine-snapchat-isnt-mobile-first-its-something-else-entirely]] uses Uber’s always-present location devices on both sides of a marketplace as the classic example.
- Camera-centered collection: [[ghost-in-the-machine-snapchat-isnt-mobile-first-its-something-else-entirely]] uses Snapchat’s camera default, vertical media, and shared capture-consumption device to extend the category into social communication.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The category is supplied through one 2016 strategy essay that paraphrases an earlier venture-capital article; the archived comparison table is unreadable, and no independent taxonomy or empirical test is provided. “Could not exist without smartphones” can be a fuzzy threshold because many services have partial web, tablet, wearable, or desktop analogues. Device dependence can also create platform exposure, safety risks, battery and bandwidth constraints, accessibility problems, and rapid imitation. The source’s claim that authentic mobility points beyond mobile is a forecast, not a demonstrated transition mechanism.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from the article’s distinction between phone-adapted and phone-dependent product experiences.
