Startup joining diligence means evaluating a startup as an employee before joining, not only as an exciting team or product. The source argues that candidates may accept lower cash compensation, harder work, and years of career risk, so they should assess the company's chances rather than treating the equity as a harmless bonus or the outcome as pure luck.

The diligence starts with survival math. A candidate should ask how much cash is actually in the bank, what the monthly burn rate is, and what runway management projects from those numbers. Funding headlines are incomplete because the same bank balance can support years or only months depending on spending.

The next layer is judgment and control. Investor selection shows whether the founders attracted and chose credible backers. Founder qualifications show whether the people running the company have the experience, temperament, motivation, and learning capacity to handle a startup. Board structure shows who can set direction and whether unqualified relatives or angels have formal power.

The final layer is operating evidence. Early employees reveal whether the company protects talent quality under pressure. Milestones reveal whether leaders value revenue, paid customers, and active use more than press or fundraising optics. Belief in the idea tests both candidate fit and the founders' ability to sell the vision to people who must choose the company before it is proven.

## Diligence Questions

- How much cash is in the bank, what is monthly burn, and how much runway remains?
- Who invested, and why did the founders choose those investors?
- What makes the founders credible enough to run this specific company?
- Who controls the board, and does anyone unqualified have formal influence?
- Would the early employees make the candidate want to raise their own standard?
- Are milestones business outcomes or vanity signals?
- Does the candidate believe the idea after hearing the founders' best case?
