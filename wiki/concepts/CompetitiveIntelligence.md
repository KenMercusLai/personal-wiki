## Definition
[[CompetitiveIntelligence]] is the collection and interpretation of signals about rivals, markets, and user behavior to inform product, acquisition, and competitive decisions; when derived from consumer telemetry, its strategic value is inseparable from consent, purpose, and market-power questions.

## Current Synthesis
The sources describe two levels of competitive learning. The Onavo case shows how a consumer product can become an early-warning system: aggregated frequency and duration data reportedly let [[Facebook]] observe changes in rival use before public results, assess [[WhatsApp]]'s geographic strength, and respond to emerging live-video behavior. The Product Habits essay adds the ordinary product-team level: direct customer research can reveal present pain while competitor pricing, positioning, platform focus, demonstrations, and features reveal how expectations are changing. The advantage comes from closing observation into a tested product, acquisition, pricing, or entry decision. The evidence must still be interpreted: formal disclosure does not demonstrate understood consent, and neither telemetry correlation nor a founder's retrospective establishes causal decisiveness.

## Key Claims
- Behavioral telemetry can reveal competitor momentum before public financial or adoption reports.
- Frequency, duration, geography, and change after a launch can inform different strategic choices.
- Public product, pricing, positioning, and sales behavior can reveal customer expectations that direct interviews miss.
- Intelligence becomes more powerful when one firm can observe, copy, acquire, and distribute a response across an existing network.
- A security or utility product's user-facing purpose may differ materially from the owner's strategic use of its data.
- Good competitive intelligence updates a market hypothesis; it does not require copying every visible rival move.
- Disclosure, comprehension, consent, causal inference, strategic fit, and competitive fairness are distinct tests.

## Evidence
- Early warning: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] reports that Facebook detected slowing Snapchat use after Instagram Stories before the struggle was public.
- Acquisition signal: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] says WhatsApp's reach, including a reported 99% of Android phones in Spain, influenced Facebook's decision to buy it.
- Product response: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] links Meerkat and Periscope usage observations to Facebook adding live video.
- Governance tension: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] contrasts Facebook's disclosure defense with concerns about customer-data use and Apple's rules.
- Ordinary competitor signals: [[facebooks-shameless-copying-of-snapchat-and-what-it-means-for-your-product-strategy-product-habits]] says KISSmetrics saw Mixpanel's free plan and badges, stronger real-time demonstration, and mobile focus but discounted their significance.
- Feature signal: [[facebooks-shameless-copying-of-snapchat-and-what-it-means-for-your-product-strategy-product-habits]] treats Heap's automatic event capture as a value proposition KISSmetrics could have evaluated and answered earlier.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The Onavo source is a journalistic summary rather than a telemetry audit or decision study; it gives no sampling frame, aggregation method, uncertainty, false-signal history, or counterfactual decisions. The Product Habits source is a founder retrospective that may overstate how easy, cheap, or effective a response would have been and does not establish that copying would have reversed KISSmetrics' trajectory. Competitive research is not inherently abusive, but telemetry and ordinary market observation carry different consent and power implications. In both cases, a visible rival move is evidence to investigate rather than proof of customer value, contextual fit, causation, or the correct response.

## What Changed
- Expanded the concept from privileged telemetry to public product, pricing, positioning, and sales signals.
- Added competitor observation as a complement to direct customer research and market-hypothesis revision.
- Preserved the distinction between observing a move and proving that imitation is the right response.
