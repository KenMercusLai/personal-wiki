## Definition
[[CompetitiveIntelligence]] is the collection and interpretation of public company data, product and market signals, or user behavior to infer rivals' scale, momentum, positioning, and likely strategic choices; the evidence and governance requirements depend on how those signals were obtained.

## Current Synthesis
The sources describe three levels of competitive learning. The Onavo case shows privileged behavioral telemetry as an early-warning system: frequency and duration data reportedly let [[Facebook]] observe rival use before public results, assess [[WhatsApp]]'s geographic strength, and respond to live-video behavior. The Product Habits essay adds ordinary product observation: pricing, positioning, platform focus, demonstrations, and features can reveal changing expectations that direct customer research misses. Lemkin adds lightweight financial inference: multiply public [[LinkedIn]] headcount by a funding- and business-model-adjusted [[RevenuePerEmployee]] benchmark to estimate a private SaaS company's broad scale. Each mode has a different error and governance profile. A product move is not proof of customer value, a selected headcount example is not a validated estimator, and formal telemetry disclosure is not proof of meaningful consent.

## Key Claims
- Behavioral telemetry such as frequency, duration, geography, and post-launch change can reveal competitor momentum before public reports and inform different strategic choices.
- Public product, pricing, positioning, and sales behavior can reveal customer expectations that direct interviews miss.
- Public workforce data can support a rough private-company revenue range when direct financial evidence is unavailable.
- Intelligence becomes more powerful when one firm can observe, copy, acquire, and distribute a response across an existing network.
- A security or utility product's user-facing purpose may differ materially from the owner's strategic use of its data.
- Good competitive intelligence updates a market hypothesis; it does not turn a proxy into a fact or require copying every visible rival move.
- Data freshness, measurement comparability, disclosure, comprehension, consent, causal inference, strategic fit, and competitive fairness are distinct tests.

## Evidence
- Early warning: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] reports that Facebook detected slowing Snapchat use after Instagram Stories before the struggle was public.
- Acquisition signal: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] says WhatsApp's reach, including a reported 99% of Android phones in Spain, influenced Facebook's decision to buy it.
- Product response: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] links Meerkat and Periscope usage observations to Facebook adding live video.
- Governance tension: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] contrasts Facebook's disclosure defense with concerns about customer-data use and Apple's rules.
- Ordinary competitor signals: [[facebooks-shameless-copying-of-snapchat-and-what-it-means-for-your-product-strategy-product-habits]] says KISSmetrics saw Mixpanel's free plan and badges, stronger real-time demonstration, and mobile focus but discounted their significance.
- Feature signal: [[facebooks-shameless-copying-of-snapchat-and-what-it-means-for-your-product-strategy-product-habits]] treats Heap's automatic event capture as a value proposition KISSmetrics could have evaluated and answered earlier.
- Workforce proxy: [[jason-lemkin-how-to-figure-out-your-competitors-revenues-in-about-70-seconds]] uses LinkedIn employee counts and a calibrated $100,000-$300,000 revenue-per-employee range to infer private SaaS scale.
- Worked limitations: [[jason-lemkin-how-to-figure-out-your-competitors-revenues-in-about-70-seconds]] compares Marketo, [[HubSpot]], and YouSendIt estimates with selected reported figures while mixing ARR and GAAP bases.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The Onavo source is a journalistic summary rather than a telemetry audit or decision study; it gives no sampling frame, aggregation method, uncertainty, false-signal history, or counterfactual decisions. The Product Habits source is a founder retrospective that may overstate how easy, cheap, or effective a response would have been and does not establish that copying would have reversed KISSmetrics' trajectory. Lemkin's 2012 heuristic uses selected examples, adjustable multipliers, an untested LinkedIn-coverage assumption, and inconsistent ARR/GAAP comparisons rather than a declared validation set. Competitive research is not inherently abusive, but public observation and privileged telemetry carry different consent and power implications. Every signal remains evidence to investigate rather than proof of scale, customer value, causation, contextual fit, or the correct response.

## What Changed
- Expanded the concept from privileged telemetry to public product, pricing, positioning, and sales signals.
- Added competitor observation as a complement to direct customer research and market-hypothesis revision.
- Preserved the distinction between observing a move and proving that imitation is the right response.
- Added public headcount-based revenue estimation and its comparability and validation limits.
