## Definition
[[CompetitiveIntelligence]] is the collection and interpretation of signals about rivals, markets, and user behavior to inform product, acquisition, and competitive decisions; when derived from consumer telemetry, its strategic value is inseparable from consent, purpose, and market-power questions.

## Current Synthesis
The Onavo case shows how a consumer product can become an early-warning system about other products. Aggregated frequency and duration data reportedly let [[Facebook]] observe changes in rival use before public results, assess [[WhatsApp]]'s geographic strength, and respond to emerging live-video behavior. The advantage is not merely having more data: it is closing the loop from observation to imitation, acquisition, or product entry. Formal disclosure can establish notice without demonstrating that users understood this secondary competitive purpose, and the report does not establish how accurate, representative, or causally decisive the telemetry was.

## Key Claims
- Behavioral telemetry can reveal competitor momentum before public financial or adoption reports.
- Frequency, duration, geography, and change after a launch can inform different strategic choices.
- Intelligence becomes more powerful when one firm can observe, copy, acquire, and distribute a response across an existing network.
- A security or utility product's user-facing purpose may differ materially from the owner's strategic use of its data.
- Disclosure, comprehension, consent, platform-policy compliance, and competitive fairness are distinct tests.
- Observed correlation around a product launch does not by itself prove that the launch caused a rival's slowdown.

## Evidence
- Early warning: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] reports that Facebook detected slowing Snapchat use after Instagram Stories before the struggle was public.
- Acquisition signal: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] says WhatsApp's reach, including a reported 99% of Android phones in Spain, influenced Facebook's decision to buy it.
- Product response: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] links Meerkat and Periscope usage observations to Facebook adding live video.
- Governance tension: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] contrasts Facebook's disclosure defense with concerns about customer-data use and Apple's rules.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a journalistic summary of reported practices, not a telemetry audit or decision study. It gives no sampling frame, representativeness test, aggregation method, uncertainty, false-signal history, or counterfactual decisions without Onavo. The temporal relationship between Instagram Stories and slowing Snapchat use does not isolate causation. Competitive research is not inherently abusive, and disclosed aggregated market research can be legitimate; the unresolved issue is whether the data source, purpose, user understanding, platform rules, and incumbent action are acceptable together.

## What Changed
- Created a telemetry-centered account of competitive intelligence.
- Distinguished early detection from causal proof and decision attribution.
- Added consent, purpose compatibility, platform policy, and incumbent response capacity as governance dimensions.
