## Definition
[[ProductReviewMeetings]] are structured product discussions where teams present work for senior review, feedback, risk framing, and alignment without surrendering ownership of the solution.

## Current Synthesis
The P Rules frame product reviews as high-leverage but power-sensitive meetings. Reviews fail when senior people make the room about their own acuity: repeating criticism, hunting for small errors, chasing tangents, prescribing fixes, accepting transferred responsibility, reflexively rejecting ideas, or interrupting before presenters can finish. In that mode, the review may surface issues, but it also makes teams defensive and less trusted.

Healthy product-review meetings preserve ownership while improving judgment. Leaders can identify weaknesses, clarify acceptable constraints, frame risk-reward tradeoffs, and probe with curiosity, but the presenting team remains responsible for decisions and solutions. Operational constraints such as slide limits, numbered decks, and pre-reads help the group stay informed without taking the floor away from presenters. Bosworth's broader meeting essay adds that the owner should justify the meeting's time cost through clear purpose, appropriate size, agenda, expected outcomes, notes, feedback, and periodic expiration. The leader's presence matters: eye contact, open questions, explicit references to people and ideas, shared-goal reminders, naming concern, and light humor can make disagreement feel safer.

## Key Claims
- Product reviews can damage trust when senior reviewers make critique repetitive, nitpicky, tangential, prescriptive, pessimistic, or interruptive.
- Reviewers add the most value by surfacing material issues once, then letting the team continue reasoning.
- Leaders should define acceptable solution boundaries without solving on the team's behalf.
- Teams should retain responsibility for decisions rather than transferring permission upward.
- Curiosity toward weak ideas can reveal deeper misunderstandings on either side of the review.
- Product reviews need explicit facilitation and ownership mechanics so presenters can finish, answer questions fully, and the meeting's time cost is justified.
- The leader's power gradient makes tone, body language, and emotional awareness part of the meeting system.

## Evidence
- Review dysfunction: [[boz-the-p-rules]] begins with Bosworth's team learning that their product reviews were hated and feared.
- Anti-piling-on: [[boz-the-p-rules]] says repeated agreement after one person identifies a weakness adds little value and makes presenters defensive.
- Anti-prescription: [[boz-the-p-rules]] says leaders should provide guidelines for acceptable solutions while letting teams own the solution.
- Decision ownership: [[boz-the-p-rules]] says teams asking permission may be moving responsibility to the leader, who should instead frame tradeoffs.
- Curiosity over pessimism: [[boz-the-p-rules]] says apparently bad ideas can reveal either team misunderstanding or leader misunderstanding.
- Presenter agency: [[boz-the-p-rules]] recommends letting presenters finish, giving full answers, limiting slides, numbering decks, and using pre-reads.
- Power-aware facilitation: [[boz-the-p-rules]] describes eye contact, open questions, shared goals, naming concern, and humor as leader practices.
- Meeting stewardship: [[boz-time-isnt-money]] says meetings should have a clear purpose, size suited to the purpose, an owner, an agenda, expected outcomes, notes, feedback, and recurring expirations.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources give a leader's retrospective practice rather than controlled evidence that the P Rules or meeting-ownership defaults universally improve product reviews. Some reviews may require direct intervention, explicit decision ownership by senior leaders, or rapid shutdown of unsafe proposals when legal, reliability, financial, or trust risk is high. The rules are strongest as default meeting norms, not as a replacement for clear decision rights.

## What Changed
- Created Product Review Meetings as a concept for the P Rules' meeting-specific leadership model.
- Added Bosworth's broader meeting-stewardship requirements as operating defaults for high-cost review meetings.
