## Definition
[[PsychologicalSafety]] is a working condition in which people can ask questions, voice disagreement, acknowledge uncertainty, give feedback, and revisit mistakes without anticipating punishment or humiliation for doing so. In this Wiki it names a condition for candid participation and learning, not a guarantee of agreement or freedom from accountability.

## Current Synthesis
The architecture-governance account makes the condition visible through practices: decision-takers seek advice from people likely to disagree, record dissent in [[ArchitectureDecisionRecords]], and bring proposals to an [[ArchitectureAdvisoryForum]] where imperfect or reversed decisions can become shared learning. The author says people need to feel safe in order to learn, but does not name or formally test a psychological-safety construct. An open meeting alone cannot prove that quieter or less powerful participants feel able to speak.

A product-management essay adds a related boundary: admitting that one does not know everything makes questioning possible, but repeated challenges must still respect expertise and delivery constraints. A junior developer's first-person account supplies the personal side: asking questions, discussing insecurity with a boss, and receiving code-review feedback were supported by colleagues and a mentor. Together these accounts suggest that safety depends on how people with authority and peers respond to uncertainty, dissent, mistakes, and requests for help across repeated interactions.

## Key Claims
- People need room to express disagreement and ask questions before group advice can be genuinely informative.
- Recording dissent and making changed decisions visible can turn an error into organizational learning rather than a reason to conceal it.
- An invitation to participate does not by itself ensure that all affected people are heard or have equal influence.
- Leaders and peers shape whether uncertainty, feedback, and learning are practically safe to surface.
- Safety supports candid discussion without requiring consensus, removing decision ownership, or excusing harmful behavior.

## Evidence
- Dissent and decision ownership: [[blog-andrew-harmel-law-martinfowler-com-scaling-the-practice-of-architecture-conversationally]] calls for affected people and experts to be consulted, for disagreement to be sought and recorded, and for the originator to retain the decision.
- Learning and participation: [[blog-andrew-harmel-law-martinfowler-com-scaling-the-practice-of-architecture-conversationally]] says learning requires feeling safe, praises open discussion of changed decisions, and warns that the same familiar voices can dominate an apparently successful forum.
- Admitting uncertainty: [[your-product-manager-super-power-not-knowing-everything-mind-the-product]] treats not knowing every specialist answer as a reason to ask useful questions and challenge assumptions rather than pretend expertise.
- Feedback in a junior role: [[being-a-junior-developer-at-30-by]] describes asking questions, discussing insecurities with a boss, and learning through feedback from supportive teammates.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
These are practitioner and personal accounts, not controlled comparisons or measurements of psychological safety. The architecture author discusses safety, dissent, trust, and learning without using the exact term "psychological safety"; the label here is a cross-source synthesis, not a quotation or a finding attributed to that article. The product-management source addresses questioning, while the junior-developer account describes one person's supportive environment; neither establishes a universal causal effect. A forum can be formally open while hierarchy, confidentiality, time, retaliation risk, or exclusion still discourage participation. Some decisions also need clear authority and non-negotiable safeguards, not unlimited debate.
