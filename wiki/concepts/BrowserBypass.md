## Definition
[[BrowserBypass]] is the movement of internet-mediated activity away from open browser-land into native apps, assistants, notifications, voice devices, AR/VR environments, proprietary clouds, or other controlled runtimes.

## Current Synthesis
Staltz's source treats browser bypass as the common strategic pattern behind Google, Facebook, and Amazon even when their motives differ. Google can answer or suggest directly through AI, assistants, notifications, and Home devices instead of sending users to web pages. Facebook can make social communication happen through mobile apps, Messenger, WhatsApp, Instagram, and future AR/VR surfaces. Amazon can route commerce through apps, Echo, and cloud-tied services. In all cases, the internet still transports data, but the Web loses its role as the default user-facing environment.

## Key Claims
- Browser bypass separates internet growth from open-Web health.
- Mobile apps and assistants can improve user experience while reducing visits to independent websites.
- Google, Facebook, and Amazon each have product incentives to make their own controlled surfaces more useful than browser-mediated navigation.
- Proprietary surfaces increase the platform's authority over data, access, discovery, and inter-platform conflict.

## Evidence
- Google strategy: [[andre-staltz-the-web-began-dying-in-2014-heres-how]] links Search-to-Suggest, Google Now, Google Assistant, Android notifications, Google Home, AMP, Firebase, and Cloud TPU to a broader AI/cloud direction.
- Facebook strategy: [[andre-staltz-the-web-began-dying-in-2014-heres-how]] links Facebook's app portfolio and AR/VR ambitions to social interaction outside the browser.
- Amazon strategy: [[andre-staltz-the-web-began-dying-in-2014-heres-how]] says Amazon's mobile apps and Echo purchases bypass the Web while still depending on internet communication.
- Platform authority: [[andre-staltz-the-web-began-dying-in-2014-heres-how]] cites the YouTube/Echo conflict as an example of companies blocking each other in non-web contexts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source treats bypass as strategically coherent, but it does not measure how much browser usage persisted, how web standards evolved afterward, or whether PWAs, regulation, alternative browsers, decentralized protocols, or later AI interfaces changed the balance.
