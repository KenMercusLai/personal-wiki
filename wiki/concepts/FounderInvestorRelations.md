## Definition
[[FounderInvestorRelations]] is the continuing exchange of information, assistance, access, and participation between a startup's founders and its investors after financing closes.

## Current Synthesis
The sources present investor relations as recurring operating and behavioral work rather than a ceremonial update. Quarterly requests can combine a fully diluted cap table, financial results and forecasts, cash and debt, headcount, burn, valuation documents, D&O insurance, and financing plans. Founders can delegate data preparation, but they remain responsible for reviewing what is sent and for understanding how investors use it in portfolio reviews. The relationship can extend beyond company reporting into LP references, prospective-deal diligence, and portfolio events. Harper supplies the reciprocal founder expectation: investors should reserve follow-on capital, remain consistent after closing, communicate hard judgments directly, avoid unsolicited operational interference, and support sensible strategic change. Both investor requests and investor intervention consume attention and can become difficult to refuse after the cap table is set.

## Key Claims
- Post-close investor relations combines formal reporting with informal cooperation and participation.
- Recurring requests make accurate financial, ownership, insurance, staffing, burn, valuation, and financing information part of founder accountability.
- Preparation can be delegated to finance or domain specialists, while review and relationship judgment remain with the founder.
- LP fundraising and portfolio review transmit venture-fund obligations downstream into requests made of portfolio companies.
- Investor requests can draw on portfolio founders' operating expertise to evaluate other companies.
- Events can create access and network value while imposing travel, cost, and opportunity-cost burdens that differ by investor style.
- Useful investor conduct includes candid direct communication, consistent treatment, bounded advice, strategic flexibility, and credible capacity to participate in later rounds.

## Evidence
- Reporting scope: [[brett-fox-boring-obligations-from-investors]] reproduces a quarterly request covering a detailed cap table, actual results, forecasts, margins, expenses, cash, debt, equity, headcount, burn, valuation, D&O insurance, and next financing.
- Delegation with accountability: [[brett-fox-boring-obligations-from-investors]] says a controller completed the request before she and Fox reviewed it together.
- Fund-level support: [[brett-fox-boring-obligations-from-investors]] describes Fox serving as a reference when his investor was raising another fund from limited partners.
- Portfolio assistance: [[brett-fox-boring-obligations-from-investors]] describes Fox and a colleague assessing a prospective investment's technical claim for the investor.
- Participation burden: [[brett-fox-boring-obligations-from-investors]] contrasts a low-cost annual dinner with a costly two-day retreat that also provided access to prominent attendees.
- Investor-side expectations: [[not-all-vcs-are-assholes-mitchell-harper-medium]] favors direct candor, advice on request, support for a sensible pivot, stable pre- and post-close conduct, and reserved follow-on capital.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is grounded in two founders' retrospective accounts. Fox does not distinguish contractual information rights from courtesy requests or perceived social pressure, and Harper does not show that his preferred investor behaviors caused better outcomes. Reporting burden and appropriate intervention vary by stage, governance rights, investor type, company condition, and internal capacity; confidentiality, conflicts, fiduciary duties, and team opportunity cost can justify limits. Advice “only when asked” may preserve autonomy but can also withhold a material warning, while follow-on reserves indicate capacity rather than a guaranteed investment. Neither source compares relationship practices with financing, governance, or company outcomes.

## What Changed
- Added reciprocal investor duties around candor, advice boundaries, strategic support, behavioral consistency, and follow-on capacity.
