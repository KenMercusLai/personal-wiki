## Definition
[[SocialLivestreaming]] is real-time media in which interaction among broadcasters and viewers—through chat, recognition, gifts, requests, competition, or participation—is part of the content rather than merely feedback after transmission.

## Current Synthesis
Chan's China-focused source presents social livestreaming as selfie-like mobile entertainment: front cameras make broadcasting continuously available, while viewers co-produce unscripted content and monetize attention through gifts and status. Danco supplies a wider media interpretation. Synchronous formats can create [[HereAndNowMedia]] when participants feel located inside an unfolding experience; Twitch is his example of gameplay, viewing, and audience action combining into a native medium. Together, the sources distinguish interaction-centered presence from simply sending old content live.

## Key Claims
- Front-facing mobile cameras shift livestreaming toward personal, inward-facing performance.
- Real-time viewer interaction can supply content that would otherwise require scripting or traditional talent.
- Gifts, leaderboards, questions, and requests turn recognition and audience attention into product mechanics and revenue.
- Shared presence becomes stronger when audiences can affect the unfolding event.
- Social livestreaming can serve entertainment, companionship, commerce, and participatory experience, with different governance needs.

## Evidence
- Mobile self-expression: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] contrasts inward selfie-like streams with older outward capture and scheduled performance.
- Audience co-production: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] describes chat, gifts, requests, leaderboards, and attention competition shaping broadcasts.
- Presence interpretation: [[from-pull-and-push-to-here-and-now-the-grand-bargain-of-facebook-and-the-feed-is-unraveling-what-comes-next]] treats Twitch as a shared participatory setting rather than old content plus a live audience.
- Use-case range: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] covers companionship, creator income, brands, commerce, and moderation; [[from-pull-and-push-to-here-and-now-the-grand-bargain-of-facebook-and-the-feed-is-unraveling-what-comes-next]] hypothesizes digitally native experiences and markets.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Both sources are 2016 snapshots. Chan's China market observations do not automatically transfer to other regions, and Danco explicitly had no firsthand Twitch use. Neither source isolates which product mechanics cause retention, presence, healthy communities, or durable economics. Real-time interaction also creates moderation, fraud, exploitation, and regulatory risks; the CS:GO gambling example illustrates blurred boundaries rather than uncomplicated market opportunity.

## What Changed
- Added shared presence and audience agency as the distinction between native social livestreaming and merely live delivery.
- Added Twitch as a speculative cross-market participatory-media case.
- Strengthened the evidence boundary around geography, firsthand use, causality, and economic outcomes.
