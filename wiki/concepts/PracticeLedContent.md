## Definition
[[PracticeLedContent]] is public material derived from what a person genuinely uses, learns, builds, or believes during ongoing work rather than from a detached attempt to manufacture a high-performing post.

## Current Synthesis
[[MaiYang]] proposes three recurring inputs: direct opinions about tools, notes from active learning, and testimony grounded in repeated use. The useful mechanism is upstream of publishing: actual work creates specific observations, examples, and stakes that can be documented with less need to invent a content premise. The retained screenshots show that all three forms received substantial distribution in this author's account, but they do not establish authenticity as a ranking feature or make spontaneous documentation inherently accurate, useful, or commercially effective.

## Key Claims
- Genuine work can generate a recurring supply of concrete publishing material.
- Opinion, learning, and real usage are distinct content forms that can all emerge from the same practice-led posture.
- Specific experience can make a post legible and debatable without a formal viral-content strategy.
- Visible reach is evidence of distribution, not proof that authenticity caused ranking or that the audience gained value.
- Practice-led publishing still requires claim discipline because sincere experience can be selective, mistaken, or unrepresentative.

## Evidence
- Opinion from experience: [[what-my-most-read-tweets-taught-me-about-the-twitter-algorithm]] links a direct Cursor preference to the author's observation of developer-tool use and retains the post's 65,000-view snapshot.
- Learning in public: [[what-my-most-read-tweets-taught-me-about-the-twitter-algorithm]] turns a completed podcast, repeated OpenClaw-inspired assistant examples, and a context-engineering recommendation into a post with 23,000 displayed views.
- Product use: [[what-my-most-read-tweets-taught-me-about-the-twitter-algorithm]] grounds the Raycast post in reduced mouse and trackpad use, repeated sharing, an internal presentation, and a demonstration site.
- Retrospective rule: [[what-my-most-read-tweets-taught-me-about-the-twitter-algorithm]] explicitly groups the posts as opinion, learning, and usage and concludes that documenting actual use, learning, and building is preferable to manufacturing content.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept rests on one author's retrospective selection of three successful posts. There is no comparison with low-reach practice-led posts, manufactured posts, other accounts, audience baselines, timing, media format, network effects, or platform experiments. “Real” documentation can still be strategically packaged, and sincere personal use does not establish general product quality or technical truth. The screenshots also show sharply different engagement mixes, so a common authenticity explanation should not erase differences among debate, reference value, novelty, audience fit, and product interest.

- Created the concept with a narrow three-form taxonomy and an explicit causality boundary around algorithmic reach.
