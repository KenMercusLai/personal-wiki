## Definition
[[EarlyStartupDemandValidation]] is the staged process of replacing founder speculation with progressively stronger evidence that a specific group has a real problem and will adopt, retain, and pay for a proposed solution.

## Current Synthesis
The Segment retrospective shows validation as an evidence ladder rather than one launch event. A public post first generated attention, but the stronger signals were professional engineers saying they needed the tool at work, hundreds of hosted-product signups, requests made during real integration attempts, projects that progressed from account creation to sending data, annual contracts, and recurring revenue. Each step reduced a different uncertainty: whether the problem resonated, whether users wanted a product rather than code, whether they could activate, whether broader integration supply increased value, and whether the company could monetize without undermining trust.

The same source shows why the ladder matters. An Analytics Academy launch attracted interest without producing useful content engagement until reader criticism made lessons specific and actionable. Six large companies expressed interest before any used the product, while five reportedly became customers a year later. Early evidence should therefore be interpreted by what behavior it actually demonstrates, not promoted into a stronger claim because it is visible or impressive.

## Key Claims
- Public attention is a discovery signal, not proof of durable demand.
- Qualified requests tied to real work are stronger than generic praise because they identify a user, context, and problem.
- Signup, setup, activation, retention, payment, and expansion answer different validation questions and should not be collapsed into one traction number.
- Customer feedback is most useful when captured during attempted use and translated into a testable product change.
- Investor evidence can include pre-revenue operating metrics, but prospects, testimonials, and growth rates need explicit boundaries around actual use and willingness to pay.
- Business-model hypotheses remain part of validation because pricing or partner incentives can reduce the product value they are intended to monetize.

## Evidence
- Problem resonance: [[from-show-hn-to-series-d-segment-blog]] says the Analytics.js launch produced professional workplace requests and hundreds of hosted-version emails.
- Product behavior: [[from-show-hn-to-series-d-segment-blog]] reports that the team tracked active projects, signup-to-project conversion, and the share of projects that actually sent data.
- In-context feedback: [[from-show-hn-to-series-d-segment-blog]] shows an Olark conversation in which a user asks for another destination while using the product.
- Interest boundary: [[from-show-hn-to-series-d-segment-blog]] says six large prospects were only interested at seed-deck time, then reports that five had become customers one year later.
- Payment progression: [[from-show-hn-to-series-d-segment-blog]] places the first annual contracts in November 2013 and says the bridge round extended runway through $1 million in annual recurring revenue.
- Model correction: [[from-show-hn-to-series-d-segment-blog]] says Segment abandoned partner revenue sharing and premium-integration fees after learning that neutral recommendations and broad integration use created more customer value.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is derived from one successful company's company-authored retrospective, so survivorship and hindsight can make the evidence sequence look more coherent than it was. Hacker News has a specialized technical audience and cannot generally stand in for a target market; hundreds of emails do not reveal activation, retention, or payment; project counts can be affected by onboarding changes; and annual contracts do not by themselves establish healthy acquisition cost, churn, margin, or market size. Segment also began with Y Combinator, seed capital, four technical founders, an existing library, and a problem those founders personally experienced, so its seven-day product response is not a general baseline.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Segment's progression from launch attention to workplace requests, signups, activated projects, contracts, and recurring revenue.
- Separated prospect interest from actual customer adoption.
- Added business-model correction as part of demand validation rather than a later pricing-only concern.
