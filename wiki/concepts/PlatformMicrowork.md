## Definition
[[PlatformMicrowork]] is labor organized by software into small, separately priced tasks that distributed workers discover, claim, complete, and submit through an online marketplace or internal crowd platform.

## Current Synthesis
The TechRepublic account shows that the task is small but the labor system is not. A platform can expose work on demand, enforce qualifications, route submissions, collect results, and mediate payment at global scale. Requesters gain flexible capacity and pay for granular outputs; workers gain remote access and schedule choice. The operational burden that disappears from the requester's view often reappears as worker search, waiting, interruption, equipment, rejection, currency-conversion, and income risk.

Microwork also makes automation and labor complementary. People label training examples, clean data, moderate content, or resolve edge cases that machines cannot handle, while software increasingly identifies worker strengths and automates easier portions. This shifts the task boundary rather than proving either durable employment growth or complete replacement. Governance determines who benefits: pricing authority, qualification opacity, reciprocal reputation, explanation, appeal, payment access, worker support, and protection from traumatic material all affect whether flexible work becomes usable opportunity or exploitation.

## Key Claims
- Digital platforms make labor modular by separating a job into independently allocated and priced tasks.
- Granularity and global reach give requesters flexible capacity while transferring coordination and demand volatility to workers.
- Effective hourly pay must include search, waiting, setup, screening, rejected work, and payment-conversion losses, not only paid task time.
- Qualification, geography, reputation, and first-come availability can segment access more strongly than the nominal openness of the marketplace suggests.
- Platform opacity is a labor condition because workers may not know the requester, downstream purpose, acceptance criteria, or route for appeal.
- Worker forums, reputation tools, and organizing sites function as counter-infrastructure for information and support.
- Automation can remove routine cases while generating new labeling, exception-handling, verification, and coordination work.

## Evidence
Task decomposition and reach:
- [[inside-amazons-clickworker-platform-how-half-a-million-people-are-being-paid-pennies-to-train-ai-techrepublic]] describes tasks ranging from receipt transcription and categorization to AI labeling and content moderation, allocated across a global workforce.

Hidden labor time:
- [[inside-amazons-clickworker-platform-how-half-a-million-people-are-being-paid-pennies-to-train-ai-techrepublic]] documents alerts, unpredictable batches, requester research, scripts, statistics checks, and uncompensated rejection.

Access and governance:
- [[inside-amazons-clickworker-platform-how-half-a-million-people-are-being-paid-pennies-to-train-ai-techrepublic]] reports geographic restrictions, opaque Masters status, one-way ratings, pseudonymous requesters, and weak formal support.

Human-machine boundary:
- [[inside-amazons-clickworker-platform-how-half-a-million-people-are-being-paid-pennies-to-train-ai-techrepublic]] describes platforms that automate easier cases while routing difficult examples to people who also help models learn.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a historical account of AMT and adjacent platforms, not a representative study of every crowd-labor market. Some workers used the platform for optional income and valued flexibility, while others depended on it for basic needs; neither experience should erase the other. Piece rates do not alone determine effective pay, and interview examples do not establish population-wide outcomes. The article also forecasts sustained demand for human-machine work without later employment, wage, or task-quality evidence.

## What Changed
- Created a labor-system synthesis that includes unpaid coordination and risk around the paid microtask.
- Connected platform governance and information asymmetry to effective job quality.
- Framed automation as a moving task boundary rather than a simple worker-versus-machine substitution.
