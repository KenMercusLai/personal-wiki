## Definition
[[ZebraCompanies]] are businesses designed to be both profitable and socially beneficial, emphasizing endurance, capital efficiency, institutional repair, and cooperation rather than treating rapid growth, disruption, valuation, or exit as ends in themselves.

## Current Synthesis
The source offers the zebra as a deliberate counter-model to the venture-backed unicorn. Its defining claim is not that profit should disappear, but that profit and purpose should constrain and reinforce one another. The metaphor adds three operating expectations: the company should be real and durable rather than valued through speculative mythology, should survive with disciplined use of capital, and should cooperate with peers because shared infrastructure and knowledge can strengthen the whole field.

The model extends beyond company intent. If a business model shapes culture, strategy, user experience, and social effects, then zebra outcomes depend on governance and finance as well as mission statements. The essay therefore treats [[MissionAlignedCapital]], compatible legal structures, patient institutional adoption, visible role models, and founder networks as parts of the company model. This is a normative framework and organizing vocabulary; the source does not supply comparative survival, return, impact, or governance data showing that zebra companies consistently achieve the proposed balance.

## Key Claims
- Zebra companies pursue profitability and social benefit together rather than sacrificing either by design.
- Endurance and capital efficiency matter more than valuation-driven growth or a quick exit.
- Mutual support among companies can create stronger collective outcomes than isolated competition.
- Repairing important social systems requires investment in institutional process, adoption, deployment, and measurement, not just new products.
- Company purpose remains credible only when financing, governance, culture, and strategy reinforce it.
- Zebra companies need an ecosystem of aligned capital, legal options, role models, and peer infrastructure to survive.

## Evidence
- Dual purpose: [[zebras-fix-what-unicorns-break-jennifer-mara-astrid-aniyia-medium]] defines zebras as simultaneously profitable and socially improving.
- Durability and efficiency: [[zebras-fix-what-unicorns-break-jennifer-mara-astrid-aniyia-medium]] contrasts zebra stamina and capital efficiency with unicorn-style quick exits and growth at all costs.
- Mutualism: [[zebras-fix-what-unicorns-break-jennifer-mara-astrid-aniyia-medium]] argues that zebras protect one another in groups and turn individual contributions into stronger collective output.
- Process over product: [[zebras-fix-what-unicorns-break-jennifer-mara-astrid-aniyia-medium]] says institutional problems require time and investment for adoption, deployment, and evaluation rather than an app alone.
- Incentive alignment: [[zebras-fix-what-unicorns-break-jennifer-mara-astrid-aniyia-medium]] traces business models into beliefs, strategy, user experience, and societal effects.
- Ecosystem requirement: [[zebras-fix-what-unicorns-break-jennifer-mara-astrid-aniyia-medium]] identifies funding, legal, tax, role-model, and network barriers and calls for coordinated founder and funder infrastructure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a manifesto written to recruit a movement, not an empirical comparison of company types. "Profit and purpose" can conceal tradeoffs over who defines impact, how benefits and harms are measured, how surplus and control are distributed, and what happens when survival conflicts with mission. Capital efficiency can also reflect funding scarcity rather than superior operations. The named examples illustrate plausibility but do not establish a common ownership structure, financing method, return profile, or verified social outcome.

The source's claims about women, people of color, and capital allocation use 2017 figures whose definitions and present-day validity are not established in the captured article. The zebra label should therefore function as a design and evaluation framework, not as automatic evidence that a company is inclusive, durable, or beneficial.

## What Changed
- Created the concept as a profit-and-purpose alternative to unicorn-oriented startup building.
- Made ecosystem support, institutional adoption, and mutualism part of the model rather than optional context.
- Preserved the distinction between an organizing framework and demonstrated comparative performance.
