Founder time leverage is the practice of spending small amounts of money to recover time from repetitive or low-value work. The Groove source frames founder time as the scarcest resource in the business: every hour spent on avoidable administration competes with customers, product improvement, team support, and strategy.

The source's examples include automating reports, using scheduling tools, and delegating travel or administrative work. The underlying rule is not that every task should be outsourced; it is that founders should notice when being "scrappy" has become a false economy. A cheap manual habit can be expensive if it consumes attention that would create more value elsewhere.

Time leverage also supports work-life boundaries. The article argues that founders who want a sustainable life need to protect time ruthlessly, which requires removing tasks that do not need founder judgment.

## Signals

- A recurring task can be handled by a tool, assistant, or service without losing important judgment.
- The saved time can be redirected to customers, product, sales, hiring, or recovery.
- Manual execution is defended as frugality even though it consumes more scarce founder attention than money.
- The founder has no realistic path to sustainable hours without changing the task mix.
