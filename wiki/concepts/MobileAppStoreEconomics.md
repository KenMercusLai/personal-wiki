## Definition
[[MobileAppStoreEconomics]] is the distinction between app marketplace scale, monetization, geography, category mix, and platform-owner revenue in mobile ecosystems.

## Current Synthesis
The VentureBeat/App Annie source shows that app-store advantage can split across different metrics. In 2015, [[GooglePlay]] had twice as many downloads as Apple's [[AppStore]], but Apple's store generated 75% more revenue. The source therefore turns [[Android]] and [[IOS]] into different economic profiles rather than a single winner-take-all ranking: Android/Google Play held broader install and download reach, especially through emerging markets, while iOS/App Store concentrated higher user spending in China, the U.S., and Japan. Games were the shared revenue engine, but the dependence was stronger on Google Play. The Sensor Tower source adds a third economic dimension: even the most visible download-chart rank can decay quickly, so acquisition spend that buys a No. 1 position may not produce lasting top-chart discovery.

## Key Claims
- Download volume and store revenue can point to different platform winners.
- In 2015, Google Play's download advantage widened while Apple's App Store revenue advantage also widened.
- Emerging-market growth can expand app-store downloads without immediately erasing monetization gaps.
- iOS App Store revenue strength reflected higher-spending user bases and country mix in the source.
- Games dominated mobile app-store revenue on both platforms, especially on Google Play.
- Ranking visibility has its own half-life: reaching No. 1 in downloads does not necessarily preserve top-25 placement or long-term discovery.

## Evidence
- Download/revenue split: [[app-annie-2015-google-play-saw-100-percent-more-downloads-than-ios-app-store-venturebeat]] reports Google Play at 100% more downloads while Apple's App Store had 75% more revenue.
- Download momentum: [[app-annie-2015-google-play-saw-100-percent-more-downloads-than-ios-app-store-venturebeat]] says Google Play's download lead grew from 60% in 2014 to 100% in 2015.
- Emerging-market downloads: [[app-annie-2015-google-play-saw-100-percent-more-downloads-than-ios-app-store-venturebeat]] names Brazil, India, Indonesia, Turkey, and Mexico as Google Play download-growth drivers.
- iOS revenue geography: [[app-annie-2015-google-play-saw-100-percent-more-downloads-than-ios-app-store-venturebeat]] names China, the U.S., and Japan as primary drivers of Apple's App Store revenue growth.
- Game revenue share: [[app-annie-2015-google-play-saw-100-percent-more-downloads-than-ios-app-store-venturebeat]] reports games at 90% of Google Play revenue and 75% of iOS App Store revenue.
- Ranking decay: [[74-of-no-1-iphone-apps-dont-last-a-month-in-the-top-25]] reports that among 53 first-time No. 1 U.S. iPhone apps in 2015-2016, 74% fell out of the top 25 within a month and only 8% remained after three months.
- Acquisition-return limit: [[74-of-no-1-iphone-apps-dont-last-a-month-in-the-top-25]] argues that resources used to drive apps to No. 1 through traditional user acquisition often did not create lasting chart visibility.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The App Annie/VentureBeat source reports relative metrics from a 2015 retrospective and does not include the original methodology, absolute downloads, absolute revenue, regional revenue breakdowns, or later shifts in subscription, ads, alternative payments, regulation, or app-store policy. The Sensor Tower source reports U.S. iPhone download-chart retention for first-time No. 1 apps in 2015-2016 and does not provide acquisition costs, revenue, category mix, paid ranking behavior, or later cohorts. Together, the sources show app-store economics through selected snapshots rather than current comprehensive market data.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to capture the 2015 split between Google Play's download scale and Apple's App Store revenue concentration.
- Added ranking-duration economics: No. 1 download rank often decayed too quickly to be treated as durable visibility.
