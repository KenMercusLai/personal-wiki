## Definition
[[BreakthroughKnowledge]] is information that materially changes a person's governing beliefs, questions, or decisions by supplying a durable new lens rather than another confirmation of what is already known.

## Current Synthesis
The concept is a prioritization heuristic for scarce learning time. Simmons contrasts rare, memorable "quake" books or passages with incremental material that confirms an existing view or briefly entertains. He proposes screening media by whether it could fundamentally change one's life and argues that disconfirming evidence has asymmetric value because one counterexample can overturn a broad belief. The heuristic is useful for asking about expected learning value, but it cannot reliably predict transformation in advance and may undervalue cumulative basics, practice, and apparently minor facts that become important later.

## Key Claims
- Knowledge differs in leverage, not only in accuracy or novelty.
- A new lens or disconfirming observation can outweigh many repetitions of an existing belief.
- Learners should evaluate potential value before surrendering attention to a source.
- High-leverage learning is rare enough that better search and selection can matter more than consuming more material.

## Evidence
- Quake-book examples: [[while-everyone-is-distracted-by-social-media-successful-people-double-down-on-an-underrated-skill]] uses books associated with Warren Buffett, Elon Musk, and Simmons to illustrate long-lived changes in thought.
- Selection question: [[while-everyone-is-distracted-by-social-media-successful-people-double-down-on-an-underrated-skill]] asks whether a source has the potential to fundamentally change the reader's life.
- Disconfirmation: [[while-everyone-is-distracted-by-social-media-successful-people-double-down-on-an-underrated-skill]] treats evidence against an existing belief as more informative than additional confirmation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supplies anecdotes rather than outcome comparisons, and retrospective stories about life-changing books are vulnerable to selection and attribution bias. A transformation-first filter can favor grand claims, neglect foundational repetition, and make ordinary skill accumulation look less valuable than it is. The best use of the concept is as one portfolio criterion, not as a rule that every item consumed must promise a dramatic life change.

## What Changed
- Established a distinction between high-leverage belief-changing knowledge and incremental confirmation.
- Qualified the dramatic filter with the value of cumulative practice and unpredictable future relevance.
