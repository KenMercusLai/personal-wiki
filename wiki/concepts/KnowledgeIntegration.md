## Definition
[[KnowledgeIntegration]] is the process of filtering, externalizing, organizing, linking, retrieving, and recombining knowledge so it can support a present question or action.

## Current Synthesis
The Wulc note presents knowledge use as more than collection. It first asks the learner to distinguish raw information from claims and methods that have been tested, and repeatable regularities from isolated experience. Useful material is then moved into an external note system, classified without obvious overlap or omission, structured, and linked. When a problem appears, thought quality depends on retrieving relevant background knowledge and rearranging it into a conclusion rather than merely remembering individual notes. This gives the workflow four interacting levers: the amount of relevant background knowledge, the strength of meaningful links, the quality of structure, and the ability to synthesize the retrieved material.

## Key Claims
- Intake should be filtered by evidence, repeatability, relevance, and source quality rather than treated as knowledge merely because it was encountered.
- External notes reduce reliance on memory, but storage becomes useful only when material remains retrievable.
- Classification and structure make relationships, omissions, and overlap easier to inspect.
- Links are valuable when they encode meaningful relationships that support later retrieval or comparison.
- Applying knowledge requires retrieving relevant background material and recombining it for the current problem.
- Thought quality depends jointly on knowledge breadth, link quality, structure, and synthesis ability.

## Evidence
- Intake filters: [[blog-wulc-ren-zhi-hong-li-yue-du-bi-ji-2-da-nao-sheng-ji]] distinguishes information from tested concepts, laws, and methods, and distinguishes one-off experience from repeatable causal regularity.
- Externalization and organization: [[blog-wulc-ren-zhi-hong-li-yue-du-bi-ji-2-da-nao-sheng-ji]] proposes an external note system followed by classification, structure, and links.
- Application: [[blog-wulc-ren-zhi-hong-li-yue-du-bi-ji-2-da-nao-sheng-ji]] describes thinking as retrieving relevant background knowledge and rearranging it into a conclusion.
- Four levers: [[blog-wulc-ren-zhi-hong-li-yue-du-bi-ji-2-da-nao-sheng-ji]] names knowledge quantity, link strength, structural quality, and synthesis ability as routes to better thinking.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers a compact practitioner framework rather than a measured learning intervention. Information does not become knowledge through a single binary verification step; evidence can be incomplete, probabilistic, contested, or domain-specific. MECE-style classification can clarify a bounded analysis but may distort overlapping or evolving subjects, and dense links can create maintenance burden without improving recall or judgment. External storage also cannot substitute for understanding, practice, retrieval skill, or correction against outcomes.

## What Changed
- Established knowledge integration as the bridge between stored material and problem-specific synthesis.
- Separated useful linking and structure from accumulation for its own sake.
