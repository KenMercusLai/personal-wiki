## Definition
[[ExtremeProgramming]] is the agile practice tradition Fowler presents as a central pillar of the Agile movement and a technical foundation for executing agile work properly.

## Current Synthesis
In the source, Extreme Programming matters because it keeps agile grounded in concrete software-development practices. Fowler's critique of mainstream "agile" turns on the loss of technical execution: without testing, refactoring, design, and collaborative development, teams may keep agile vocabulary while missing the capabilities that make adaptation safe.

XP also functions as a lineage marker. [[JamesShore]] is described as an early pioneer through XP, and Fowler says his own [[Thoughtworks]] teams used these techniques successfully over two decades.

Caroli's PBB article adds [[UserStories]] to that lineage: [[KentBeck]] introduced the term inside XP to support a more conversational style of requirements gathering. Later Product Backlog Building practice keeps that thread alive by treating stories as brief prompts for team conversation, acceptance criteria, and refinement.

## Key Claims
- XP is a central pillar of the Agile movement in Fowler's account.
- XP supplies technical practices that ceremony-led agile adoption often lacks.
- XP-informed work supports high productivity and reliability when combined with value focus.
- XP practice at Thoughtworks is presented as field-tested rather than theoretical.
- XP connects agile values to concrete engineering habits such as testing and refactoring.
- XP is the historical source of the User Story term in Caroli's account.

## Evidence
- Movement role: [[blog-martin-fowler-foreword-to-the-art-of-agile-development]] calls XP a central pillar of the Agile movement.

- Practice base: [[blog-martin-fowler-foreword-to-the-art-of-agile-development]] says Fowler and Shore both used XP techniques and found them foundational.
- Technical grounding: [[blog-martin-fowler-foreword-to-the-art-of-agile-development]] links reliable delivery to testing, refactoring, design, and collaborative development.
- User-story lineage: [[blog-paulo-caroli-martinfowler-com-product-backlog-building-canvas]] says Kent Beck introduced the User Story term as part of XP.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This page currently reflects Fowler's favorable use of XP as a practice foundation and Caroli's use of XP as user-story lineage. It does not catalogue XP's full practice set or compare XP with Scrum, Kanban, Lean, or other agile traditions.
