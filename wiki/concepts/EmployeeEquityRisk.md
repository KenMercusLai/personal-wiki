## Definition
[[EmployeeEquityRisk]] is the risk that startup employee equity will fail to become usable wealth because the company's outcome, liquidity path, vesting terms, preferences, dilution, or employment duration do not line up in the employee's favor.

## Current Synthesis
The sources converge on treating employee equity as a high-variance, illiquid compensation instrument rather than salary with a delayed payment date. [[4-hard-truths-about-equity-while-west]] emphasizes uncertainty, vesting exposure, founder-employee asymmetry, and hindsight bias. [[wtf-is-a-funding-round-techcrunch]] supplies the conventional upside case: early employees can benefit as valuation rises, and some companies offer secondary sales before an exit, but the shares otherwise remain paper value and can become worthless. Candidate-side analysis should therefore translate an offered percentage into plausible proceeds, reduce the headline result for time, incomplete vesting, tax, dilution, preferences, exercise conditions, and liquidity probability, and ask whether cash compensation and career capital justify the role if the residual upside is modest.

At the late stage, the payout mechanism becomes central. Multiplying a grant by the last-round valuation ignores the fully diluted share count, liquidation preferences, debt, and the order in which exit proceeds are distributed, as [[dont-get-trampled-the-puzzle-for-unicorn-employees]] explains. A financing can therefore preserve or increase a unicorn headline valuation while giving new investors protection that reduces what common employee shares receive in a moderate acquisition. The practical risk assessment must combine grant terms with company runway, future financing pressure, audited financials, leadership’s tolerance for a lower valuation, and a plausible route to liquidity. A scenario table showing employee proceeds at several sale or IPO values is more decision-useful than one nominal grant value.

Tradeable project tokens change the liquidity mechanism without removing employee risk. [[employee-incentives-in-a-tokenized-world-token-economy]] proposes earlier saleability and voluntary secondary purchases, but those features expose employees to volatile public pricing, shallow markets, short-term promotion incentives, concentrated downside, and historically unsettled insider-conduct rules. Liquidity is therefore an attribute to verify, not a categorical distinction between tokens and equity.

The 2016 [[Palantir]] case adds an organizational consequence when cash compensation is deliberately below market and stock options carry much of the expected upside. Employees became sensitive to company targets and sought ways to convert shares into cash while reported departures accelerated. Periodic liquidity events and a broad salary increase could reduce pressure, but they did not make private shares continuously saleable or prove that the company's large bookings would become cash. Employee equity risk therefore affects retention and compensation policy as well as individual payout, while [[is-it-time-for-you-to-earn-or-to-learn-both-sides-of-the-table]] supplies the complementary career judgment that a role can still be worthwhile for responsibility, skills, relationships, mission, and future options without a credible retirement narrative.

## Key Claims
- Employee equity outcomes are easy to judge in hindsight but hard to evaluate at the time of the offer or liquidity choice.
- Illiquidity makes startup equity meaningfully different from salary, while secondary sales or token trading remain conditional on access, restrictions, market depth, and price.
- Vesting cliffs, termination rights, and exercise constraints can leave employees without the economic benefit an award appears to promise.
- The fully diluted denominator, liquidation preferences, and debt can make common employee equity worth little or nothing in a moderate exit despite a high headline valuation.
- Founder and investor positions differ from employee common equity in control, information, seniority, and access to liquidity; stock-heavy pay can also concentrate retention risk when operating results become uncertain.
- Equity can align employees with company goals, but realistic valuation requires scenario-based common-share payouts rather than one last-round price; if those scenarios do not support wealth-scale expectations, candidates should judge the role on cash compensation and learning value.
- Tradeable tokens may accelerate liquidity and widen participation, but market depth, lockups, volatility, concentration, conduct rules, and sustainable utility determine whether that liquidity is usable.

## Evidence
- Hindsight risk: [[4-hard-truths-about-equity-while-west]] describes an early [[TinySpeck]] employee who sold shares before the later [[Slack]] pivot and argues that this looked foolish only after Slack became valuable.
- Illiquidity: [[4-hard-truths-about-equity-while-west]] says employee equity usually cannot be spent, liquidated, or traded before a future event.
- Partial liquidity: [[wtf-is-a-funding-round-techcrunch]] says many startups permit their employees to sell shares through a secondary offering and that those shares are usually sold based on the latest valuation.
- Selective-liquidity qualification: [[dont-get-trampled-the-puzzle-for-unicorn-employees]] says employees should ask whether founders or employees have sold shares when the company plans to remain private, because secondary sales may be their only path to liquidity.
- Early-join advantage: [[wtf-is-a-funding-round-techcrunch]] says the upside potential is greatest when an employee joins in the startup's early days, because valuations generally rise over time and the value of the equity compensation grows with them.
- Paper value until an exit: [[wtf-is-a-funding-round-techcrunch]] describes employee shares as often paper money until a liquidity event such as an IPO or acquisition, and as worth nothing if the startup goes bust.
- Vesting exposure: [[4-hard-truths-about-equity-while-west]] notes that a typical one-year cliff allows the company to terminate an employee before any equity vests.
- Preference risk: [[4-hard-truths-about-equity-while-west]] highlights share preference as the rule that determines who gets paid first in a liquidation.
- Preference magnitude: [[dont-get-trampled-the-puzzle-for-unicorn-employees]] distinguishes a standard 1x non-participating preference from higher multiples that can absorb exit proceeds before common shareholders receive anything.
- Senior claims and financing pressure: [[dont-get-trampled-the-puzzle-for-unicorn-employees]] links debt and unfavorable late-stage financing terms to reduced common-share payouts, especially when runway is short.
- Scenario valuation: [[dont-get-trampled-the-puzzle-for-unicorn-employees]] recommends asking what an employee’s options would pay across several hypothetical sale or IPO values.
- Founder asymmetry: [[4-hard-truths-about-equity-while-west]] says founders can use equity to hire, raise capital, and sometimes sell portions privately while employees hold smaller, less liquid positions.
- Alignment qualification: [[4-hard-truths-about-equity-while-west]] acknowledges that ownership can align employees with company goals while still warning that cash may be better under uncertainty.
- Tokenized alternative: [[employee-incentives-in-a-tokenized-world-token-economy]] proposes early trading and employee purchases while warning that weak liquidity, price-focused short-termism, and insider conduct can replace the waiting risk of private options with market and governance risk.
- Compensation concentration: [[inside-palantir-silicon-valleys-most-secretive-company]] reports salary caps below the market described by its sources and large stock-option grants that made employees sensitive to missed targets.
- Liquidity and retention: [[inside-palantir-silicon-valleys-most-secretive-company]] connects employee anxiety about converting shares into cash with periodic liquidity events, more than 100 departures through mid-April 2016, and a 20% tenure-based salary increase.
- Small-grant scenario: [[is-it-time-for-you-to-earn-or-to-learn-both-sides-of-the-table]] shows how a nominal percentage can become modest annualized proceeds after exit timing, vesting, tax, dilution, and liquidation preferences.
- Career-value alternative: [[is-it-time-for-you-to-earn-or-to-learn-both-sides-of-the-table]] argues that responsibility, skills, relationships, mission, and resume value should justify a role when option wealth is unlikely.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are practitioner, explanatory, or investigative articles rather than quantified compensation studies. Their warnings apply most strongly to private-company common equity or speculative 2017-era tokens; public-company RSUs, tender offers, extended exercise windows, grant size, strike price, tax treatment, and company-specific liquidity programs can materially change the risk. TechCrunch describes secondary offerings but does not establish how common, broad, or fairly priced they are. Belsky explains the payout-waterfall problem without providing a worked cap table, observed employee-outcome distribution, or evidence that candidates can obtain every requested term. Suster's 2009 examples are illustrative rather than current benchmarks and simplify dilution, strike price, exercise cost, tax character, exit distributions, and probability. The token essay assumes useful and durable token demand and provides no evidence that purchase-based loss aversion improves work. The Palantir report does not establish why each employee left, the distribution or realized value of option grants, or whether compensation caused turnover; loyalty, new opportunities, culture fit, and rapid headcount growth complicate the interpretation. A standard 1x non-participating preference is not itself evidence of abuse, and debt or a large round can fund durable growth; the relevant issue is the total payout structure under plausible outcomes.
