## Definition
[[FocusSupportiveWorkspaceDesign]] is the design of physical work settings around the trade-off between low-friction collaboration and protection of the uninterrupted cognitive state required by demanding work.

## Current Synthesis
The Rands source rejects a binary choice between isolated private offices and an undifferentiated open floor. Open space can increase serendipity, weaken status signals, reduce real-estate cost, and make team membership visible. It can also expose focused workers to irrelevant visual and auditory interruptions whose recovery cost is not captured by square-foot calculations. The proposed middle form is team-controlled common space: enclosed pods or claimed rooms shared by people doing related work, with boundaries against unrelated traffic. The durable principle is to measure workspace design against actual work modes and interruption recovery, not to infer productivity from density or proximity alone.

## Key Claims
- Real-estate efficiency and work productivity are separate outcomes and should be measured separately.
- Decision makers who work mainly in private meeting rooms may not observe the interruption burden of open desks.
- Open layouts can provide serendipity, weaker hierarchy cues, and team identity while still harming sustained focus.
- Team-controlled common space can retain relevant collaboration while filtering unrelated interruption.
- Workspace choice should vary with the task and worker rather than assume one layout universally produces the best work.

## Evidence
Decision-maker exposure:
- [[rands-your-best-work]] argues that meeting-heavy managers often make space decisions from quiet conference rooms rather than from the open desks affected by those choices.

Self-organized boundaries:
- [[rands-your-best-work]] reports fast-moving teams occupying corners, conference rooms, or unused floors to create space they controlled.

Pod proposal:
- [[rands-your-best-work]] favors enclosed pods for people working on related technologies and projects as a balance between collaboration and interruption protection.

Open-space benefits:
- [[rands-your-best-work]] identifies low-friction access, serendipity, reduced hierarchy signaling, team visibility, and lower space cost as genuine advantages.

Focus cost:
- [[rands-your-best-work]] describes visual and auditory interruption as a threat to an engineer's train of thought, context, and mental momentum.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept currently rests on one 2015 practitioner essay. It reports observations but provides no floor plans, before-and-after comparison, output measure, acoustic data, survey, cost model, or evidence that pods outperform alternatives. Enclosure can create silos, unequal access, ventilation or accessibility problems, underused space, and barriers to cross-team learning. Open layouts may be more suitable for work requiring rapid shared awareness, while private or remote settings may better support individual concentration. Worker preference also varies, and the source's introvert, extravert, stable, and volatile labels do not substitute for individual assessment. Team-controlled space should therefore be treated as a testable design option rather than a universal optimum.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to separate workspace productivity from real-estate density and generic collaboration claims.
- Added team-controlled common space as a middle form between private isolation and organization-wide open floors.
