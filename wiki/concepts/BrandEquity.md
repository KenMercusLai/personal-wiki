## Definition
[[BrandEquity]] is the accumulated customer belief and referability a company earns by consistently fulfilling promises across its product, price, channel, and marketing.

## Current Synthesis
[[AdamTaussig]] frames brand equity as an effect of repeated delivery rather than a visual asset created independently by branding. The company promises a safe and effective product or service, fair pricing, convenient access, and marketing that makes effective use salient. When customers repeatedly see those promises fulfilled, their belief may become recommendation and word of mouth; when a promise fails, the accumulated equity may be impaired.

This model connects brand to the entire operating offer, but its measurement argument is narrower than its definition. [[NetPromoterScore]] captures stated recommendation likelihood and may help identify referability, yet it does not separately verify product safety, effectiveness, price fairness, channel convenience, actual referral, or durable market leadership.

Recognition, trust, and willingness to pay are additional possible economic expressions in [[GibsonBiddle]]'s account. His Netflix case argues that accumulated meaning eventually let a simpler homepage rely on the brand to communicate value, while the Apple example attributes part of a price premium to promised “revolutionary innovation.” These examples make the commercial hypothesis concrete but do not isolate brand from product quality, ecosystem effects, catalog, pricing, or market power.

The [[Tesla]] case adds anticipation and forgiveness as two further possible expressions. [[BenThompson]] interprets 115,000 Model 3 deposits placed before the reveal as demand for what “Tesla” had come to mean—performance, sustainability, and Silicon Valley identity—and predicts that this reputation could buy patience for delay or a higher final price. This strengthens the concept's connection to future expectations, but the source observes refundable reservations rather than completed purchases and forecasts customer grace rather than measuring it.

## Key Claims
- Brand equity is accumulated belief produced by consistent delivery, not a synonym for visual identity.
- Product safety and effectiveness, price fairness, channel convenience, and useful marketing are interdependent customer promises.
- Failure on any promise can weaken the broader brand even when visual branding remains coherent.
- Recommendation can spread customer belief through word of mouth and make brand strength partly social.
- NPS is a possible referability proxy but an incomplete measure of brand equity.
- Strong brand meaning may reduce the explanation needed at conversion surfaces, support willingness to pay, create demand before full product inspection, and buy limited patience, but those outcomes have multiple causes.

## Evidence
- Four-promise model: [[four-promises-a-brand-makes-to-its-customers-lightspeed-venture-partners-medium]] names product, price, channel, and marketing as the promises constituting a brand.
- Accumulation and impairment: [[four-promises-a-brand-makes-to-its-customers-lightspeed-venture-partners-medium]] compares consistent brand-promise delivery with belief in future cash flows and says failures can impair accumulated equity.
- Recommendation loop: [[four-promises-a-brand-makes-to-its-customers-lightspeed-venture-partners-medium]] links customer belief, recommendation, referability, and word-of-mouth growth.
- Operational priority: [[four-promises-a-brand-makes-to-its-customers-lightspeed-venture-partners-medium]] argues that entrepreneurs should deliver the four promises before relying on artful branding.
- Recognition and trust: [[gibson-biddle-branding-for-builders]] contrasts Netflix's early obscurity with later logo recognition and willingness to provide payment details.
- Reduced explanation: [[gibson-biddle-branding-for-builders]] says simpler Netflix homepages began winning only after the brand itself communicated more value.
- Price-premium hypothesis: [[gibson-biddle-branding-for-builders]] presents customers paying more for Apple devices as an illustration of brand's economic power.
- Anticipatory demand: [[its-a-tesla-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] reports 115,000 refundable Model 3 deposits before the car was shown and attributes part of that response to Tesla's accumulated meaning.
- Grace hypothesis: [[its-a-tesla-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] predicts that a long-standing “best” reputation can give a company time to recover from delay or price slippage.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence consists of a short investor essay, a former executive's retrospective, and a 2016 strategy analysis, not comparative studies or a validated measurement model. Safety, effectiveness, fairness, convenience, and salience are category- and customer-dependent, and the sources do not explain how to adjudicate tradeoffs among them. They provide no evidence that all brands require exactly four promises, that one failure has a predictable effect, or that collective belief constitutes a true network effect rather than ordinary reputation and word of mouth. NPS, homepage conversion, logo recognition, simplified design, willingness to pay, and refundable reservations are partial signals; none independently separates brand from product quality, price, specifications, distribution, familiarity, selection, founder attention, or market structure. The Tesla essay predicts customer grace but does not observe fulfillment behavior.

## What Changed
- Added sight-unseen reservation demand as a possible expression of accumulated brand expectation.
- Added limited customer patience as a hypothesis while preserving the gap between forecast grace and observed behavior.
- Expanded causal qualifications around product performance, novelty, specifications, founder attention, and refundable deposits.
