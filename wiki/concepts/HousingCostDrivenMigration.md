## Definition
[[HousingCostDrivenMigration]] is relocation motivated primarily by the possibility that lower housing and commuting costs elsewhere will improve household security and attainable living standards, even without higher destination pay.

## Current Synthesis
The Lopez column makes the mechanism visible through household budgets. A move can be economically upward without being occupationally upward when it reduces rent or mortgage payments, shortens commuting time, and makes room for retirement saving, education, caregiving, or homeownership. The same move can still be a loss of place: the profiled Californians retained strong attachments to family, culture, weather, and familiar institutions, so migration appears as an affordability constraint rather than a simple preference for the destination.

## Key Claims
- Relative housing and commuting costs can matter more to household wellbeing than a nominal salary increase.
- Cost-led migration can convert the same broad class of employment into savings, ownership, time, space, or reduced financial stress.
- A destination's value depends on a bundle of housing, jobs, proximity, taxes, transport, and social fit rather than cheap homes alone.
- Reluctant departure can signal that migration is constraint-driven even when the resulting household budget improves.
- Repeated individual exits may relieve particular households while indicating unresolved housing-supply and affordability problems in the origin region.

## Evidence
- Budget mechanism: [[column-theyre-leaving-california-for-las-vegas-to-find-the-middle-class-life-that-eluded-them-los-angeles-times]] compares Southern California rents and long commutes with lower Nevada rents, mortgages, and home prices.
- Household gains: [[column-theyre-leaving-california-for-las-vegas-to-find-the-middle-class-life-that-eluded-them-los-angeles-times]] reports 401(k) saving, house saving, larger homes, reduced stress, shorter commutes, and a one-income family option after relocation.
- Destination bundle: [[column-theyre-leaving-california-for-las-vegas-to-find-the-middle-class-life-that-eluded-them-los-angeles-times]] presents [[LasVegas]] as close to California, job-bearing, lower-cost, and tax-advantaged.
- Reluctant exit: [[column-theyre-leaving-california-for-las-vegas-to-find-the-middle-class-life-that-eluded-them-los-angeles-times]] records interviewees' continuing attachment to California family, culture, weather, and outdoor life.
- Structural interpretation: [[column-theyre-leaving-california-for-las-vegas-to-find-the-middle-class-life-that-eluded-them-los-angeles-times]] cites a housing economist's conditional expectation that continued cost increases would drive more departures and argues that California's housing response lacked scale.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a 2017 opinion column using selected respondents rather than a representative migration sample. Its census reference is not reproduced in detail, and it does not control for wages, taxes, household composition, amenities, later housing appreciation, moving costs, or selection effects. Reported improvements show that relocation can work for these households, not that housing costs alone caused statewide migration or that Las Vegas is broadly more advantageous. Exit can be a practical individual response without being an equitable or scalable substitute for housing reform.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish cost-led geographic exit from moves driven by higher pay or promotion.
