## Definition
[[WorkBreaks]] are deliberate pauses from focused work used to restore energy, attention, and perspective so later work can be done more effectively.

## Current Synthesis
The current source frames breaks as a productivity practice for knowledge work rather than a reward after work is complete. Its strongest claim is qualitative: sustained work drains focus and energy, while breaks can replenish those resources and create enough distance to reassess goals or make creative connections. Its timing advice is practical and source-scoped: use longer 90-minute work blocks in the morning, Pomodoro intervals later in the day, and roughly 15 minutes of break time per hour as a general starting rule.

## Key Claims
- Breaks are productive when they restore energy and focus for knowledge work.
- Morning work can be organized around roughly 90-minute focus blocks followed by at least 20 minutes off.
- Afternoon work may benefit from shorter Pomodoro cycles that force single-tasking and recurring recovery.
- A general hourly rule of thumb is to break for about 15 minutes per hour worked.
- Recovery time can improve work quality by helping the worker step back, make creative connections, and reevaluate goals or approach.

## Evidence
- Recovery mechanism: [[chris-bailey-exactly-how-long-your-work-breaks-should-be]] says breaks replenish energy reserves, recharge focus, support creative connections, and create space to reconsider goals or methods.
- Morning rhythm: [[chris-bailey-exactly-how-long-your-work-breaks-should-be]] cites daytime 90-minute wakefulness cycles and recommends 90-minute morning work chunks followed by at least 20 minutes of break.
- Afternoon cadence: [[chris-bailey-exactly-how-long-your-work-breaks-should-be]] recommends Pomodoro cycles of 25 minutes on one task, 5 minutes off, and a longer break after the fourth round.
- Hourly heuristic: [[chris-bailey-exactly-how-long-your-work-breaks-should-be]] cites DeskTime's 52-minute work and 17-minute break pattern among top productive workers, then translates it into an easier starting rule of a 15-minute break every hour.
- Perspective benefit: [[chris-bailey-exactly-how-long-your-work-breaks-should-be]] argues that time away from work can help workers reevaluate what they are doing and how they are approaching it.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a short practitioner article, not a primary research review. Its cited study links are present, but the saved Markdown does not include methods, sample details, effect sizes, or evidence quality. The exact 52/17 and Pomodoro numbers should therefore be treated as useful heuristics rather than universal laws. Some roles also have externally imposed response times, shift patterns, caregiving constraints, or collaborative obligations that limit how freely break cadence can be scheduled.
