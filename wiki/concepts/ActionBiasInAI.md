## Definition
[[ActionBiasInAI]] is the stance that people should respond to AI by doing concrete work, building artifacts, and learning from real problems instead of waiting for perfect methods or consuming motivational content.

## Current Synthesis
The sources frame AI-era opportunity as a test of agency. Mai Yang targets a passive loop where people consume success methods, founder stories, and strategic advice while avoiding the exposure of actually making something. Leetao identifies a subtler version inside apparently productive work: AI research and experiments can repeatedly justify abandoning ideas until thinking and evaluation displace craft.

Mai Yang's founder stories—[[ElonMusk]] after PayPal, [[MarkZuckerberg]] building Thefacebook, [[SteveJobs]] losing sleep over product details, and [[JeffBezos]] leaving Wall Street to start Amazon—are rhetorical rather than empirical. Leetao supplies a smaller first-person counterweight: he restarted [[Memox|memox]], removed most of its code, and released [[VoiceFloat]] around one core task. Together they make the defensible claim narrower: action creates a learning and motivational environment that plans, reports, and model judgments cannot fully substitute for, while evidence should still shape scope and risk.

## Key Claims
- AI makes action more urgent because waiting for perfect methods can become disguised spectatorship.
- Passive success-content consumption can produce stimulation without creating artifacts, feedback, or skill.
- Genuine desire is distinguished from shallow interest by whether it creates sustained urgency and willingness to pay costs.
- Founder stories are used to show learning through commitment rather than preparation alone.
- Self-honesty is required to separate internally owned goals from hesitation and [[MimeticDesire]].
- AI-supported validation can become another avoidance loop when every negative result ends practice before craft, personal fit, or a bounded release can be explored.

## Evidence
- Central imperative: [[blog-maiyang-naval-insights-taking-action-with-ai]] repeatedly states that people need to act toward AI.
- Passive trap: [[blog-maiyang-naval-insights-taking-action-with-ai]] compares success-method consumption to reading swimming manuals without entering the pool.
- Desire test: [[blog-maiyang-naval-insights-taking-action-with-ai]] asks whether the desire is strong enough to make someone lose sleep.
- Founder examples: [[blog-maiyang-naval-insights-taking-action-with-ai]] uses Musk, Zuckerberg, Jobs, and Bezos as examples of drive becoming concrete work.
- False motivation: [[blog-maiyang-naval-insights-taking-action-with-ai]] warns against hesitation and imitating others' desires.
- Analysis trap: [[guo-qing-sui-bi-leetao]] reports that many ideas and repeated reasons to abandon them produced thought without hands-on practice.
- Scoped return to action: [[guo-qing-sui-bi-leetao]] describes restarting memox, removing about 80% of its code, and releasing single-purpose VoiceFloat as the point when creative enjoyment returned.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Both sources are motivational or reflective essays, not evidence that all successful AI use or entrepreneurship begins with overwhelming desire or continued building. Founder anecdotes can compress luck, social position, capital access, teams, timing, legal context, and later consequences into a clean action narrative. Leetao reports renewed enjoyment, not demand, quality, retention, or commercial success; abandoning a weak idea can still be the correct outcome. Some AI work also requires safety review, domain knowledge, data governance, and verification before action is responsible. The strongest reading is therefore practical and personal: avoid passive preparation and analysis loops, but pair bounded action with judgment and feedback.
