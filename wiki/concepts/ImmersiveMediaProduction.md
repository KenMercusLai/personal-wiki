## Definition
[[ImmersiveMediaProduction]] is the design of captured live-event media around sustained spatial presence, using camera placement, shot duration, sound, information, and viewer agency to preserve the feeling of occupying a location.

## Current Synthesis
Production grammar is medium-specific. Television compensates for a flat, bounded screen by selecting among many cameras and adding commentary, graphics, replays, and studio segments. Immersive video can instead make the camera position feel like the viewer's body, so an editorial cut behaves less like a new shot and more like involuntary teleportation. A cut that clarifies television action may therefore impose orientation cost and break presence in a headset.

The presence-first alternative is not literally production-free. It moves effort away from constant live direction and toward camera placement, reliable capture, spatial audio, transmission, rights, accessibility, and giving the viewer stable seats or explicit viewpoint choice. This may lower marginal production complexity and expand coverage, but the source does not quantify those savings or establish one camera as universally optimal.

## Key Claims
- Production conventions should follow a medium's distinctive affordance rather than inherit the previous medium's defaults.
- In immersive video, camera position functions like viewer location, making unchosen cuts spatially and physically disruptive.
- Long, stable shots can preserve presence even when several independently valuable camera positions exist.
- Venue sound and information visible within the scene can replace some commentary, graphics, and studio packaging.
- Viewer-controlled seat selection can preserve agency while still supporting multiple viewpoints.
- Simpler live direction may improve both experience and content supply by reducing per-event production overhead.

## Evidence
- Medium mismatch: [[apple-you-still-dont-understand-the-vision-pro]] contrasts television's evolution toward large multi-camera productions with immersive video's ability to simulate being in one place.
- Cut frequency: [[apple-you-still-dont-understand-the-vision-pro]] documents 54 shots in an approximately five-minute MLS video and reports disorientation during a within-play camera change.
- Stable-seat value: [[apple-you-still-dont-understand-the-vision-pro]] says both scorer's-table and baseline NBA views were compelling when experienced as continuous seats.
- Cross-event pattern: [[apple-you-still-dont-understand-the-vision-pro]] reports that a 14-camera Metallica production felt like a documentary rather than concert attendance.
- Economic hypothesis: [[apple-you-still-dont-understand-the-vision-pro]] links hosts, announcers, multiple cameras, and production crews to scarce coverage, then proposes fixed cameras as a path to more events.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one author's subjective account of selected Apple productions. Some sports require wide tactical views, replays, close-ups, explanation, or accessibility layers; some viewers may prefer directed storytelling; and stable head-mounted viewpoints can create their own comfort or visibility problems. "No production" understates capture, audio, encoding, networking, rights, safety, app, and venue work. No controlled comparison establishes the ideal cut rate, degree of viewer control, cost reduction, or transfer across sports, concerts, and conventions.

## What Changed
- Added a medium-specific distinction between television editing and presence-first immersive production.
- Identified spatial reorientation as a cost of producer-controlled cuts.
- Separated reduced live direction from the broader production work that fixed-camera delivery still requires.
