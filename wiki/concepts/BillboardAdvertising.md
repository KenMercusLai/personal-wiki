## Definition
[[BillboardAdvertising]] is paid out-of-home advertising on fixed or mobile physical surfaces, used mainly to create broad public awareness rather than precisely targeted or directly attributable conversion.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats billboards as a real but narrow-fit marketing option for small businesses. Their advantage is public visibility: they can make a local or mass-market brand familiar, and a creative campaign can delight existing fans. Their weakness is bluntness: the audience is broad, the message is hard to personalize, the lifespan is short, and the connection to sales can be unclear. A billboard therefore works best when the business has a high enough [[CustomerAcquisitionCost]] tolerance, a broad enough target market, a carefully vetted location, and a goal closer to awareness or [[BrandDistinctiveness]] than immediate direct response.

## Key Claims
- Billboard buying should be evaluated against expected customer value and CAC tolerance, not only raw visibility.
- Location quality is part of the product because traffic, neighborhood fit, and road context shape whether impressions are useful.
- Billboards are poor personalization surfaces because every passerby receives the same message.
- Short campaign lifespans make billboards weaker as a compounding channel than durable assets such as SEO content.
- Branded vehicles can provide a cheaper mobile alternative for some local businesses.
- Creative billboard campaigns can be worthwhile when the goal is brand delight rather than directly attributable sales.

## Evidence
- Cost ranges: [[billboards-for-small-businesses-costs-advice-and-thinking-twice]] cites four-week bulletin ranges for Boston, San Diego, and Milwaukee to show that the channel can consume meaningful small-business budget.
- CAC threshold: [[billboards-for-small-businesses-costs-advice-and-thinking-twice]] says billboards may make sense for businesses willing to spend roughly $1,000 to gain a customer.
- Attribution problem: [[billboards-for-small-businesses-costs-advice-and-thinking-twice]] reports that Grasshopper saw more advertiser sales calls than customer sales from its billboard.
- Targeting and lifespan limits: [[billboards-for-small-businesses-costs-advice-and-thinking-twice]] contrasts mass roadside exposure and four-to-six-week placements with targeted content, SEO, PPC, lead generation, blogging, PR, and email.
- Location vetting: [[billboards-for-small-businesses-costs-advice-and-thinking-twice]] uses a poorly placed zoo billboard anecdote to argue that buyers must inspect the site themselves.
- Brand-delight case: [[billboards-for-small-businesses-costs-advice-and-thinking-twice]] presents [[Mailchimp]]'s mascot billboard as a campaign intended to make users smile rather than sell features.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is practitioner advice rather than a controlled study of billboard ROI. Its cost figures are location- and period-specific, and the Grasshopper example should not be generalized to every local, high-ticket, or mass-market advertiser. The source also notes that billboards can work for local businesses, cars, mortgages, insurance, and creative brands when the placement and goal fit.
