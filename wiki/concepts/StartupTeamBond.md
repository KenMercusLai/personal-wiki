## Definition
[[StartupTeamBond]] is the trust, care, humor, and shared resilience among founders and early employees that helps a startup survive pressure and remain valuable beyond a single product outcome.

## Current Synthesis
The Bump retrospective treats team bond as the most durable artifact of the startup. [[DavidLieb]] says the most lasting thing about most startups is the people, especially cofounders, and supports that claim with stories about [[AndyHuibers]], Jake, Halloween humor, burnout, office injury, and post-acquisition retention at [[Google]]. The concept overlaps with startup culture, but its emphasis is more intimate: cofounders and early teammates become the support system that carries people through uncertainty, failure, and later transitions.

## Key Claims
- Cofounder choice can matter as much as product choice under extreme pressure.
- Shared humor helps a team avoid taking itself too seriously.
- Early teams may become durable relationships even when products change or fail.
- Post-acquisition retention can indicate that a startup created strong team cohesion.

## Evidence
- People as durable outcome: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says the most lasting thing about most startups is the people, especially cofounders.
- Cofounder care: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says [[AndyHuibers]] washed Lieb's hair after Lieb was injured and could not get stitches wet.
- Humor ritual: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] describes the team dressing like Andy for Halloween.
- Team continuity: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says 17 of 17 Bump team members were still at [[Google]] after four years and 15 of 17 after five years.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source highlights affection and loyalty but also includes burnout and injury, so team bond should not be romanticized as a substitute for sustainable work practices. It is also a founder-centered account and does not include every employee's perspective.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from the Bump source's cofounder and retention lessons.
