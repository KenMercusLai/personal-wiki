## Definition
[[TechnologyEnabledProductInnovation]] is product discovery that begins with a real customer or business problem while using engineering knowledge of newly practical technology to invent and test solutions customers could not have specified in advance.

## Current Synthesis
The source separates inspiration from specification. Customer frustration, behavior, and willingness to adopt reveal the problem and test whether a solution creates value; they do not reliably reveal what technology now makes possible. Engineers contribute product judgment by recognizing enabling capabilities, constructing prototypes, and helping the team explore the solution space before committing to delivery.

This model is organizational as well as technical. Engineers need business and customer context, direct exposure to pain, constraints rather than predetermined requirements, time in discovery, shared outcome measures, and a product manager able to integrate customer, business, data, and company knowledge without claiming sole authorship of the answer. Alexa, MagicBand, machine-learning translation, the iPhone, and Workiva's web software are presented as examples, but the source does not establish how much of each outcome is attributable to engineering participation alone.

## Key Claims
- Customer evidence is strongest as a signal of pain, behavior, and value, not as a specification of an unfamiliar solution.
- Engineers can identify product opportunities by connecting customer problems to newly practical technical capabilities.
- Prototypes make an enabling technology concrete enough for teammates and customers to evaluate.
- Fixed requirements unnecessarily narrow the solution space when the real obligations can be expressed as legal, financial, commercial, manufacturing, or technical constraints.
- Discovery works best when engineers receive business context, meet customers, and share responsibility for product outcomes.
- Product managers enable the model by integrating context and collaborating confidently with technical specialists.

## Evidence
- Innovation cases: [[customer-inspired-technology-enabled-silicon-valley-product-group]] attributes Alexa's expansion, MagicBand, Google Translate's machine-learning improvement, the iPhone, and Workiva WDesk to teams using enabling technology against observed needs.
- Prototype mechanism: [[customer-inspired-technology-enabled-silicon-valley-product-group]] says an engineer-built Echo/TV prototype made Alexa's broader smart-home potential visible.
- Customer-pain boundary: [[customer-inspired-technology-enabled-silicon-valley-product-group]] says engineers should observe customers using products and prototypes to understand friction and willingness to buy, not ask customers to design the answer.
- Operating conditions: [[customer-inspired-technology-enabled-silicon-valley-product-group]] recommends full business context, constraint translation, engineering discovery time, whole-team measurement, and competent collaborative product management.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The article uses selected success stories and an author's practitioner experience rather than comparative data. Customers can sometimes originate valuable solutions, and engineers can also overvalue technical novelty, miss accessibility or domain constraints, or build impressive prototypes without durable demand. Large bets such as Disney's reported infrastructure investment also depend on executive sponsorship, capital, design, operations, distribution, and implementation quality, so "engineer-enabled" should not be read as "engineer-only."

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish customer-derived problem evidence from technology-derived solution possibilities.
