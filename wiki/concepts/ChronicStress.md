## Definition
[[ChronicStress]] is prolonged or repeatedly inescapable stress that persists without adequate control, resolution, reward, or recovery.

## Current Synthesis
In the source's framework, duration alone does not fully distinguish harmful stress. Loss of autonomy, steep demands, unpredictable threat, inability to retreat, and inadequate recovery turn challenge into a persistent load. School examinations, constrained workplaces, and political restrictions are offered as contexts where decision control can be removed.

The proposed harms are mutually reinforcing: sustained stress hormones and disrupted sleep can impair learning and emotional regulation, while poorly calibrated threat appraisal can make low-probability dangers feel urgent. This directly qualifies the idea that pressure automatically prepares people for adult life; externally imposed overload may damage the [[ProblemValuationNetwork]] and [[StressResilience]] it supposedly trains.

## Key Claims
- Chronic stress is associated with low control, unresolved threat, and insufficient recovery.
- Educational pressure can become harmful when obstacles are too steep or stakes feel inescapable.
- Sleep disruption is presented as both an effect and amplifier of prolonged stress.
- Excessive childhood pressure may distort threat appraisal rather than build resilience.
- The same challenge can remain useful or become harmful depending on autonomy, dose, predictability, and reward.

## Evidence
- Education example: [[liang-xing-ya-li-de-ji-zhi]] contrasts manageable challenge with a high-stakes examination experienced as an imminent threat.
- Control mechanism: [[liang-xing-ya-li-de-ji-zhi]] identifies inability to control decisions in school, work, or society as a major source of harmful stress.
- Sleep pathway: [[liang-xing-ya-li-de-ji-zhi]] links chronic stress to insomnia and delayed sleep timing.
- Developmental qualification: [[liang-xing-ya-li-de-ji-zhi]] argues that too much childhood stress can weaken rather than strengthen future threat valuation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source makes strong claims about brain damage, hormones, sleep, anxiety, and depression without presenting studies or diagnostic criteria. This page preserves those claims as source-scoped mechanisms, not medical advice or proof that every demanding school, job, or society causes the same outcomes.

## What Changed
- Created the concept as the harmful, low-control contrast to eustress.
