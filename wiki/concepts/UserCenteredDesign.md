## Definition
[[UserCenteredDesign]] is the practice of making product decisions around the intended audience's goals, knowledge, behavior, and context rather than treating the designer's own preferences as representative.

## Current Synthesis
[[MichalTurjeman]]'s [[TwelveUp]] account frames user-centered design as disciplined decentering when the designer is not part of the target audience. Domain ignorance is not automatically disqualifying, but it must be made visible and reduced through persistent questions. Similarly, intuitive reactions can begin the work but should not end it: personas can reintroduce the intended users' perspective, and a deliberately constructed alternative with an honest pro/con comparison can expose attachment to a favored design.

Schippers identifies an earlier failure mode: teams can begin from a technical solution and explore its possibility space before framing the human problem correctly. User-centered design therefore constrains both perspective and scope. It asks not only whose judgment is being substituted for the user's, but which technically possible capabilities should be withheld because they do not advance the user's purpose. These sources support a practical attitude more than a complete research method. They explain how to resist self-reference, technology-led framing, and attachment to capability, but report no direct observation, interviews, recruitment method, or measured product outcomes.

## Key Claims
- Designers do not need to share an audience's identity or interests, but they must treat their knowledge gap as a research and communication problem.
- Basic questions are productive when they reveal the domain constraints needed to define the problem correctly.
- "I would" judgments are weak evidence when the designer is not representative of the intended audience.
- Personas can redirect attention toward users, but their value depends on whether they are grounded in real audience evidence.
- Generating a second solution and comparing explicit pros and cons can counter attachment and make intuitive judgment discussable.
- Correct problem framing starts from the improvement sought in users' lives, not from the available technology or the breadth it can expose.

## Evidence
- Domain gap: [[designing-a-product-youre-not-going-to-use-michal-turjeman-medium]] describes an ex-ballerina with little sports knowledge designing football and American-sports media products.
- Questioning: [[designing-a-product-youre-not-going-to-use-michal-turjeman-medium]] argues that openly asking even elementary questions is necessary to understand an unfamiliar problem.
- Perspective separation: [[designing-a-product-youre-not-going-to-use-michal-turjeman-medium]] rejects designer self-reference and recommends returning to named personas when decisions become difficult.
- Alternative comparison: [[designing-a-product-youre-not-going-to-use-michal-turjeman-medium]] describes creating a second solution, listing each option's pros and cons, and selecting only after the comparison.
- Problem and scope: [[engineers-build-ugly-products-techcrunch]] argues that product work can begin from a misframed technical solution and that usable products require choosing and removing capabilities around a human purpose.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is two short practitioner essays with no direct user testimony, research protocol, shipped-decision trace, or outcome measures. Turjeman advises asking questions but does not specify whether answers came from representative users, coworkers, domain experts, or secondary research; Schippers generalizes about engineer preferences without observed comparisons. Personas can reproduce team assumptions when they are not evidence-based, and subtraction can remove valuable agency, accessibility, or expert depth when teams misunderstand the audience. These practices should therefore be complemented by direct research and [[UserTesting]] when decisions materially affect users.

## What Changed
- Added human problem framing and deliberate capability subtraction as parts of user-centered scope control.
- Created a synthesis of user-centered design as disciplined separation between designer preference and audience evidence under domain unfamiliarity.
