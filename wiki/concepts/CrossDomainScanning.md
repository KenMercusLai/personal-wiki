## Definition
[[CrossDomainScanning]] is the practice of deliberately collecting concrete observations from many unrelated fields so that weak signals, surprising constraints, and transferable patterns become visible.

## Current Synthesis
Whitwell's 2017 list shows cross-domain scanning as a lighter, messier complement to [[SystematicLearning]]. Instead of building a deep curriculum, it gathers small sourced facts from many fields: AI translation in Shenzhen markets, DIY medical devices, autonomous ships, old pagers and typewriters, GPS spoofing, solar power, WEIRD sampling, poverty research, headline sharing, and customer research. The value comes from juxtaposition. The reader sees how new technology enters informal settings, how old infrastructure persists inside modern systems, how social incentives shape risk and privacy, and how some spectacular claims need source-level caution.

## Key Claims
- Broad factual intake can surface weak signals before they become coherent theories.
- Juxtaposing unrelated domains helps expose recurring patterns around adoption, legacy infrastructure, behavior, and institutional constraint.
- Concrete oddities are useful when they point back to grounded sources rather than becoming free-floating trivia.
- Cross-domain scanning complements systematic study but cannot replace deep domain validation.
- Dated observation lists should be treated as historical snapshots and prompts for follow-up, not as current measurements.

## Evidence
- Weak-signal variety: [[52-things-i-learned-in-2017-fluxx-studio-notes-medium]] moves from older founder age and smartphone translation to nuclear-decommissioning robots, insurance scale, solar generation, DNA sequencing, and poverty data.
- Adoption pattern: [[52-things-i-learned-in-2017-fluxx-studio-notes-medium]] notes translation apps in Shenzhen markets, QR-code donations, voice search, Amazon Echo use by people with dementia, and DIY home batteries.
- Legacy persistence: [[52-things-i-learned-in-2017-fluxx-studio-notes-medium]] cites NHS pager use, prison typewriters, paper ATM vouchers, divining rods for water leaks, and surviving iron lungs.
- Behavioral evidence: [[52-things-i-learned-in-2017-fluxx-studio-notes-medium]] includes founder-age statistics, WEIRD sampling concerns, cash-transfer findings, a customer-objection question, and the sports-car hedge-fund risk signal.
- Snapshot qualification: [[52-things-i-learned-in-2017-fluxx-studio-notes-medium]] is explicitly a 2017 annual list, so its quantitative and market claims need newer sources before being used as current facts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Scanning widely can create false coherence. A list of surprising facts is not a causal model, and many items depend on linked underlying sources that the wiki has not separately ingested. The method is strongest as a discovery practice: it supplies leads, metaphors, and questions that still need deeper validation.
