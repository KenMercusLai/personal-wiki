## Definition
[[StartupCompensationDesign]] is the deliberate construction of a startup's cash, equity, rewards, levels, review cadence, negotiation boundaries, and exception governance so offers and promotions express company values and remain explainable as the organization grows.

## Current Synthesis
[[Homebrew]] argues that compensation should become an explicit operating system before improvised early offers harden into inequity. The system begins with a stated philosophy—how cash, equity, performance, recognition, and internal fairness support the mission—then turns that philosophy into role levels, salary and equity bands, promotion rules, review timing, negotiation boundaries, and a documented exception path. Everyone making or communicating offers should be able to explain the inputs and rationale consistently.

Its illustrative policy balances employee cash needs, company runway, early-stage risk, and future equity capacity by targeting median cash and upper-quartile equity. Meaningful ownership can compensate for uncertainty and expose candidate risk preference, including through two packages with different cash-equity mixes. Yet equity is not cash: a higher nominal percentile remains speculative unless capitalization, vesting, exercise, tax, dilution, preference, and liquidity conditions are intelligible. The same governance logic applies after hire through promotion bands and refresher grants.

The offer process is part of the design rather than a separate sales activity. The source assigns responsibility, recommends verbal agreement before paperwork, allows defined decision windows, protects candidate confidentiality, and discourages reactive concessions. These practices can create clarity, but references, gifts, investor calls, deadlines, and family assistance require consent and anti-pressure safeguards.

## Key Claims
- A compensation philosophy should be defined early and tied explicitly to company mission and values.
- Levels, salary and equity bands, review cadence, and exception authority turn the philosophy into repeatable decisions.
- Market benchmarks reduce guesswork but remain contingent on role matching, stage, geography, dataset quality, and time.
- Cash-equity allocation must balance employee financial security, startup risk, runway, ownership capacity, and candidate preference.
- Negotiation rules should limit arbitrary peer differences and specify which terms can change and who may approve exceptions.
- Promotion, refresher, and offer practices belong to one compensation system and should be consistently explainable.
- Candidate-closing tactics need privacy, consent, fairness, and anti-pressure controls in addition to commercial effectiveness.

## Evidence
- Philosophy and values: [[quip]] says compensation should support the company's mission and values and that offer participants must explain how decisions were reached.
- Repeatable structure: [[quip]] recommends preset salary and equity levels, promotion-linked ranges, annual or at most twice-yearly review, and a clear exception-approval path.
- Benchmark use: [[quip]] shows a historical Engineering Manager survey segmented by capital raised and recommends targeting the 50th salary percentile and 75th equity percentile.
- Cash and risk: [[quip]] says cash should not become a personal stressor while early equity should reward commitment to an uncertain company, team, and mission.
- Preference discovery: [[quip]] gives two differently weighted cash-equity offers as a way to let a candidate express risk preference.
- Offer governance: [[quip]] assigns negotiation responsibility, recommends verbal agreement before the written letter, and sets role-sensitive consideration periods.
- Closing boundaries: [[quip]] discusses backchannel references, confidentiality, offer expiry, investor calls, gifts, family support, and limits on repeated concessions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework comes from a venture firm's practitioner guide, not a comparative study. It does not demonstrate that a particular percentile mix, annual review cycle, refresher cadence, or offer sequence improves acceptance, performance, retention, or fairness. The numerical tables are historical 2015 survey results, including a ten-company early-stage segment, and cannot be treated as current market rates. Bands can move discretion into job matching, leveling, promotion, and exceptions; a formula can reproduce inequity if inputs or governance are biased.

Higher equity cannot by itself compensate employees who need cash or remove illiquidity and loss risk. Dual offers may reveal preference, but only if both are fairly valued and employees understand the underlying equity. Backchannel references and candidate-closing tactics may create privacy, consent, access, discrimination, or legal problems. Current compensation, employment, tax, and securities decisions require updated data and appropriate professional review.

## What Changed
- Established compensation as an integrated philosophy, banding, governance, negotiation, promotion, and offer system.
- Added cash-equity allocation as a balance among employee security, company constraints, risk, and preference.
- Added privacy, fairness, and anti-pressure qualifications to candidate-closing practices.
