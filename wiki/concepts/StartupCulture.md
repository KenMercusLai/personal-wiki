## Definition
[[StartupCulture]] is the shared personality, mission, values, and behavioral standard of a startup, maintained through hiring, leadership example, reinforcement, repeated communication, and everyday rituals.

## Current Synthesis
The sources treat culture as a system that can either weaken or become stronger under scale. [[BrianChesky]] emphasizes mission, founder example, interviewing, trained culture carriers, orientation, and repeated company-wide messages. [[JeffWeiner]] frames culture as both current and aspirational identity, while the Netflix case adds written debate, talent density, and context over control. The Grady interview adds a coordination role: once a company nears 150 employees, culture helps replace the shared ambient context that smaller teams inherit informally. The SmugMug profile adds a smaller, family-business version: culture can be carried through visible family participation, customer tone, rituals, benefits, flexible schedules, and shared meals, but the same intimacy can raise inclusion risks for non-family employees.

## Key Claims
- Strong culture is recognizable in everyday behavior, not just branding or wall text.
- Hiring is one of the strongest levers for preserving or changing culture.
- Leaders must visibly live, reinforce, reward, and evaluate against stated values.
- Culture needs repetition and everyday rituals because new employees lack the company's accumulated context.
- Writing culture down can make it more debatable and participatory.
- High talent density enables more freedom and less dummy-proof process.
- Around 150 employees, culture becomes a guardrail against divergent beliefs about why the company exists.

## Evidence
- Mission and repetition: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] cites [[BrianChesky]] on Airbnb as a calling and on culture at scale requiring repeated messages.
- Values in practice: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] cites [[JeffWeiner]] that values are empty unless leaders practice, hire, reward, and evaluate against them.
- Distinctiveness: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] cites [[MarissaMayer]] and Bret Taylor on strong cultures at Google and Facebook being recognizable even without logos.
- Written culture: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] cites [[ReedHastings]] on the Netflix culture deck as a debatable and candidate-visible statement.
- Post-150 context: [[8-lessons-from-one-of-silicon-valleys-top-vcs-drift]] says employees who joined recently may have a different idea of why the company exists and where it is going.
- Systems plus culture: [[8-lessons-from-one-of-silicon-valleys-top-vcs-drift]] says the answer to that coordination loss is intentional systems and culture.
- Family atmosphere: [[a-focused-family-business-los-angeles-times]] quotes [[DonMacAskill]] saying [[SmugMug]] works to create a family atmosphere.
- Rituals and benefits: [[a-focused-family-business-los-angeles-times]] describes flexible schedules, collective decision-making, bonuses, matching 401(k)s, stock options, full medical coverage, health club memberships, company lunches, and family food rituals.
- Inclusion risk: [[a-focused-family-business-los-angeles-times]] quotes Don's worry that non-family hires might feel alienated or steamrolled by the family core.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources favor strong-culture companies and do not deeply examine failure modes such as conformity, exclusion, founder mythmaking, family favoritism, or values being used to rationalize overwork. Their claims should be read as operating advice and source-specific profile evidence rather than proof that stronger culture is always healthier.

## What Changed
- Added Grady's view of culture as a coordination system when informal context weakens near 150 employees.
- Added SmugMug as a family-business case where rituals, benefits, customer tone, and inclusion risk carry culture before large-scale formalization.
