## Definition
[[WorkplaceIncentiveDesign]] is the deliberate shaping of rewards, penalties, metrics, prices, and feedback loops so daily workplace behavior aligns with organizational goals.

## Current Synthesis
The Disco case shows incentive design as an operating system rather than a compensation footnote. Meeting-room charges, desk and PC costs, work-assignment auctions, team billing, overtime penalties, help payments, Colosseum wagers, and internal crowdfunding all push employees to ask whether an action is worth its cost. That can reduce waste and clarify contribution, but the same mechanisms can make work feel like constant competition and can bias attention toward near-term measurable profit.

## Key Claims
- Incentives shape everyday behavior most strongly when they are visible at the moment of choice.
- Pricing shared resources can reduce casual overuse by making opportunity cost concrete.
- Penalties can redirect unwanted behavior such as overtime or excess inventory, but they also signal what management values.
- Rewards and bonuses can make contribution legible, yet uncertain payouts can intensify pressure.
- Incentive systems need horizon control because quarterly or easily measured targets can crowd out longer-term work.

## Evidence
- Meeting behavior: [[charging-employees-for-conference-rooms-helps-disco-boost-profit-bloomberg]] reports employees cut back on useless meetings after room charges.
- Assignment selection: [[charging-employees-for-conference-rooms-helps-disco-boost-profit-bloomberg]] says workers bid for tasks and unwanted tasks often prove unnecessary.
- Overtime penalty: [[charging-employees-for-conference-rooms-helps-disco-boost-profit-bloomberg]] reports overtime hours falling 9% after penalties were implemented in 2015.
- Helping market: [[charging-employees-for-conference-rooms-helps-disco-boost-profit-bloomberg]] says employees can pay colleagues to finish reports or provide coding and translation help.
- Bonus pressure: [[charging-employees-for-conference-rooms-helps-disco-boost-profit-bloomberg]] says quarterly bonuses can rival annual pay for top performers, but payouts are not guaranteed.
- Horizon risk: [[charging-employees-for-conference-rooms-helps-disco-boost-profit-bloomberg]] quotes Waseda professor Takashi Shimizu warning that senior management must avoid a short-term loop.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not prove that every workplace should price every behavior. Some valuable work is collective, slow, ambiguous, or relational, and can be harmed by narrow measurement. Incentive design also depends on baseline trust, managerial integrity, labor-market options, payout fairness, and whether workers can challenge distorted metrics.
