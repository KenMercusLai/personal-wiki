## Definition
[[UXDesignClicheRhetoric]] is the use of familiar UX sayings, analogies, and process phrases as shorthand arguments in design work, especially when they replace context-specific evidence or judgment.

## Current Synthesis
The UX Collective satire treats UX cliches as organizational speech acts. Phrases such as "you are not your user," "we are testing the design," "content is king," "people don't scroll," "the fold doesn't exist," and "designers should have a seat at the table" often point to real concerns, but the article's joke is that they can also help designers escape constraints, win meetings, blame users, defer responsibility, or sound strategically mature. The ketchup-bottle UI-vs-UX image strengthens the critique by showing how a popular analogy can become so portable that it flattens the distinction it was meant to explain.

## Key Claims
- UX cliches usually begin from legitimate design concerns but become weaker when used as context-free proof.
- Research and testing language can be used to postpone disagreement or defend decisions without clarifying what evidence would change the team's mind.
- Accessibility, onboarding, scrolling, content, and cognitive-load concerns can become rhetorical weapons when detached from user behavior and interface evidence.
- Design-process slogans can mask status anxiety about strategic influence, deadlines, and stakeholder control.
- Visual analogies such as the ketchup-bottle UI-vs-UX comparison can make a distinction memorable while also oversimplifying it.

## Evidence
- Context-free proof: [[a-comprehensive-and-honest-list-of-ux-cliches-ux-collective]] catalogs stock phrases across user research, UX process, accessibility, content, scrolling, UI/UX boundaries, and graceful degradation.
- Research rhetoric: [[a-comprehensive-and-honest-list-of-ux-cliches-ux-collective]] pairs "you are not your user" with the Henry Ford counter-cliche and frames "let the users decide" as a way to move an unresolved argument into testing.
- Responsibility shifting: [[a-comprehensive-and-honest-list-of-ux-cliches-ux-collective]] mocks claims about user stupidity, users not reading, and interfaces needing no explanation as ways designers can avoid giving enough context.
- Status and deadline pressure: [[a-comprehensive-and-honest-list-of-ux-cliches-ux-collective]] treats "seat at the table," "UX should be a mindset," "content is king," and "double diamond" as language used around influence, process appearance, and delivery pressure.
- Oversimplified analogy: [[a-comprehensive-and-honest-list-of-ux-cliches-ux-collective]] includes repeated UI-vs-UX ketchup-bottle imagery, making the analogy itself part of the cliche landscape.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is explicitly satire, so it should not be read as evidence that every quoted phrase is empty or that UX heuristics are useless. Many slogans can be productive when tied to a concrete research plan, accessibility standard, observed behavior, product constraint, or decision rule; the risk is their use as portable authority without that grounding.
