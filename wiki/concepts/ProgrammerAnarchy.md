## Definition
[[ProgrammerAnarchy]] is an organization model, attributed to [[FredGeorge]], in which programmers choose their work, operate without separate manager or product-manager roles, and accept broad responsibility for outcomes.

## Current Synthesis
The available source uses Programmer Anarchy as the organizational extreme of its engineer-leverage thesis. Better tools and more widely distributed engineering knowledge are said to let programmers absorb decisions previously separated into management and product functions. The model therefore exchanges hierarchy and role specialization for autonomy and end-to-end accountability, but the source offers neither an implementation case nor evidence about coordination, customer discovery, prioritization, conflict resolution, or scale.

## Key Claims
- Programmers choose their own work instead of receiving it through a management hierarchy.
- Separate product-manager and manager roles are removed or absorbed into engineering responsibility.
- Increased autonomy is paired with end-to-end accountability rather than reduced responsibility.
- The model assumes modern tools and engineering knowledge make broader ownership feasible.

## Evidence
- Work selection: [[you-are-not-a-commodity-dev-community]] describes programmers choosing their own work.
- Role structure: [[you-are-not-a-commodity-dev-community]] says the model operates without product managers or managers.
- Accountability: [[you-are-not-a-commodity-dev-community]] pairs autonomy with total responsibility.
- Enabling conditions: [[you-are-not-a-commodity-dev-community]] attributes feasibility to improved tools and wider understanding of engineering practice.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is an approving secondary summary, not a direct specification or evaluated case study. It does not show how the model incorporates user research, business constraints, specialist expertise, mentoring, performance management, accessibility, security, or disagreement among programmers. The claim that engineering can subsume all other roles should therefore remain source-scoped.

## What Changed
- Established the concept as a source-scoped autonomy-and-accountability model rather than a validated default organization design.
