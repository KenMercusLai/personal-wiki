Self-defined founder success is the refusal to let another founder's ambition become the default definition of achievement. The Groove source uses the contrast with hustle culture to argue that "what it takes" depends on what "it" means: buying a major sports team and building a calm, comfortable family life imply different companies, schedules, and sacrifices.

The concept turns lifestyle into a business design input. Instead of treating maximum growth or constant work as the only serious path, a founder can describe the life they want, estimate what it costs, and build the business backward from that target. This does not remove hard work, but it makes the amount and kind of work accountable to a chosen outcome.

The risk is borrowing a goal without noticing its price. When founders imitate admired hard-chargers without sharing their desired endpoint, they can accept burnout as proof of seriousness even when it does not serve their own definition of success.

## Questions

- What specific life and business outcome is the founder trying to create?
- How much revenue, profit, flexibility, or scale is actually required for that outcome?
- Which sacrifices belong to the chosen goal, and which are copied from someone else's path?
- Does the current operating pace serve the founder's definition of success or replace it?
