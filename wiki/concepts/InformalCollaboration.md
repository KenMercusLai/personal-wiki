## Definition
[[InformalCollaboration]] is useful coordination or idea generation that happens through spontaneous, loosely bounded conversation rather than scheduled formal meetings.

## Current Synthesis
Barnett presents informal collaboration as the positive alternative to default meeting culture. Its advantage is optionality: people continue the conversation only while it has value, and they can stop when it turns into small talk or loses purpose. The source's examples span intellectual work and workplace design: a spontaneous co-evolution conversation with [[BillDurham]] produced later scholarship, while [[SteveJobs]]' Apple campus vision and memory of the [[HomebrewComputerClub]] frame architecture and shared space as ways to increase productive random encounters.

## Key Claims
- Informal conversations have a natural value filter because participants can end them when they stop being useful.
- Spontaneous intellectual exchange can generate ideas that later become formal work.
- Workspace design can increase the probability of useful chance encounters.
- Informal collaboration is not aimless socializing; its strength is staying bounded by perceived usefulness.
- Protecting actual work time can coexist with making room for spontaneous coordination.

## Evidence
- Value filter: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] says informal talks continue only when they matter and small talk is cut short when not useful.
- Scholarship case: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] says a spontaneous conversation between Barnett and [[BillDurham]] about co-evolution led to papers and a book.
- Campus design: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] says [[SteveJobs]] designed Apple's new campus circle to increase random informal interactions in the center.
- Historical memory: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] links Jobs' preference to the [[HomebrewComputerClub]] period where Jobs and [[SteveWozniak]] showed early Apple computers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source gives anecdotes and design intuition rather than measurement. Informal collaboration can exclude remote workers, introverts, caregivers, or people outside high-status spaces if it becomes the only path to influence. Some teams still need scheduled forums for equity, documentation, decisions, and cross-time-zone coordination.

- Created the concept from Barnett's contrast between default meetings and spontaneous useful conversations.
