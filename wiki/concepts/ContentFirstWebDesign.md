## Definition
[[ContentFirstWebDesign]] is an interface approach that makes the reader's primary material visually and behaviorally dominant, requiring every control, ornament, recommendation, and brand cue to justify the attention it consumes.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents content-first design as disciplined subtraction rather than generic minimalism. On an article page, paper-like color, readable type, generous leading, paragraph spacing, and whitespace establish a calm document surface. Pop-ups, floating sharing buttons, progress indicators, animated banners, and unrelated widgets are excluded because they compete with the reason the reader arrived. Author details, licensing, and related articles are delayed until after the text, where they can serve a legitimate next step without interrupting the current one.

Visual identity does not disappear. Technical-note labels, end-of-report markers, and subtle author references can provide hierarchy, atmosphere, and recognition when they remain peripheral and optional. The design standard is therefore not the absence of decoration but attentional subordination: missing a decorative cue must not reduce comprehension or prevent completion of the main task.

The source links this approach to Swiss International Style and historical NASA documentation, where grids, numbering, typography, whitespace, and print constraints are framed as functionally honest decisions that acquired aesthetic force. That genealogy and the claimed reading benefits remain the author's interpretation rather than measured design history or user research.

## Key Claims
- Minimalism is useful when it removes elements without a defensible reader-facing job, not when it merely produces an empty visual style.
- Article typography, spacing, color, and layout should support sustained reading before secondary engagement goals.
- Controls and commercial or social prompts can impose attention costs even when they add functionality.
- Secondary material is less disruptive when placed after completion of the primary content.
- Decoration and branding can coexist with content priority when they remain peripheral, legible, and non-blocking.
- A coherent interface may evolve through repeated use rather than arrive as a final, fixed design.

## Evidence
- Subtraction rule: [[my-blog-design]] describes Swiss-style “less” as removing what cannot explain why it exists, rather than equating reduction with laziness.
- Reading surface: [[my-blog-design]] specifies a beige background, larger text, generous line spacing, paragraph breathing room, and no floating or surprise elements.
- Sequencing: [[my-blog-design]] places author information, licensing, and related recommendations only after the article has ended.
- Peripheral identity: [[my-blog-design]] uses technical-document labels and quiet author references as optional atmosphere rather than dominant content.
- Iterative coherence: [[my-blog-design]] says the site is continually adjusted when lived experience reveals that something feels wrong.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one designer-author's reflective rationale and supplies no task study, accessibility audit, preference segmentation, performance data, or reading outcomes. Some readers benefit from progress cues, persistent navigation, sharing controls, theme choice, or other aids; their value depends on implementation and context rather than categorical removal. Paper-like color and sparse layouts can still fail contrast, responsive, localization, or assistive-technology requirements. The proposed lineage from Swiss design through NASA documents to a game interface is an interpretive influence map, not a demonstrated historical succession.

## What Changed
- Established content-first design as attentional prioritization rather than decoration-free minimalism.
- Added sequencing and peripheral ornament as ways to preserve useful secondary functions without interrupting reading.
