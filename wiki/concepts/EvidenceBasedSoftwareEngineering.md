## Definition
[[EvidenceBasedSoftwareEngineering]] is the practice of matching confidence in software-method, tool, and outcome claims to the quality of empirical support, while stating uncertainty when evidence is missing or weak.

## Current Synthesis
The source's most durable principle is that “not proven” is a distinct judgment. A claim that domain-specific languages improve productivity, or that an Agile method produces a large return, is not thereby false when it lacks citations or data; it is unsupported at the level asserted. This makes uncertainty reporting part of professional rigor rather than an admission of failure.

The concept also separates evidence from social signals. Influence, job title, publication venue, consulting success, and memorable anecdotes may identify ideas worth examining, but they do not establish causal outcomes. Better practice would formulate testable claims, fund and publish studies, cite relevant evidence, and preserve qualifications about context and generalizability.

The source itself demonstrates why the standard must apply symmetrically. Its critique of weak proof is useful, but its allegations about advocates' motives, revenue dependence, intellectual humility, and industry-wide anti-intellectualism are not supported by the evidence it presents.

The engineering-principles essay adds an organizational application: teams can make “be a scientist” an explicit default, preferring research and objective data to hunches or superstition. That norm can improve which questions are asked and which claims are tested, but stating it is not itself evidence that a decision process or team outcome improved. Indeed, the essay's assertion that introducing principles always produces higher coherence illustrates why declared evidence-seeking and calibrated claims must be assessed separately.

## Key Claims
- Unsupported is not synonymous with false; “not proven” preserves the distinction.
- Authority, popularity, venue, and anecdote do not establish causal claims about software outcomes.
- Methodology advocates should define testable outcomes and support comparative empirical research.
- Evidence claims should state their context, limitations, and degree of uncertainty.
- Critiques demanding rigor must apply the same standard to their own causal and motivational claims.
- Teams can encode evidence-seeking as a shared decision norm, but the norm's effects still require evaluation.

## Evidence
- Not-proven discipline: [[elegant-coding-the-problem-with-todays-software-thought-leaders]] quotes [[GregWilson]] distinguishing absent support from either truth or falsity.
- Authority versus evidence: [[elegant-coding-the-problem-with-todays-software-thought-leaders]] centers an example of substantive DSL benefit claims reportedly published without citations or data.
- Research prescription: [[elegant-coding-the-problem-with-todays-software-thought-leaders]] calls for methodology advocates to promote and fund studies rather than rely on opinion.
- Symmetric skepticism: [[elegant-coding-the-problem-with-todays-software-thought-leaders]] supplies no comparable evidence for its own claims about consultant incentives, advocates' motives, or widespread anti-intellectualism.

- Norm-versus-proof boundary: [[engineering-principles-incyte-studios-medium]] also makes an absolute coherence claim from the author's experience without comparative measurements.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The current evidence consists of two practitioner essays, one polemical and one prescriptive. Neither provides a systematic review, defines an evidence hierarchy, evaluates concrete study designs, or measures whether an evidence-seeking principle improves decisions. Empirical studies can also be narrow, confounded, or difficult to generalize across teams and systems; “more science” does not remove the need for contextual engineering judgment, and scientific language can obscure rather than resolve contested values or objectives.

## What Changed
- Established a standard-of-proof concept centered on explicit uncertainty and the difference between unsupported and disproved claims.
- Added symmetric scrutiny: evidence demands also constrain the critic making them.
- Added evidence-seeking as a possible team operating norm while separating the declaration from proof of its effects.
