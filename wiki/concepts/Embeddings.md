## Definition
[[Embeddings]] are vector representations that place things - words, whole text sequences, or images - as points in a numeric space where nearby points mean similar things, so software can compare, search, and feed them to a network.

## Current Synthesis
The wiki's sources now cover embeddings in two registers that turn out to be the same idea. The retrieval sources treat embeddings as plumbing: raw text is chunked and vectorised, user questions are vectorised with the same model, and similarity between vectors decides which passages become context for a language model. The ChatGPT source treats them as a representational claim with a geometric flavour - an embedding is a way to capture the "essence" of something as an array of numbers such that nearby things get nearby numbers - and shows how such spaces are produced.

The construction recipe is to intercept the inside of a network trained on a concrete task. For images, a digit-recognition network's late layers characterise an image as "mostly 4-like but a bit 2-like"; tapping the 500-number layer yields an image embedding, and projecting it down to three dimensions makes digit clusters visible. For words, the standard task is prediction - fill in the blank in "the ___ cat" - and the vector that forms just before the network commits to an answer characterises the word. Word2vec, GloVe, BERT, and GPT are successive versions of that recipe, and all of them represent words with hundreds to thousands of numbers. Raw vectors are uninformative to read, but distances, projections, and analogies expose structure: animal and fruit terms separate, parts of speech separate, and a polysemous word such as "crane" splits into bird and machine senses.

Embedding choices also affect database behaviour. Dimensionality changes the cost of exact search because each candidate comparison spans every vector dimension; the pgvector example uses 1,536-dimensional Titan vectors in PostgreSQL and searches prompt embeddings generated by the same model, and its benchmark measured a sequential scan at about 650 ms against roughly 2.4 ms for IVFFlat and 1.5 ms for HNSW on the same query.

## Key Claims
- An embedding is defined by adjacency: things that are nearby in meaning or kind are placed nearby in the vector space.
- A usable embedding can be produced by tapping the internal state of a network trained on a concrete task - digit recognition for images, word prediction for words - rather than by defining similarity directly.
- The same machinery embeds retrieval chunks, tokens, and whole text sequences, which is why embeddings sit under both search and language modelling.
- GPT-style models compute a token embedding and a position embedding separately and add them, so one vector carries both identity and order.
- Word embeddings from word2vec, GloVe, BERT, and GPT all encode words as hundreds to thousands of numbers, and raw values are meaningless until distances, projections, or analogies are used to interpret them.
- Both stored chunks and incoming questions need embeddings from the same model, or the similarity comparison loses meaning.
- Vector dimensionality drives retrieval cost, because exhaustive search compares every dimension of every candidate, which is why approximate indexes such as IVFFlat and HNSW are used for large collections.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not evaluate embedding model choice across providers, multilingual retrieval quality, chunk overlap, drift, or recall metrics. The AWS source gives one Titan configuration and one benchmark rather than a general result. The ChatGPT source's embedding spaces are illustrations on small reference models, and the essay itself notes that projecting a meaning space down to two dimensions gives only a hint of its structure, so readable clusters should not be treated as a complete account of what an embedding encodes.
