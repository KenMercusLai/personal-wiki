## Definition
[[SurveillanceGap]] is the harmful invisibility that occurs when people are outside formal data systems needed to access rights, resources, work, or political recognition.

## Current Synthesis
The source complicates a simple anti-surveillance frame by arguing that too little formal data can also harm marginalized people. Undocumented immigrants, day laborers, homeless people, and people with criminal convictions may avoid or be excluded from record systems because surveillance creates risk, but that invisibility can leave them unable to prove wage theft, access resources, work legally, vote, or be seen by policymakers. The desired condition is therefore not absolute privacy, but control over when to disclose and when to withhold personal information.

## Key Claims
- Avoiding data collection can be a rational response to punitive or intrusive surveillance.
- Lack of records can make people unable to prove claims, access resources, or gain political visibility.
- Surveillance and invisibility are not opposites in practice; marginalized people can suffer from both.
- Documentation systems shape who can work legally, vote, claim benefits, and be counted.
- Privacy justice requires meaningful choice over disclosure rather than total visibility or total invisibility.

## Evidence
- Affected groups: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] names undocumented immigrants, day laborers, homeless people, and people with criminal convictions as residents of the surveillance gap.
- Missing credentials: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] lists lack of permanent address, Social Security number, or immigration papers as barriers.
- Wage-claim proof: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] quotes [[MicheleGilman]] saying wage cases require records that often do not exist in underground economies.
- Political invisibility: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] says people outside data systems may be invisible to policymakers.
- Disclosure choice: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] quotes Gilman saying people want to choose when to give up privacy and when to retain it.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source introduces the surveillance gap concept but does not deeply analyze specific documentation reforms, community data practices, or tradeoffs between protective invisibility and access to rights.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to record harmful under-documentation alongside harmful over-surveillance.
