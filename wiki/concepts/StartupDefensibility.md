## Definition
[[StartupDefensibility]] is the capacity of a startup's business advantage to persist against imitation and competition rather than disappearing when another company reproduces its current technical implementation.

## Current Synthesis
The available source treats defensibility as a required business-diligence question for ML/AI employers. Its central distinction is between an algorithm, which is often reproducible or replaceable in software, and a reinforcing business structure such as network effects, which can become stronger as adoption grows. The practical test is not whether the technology is clever but why the company can continue capturing value after competitors understand what it does.

## Key Claims
- Technical novelty and durable business advantage are different properties.
- An algorithm alone is rarely a sustainable software moat because competitors can reproduce, substitute, or route around it.
- Defensibility should identify a mechanism that persists or compounds as the company operates.
- Network effects are one example of a reinforcing mechanism, but the source does not claim they are the only defensible structure.

## Evidence
- Algorithm boundary: [[business-questions-engineers-should-ask-when-interviewing-at-ml-ai-companies]] calls “an algorithm” a bad answer to the defensibility question.
- Reinforcing advantage: [[business-questions-engineers-should-ask-when-interviewing-at-ml-ai-companies]] contrasts PageRank's role in Google's initial quality with network effects as an explanation for its continued strength.
- Candidate use: [[business-questions-engineers-should-ask-when-interviewing-at-ml-ai-companies]] includes defensibility among the business questions engineers should ask before joining an ML/AI company.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers a compact practitioner heuristic, not a taxonomy or comparative study of moats. Algorithms can remain valuable when protected by tacit know-how, proprietary data rights, infrastructure, regulatory approval, distribution, switching costs, brand, execution speed, or continuous research, and network effects can be weak, local, multi-homing-prone, or vulnerable to governance failure. The Google example is illustrative and does not isolate which mechanisms caused long-run advantage.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to separate technical novelty from a persistent value-capture mechanism.
