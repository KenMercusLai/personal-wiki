## Definition
[[MobilityOnDemand]] is a transportation model in which customers obtain convenient access to a vehicle suited to a particular trip or task instead of owning one multipurpose vehicle that must compromise across every use.

## Current Synthesis
The source frames mobility on demand as both a product-design change and a business-model threat to incumbent automakers. Household car ownership historically bundles commuting, school travel, errands, and long trips into one or two expensive general-purpose purchases. Apps, ride-hailing, shared fleets, and eventually autonomous vehicles could unbundle those needs by making specialized vehicles available only when required. In that model, a vehicle dismissed as too niche for individual ownership can reach mass utilization through sharing, while the automaker's competitive task shifts from selling annual models to coordinating convenient, purpose-fit usage.

The transition also changes vehicle design and organizational capability. Automakers would need customer research grounded in lived routines, integrated hardware and software teams, strong sensor and mobile capabilities, and architectures that can be reconfigured during a vehicle's life. The source argues that Detroit had begun investing in ride-hailing and autonomy but had not yet changed its underlying conception of innovation enough to make these capabilities central.

## Key Claims
- Ownership forces most households to compromise across many transport purposes in one or two vehicles.
- On-demand access can match a more specialized vehicle to a particular trip without requiring the customer to own it.
- Sharing can turn a low-volume vehicle design into a high-utilization service asset.
- Competition shifts from annual model and brand optimization toward convenience, availability, and fitness for purpose.
- Apps, sensors, vehicle software, fleet operations, and hardware design become one product system.
- Reconfigurable architectures could let the same vehicle serve different purposes during its operating life.

## Evidence
- Ownership compromise: [[detroit-must-rediscover-the-cutting-edge]] describes cars as costly “Swiss Army knives” purchased mainly around commuting but expected to serve family and travel needs too.
- Purpose-specific access: [[detroit-must-rediscover-the-cutting-edge]] points to differentiated Uber services as an early example of customers selecting a vehicle experience for the moment.
- Shared niche scale: [[detroit-must-rediscover-the-cutting-edge]] argues that specialized concepts rejected as niche can become mass offerings when shared.
- Organizational requirement: [[detroit-must-rediscover-the-cutting-edge]] calls for integrated internal teams spanning hardware, sensors, vehicle software, and mobile software.
- Lifecycle reconfiguration: [[detroit-must-rediscover-the-cutting-edge]] proposes modular architectures that could transform a passenger vehicle into a delivery vehicle after initial acquisition.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept currently rests on one 2016 opinion column, not comparative fleet, customer, or financial evidence. The source groups apps, ride-hailing, electric vehicles, autonomous driving, sharing, and reconfigurable vehicles into one direction even though these technologies can mature independently and have different capital, safety, regulatory, labor, and utilization constraints. Differentiated Uber products demonstrate service segmentation, not profitable unit economics; [[SubsidizedUnitEconomics]] remains a direct qualification. Full autonomy is also not a prerequisite for on-demand mobility, while [[AutonomousDrivingSafety]] shows why it should not be assumed technically or socially complete.

## What Changed
- Created the concept around purpose-fit access rather than ownership of a single compromise vehicle.
- Added shared utilization as the mechanism that can turn specialized vehicle designs from niche products into broader services.
- Added integrated software, sensors, hardware, and lifecycle reconfiguration as enabling capabilities rather than guaranteed outcomes.
