## Definition
[[StartupEvaluationChecklists]] are compact diagnostic frameworks that help founders repeatedly test core startup questions about strategy, fundraising readiness, metrics, and marketplace quality.

## Current Synthesis
David Lee's source treats startup checklists as borrowed operating aids rather than original theory. Its practical claim is that entrepreneurs do not need to reinvent every evaluation framework: Thiel's seven questions can pressure-test the startup thesis, Sequoia's list can structure business-plan and pitch-deck preparation, Andreessen Horowitz's metrics lists can orient monetization and performance analysis, and Gurley's marketplace factors can focus marketplace-specific evaluation. The value is not in the checklist artifact alone but in forcing repeated attention to questions founders can otherwise avoid: whether the technology is distinctive, the timing is right, the market wedge is focused, the team can execute, distribution exists, the position can last, and the opportunity contains a real secret.

## Key Claims
- Startup checklists are most useful when they compress hard strategic questions into repeatable prompts.
- A founder can borrow mature external checklists instead of creating a bespoke framework for every decision.
- Thiel's seven questions function as a startup-thesis diagnostic across technology, timing, market, team, distribution, durability, and secrets.
- Fundraising checklists help convert a business into a clear pitch and plan.
- Metrics checklists help founders think about how the company will make money and measure progress.
- Marketplace businesses need additional evaluation because liquidity, trust, quality, and defensibility differ from ordinary software products.
- Execution, not writing, is the hard part of checklist use.

## Evidence
- Checklist reuse: [[checklists-for-startups-david-lee-medium]] says Lee could create his own list but sees no reason to reinvent the wheel.
- Startup-thesis diagnostic: [[checklists-for-startups-david-lee-medium]] lists Thiel's engineering, timing, monopoly, people, distribution, durability, and secret questions.
- Fundraising preparation: [[checklists-for-startups-david-lee-medium]] recommends Sequoia's business-plan and pitch-deck checklist under "How to Get Money."
- Metrics orientation: [[checklists-for-startups-david-lee-medium]] recommends Andreessen Horowitz's startup metrics lists under "How to Make Money."
- Marketplace evaluation: [[checklists-for-startups-david-lee-medium]] points marketplace builders to Gurley's factors for evaluating digital marketplaces.
- Execution gap: [[checklists-for-startups-david-lee-medium]] explicitly warns that checklists and simple rules are easier to write down than execute.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a short curated pointer, not a detailed validation study. It does not reproduce Sequoia's, Andreessen Horowitz's, or Gurley's full lists, and it does not show whether founders who use these checklists perform better. The checklist frame may also understate the amount of judgment needed when a startup has conflicting signals, a nonstandard market, or a business model that does not fit venture-scale assumptions.
