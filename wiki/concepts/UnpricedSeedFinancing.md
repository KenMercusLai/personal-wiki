## Definition
[[UnpricedSeedFinancing]] is an early-stage fundraising structure where investors provide capital before the company receives a priced valuation, usually in exchange for rights to future equity when a priced round occurs.

## Current Synthesis
The Crunchbase case frames unpriced seed financing as a bridge between incorporation and a later priced round. Instead of buying a known percentage immediately, investors sign instruments such as convertible notes or SAFEs that convert into equity under later conditions. The investor's eventual ownership is then shaped by protective terms, especially discounts and valuation caps, that reward taking early risk before the market has set a clearer company price.

## Key Claims
- Unpriced rounds avoid assigning a company valuation at the moment seed capital is raised.
- Investors receive a contractual path to future shares rather than a fixed current ownership percentage.
- Convertible notes begin as debt and convert to equity under predetermined conditions.
- SAFEs are presented as similar to convertible notes but without debt status, maturity dates, or interest obligations.
- Discounts let seed investors buy future shares below the priced-round share price.
- Valuation caps limit the effective valuation used for conversion, protecting early investors from excessive dilution if the next round is priced high.

## Evidence
- Delayed valuation: [[cap-tables-share-structures-valuations-oh-my-a-case-study-of-early-stage-funding-crunchbase-news]] says an unpriced round does not assign a valuation or necessarily purchase a known amount of equity at signing.
- Instrument comparison: [[cap-tables-share-structures-valuations-oh-my-a-case-study-of-early-stage-funding-crunchbase-news]] distinguishes convertible notes as debt from SAFEs as non-debt future-equity agreements.
- Founder-friendly SAFE framing: [[cap-tables-share-structures-valuations-oh-my-a-case-study-of-early-stage-funding-crunchbase-news]] says SAFEs are generally considered founder-friendlier because they lack debt-like maturity dates and interest.
- Discount example: [[cap-tables-share-structures-valuations-oh-my-a-case-study-of-early-stage-funding-crunchbase-news]] shows Opaque Ventures converting a $2.5 million SAFE at a 20 percent discount to the Series A price.
- Cap example: [[cap-tables-share-structures-valuations-oh-my-a-case-study-of-early-stage-funding-crunchbase-news]] shows BlackBox Capital converting at a $10 million valuation cap rather than the $15 million Series A pre-money valuation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source uses a simplified SAFE example and does not compare pre-money versus post-money SAFE forms, interest-bearing convertible-note variants, legal negotiation details, pro rata rights, investor information rights, or jurisdictional differences. It also describes SAFE popularity and founder-friendliness at a high level rather than as universal advice.
