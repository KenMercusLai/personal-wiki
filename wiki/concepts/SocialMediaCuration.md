## Definition
[[SocialMediaCuration]] is the deliberate shaping of social-platform inputs, defaults, and interaction boundaries so a feed becomes a useful information source rather than an uncontrolled conversation or distraction stream.

## Current Synthesis
Fowler's Twitter workflow treats social media as a personally configured discovery tool. He follows accounts selectively, relies on retweets from trusted follows to surface occasional good material from high-volume accounts, uses topical lists for information he wants to read at chosen times, keeps the main feed chronological, rarely uses hashtags or discovery surfaces, and routes private communication to email when record keeping matters. The practice depends as much on refusal as selection: he generally avoids replies and arguments because online conversation has long tended toward low signal and toxic behavior.

The article also shows that curation is not a complete safety strategy. Fowler says his ability to ignore replies is partly a privilege of not being a common harassment target; people facing bullying may need platform-level protections rather than just personal filters. Social media curation therefore helps manage attention and information quality, but it does not replace [[PlatformAbuseResponse]].

## Key Claims
- Curated following can make a social platform useful for article discovery and situational awareness.
- High-volume but occasionally valuable accounts may be better consumed indirectly through retweets from trusted accounts.
- Lists let users separate topical reading from the main daily feed.
- Chronological order preserves more user control than algorithmic ranking when the goal is chosen intake.
- Avoiding replies and arguments can protect attention, but that tactic is not an adequate answer to harassment.
- External tools such as [[Instapaper]] and email can move reading and record keeping out of the social feed.

## Evidence
- Selective following: [[blog-martin-fowler-how-i-use-twitter]] says Fowler is careful about whom he follows and avoids good-but-too-frequent accounts.
- Retweet filtering: [[blog-martin-fowler-how-i-use-twitter]] says especially good material from unfollowed high-volume accounts can still appear through trusted retweets.
- Lists: [[blog-martin-fowler-how-i-use-twitter]] says topic-specific lists are useful for material he wants to read when ready.
- Chronology: [[blog-martin-fowler-how-i-use-twitter]] says Fowler keeps Twitter in chronological order and avoids Explore, Top Articles, and most hashtags.
- Interaction boundary: [[blog-martin-fowler-how-i-use-twitter]] says he mostly does not read or make replies and tries to avoid arguments.
- External workflow: [[blog-martin-fowler-how-i-use-twitter]] says interesting article announcements are saved into [[Instapaper]], while private communication is usually routed to email.
- Harassment limit: [[blog-martin-fowler-how-i-use-twitter]] says Twitter has lacked sufficient tools to prevent harassment and that his own experience may underweight problems others face.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This page is grounded in one author's personal workflow and does not prove that the same setup works for people with different jobs, communities, risk exposure, or accessibility needs. The article also predates the later ownership, branding, verification, API, and moderation changes around Twitter/X, so its product observations are a 2022 snapshot rather than a current feature audit.
