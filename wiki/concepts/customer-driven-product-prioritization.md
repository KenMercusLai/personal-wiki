Customer-driven product prioritization means treating customer evidence as the main input for deciding what to build. Anand Sanwal's CB Insights list contrasts useful feedback from existing customers, credible prospective customers, and people who have built similar companies with advice from non-customers who will never live with the product's consequences.

The source also separates customer evidence from competitor anxiety. Sanwal says he became too interested in competitors and sometimes pushed product development because another company had a feature. Those choices went poorly because competitors did not pay CB Insights; customers did. Copying competitors can therefore turn product work into an ego response instead of a market response.

Direct customer contact is one mechanism for keeping priorities grounded. The article describes real-life customer meetings, product-team conversations with customers, and customer success as ways to learn which capabilities customers notice, miss, or need help adopting. One example is customer feedback that revealed the CB Insights Chrome Plugin deserved more attention than the team had been giving it.

The Groove source turns the same mechanism into a recurring founder practice. It recommends a simple customer-conversation cadence so founders can learn how customers think, how they use the product, what distinguishes customer personas, and what frustrations would otherwise stay unspoken until dissatisfaction or churn. That makes customer contact a source of prioritization evidence, not only support goodwill.

## Signals

- Priorities come from current customers, qualified prospects, and observed product usage.
- The team can explain which customer problem a feature addresses.
- Competitive analysis informs context without becoming the reason to build.
- Customer success and sales feedback reach product teams before roadmap choices harden.
- The company has a repeatable habit for direct customer conversations rather than waiting for complaints or cancellation data.
