## Definition
[[ExecutiveHiring]] is the practice of recruiting and evaluating senior leaders whose functional expertise, leadership style, and fit will shape a company's trajectory.

## Current Synthesis
The sources frame executive hiring as a high-stakes process where founder intuition, resume prestige, and generic respect for experience are all insufficient. A founder may not know enough about engineering, product, finance, HR, operations, marketing, legal, or marketplace leadership to judge skill directly, so the process must generate signal from role focus, structured fit conversations, broad references, and self-awareness tests. The beautiful-resume warning adds a behavioral filter: even real name-brand experience can be dangerous if the candidate values status, budget, headcount, security, and territory more than company-level momentum. Lonsdale adds a timing filter: senior operators should be hired when their repeatable-process expertise matches a scaling need, not simply because their presence makes the company look safer. The strongest pattern is humility plus evidence: define the few strengths the role truly needs, validate what the candidate personally created, test whether the company is ready for the function, use references to test skill and behavior, and use reciprocal feedback conversations to test whether the working relationship can become candid and non-territorial.

## Key Claims
- Executive roles should be defined by a small number of critical strengths rather than a comprehensive wish list.
- Executive hires should be tied to a real scaling process the company is ready to instantiate.
- Founder interviews should separate mutual interest from deeper fit evaluation.
- Fit includes ambition, motivation, leadership style, failure history, vulnerability, and how the candidate wants to work.
- Skill validation should rely heavily on broad, targeted reference checks rather than candidate self-report or resume prestige.
- Resume prestige should be treated as weak evidence until the process separates personal contribution from riding an already-successful organization.
- Hiring experienced operators too early can obstruct invention; hiring them too late can leave repeatable sales, HR, engineering-management, marketing, finance, or strategy work underbuilt.

## Evidence
- Role focus: [[assembling-an-executive-leadership-team-is-daunting-let-thumbtacks-ceo-help-first-round-review]] says Thumbtack narrows hiring plans to the three essential skills for the role, such as team-building, recruiting, and mentorship for an executive expected to grow a team from 20 to 200.
- Interview structure: [[assembling-an-executive-leadership-team-is-daunting-let-thumbtacks-ceo-help-first-round-review]] describes splitting the founder conversation into an initial mutual-interest session and a second substantive fit conversation.
- Fit questions: [[assembling-an-executive-leadership-team-is-daunting-let-thumbtacks-ceo-help-first-round-review]] recommends probing desired business outcomes, proud accomplishments, and proud failures to reveal ambition, motivation, vulnerability, and self-awareness.
- Reference checks: [[assembling-an-executive-leadership-team-is-daunting-let-thumbtacks-ceo-help-first-round-review]] says Zappacosta speaks with 10 to 20 references across managers, peers, and reports, and uses references to identify both best-case and worst-case views.
- Feedback response: [[assembling-an-executive-leadership-team-is-daunting-let-thumbtacks-ceo-help-first-round-review]] describes presenting reference themes to the candidate, asking why those themes appeared, sharing the CEO's 360 review, and asking the candidate to share team feedback.
- Process need: [[bring-in-the-adults-8vc-news-medium]] says senior hires become valuable when the startup needs to leverage a business process that has been repeated many times before.
- Functional examples: [[bring-in-the-adults-8vc-news-medium]] names VP of sales, head of HR, experienced engineering management, CMO, CFO, CSO, and related leaders as valuable at the right scaling moment.
- Prestige risk: [[beware-of-the-beautiful-resume-small-business-forum]] says founders can become excited by candidates from admired companies and offer major compensation before evaluating whether they created success or rode a rocket ship.
- Behavioral fit: [[beware-of-the-beautiful-resume-small-business-forum]] distinguishes startup people from big-company people by whether they seek company impact and ownership or relative status, security, territory, and visible-error avoidance.
- Early obstruction risk: [[bring-in-the-adults-8vc-news-medium]] argues that veteran management brought into year-one invention work can stultify startup innovation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The model comes from founder/operator/investor advice and is optimized for startup executive searches. It does not prove that 10 to 20 references are feasible for every role, that backchanneling is equally appropriate across labor markets, or that vulnerability signals should outweigh measurable functional performance. The big-company-versus-startup contrast is a useful behavioral and timing filter but can become an overbroad stereotype if it treats all large-company experience as suspect. The process also depends on careful handling of confidentiality around current employers.
