## Definition
[[StartupFinancingMechanics]] is the practical system of shares, valuation, investor instruments, conversion terms, and ownership math that determines how startup fundraising changes a company's capitalization table.

## Current Synthesis
The Crunchbase case treats startup financing as a sequence of mechanical events rather than as a vocabulary list. A company starts with common stock, founder allocations, and an employee pool; unpriced seed capital can then enter through instruments that promise future shares; and a priced Series A turns valuation assumptions into share prices, conversions, new shares, dilution, and control changes. The main lesson is that a headline amount raised or pre-money valuation is not enough to understand ownership: discounts, valuation caps, and conversion triggers determine who owns what after the round closes.

## Key Claims
- Financing mechanics begin before outside capital, when founders create common shares and set aside an employee pool.
- Unpriced seed rounds exchange current cash for future equity rather than a known ownership stake at signing.
- A priced round converts valuation into a share price, which then drives investor share counts.
- Seed discounts and caps can increase investor ownership by letting early investors buy shares below the Series A price.
- The actual post-money valuation can exceed simple pre-money plus new cash when conversion terms create additional share value.
- Understanding financing requires modeling both dollar value and percentage ownership.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a simplified educational case using fictitious companies and investors. It does not model liquidation preferences, pro rata rights, board seats, option-pool refreshes, tax consequences, debt maturity, legal-document variation, or down-round anti-dilution formulas. Its usefulness is mechanical clarity, not legal completeness.
