## Definition
[[AATesting]] is the practice of running nominal experiment variants that are intentionally identical so observed differences reveal sampling variation, instrumentation problems, allocation effects, or overconfident interpretation rather than a treatment effect.

## Current Synthesis
[[DavidKadavy]] uses repeated identical-email tests as a practical calibration exercise for people tempted to treat every visible lift as evidence. Across 56 campaigns and more than 750,000 sends, identical variants generated dramatic-looking relative differences, including a reported 300% click lift. Those outcomes do not show that experiments are inherently useless; they show why a measured difference must be interpreted through the baseline rate, absolute effect, sample size, statistical power, false-positive risk, repeated testing, and business consequence. An A/A test can expose how often an experiment pipeline and its users produce winners when no treatment exists, but it cannot establish that a later A/B treatment caused value or substitute for sound design.

## Key Claims
- Identical variants provide a zero-effect benchmark for an experimentation system.
- Large relative differences can arise from small absolute counts when the baseline event is rare.
- A conventional significance threshold does not eliminate false positives or compensate for inadequate power and repeated testing.
- A/A tests can calibrate operator intuition and reveal allocation or instrumentation problems.
- Experiment results should be connected to consequential downstream outcomes, not interpreted from intermediate clicks or opens alone.
- Calibration improves experiment judgment but does not replace sample-size planning, statistical expertise, or post-deployment monitoring.

## Evidence
- Repeated calibration: [[david-kadavy-a-a-testing-how-i-increased-conversions-300-percent-by-doing-absolutely-nothing]] reports identical variants across 56 campaigns, more than 750,000 total emails, and eight months.
- Apparent effects: [[david-kadavy-a-a-testing-how-i-increased-conversions-300-percent-by-doing-absolutely-nothing]] lists nominal changes of 9% in opens, 20% in clicks, 51% in unsubscribes, and 300% in clicks despite no treatment difference.
- Power example: [[david-kadavy-a-a-testing-how-i-increased-conversions-300-percent-by-doing-absolutely-nothing]] calculates that a 2.2% baseline and 29,034 total recipients would begin detecting roughly a 0.49-percentage-point change at 80% power and 5% significance.
- Outcome quality: [[david-kadavy-a-a-testing-how-i-increased-conversions-300-percent-by-doing-absolutely-nothing]] notes that a lower-click email could still attract warmer prospects who convert more often.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a first-person practitioner essay, not a published statistical analysis of the 56 campaign-level results. It does not provide the full outcome table, allocation details, multiplicity correction, confidence intervals, or independent replication, and the cited sample-size calculation is specific to one baseline and design. A/A tests are useful diagnostics, but random variation means an isolated A/A result need not indicate a broken platform, and passing an A/A test does not prove that allocation, instrumentation, metric definition, or analysis will remain valid in a later experiment.
