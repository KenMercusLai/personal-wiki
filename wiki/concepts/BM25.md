## Definition
[[BM25]] is a lexical document-ranking function that combines term informativeness, saturating term frequency, and document-length normalization.

## Current Synthesis
BM25 addresses three weaknesses of raw term counting: corpus-common words are poor evidence, repeated occurrences have diminishing value, and long documents receive matches partly because they contain more words. For each query term it multiplies a probabilistic IDF weight by a bounded term-frequency contribution, then adjusts that contribution according to document length relative to the collection average.

The source treats `k1 = 1.2` and `b = 0.75` as useful starting points, not universal constants. Lower `k1` makes repetition saturate sooner; higher `b` penalizes above-average document length more strongly. Variants change the boundary conditions: BM25L and BM25+ add lower-bound behavior for collections with extreme length variation, while BM25F normalizes and weights structured fields separately.

## Key Claims
- IDF gives rare, discriminative query terms more influence than collection-common terms.
- The `k1` term bounds the gain from repetition and reduces susceptibility to keyword stuffing.
- The `b` term controls how strongly document length changes the effective term frequency.
- BM25 can score only documents reached through a sparse lexical index, making it fast and interpretable without training data.
- BM25L, BM25+, and BM25F adapt the basic model to length extremes and structured fields.
- Exact-token matching and bag-of-words independence prevent BM25 from understanding synonyms, paraphrases, word order, and polysemy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a pedagogical practitioner article, not a reproduced benchmark. Its worked examples use small synthetic corpora, several displayed code outputs and charts are absent from the Markdown export, and no standard retrieval dataset, relevance labels, significance test, latency measurement, or comparison with tuned neural and hybrid baselines is supplied. Parameter defaults and claims about production prevalence should therefore be treated as guidance, not evidence that BM25 is optimal for a particular corpus.
