## Definition
[[AmazonCapabilityLedExpansion]] is the strategy pattern where Amazon turns capabilities learned for one business into external products, adjacent markets, or cross-business systems.

## Current Synthesis
The Forbes profile argues that Amazon's expansion is not random conglomeration. It begins with customer needs and internal operating problems, then converts solved capabilities into businesses. Book retail produced inventory, recommendation, catalog, and fulfillment skills that enabled broader ecommerce and Marketplace. Internal development, delivery, payment, and data-storage problems became Mechanical Turk, Fulfillment by Amazon, Amazon Pay, and [[AWS]]. Kindle forced hardware learning, which later helped [[AmazonEcho]]. [[AmazonPrime]] then connected retail frequency, subscriptions, entertainment, seller economics, and physical-store logistics, while [[AmazonGo]] combined grocery, payments, AI, sensors, cameras, app identity, and physical retail.

## Key Claims
- Amazon expands by asking both backward-from-customer-needs and forward-from-capability questions.
- Internal capabilities can become external businesses when they solve problems other customers also have.
- Failed or limited products can still be strategically valuable if they teach a capability Amazon later uses elsewhere.
- Prime functions as a horizontal connective system that makes multiple adjacencies more attractive.
- AWS supplies both a cloud-market business and a profit engine that funds further capability learning.
- Amazon Go is the article's most explicit example of several capabilities being recombined into one new retail format.

## Evidence
- Retail adjacency: [[bezos-unbound-exclusive-interview-with-the-amazon-founder-on-what-he-plans-to-conquer-next]] says Amazon moved from books to music, DVDs, toys, electronics, and nearly anything sold at retail by reusing tools learned in books.
- Platform turn: [[bezos-unbound-exclusive-interview-with-the-amazon-founder-on-what-he-plans-to-conquer-next]] says Amazon opened its retail platform to independent sellers, formerly competitors.
- Internal-problem conversion: [[bezos-unbound-exclusive-interview-with-the-amazon-founder-on-what-he-plans-to-conquer-next]] links Mechanical Turk, Fulfillment by Amazon, Amazon Pay, and AWS to internal development, delivery, payment, and data-storage capabilities.
- Hardware learning: [[bezos-unbound-exclusive-interview-with-the-amazon-founder-on-what-he-plans-to-conquer-next]] says Kindle did not transform the company by itself but helped Amazon learn hardware before Echo.
- Prime system: [[bezos-unbound-exclusive-interview-with-the-amazon-founder-on-what-he-plans-to-conquer-next]] frames Prime as the central nervous system connecting consumer offering, entertainment, fulfillment, seller economics, and physical retail.
- Go recombination: [[bezos-unbound-exclusive-interview-with-the-amazon-founder-on-what-he-plans-to-conquer-next]] says Amazon Go combines Whole Foods knowledge, AI, cameras, sensors, and Amazon Pay.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a 2018 profile and largely accepts Amazon's strategic self-description. It does not measure which adjacencies actually produced durable profits, where expansion failed, or how regulatory, labor, privacy, supplier, or competition costs alter the capability-led story. Capability transfer can also be overread after the fact; some adjacencies may look obvious only because Amazon survived long enough to connect them.
