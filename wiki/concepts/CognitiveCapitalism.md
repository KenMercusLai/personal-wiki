## Definition
[[CognitiveCapitalism]] is the source's demand-side hypothesis that abundant, cheaper intelligence may release people from “subsistence cognition” and reveal previously unaffordable cognitive wants, tasks, products, and markets.

## Current Synthesis
The concept enters the wiki through [[BenThompson]]'s summary of [[VenkateshRao]]'s “Cognitive Mercantilism.” Its analogy is the transition from agricultural subsistence to industrial and commercial society: the largest effect of greater cognitive productivity may be new demand and forms of activity, not merely doing today's intellectual work more cheaply.

Thompson connects that thesis to [[SmallnessAtScale]]. If AI makes creation and software implementation accessible to individuals and associations, demand may appear at the level of particular households, teams, communities, and private networks rather than only through global mass markets. This remains a scenario about possible demand formation; the source does not establish who captures the gains, whether income follows new wants, or whether displacement and concentration are temporary.

## Key Claims
- Intelligence abundance need not imply that human cognitive capacity becomes economically useless.
- Lower cognitive production cost may expose wants and activities that were previously too expensive to articulate or satisfy.
- The important unit of expansion may be many association-level markets rather than one uniform global market.
- Demand creation, purchasing power, distribution, ownership, and welfare remain separate questions from technical abundance.

## Evidence
- Demand analogy: [[the-end-of-the-internet]] summarizes Rao's comparison between release from subsistence farming and release from subsistence cognition.
- Competing frames: [[the-end-of-the-internet]] contrasts cognitive mercantilist and Malthusian oversupply fears with the cognitive-capitalist expectation of demand expansion.
- Local-market application: [[the-end-of-the-internet]] argues that individuals and associations can surface new needs when AI lowers the cost of making software and other cognitive goods.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page is grounded in Thompson's secondary summary and excerpt of Rao rather than an independently ingested copy of “Cognitive Mercantilism.” Historical analogy does not establish the pace, magnitude, distribution, or welfare effects of AI. New wants do not guarantee purchasing power, employment, competition, autonomy, or equitable access; abundance can coexist with concentrated ownership, reduced wages, dependency, environmental cost, or unmet public needs. The concept should therefore be treated as a demand-formation hypothesis rather than a forecast that displacement will resolve itself.

## What Changed
- Created the concept for the demand-expansion alternative to intelligence-oversupply and economically redundant-mind narratives.
- Connected the thesis to association-level software and markets while preserving distribution and purchasing-power gaps.
