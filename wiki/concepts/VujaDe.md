## Definition
[[VujaDe]] is the deliberate practice of seeing a familiar situation as if for the first time so that experience informs judgment without automatically closing off new possibilities.

## Current Synthesis
[[AndrewChen]] uses the IDEO-associated phrase as an antidote to expert pessimism. Because most technology ventures fail, experience supplies many valid reasons that a new company or category resembles an earlier failure. Yet platform shifts such as social networking, smartphones, and ridesharing can change the conditions that made the earlier idea fail. Vuja de asks the evaluator to examine those present conditions with humility rather than treating a historical base rate as a verdict.

The practice is not indiscriminate optimism. Search engines, social networks, and mobile had weak or disappointing precedents before Google, Facebook, and the iPhone, but those winners do not prove that every repeated idea will work. The useful judgment combines historical evidence with an explicit search for what has materially changed.

## Key Claims
- Accumulated experience can create reflexive rejection because failure examples are abundant.
- New technology cycles can invalidate assumptions learned under earlier infrastructure, distribution, or product conditions.
- Familiar categories deserve fresh evaluation when the enabling context has materially changed.
- Humility includes acknowledging confident misses, such as Chen's early doubts about Facebook.
- Fresh sight should complement rather than erase base rates and historical evidence.

## Evidence
- Experience trap: [[andrewchen-10-years-in-the-bay-area]] says greater technology experience makes it easier to become negative because most ideas and companies fail.
- Cycle changes: [[andrewchen-10-years-in-the-bay-area]] cites social, smartphones, and ridesharing as recurring waves that overturned prior assumptions.
- Historical reversals: [[andrewchen-10-years-in-the-bay-area]] points to search before Google, social networks before Facebook, and mobile before the iPhone as categories whose earlier weakness could mislead later judgment.
- Personal humility: [[andrewchen-10-years-in-the-bay-area]] uses Chen's incorrect view that Facebook could not become a billion-dollar business as a caution against expertise becoming closure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers retrospective examples selected from successful companies and does not compare them with repeated categories that failed again. “Seeing afresh” can therefore become a license to ignore base rates, technical constraints, economics, or prior lessons. A disciplined version must state what has changed, what has not, what evidence could disconfirm the new thesis, and how much downside the experiment permits.

- Created the concept as a qualified method for balancing experience with openness to changed conditions.
