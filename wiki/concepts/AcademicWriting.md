## Definition
[[AcademicWriting]] is writing used as structured problem-solving: defining a question, setting boundaries, building an argument, organizing evidence, and exposing reasoning for inspection.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that academic writing should not be reduced to formatting a paper. Its educational value is metacognitive: it forces vague thought into visible, testable structure, making gaps in evidence and logic easier to find. In AI-era work, this matters because a clearer problem statement leads to clearer instructions and better evaluation of AI output.

## Key Claims
- Academic writing fills computational thinking's gap around problem definition.
- Writing makes hidden reasoning visible enough to inspect, challenge, and revise.
- Argument structure, logical relation markers, and paper-reading methods can be taught inside existing language curricula.
- Learners who can define problems and evidence chains can guide AI more effectively.

## Evidence
- Problem definition: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] says CT begins after a problem is given, while writing trains the step before execution.
- Visible reasoning: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] frames writing as a mirror for thought because holes in reasoning appear when claims are written down.
- Curriculum fit: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] proposes teaching logical relationships, argument skeletons, paper reading, and critique of flawed passages.
- AI use: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] argues that AI input quality depends on how clearly a person has defined the task.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source treats academic writing as a broadly useful reasoning scaffold, but it does not compare it with other reasoning curricula. Its claim is strongest when writing is taught as argument construction and evidence inspection rather than as compliance with format conventions.
