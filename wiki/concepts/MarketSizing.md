## Definition
[[MarketSizing]] is the construction of an evidence-backed estimate of the customers or economic value a business could serve, including the assumptions that connect a present market to adjacent growth or newly created demand.

## Current Synthesis
NextView Ventures treats market size as an argument to be audited, not a large number to be admired. A bare multi-billion-dollar figure is weak evidence because investors see such slides repeatedly and will conduct their own diligence. The useful signal is the founder's model: which customers count, where the inputs came from, how the calculations work, and what assumptions must hold.

The source separates three opportunity shapes. An existing market has public evidence and can be estimated top-down, bottom-up, or by blending the two, with bottom-up analysis preferred. An expanding market begins with an established category but may draw in additional customer types or adjacent markets. A created market emerges when a product unlocks latent value that was not previously expressed as a recognizable category. A company can combine all three, so the framework is best used to expose uncertainty rather than to force a single label.

Two investor-side failure modes deepen this classification. A sleeper market already contains more demand than prevailing perception recognizes, often because behavior is changing or a trend has not yet become legible at scale. A Trojan-horse market is genuinely small at entry but gives the product a wedge into much larger customer groups or adjacent uses. These map imperfectly onto NextView's categories: sleeper markets can involve underestimated existing or emerging demand, while Trojan-horse markets foreground the expansion mechanism. Both frames reinforce the need to separate current measurable size from a sourced thesis about how demand could broaden.

## Key Claims
- A large headline estimate is not persuasive unless its sources, assumptions, and calculations are visible.
- Existing markets are comparatively measurable because public category and customer data already exist.
- Bottom-up estimates are preferred for existing markets because they make the path from customers to total opportunity inspectable.
- Expanding and Trojan-horse markets require explicit hypotheses about which additional customers or adjacent categories can be reached from the initial wedge.
- Created markets are the least predictable because the demand or value category is not yet fully observable.
- Existing, expanding, and created-market characteristics can coexist within one startup opportunity.
- The quality of the reasoning is itself a diligence signal about how well founders understand the people they intend to serve.

## Evidence
- Unsupported headline numbers: [[you-cant-judge-market-size-by-this-slide-nextview-ventures]] says experienced investors have repeatedly seen multi-billion-dollar slides and may react with skepticism.
- Existing-market measurement: [[you-cant-judge-market-size-by-this-slide-nextview-ventures]] permits top-down, bottom-up, or blended analysis of known markets while expressing a preference for bottom-up work.
- Expansion hypotheses: [[you-cant-judge-market-size-by-this-slide-nextview-ventures]] defines an expanding market as an established market that draws in new customer types or tangential categories.
- Created demand: [[you-cant-judge-market-size-by-this-slide-nextview-ventures]] describes a created market as latent value unlocked for a demographic by a profound product.
- Mixed opportunity shape: [[you-cant-judge-market-size-by-this-slide-nextview-ventures]] explicitly allows a startup to combine the three market types.
- Auditable reasoning: [[you-cant-judge-market-size-by-this-slide-nextview-ventures]] asks founders to provide sources and calculations so investors can inspect their thought process.
- Sleeper markets: [[venture-capital-blind-spots-645-ventures-medium]] uses Starbucks to describe demand that existed but looked implausibly small under prevailing assumptions.
- Trojan-horse expansion: [[venture-capital-blind-spots-645-ventures-medium]] uses Facebook's progression beyond elite universities to show how a narrow initial segment can enable much broader reach.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
These are brief venture-practitioner frameworks, not an exhaustive taxonomy or an empirical comparison of forecasting methods. They provide no error ranges or prospective test that distinguishes a sleeper or Trojan-horse market from a category that remains small. Bottom-up estimates can still conceal weak customer-count, willingness-to-pay, reach, timing, or adoption assumptions, while top-down evidence may be useful as a consistency check. Created, sleeper, and expansion language all create hindsight risk when applied to known winners. Showing the derivation makes uncertainty inspectable; it does not make the forecast correct.

## What Changed
- Added sleeper markets as opportunities whose existing or emerging demand is underestimated.
- Added Trojan-horse markets as narrow entry wedges with a credible path to broader customers or uses.
- Separated present measurable size from the evidence needed to justify future expansion.
