## Definition
[[StartupValuation]] is a negotiated estimate of what a startup is worth, which sets the price investors pay for ownership and which early-stage companies support with expectations about the future rather than with current financial results.

## Current Synthesis
TechCrunch treats valuation as the number that makes a funding round legible, while insisting that it is a judgment rather than a measurement. For an early startup, and especially a pre-revenue one, the estimate is a bet on how large the company could become, informed by the estimated size of its market. Later rounds add evidence: revenue, growth trajectory, and the likelihood of a successful outcome through IPO or acquisition.

The source's practical warning is that a higher valuation is not the same as a better outcome. Raising at a high price raises the bar the company must clear. If a startup raises at a $500 million valuation and is later sold for $200 million, the investors who bought at the higher price lose money, and employees who joined at that valuation see their equity worth much less than they were promised. Valuation therefore sets expectations that later results are measured against, which is why [[EmployeeEquityRisk]] and [[StartupEquityTransparency]] connect directly to a number that is usually discussed as a founder scorecard.

## Key Claims
- Valuation is the estimate of company worth that determines the price of the shares sold in a round.
- Early-stage and pre-revenue valuations are forward-looking bets, built from estimated market size rather than current financial performance.
- Later-stage valuations incorporate revenue, growth trajectory, and the probability of an IPO or acquisition.
- A high valuation raises the outcome the company must deliver before investors and employees make money.
- An exit below the last valuation leaves late investors and recently hired employees underwater even when the sale price is large in absolute terms.
- Because valuation is negotiated rather than measured, it can diverge from the company's underlying health.

## Evidence
- Definition and price-setting role: [[wtf-is-a-funding-round-techcrunch]] says valuation is how much the company is worth and that setting it is complicated, especially for early-stage startups.
- Pre-revenue market-size bet: [[wtf-is-a-funding-round-techcrunch]] says many startups raise before revenue and that an estimated market size for the product or service is taken into account.
- Later-stage inputs: [[wtf-is-a-funding-round-techcrunch]] says revenue, growth trajectory, and the likelihood of a successful outcome are measured later on.
- High-valuation risk: [[wtf-is-a-funding-round-techcrunch]] describes a startup raising at a $500 million valuation and selling for $200 million, which means investors who bought at the higher valuation lost money.
- Employee impact: [[wtf-is-a-funding-round-techcrunch]] says a high valuation can also be bad news for employees with equity compensation who joined when the valuation was higher.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supplies a single illustrative raise-and-sale example and does not explain the mechanics that turn a valuation into a share price, such as pre-money versus post-money treatment, option-pool size, liquidation preferences, or anti-dilution provisions, so it should be read alongside [[StartupFinancingMechanics]] and [[CapTableDilution]]. It does not discuss down rounds, flat rounds, valuation marks, secondary pricing, or how a lead investor negotiates a price, and its claim that valuations generally rise over time is a generalization from a period of favorable markets. The source is also describing the founder-facing debate about valuation rather than evaluating whether high-valuation companies actually underperform.
