## Definition
[[SaaSRetention]] is the ability of a software-as-a-service business to keep customers using and paying for the product over time despite competing alternatives.

## Current Synthesis
The Product Habits source treats SaaS retention as the hard half of modern growth. Acquisition has become easier because knowledge, infrastructure, marketing tools, freemium models, and distribution are cheaper, but the same forces give customers more alternatives and less reason to stay. Retention therefore shifts from lock-in toward continuing usefulness: a product has to hold attention, deepen usage, expand with customer needs, and match marketing to the feature-level value that retained customers actually experience.

## Key Claims
- Easier acquisition can make retention harder by increasing competition and customer choice.
- Customer attention becomes scarce when many SaaS tools compete for similar workflows.
- Subscriptions, freemium trials, and portable data reduce lock-in and increase switching risk.
- Cheap distribution can attract weak-fit leads who churn before they become valuable customers.
- Retention improves when product value compounds through broader use, account expansion, or a specific sticky capability.

## Evidence
- Acquisition-retention inversion: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] says getting users is no longer the main SaaS bottleneck; keeping them is.
- Attention scarcity: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] argues that easier entry and abundant SaaS alternatives make attention the scarce resource.
- Switching risk: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] names subscription payments, freemium sampling, and data portability as reasons customers can leave more easily.
- Lead quality: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] warns that cheap distribution can fill the funnel with customers who were never strongly sold on the product.
- Retention mechanisms: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] uses [[Intercom]], [[Front]], and [[CrazyEgg]] to show suite expansion, usage/account expansion, and feature-message alignment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a strategic SaaS essay rather than a cohort analysis. It gives named company examples and cites Price Intelligently, but it does not provide a general quantitative model for retention, churn, expansion revenue, CAC, or LTV across SaaS categories.

## What Changed
- Added SaaS retention as a growth concept centered on competition, switching costs, distribution quality, and compounding product value.
