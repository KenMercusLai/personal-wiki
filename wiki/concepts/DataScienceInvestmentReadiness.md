## Definition
[[DataScienceInvestmentReadiness]] is the condition in which an organization has a strategically important data-science problem, obtainable and sufficiently informative data, the ability to act on results, and a justified need to build rather than buy the capability.

## Current Synthesis
Readiness is not the same as enthusiasm for data or access to a large dataset. The source proposes four linked tests: commitment to either data products or consequential decision support; instrumentation and operational capacity that turn findings into action; enough signal, not merely volume, to support useful inference; and a problem important or distinctive enough that outsourcing is inadequate.

Timing follows the work rather than a fixed company age. A startup should collect strategically useful data and involve expertise in foundational product, architecture, or acquisition choices before those choices become expensive to reverse, yet avoid building a large team before validated demand, usable data, and cross-functional support exist. Post-MVP is therefore a heuristic, with earlier investment justified for intrinsically data-dependent products and later or outsourced investment justified for commodity needs.

## Key Claims
- Readiness begins with a concrete product or decision objective rather than a general wish to be data-driven.
- Data must be collectable, sufficiently signal-rich, and connected to an operational path for acting on the result.
- Data volume does not compensate for weak signal-to-noise ratio or irrelevant measurement.
- Early instrumentation and acquisition planning can be valuable before a dedicated team is justified.
- Internal capability is most defensible when the problem is strategically critical or cannot be served flexibly by consultants or off-the-shelf tools.
- Premature team building consumes runway and can leave specialists unable to create visible impact.

## Evidence
- Strategic commitment: [[doing-data-science-right-your-most-common-questions-answered-first-round-review]] asks whether the company will use data science for product improvement or material strategic decisions.
- Actionability: [[doing-data-science-right-your-most-common-questions-answered-first-round-review]] says instrumentation requires engineering, product, data, and executive commitment and matters only when evidence changes action.
- Signal threshold: [[doing-data-science-right-your-most-common-questions-answered-first-round-review]] distinguishes raw event volume from the critical mass of informative behavior needed for insight.
- Build-versus-buy boundary: [[doing-data-science-right-your-most-common-questions-answered-first-round-review]] recommends outsourcing commodity needs but retaining capabilities central to a distinctive business.
- Timing: [[doing-data-science-right-your-most-common-questions-answered-first-round-review]] uses validated MVP demand as a default point for considering investment while identifying data-native exceptions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework is practitioner guidance, not a validated readiness score or comparative study. "Enough" signal and "critical" capability remain contextual, outsourcing can create privacy, lock-in, learning, or coordination costs, and waiting for post-MVP evidence can itself delay products whose value depends on early data architecture. Conversely, having data and resources does not ensure that models will be valid, fair, useful, or better than simpler experiments and judgment.

## What Changed
- Established readiness as a four-part capability test rather than a function of company age or dataset size.
- Separated early data foundations from the later decision to hire a full team.
- Added strategic distinctiveness as the boundary between outsourced and internal capability.
