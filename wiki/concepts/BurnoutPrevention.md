## Definition
[[BurnoutPrevention]] is the use of personal recovery practices and workplace systems to reduce exhaustion, always-on pressure, and the health costs of sustained overwork.

## Current Synthesis
The sources frame burnout prevention as both institutional and personal boundary work. Huffington's own turn toward sleep came after long hours, insufficient sleep, ill health, and fainting from exhaustion. At [[HuffingtonPost]], she describes structural responses to a 24-hour newsroom: nap rooms, explicit norms against checking work email after hours or on vacation, and a vacation email tool that deletes messages while an employee is away so the temptation to respond is removed rather than merely discouraged.

A new developer can feel pressure to stay in the loop through meetups, hackathons, conferences, and events, especially while trying to break into tech. Burnout prevention therefore also includes choosing a sustainable cadence for career-building activities outside the formal workday.

## Key Claims
- Burnout can follow from long work hours and insufficient sleep, even for highly successful leaders.
- Personal recovery practices matter, but they are not enough by themselves.
- Always-on work cultures require explicit communication boundaries.
- Workplace tools can remove temptation, not merely ask workers to resist it.
- Rest-supporting practices can be framed as performance support rather than anti-work softness.
- Career-building communities can also need boundaries when meetups, conferences, and hackathons take over evenings and weekends.

## Evidence
- Personal exhaustion: [[arianna-huffington-on-sleep-mornings-and-work-the-early-hour]] says Huffington's ill health and fainting from exhaustion led her to study sleep, rest, technology boundaries, and breaks.
- Newsroom pressure: [[arianna-huffington-on-sleep-mornings-and-work-the-early-hour]] says the nonstop news cycle tempts editors, reporters, and engineers to match 24-hour demand.
- Nap rooms: [[arianna-huffington-on-sleep-mornings-and-work-the-early-hour]] says HuffPost installed two nap rooms that later became full most of the time.
- Email boundaries: [[arianna-huffington-on-sleep-mornings-and-work-the-early-hour]] says no one is expected to check work email after hours, on weekends, or while on vacation.
- Vacation email deletion: [[arianna-huffington-on-sleep-mornings-and-work-the-early-hour]] describes a tool that deletes email sent during time off and asks senders to resend later or contact a designated person if urgent.
- Meetup boundaries: [[being-a-junior-developer-at-30-by]] says the author overdid tech events, almost burned out, then limited herself to 1-2 meetups per week.
- Weekend protection: [[being-a-junior-developer-at-30-by]] says the author reserved weekends for her husband and non-tech friends after recognizing the cost of constant community participation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources report leadership intent and first-person experience, but neither independently measures burnout reduction. Huffington's practices are described inside a 24-hour news organization and may need adaptation in workplaces with different urgency, labor power, or compliance requirements. The junior-developer source is also personal: limiting events helped the author, but other people may need different boundaries depending on family responsibilities, city access, health, money, or support networks.

## What Changed

- Added community-participation boundaries as a burnout-prevention case for people breaking into tech.
