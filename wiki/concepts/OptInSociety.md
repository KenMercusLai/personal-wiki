## Definition
[[OptInSociety]] is Srinivasan's term for a voluntary, technology-run social order that people join by choice rather than by inherited jurisdiction or political majority rule.

## Current Synthesis
The talk presents opt-in society as the endpoint of lowering exit costs. Rather than seizing control of existing institutions, technology builders would create alternatives that people can join at different levels: digital communities, remote work, easier relocation, special jurisdictions, seasteads, private islands, or even off-planet colonies. The important property is voluntary participation; people who prefer existing institutions can remain with them while exit experiments demonstrate counterfactuals that others may copy.

## Key Claims
- Opt-in society is proposed as a peaceful alternative to direct political takeover.
- The concept scales from low-commitment digital choices to physical relocation or new jurisdictions.
- Technology is expected to make location less important and thereby lower exit costs.
- Small jurisdictions and experimental countries are treated as laboratories whose successful policies can be copied.
- The model depends on preserving voluntary exit rather than compelling everyone into the new system.

## Evidence
- Definition: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] describes the ultimate exit as building an opt-in society outside the United States and run by technology.
- Scalability of exit: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] says people can take as much or as little exit as they want, from Reddit instead of television to telecommuting or relocation.
- Experimental jurisdictions: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] cites Singapore and Estonia as examples whose policy experiments can teach larger countries.
- Founder examples: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] names Peter Thiel's seasteading interest and Elon Musk's Mars-colony ambition as stronger forms of peaceful exit.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The talk does not solve inclusion, inequality, defense, legal recognition, public goods, migration rights, dispute resolution, or democratic accountability for opt-in societies. It also assumes that technology-mediated choice remains voluntary, though platforms and jurisdictions can create new lock-ins after people join.

## What Changed
- Created the concept for Srinivasan's proposed society-level form of technology-enabled exit.
