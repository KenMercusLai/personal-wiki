## Definition
[[SmartGlasses]] are eye-worn computing devices that combine cameras, displays, sensors, assistants, machine learning, and augmented-reality interfaces in a form factor light and useful enough for everyday wear.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents smart glasses as Apple's likely next major personal-computing frontier after the iPhone, Apple Watch, and AirPods. The argument is not that glasses were ready in 2018; it is that Apple was already preparing the technical, developer, behavioral, and retail foundations for them. [[ARKit]] seeds AR behavior on iPhones and iPads, Siri Shortcuts and machine learning suggest proactive assistance, [[AppleWatch]] teaches body-worn computing and may carry supporting technology, and [[AirPods]] show momentum in personal wearables. The strategic claim is that many WWDC 2018 technologies make most sense when cameras and displays no longer have to be held in the hand.

## Key Claims
- Smart glasses are framed as the next wearables battle after wrists and ears.
- Handheld phones and tablets are useful AR seeding devices but awkward long-term AR viewfinders.
- The category depends on technology becoming light, attractive, and comfortable enough for all-day wear.
- Apple can prepare before launch by seeding ARKit, developers, Memoji-like camera behaviors, and proactive assistant patterns across existing devices.
- Apple Watch may become a support layer for glasses by carrying body-worn technology and assistant learning.
- Apple's advantages include hardware/software integration, custom silicon, miniaturization, developers, retail demos, fashion knowledge, and health use cases.

## Evidence
- Next battle: [[above-avalon-apple-is-figuring-out-whats-next]] says the next wearables battle will be for the eyes.
- Handheld mismatch: [[above-avalon-apple-is-figuring-out-whats-next]] argues that holding an iPhone or iPad up as an AR viewfinder is not ideal for long sessions.
- Technology readiness: [[above-avalon-apple-is-figuring-out-whats-next]] cites Jony Ive's point that some ideas wait for technology to catch up.
- AR seeding: [[above-avalon-apple-is-figuring-out-whats-next]] says ARKit uses hundreds of millions of existing devices to expose developers to AR possibilities.
- Watch support: [[above-avalon-apple-is-figuring-out-whats-next]] argues Apple Watch may help power future glasses and teach Apple about proactive assistants.
- Advantage stack: [[above-avalon-apple-is-figuring-out-whats-next]] lists integration, core technology control, manufacturing miniaturization, AR work, developers, fashion, health, and retail demos as Apple's smart-glasses advantages.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is explicitly speculative about Apple's future product direction in 2018. It does not prove that Apple would ship mass-market glasses, that consumers would accept them, or that AR use cases would justify all-day wear. It also treats Apple's advantages as strategic positioning rather than measured product outcomes.
