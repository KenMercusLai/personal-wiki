## Definition
[[MobileProductivity]] is the reshaping of work software and enterprise workflows as cloud and mobile devices absorb tasks once associated with PCs, keyboards, mice, and office suites.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that productivity should not be equated with specific legacy tools such as the mouse, keyboard, Excel, or PowerPoint. Those tools may remain important for some users, but billions of others may do work through cloud software and mobile-centered devices with different interaction models. The deeper productivity question is the connective tissue of a company: the verbs and workflows that move work along.

## Key Claims
- "Real work" should not be defined by legacy PC input devices or office-suite formats.
- Some power users will keep big screens, keyboards, spreadsheets, and presentations for a long time.
- Mobile and cloud software can reshape work for broader populations beyond core office-tool users.
- Enterprise strategy should focus on workflow verbs and connective tissue, not only documents or operating systems.

## Evidence
- Legacy-tool critique: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] asks whether typing or making presentations is the job or just a tool for getting work done.
- User segmentation: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] says mouse, keyboard, Excel, and PowerPoint will linger for many core users but not necessarily for billions.
- Cloud shift: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] argues productivity software will move to the cloud and onto mobile devices.
- Connective tissue: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] says the important layer is the verbs that move company work along.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is directional and does not specify which jobs can fully leave PC workflows, which require large-screen precision, or how enterprise adoption, security, accessibility, and compliance affect mobile productivity.
