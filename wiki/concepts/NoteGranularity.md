## Definition
[[NoteGranularity]] is the degree to which a knowledge system stores ideas as small atomic notes, larger internally structured notes, or a hybrid arrangement where atomicity can live at the file, heading, block, or outline-item level.

## Current Synthesis
The sources treat note granularity as a design choice shaped by cognitive goals, source material, reuse boundaries, and tool affordances. Small notes support bottom-up idea development because concepts can be recombined through links and metadata, while big notes support top-down organization because headings, lists, outlines, and sections preserve a coherent body of knowledge. Reading workflows add two task-level views: entry, outline, and opinion notes have different natural sizes, while Tietze's cluster process begins with a general overview and separates assumptions, arguments, conclusions, or details only when they have distinct reusable concerns. Atomicity therefore concerns cohesion and separation more than uniform file size.

## Key Claims
- Small notes encourage concise expression, recombination, and bottom-up discovery when their boundaries capture reusable concerns.
- Big notes support internal structure, faster recall within a known topic, and top-down organization of established knowledge.
- Idea atomicity does not have to equal minimum file size: overviews can keep related material together while linked notes separate assumptions, arguments, conclusions, or details.
- Link density is especially important for mature small-note systems because structure lives across notes rather than inside a single document.
- Average note size and internal structure are more important for mature big-note systems because retrieval happens through headings, outlines, and sections.
- The best granularity depends on whether the user is developing new ideas, organizing existing knowledge, or mixing both tasks.
- Reading workflows may need multiple granularities at once: compact entries, outline lists, and short interpretive notes.

## Evidence
- Small-note role: [[zhong-kou-nan-tiao-de-bi-ji-ge-qu-suo-xu-de-gong-ju]] says small notes constrain each note to a concise idea and use links or Folgezettel to combine thoughts.
- Big-note role: [[zhong-kou-nan-tiao-de-bi-ji-ge-qu-suo-xu-de-gong-ju]] describes big notes as internally structured documents that use headings, lists, paragraphs, and outlines to organize a broader topic.
- Atomicity levels: [[zhong-kou-nan-tiao-de-bi-ji-ge-qu-suo-xu-de-gong-ju]] argues that one idea per note can become one idea per bullet, heading, or block in modern software.
- Link density: [[zhong-kou-nan-tiao-de-bi-ji-ge-qu-suo-xu-de-gong-ju]] says small-note maturity should correspond to dense links across the note network.
- Internal structure: [[zhong-kou-nan-tiao-de-bi-ji-ge-qu-suo-xu-de-gong-ju]] says big-note maturity corresponds to note size and structural depth.
- Task fit: [[zhong-kou-nan-tiao-de-bi-ji-ge-qu-suo-xu-de-gong-ju]] summarizes small-note thinking as bottom-up idea development and big-note thinking as top-down organization of existing knowledge.
- Reading-task fit: [[ka-pian-bi-ji-shi-cao-pian-tui-li-xiao-shuo-yu-du-shu-bi-ji-yi-obsidian-wei-li]] separates book entries, booklists, and opinion notes as different structures inside one reading workflow.
- Concern boundaries: [[create-zettel-from-reading-notes-zettelkasten-method]] defines atomicity as keeping related material together while separating concerns that can be reused independently.
- Recursive granularity: [[create-zettel-from-reading-notes-zettelkasten-method]] begins with a general cluster note and branches into linked detail notes only when prerequisites or models need separate explanation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources argue against treating note size as a rigid doctrine. Digital tools can make big notes searchable and linkable, while small notes can be assembled into structured maps, so the operational differences are smaller than the rhetoric around note size sometimes suggests. Reading examples likewise suggest that granularity should follow the job: sparse metadata, linked lists, reflective prose, cluster overviews, and reusable arguments should not be forced into one template. Tietze's example is a personal workflow rather than evidence that its boundaries optimize learning or retrieval.

## What Changed
- Refined atomicity as cohesion plus separation of reusable concerns, not mandatory minimal note size.
- Added general-to-detail branching as a practical way to discover note boundaries during synthesis.
