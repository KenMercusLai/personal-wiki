## Definition
[[AudienceCenteredGameDesign]] is the practice of developing interactive experiences from the lives, tastes, barriers, and desired forms of meaning of intended players, especially people poorly served by established game conventions.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats "non-gamer" as an unstable market label: rejection of current games may reflect narrow subject matter, hostile representation, inaccessible interaction, unfamiliar cultural references, or mandatory conflict rather than rejection of interactivity. The proposed response is not merely to simplify or subtract disliked elements, but to involve underserved people in defining worthwhile problems and experiences. Games can then use their distinctive interactivity for identity, relationships, care, experimentation, and understanding while retaining the qualifications that audiences differ, other media remain valuable, discovery and controls matter, and novel categories carry commercial risk.

## Key Claims
- Existing game conventions reflect the experiences and tastes of the people and organizations that repeatedly make them.
- Non-consumption does not by itself distinguish disinterest in a medium from rejection of its dominant products, themes, controls, or representation.
- Emotional attachment and identity play can engage someone who rejects the surrounding combat, fantasy, or progression structure.
- Additive relevance is stronger than subtraction: a game must be about something the intended audience cares about, not only remove what repels them.
- Conversation, representative play-testing, and co-design can expose assumptions and generate alternatives for unfamiliar audiences.
- Care, compassion, creation, and social connection are viable design centers, but not universal replacements for challenge, action, or linear art.

## Evidence
- Audience barriers: [[video-games-are-boring-gamesindustry-biz]] reports lack of depth, insulting representation, missing cultural references, inaccessible controls, and unwanted action among Code's friends' objections.
- Partial engagement: [[video-games-are-boring-gamesindustry-biz]] describes Code's cousin valuing identity and attachment to Lydia in *Skyrim* while eventually abandoning its fighting, swords, and dragons.
- Design-method shift: [[video-games-are-boring-gamesindustry-biz]] moves from recommending existing games toward asking prospective players about difficult parts of life and making with them.
- Positive design territory: [[video-games-are-boring-gamesindustry-biz]] proposes care, characters, creation, compassion, understanding, and connection as alternatives to shock and combat defaults.
- Counterarguments: the reader discussion preserved in [[video-games-are-boring-gamesindustry-biz]] raises discoverability, accessibility, economics, existing genre diversity, challenge-based pleasure, and the independent strengths of linear media.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's direct evidence is a small, socially connected group rather than representative research, and its strongest example is mixed: *Skyrim* briefly created attachment but still lost the player. Reader responses correctly separate workforce diversity, audience diversity, control accessibility, discoverability, genre awareness, and publisher risk rather than treating them as one cause. Existing players' preferences remain legitimate, fixed linearity can be an artistic strength, and participatory design reduces uncertainty without proving market demand or commercial viability.

## What Changed
- Created a synthesis that distinguishes rejection of dominant game conventions from rejection of interactivity itself.
- Made lived relevance and positive audience goals, not feature removal alone, the concept's central design test.
- Preserved accessibility, discovery, medium-specific, sampling, and commercial-risk qualifications from the reader discussion.
