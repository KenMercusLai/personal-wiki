## Definition
[[IncongruityResolution]] is the recognition of a mismatch with an expected frame or norm followed by an interpretation that makes the situation intelligible, acceptable, or internally coherent enough to process.

## Current Synthesis
The current source narrows humorous incongruity to a listener-relative violation of a strict or default formula designated as a norm. A statement is felt incongruous when, after setting aside that norm, the combined setup and punchline support the norm's violation and the punchline still reveals the setup. Resolution therefore does not erase the violation; it shows how the joke can be followed by “acting as if” the norm or law did not govern the case.

Absurdity is treated as a special communicative incongruity in which the story disregards obvious world knowledge, such as fitting four elephants into a small car. Socially unsuitable behavior supplies another case. Whether either is comic depends on the listener's norms, background, tolerance, and capacity to accommodate the exceptional interpretation.

## Key Claims
- Incongruity is distinct from logical inconsistency even though both can involve conflict with background knowledge.
- A norm may be a strict formula or a default rule recognized as socially or practically binding by the listener.
- Resolution requires the punchline to explain the context even while reasoning disregards the violated norm.
- Some strict norms cannot be treated as independently violated when the remaining strict knowledge already entails them.
- Norm identity, strength, shock, and comic acceptability vary by listener and remain only partly modeled.

## Evidence
- Formal definition: [[2309.02009]] requires both derivation of a norm violation from the whole statement and revelation of the context after removing that norm from the reasoning base.
- Strict-norm example: [[2309.02009]] analyzes the four-elephants riddle by disregarding the rule that elephants are huge while retaining the spatial answer.
- Default-norm example: [[2309.02009]] analyzes setting a neighbor's house on fire as a violation of expected rational conduct that is nevertheless explained by a literal reading of “make yourself at home.”

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Removing one formula is a diagnostic device, not necessarily a cognitively realistic account of how people suspend norms. Redundant strict knowledge can prevent the formal test from recognizing a violation, and the paper does not supply a general method for identifying which formulas count as norms. It also leaves degree of incongruity, emotion, taboo strength, listener-teller common ground, and empirical prediction for future work. Incongruity is not sufficient for humor because it can also evoke discomfort, danger, or wonder.

## What Changed
- Created the concept around norm violation plus revelation-in-disregard.
- Distinguished absurdity, social incongruity, and ordinary expectation surprise.
- Preserved listener dependence and the preliminary status of the formal test.
