## Definition
[[UserResearchPatternThreshold]] is a lightweight user-research rule that treats one matching signal as an anecdote, two as a possible coincidence, and three matching signals from target users as a pattern worth acting on.

## Current Synthesis
The rule of threes offers a pragmatic stopping heuristic: note one target-user observation, pay attention to two, and treat three matching signals as a pattern worth building or testing. The newer source contests where this evidence cycle should begin. Courtney argues that intensive interviews, personas, and empathy maps can delay the more actionable evidence from realistic product use, while still preserving up-front research when the problem itself is unclear. The combined judgment is conditional: use early generative research to reduce problem uncertainty, but do not require a fixed quota of interview evidence when a bounded solution hypothesis can be made tangible and tested quickly.

## Key Claims
- User interviews help teams understand target users, pain points, and possible solutions early in development.
- User testing should accompany new prototypes as interviews or standalone sessions.
- Qualitative research is costly enough that teams need a signal threshold.
- One repeated observation is an anecdote, two may be coincidence, and three can indicate a pattern.
- The threshold is reliable only when the research participants match the target audience.
- The value of collecting three pre-prototype signals depends on whether problem uncertainty or solution behavior is the team's main unknown.

## Evidence
- Research timing: [[7-ways-to-use-the-rule-of-threes-to-build-great-products]] recommends heavy interviews early in product development and user testing whenever a new prototype exists.
- Signal threshold: [[7-ways-to-use-the-rule-of-threes-to-build-great-products]] distinguishes anecdote, coincidence, and pattern by whether a team hears the same thing once, twice, or three times.
- Target-audience qualification: [[7-ways-to-use-the-rule-of-threes-to-build-great-products]] says the rule can only be followed reliably when talking and testing with target users.
- Timing challenge: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] says weeks of research artifacts often contributed less to AJ&Smart's work than the first realistic user test.
- Boundary condition: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] retains up-front research when the team has not established which problem it should solve.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Three matching observations are not a statistically generalizable sample by themselves. The rule is better read as a lean qualitative stopping heuristic for a specific hypothesis, especially when combined with product metrics, prototype behavior, and target-user screening. The sources genuinely differ on the default amount of research before prototyping: one recommends heavy early interviews, while the other calls that pattern wasteful in most bounded product work. Neither supplies comparative evidence that establishes a universal sequence.

## What Changed
- Reframed the threshold as conditional on whether the main uncertainty concerns the problem or the proposed solution.
- Added explicit disagreement over heavy research before prototyping rather than flattening the two practitioner views.
