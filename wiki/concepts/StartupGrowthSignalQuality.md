## Definition
[[StartupGrowthSignalQuality]] is the degree to which a reported startup growth rate reflects durable customer demand and viable economics rather than a small base, short measurement window, one-off stimulus, or selective presentation.

## Current Synthesis
A growth percentage is evidence about change over a defined interval, not a complete judgment about a company. Renert's YC Demo Day critique shows why the denominator, time window, acquisition cause, retention, engagement, and economics must travel with the rate. Very high early percentages can be real while still being easy to produce from small revenue bases and impossible to compound for long.

The useful distinction is between authentic growth and durable growth. A launch article, waitlist opening, or influencer campaign may cause a genuine spike, yet say little about whether customers stay, love the product, or produce positive [[UnitEconomics]]. Livingston's failure-avoidance framework explains why teams still measure it: a revenue target can reveal that the product or focus is wrong and can challenge denial. Renert explains why the same number cannot carry the whole judgment. Sustained cohort behavior and repeatable acquisition can make revenue growth strong evidence when interpreted with [[ProductMarketFit]], customer outcomes, and the cash-survival path represented by [[DefaultAlive]].

## Key Claims
- A percentage growth rate is uninterpretable without its starting base, interval, cohort definition, and underlying absolute values.
- Short-window growth should not be mechanically projected as though the same rate will persist through changing market size, capacity, and competition.
- One-time publicity, waitlist releases, and influencer bursts can create authentic measurements without establishing repeatable demand.
- Engagement, retention, customer enthusiasm, and unit economics help distinguish durable pull from temporary acceleration.
- Growth becomes more credible when it persists across cohorts and channels and remains economically supportable.
- A growth target can be operationally useful as an anti-denial forcing function even when the observed rate is not sufficient evidence of durability.

## Evidence
- Base and window risk: [[growth-as-a-false-signal-in-y-combinator-startups-techcrunch]] reports 6–200% monthly growth among 22 anonymized companies, with a 60% average and 41% median, while warning that the short window does not predict unicorn outcomes.
- Compounding test: [[growth-as-a-false-signal-in-y-combinator-startups-techcrunch]] shows that extending the reported rates for one year yields about $21 billion in combined monthly revenue, an intentionally implausible result.
- Stimulus quality: [[growth-as-a-false-signal-in-y-combinator-startups-techcrunch]] distinguishes sustainable growth from launch coverage, a newly opened waitlist, or a one-time influencer campaign.
- Foundational signals: [[growth-as-a-false-signal-in-y-combinator-startups-techcrunch]] recommends engagement, retention, user love, and early unit economics as complementary diligence evidence.
- Operating doctrine: [[jessica-livingstons-pretty-complete-list-on-how-not-to-fail]] recommends at least 10% monthly growth, prefers revenue as the measured target, and treats missed growth as evidence that the product, focus, or both need attention.
- Survival boundary: [[jessica-livingstons-pretty-complete-list-on-how-not-to-fail]] says growth is insufficient if the company cannot reach breakeven before cash exhaustion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Renert criticizes overinterpretation, not growth measurement itself. His constant-rate extrapolation is a reductio rather than a realistic investor model, and it does not show that sophisticated investors actually expected those rates to persist. The 22 companies are anonymized, their starting revenues and later outcomes are not supplied, and two revenue figures were inferred from GMV using an assumed 15% take rate. Livingston's 10% monthly threshold is likewise practitioner guidance without a sample definition, base-size rule, duration, margin test, or evidence across different business models. Revenue can be a stronger demand signal than attention while still concealing churn, discounts, negative contribution, collection risk, or a growth rate too unstable for default-alive projection. The three charts in Renert's source and all three effective image references in Livingston's source were unavailable at ingest because their URLs returned HTTP 404, so no unseen visual detail was used.

## What Changed
- Created a metric-quality concept separating measured early acceleration from durable, repeatable, and economically viable growth.
- Added base size, measurement window, acquisition cause, retention, and unit economics as the minimum interpretation context.
- Reconciled revenue growth as an anti-denial operating target with the stricter evidence needed to infer durable demand or cash survival.
