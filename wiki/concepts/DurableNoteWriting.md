## Definition
[[DurableNoteWriting]] is the practice of composing and editing a note so that its meaning, purpose, and relevance remain understandable after the writer's original context has faded.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats future comprehension as a writing-design problem. A durable note uses short sentences, paragraphs, and chunks; follows an explicit form suited to its content; states its substantive point in the title; and opens with a compact description that lets a later reader judge relevance before reading the detail. Templates are therefore semantic scaffolds rather than universal forms: entities, stocks and flows, debates, and individual arguments expose different important fields. In a [[ZettelkastenMethod|Zettelkasten]], this editorial work belongs before archival because long retention magnifies the cost of ambiguous prose, missing context, and weak retrieval cues.

## Key Claims
- Notes should be written for a future self who may no longer remember the assumptions that made an initial draft feel clear.
- Short sentences, paragraphs, and chunked prose reduce the reconstruction burden of old notes.
- Explicit structure should follow the kind of knowledge represented rather than force every note into one generic template.
- A descriptive title should state the note's content or claim, not merely name a broad subject.
- Opening sentences should function as a miniature abstract that supports rapid relevance judgment.
- Editing before archival improves both later comprehension and the search terms, tags, and links by which notes resurface.

## Evidence
- Future-reader design: [[how-to-write-a-note-that-you-will-actually-understand]] describes the author's difficulty decoding notes that had felt clear when written and frames the future self as a reader needing explicit context.
- Prose simplicity: [[how-to-write-a-note-that-you-will-actually-understand]] recommends short sentences, paragraphs, and chunks rather than long clause-heavy sentences.
- Content-specific form: [[how-to-write-a-note-that-you-will-actually-understand]] gives distinct structures for entity-like hormone notes, stock-and-flow analyses, debate overviews, and single arguments.
- Front-loaded orientation: [[how-to-write-a-note-that-you-will-actually-understand]] contrasts vague and claim-bearing titles and recommends a short description at the beginning of every Zettel.
- Retrieval and repair: [[how-to-write-a-note-that-you-will-actually-understand]] includes Christian Tietze's account of rephrasing old notes, adding introductions, reconsidering titles and tags, and connecting them to newer material when they resurface.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is a 2015 practitioner essay and comment, not a controlled comparison of note styles or long-term comprehension. Brevity can remove necessary qualifications, a fixed template can distort material that does not fit it, and not every transient or project-specific note merits lifetime-level editing. The durable approach is most applicable when a note is expected to remain reusable, searchable, and independently meaningful over a long period.

## What Changed
- Established future-reader comprehension as a distinct design criterion for long-lived notes.
- Added content-specific templates, descriptive titles, and opening abstracts as practical durability mechanisms.
- Qualified the method by note lifespan, content fit, and the absence of comparative evidence.
