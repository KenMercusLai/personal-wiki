## Definition
[[DeveloperToolPaidAdvertising]] is paid acquisition and awareness work aimed at developers, founders, and technical buyers for developer-facing software products.

## Current Synthesis
The PostHog sources frame paid advertising for developer tools as a narrow, risky, but usable layer after product-market fit rather than the marketing foundation. Search can capture existing demand, social and professional networks can create awareness, and newsletters can concentrate technical attention in bursts, but platform defaults, bots, weak placement control, and attribution blind spots can waste spend. Teams should therefore keep internal channel judgment, separate conversion from awareness, use small experiments, and combine platform metrics with qualitative signup evidence.

## Key Claims
- Paid ads should sit inside a broader developer-marketing system rather than define the whole strategy.
- Paid acquisition is more appropriate after product-market fit because weak attribution and expensive learning can obscure pre-fit product questions.
- Developer-tool teams should budget for both conversion and awareness because demand creation is different from demand capture.
- Click-based attribution can understate ad influence on developers, so qualitative attribution is part of the measurement system.
- Channel quality matters more than cheap reported CPA when bots, irrelevant conversions, or poor placement control distort results.
- Small, time-boxed experiments and explicit control of platform defaults are safer than broad rollout.

## Evidence
- Marketing boundary: [[burning-money-on-paid-ads-for-a-dev-tool-what-weve-learned-posthog]] says PostHog spends much more time writing than running ads and treats paid ads as a subset of marketing.
- Stage boundary: [[dev-tool-marketing-for-early-stage-startups-what-weve-learned]] places paid ads after product-market fit and warns that developer-tool attribution is too weak for them to be a useful early learning default.
- Demand split: [[burning-money-on-paid-ads-for-a-dev-tool-what-weve-learned-posthog]] distinguishes conversion from awareness and says PostHog splits paid budget between them.
- Measurement: both [[burning-money-on-paid-ads-for-a-dev-tool-what-weve-learned-posthog]] and [[dev-tool-marketing-for-early-stage-startups-what-weve-learned]] use qualitative signup attribution to catch influence missed or miscredited by quantitative tools.
- Channel quality: [[burning-money-on-paid-ads-for-a-dev-tool-what-weve-learned-posthog]] says Google Display produced bots and irrelevant conversions despite low apparent CPA.
- Experiments and defaults: [[burning-money-on-paid-ads-for-a-dev-tool-what-weve-learned-posthog]] recommends two to three small tests at a time, while [[dev-tool-marketing-for-early-stage-startups-what-weve-learned]] warns against default settings on Google, LinkedIn, and Twitter.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are overlapping advice from one company, not a controlled cross-channel study. Their apparent tension is resolved by scope: one assumes paid ads already fit the company, while the broader article advises waiting until after fit. Channel performance and agency costs can shift with platform policy, geography, saturation, bidding, product price, brand awareness, and whether the technical buyer is an individual developer, founder, or enterprise evaluator.

## What Changed
- Added product-market fit as the stage boundary for paid acquisition and strengthened the warning about platform defaults and misleading attribution.
