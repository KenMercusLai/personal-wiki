## Definition
[[DeveloperToolPaidAdvertising]] is paid acquisition and awareness work aimed at developers, founders, and technical buyers for developer-facing software products.

## Current Synthesis
The PostHog source frames paid advertising for developer tools as a narrow, risky, but usable marketing layer. The main discipline is to separate channel goals: search captures existing demand, social and professional networks can create awareness, newsletters can concentrate technical attention for short bursts, and community spaces such as Reddit often reward real participation more than ads. Because developers may see ads without clicking, measurement should combine platform metrics with qualitative attribution from signup and demo flows.

## Key Claims
- Paid ads should sit inside a broader developer-marketing system rather than define the whole strategy.
- Developer-tool teams should budget for both conversion and awareness because demand creation is different from demand capture.
- Click-based attribution can understate ad influence on developers, so qualitative attribution is part of the measurement system.
- Channel quality matters more than cheap reported CPA when bots, irrelevant conversions, or poor placement control distort results.
- Founder or individual voice can outperform corporate ad voice in developer-heavy social channels.
- Small, time-boxed experiments are safer than broad channel rollout when the product category is still learning paid acquisition.

## Evidence
- Marketing boundary: [[burning-money-on-paid-ads-for-a-dev-tool-what-weve-learned-posthog]] says PostHog spends much more time writing than running ads and treats paid ads as a subset of marketing.
- Demand split: [[burning-money-on-paid-ads-for-a-dev-tool-what-weve-learned-posthog]] distinguishes conversion from awareness and says PostHog splits paid budget between them.
- Measurement: [[burning-money-on-paid-ads-for-a-dev-tool-what-weve-learned-posthog]] says qualitative signup and demo attribution catches developer influence missed by quantitative tools.
- Channel quality: [[burning-money-on-paid-ads-for-a-dev-tool-what-weve-learned-posthog]] says Google Display produced bots and irrelevant conversions despite low apparent CPA.
- Social voice: [[burning-money-on-paid-ads-for-a-dev-tool-what-weve-learned-posthog]] says promoted organic founder tweets can work better than company-account ads.
- Experiment size: [[burning-money-on-paid-ads-for-a-dev-tool-what-weve-learned-posthog]] recommends two to three small experiments at a time, about $500 and two weeks each.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is one company's operating advice, not a controlled cross-channel study. PostHog explicitly assumes the reader has already decided paid ads fit the company, so the concept should not be interpreted as a universal recommendation to buy ads for every developer tool. Channel performance can shift with platform policy, audience saturation, competitive bidding, product price, brand awareness, and whether the technical buyer is an individual developer, founder, or enterprise evaluator.
