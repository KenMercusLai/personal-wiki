## Definition
[[ExitAsGovernance]] is the use of credible alternatives, switching, forking, founding, relocation, or opt-out infrastructure to discipline institutions without relying only on internal reform or political voice.

## Current Synthesis
Srinivasan adapts the voice-versus-exit frame to technology and politics. Voice means trying to change a system from within: voting, complaining, filing a patch, or proposing a turnaround. Exit means leaving for another system or creating one: emigrating, switching suppliers, founding a startup, or forking a project. The talk's key claim is that exit does not merely replace voice; it makes voice more powerful because institutions notice when people can credibly leave.

## Key Claims
- Exit is a meta-concept that includes competition, forking, founding, customer switching, and emigration.
- Voice and exit are complements rather than strict opposites.
- Lowering exit barriers can protect minority preferences when majorities or incumbents ignore them.
- Silicon Valley's distinctive institutional strength comes from making exit cheap and normal.
- Technology can extend exit from companies and software projects into money, medicine, work, property, and jurisdiction.
- Peaceful exit is presented as preferable to violent political confrontation.

## Evidence
- Definition range: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] maps voice/exit pairs across open source, customers, companies, and countries.
- Voice amplification: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] argues that voice gains attention when people leave in visible numbers.
- Minority protection: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] uses a policy-minority example to show how another jurisdiction can make dissent credible.
- Silicon Valley practice: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] cites startups, forks, non-compete limits, venture disruption, and the browser back button as exit machinery.
- Technology extension: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] lists Bitcoin, 3D printing, telepresence, quantified self, drones, and smart property as ways to reduce lock-in.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source strongly favors exit and treats institutions such as Washington, D.C. through an adversarial frame. It says less about people who lack resources to exit, collective goods that cannot be duplicated easily, harms caused by fragmented governance, or technology platforms that create their own lock-in. The thesis should therefore be paired with concepts such as [[PrivacyPovertyDivide]], [[PlatformStickiness]], and [[WebCentralization]] when evaluating who can actually opt out.
