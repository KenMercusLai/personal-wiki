## Definition
[[ExitAsGovernance]] is the use of credible alternatives, switching, forking, founding, relocation, or opt-out infrastructure to discipline institutions without relying only on internal reform or political voice.

## Current Synthesis
Srinivasan adapts the voice-versus-exit frame to technology and politics. Voice means trying to change a system from within: voting, complaining, filing a patch, or proposing a turnaround. Exit means leaving for another system or creating one: emigrating, switching suppliers, founding a startup, or forking a project. The Evernote essay supplies a narrower consumer-software case: when personal data is important and provider incentives may change, users need a contingency plan and a credible alternative before service quality, privacy, or continuity deteriorates. Across both sources, exit does not merely replace voice; it can make providers more responsive, but only when switching is realistically available and preserves what users value.

## Key Claims
- Exit is a meta-concept that includes competition, forking, founding, customer switching, and emigration.
- Voice and exit are complements rather than strict opposites.
- Lowering exit barriers can protect minority preferences when majorities or incumbents ignore them.
- Silicon Valley's distinctive institutional strength comes from making exit cheap and normal.
- Technology can extend exit from companies and software projects into money, medicine, work, property, and jurisdiction.
- Peaceful exit is presented as preferable to violent political confrontation.
- For data-bearing services, an exit is credible only if users can preserve access and move before provider decline or policy change makes departure costly.

## Evidence
- Definition range: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] maps voice/exit pairs across open source, customers, companies, and countries.
- Voice amplification: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] argues that voice gains attention when people leave in visible numbers.
- Minority protection: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] uses a policy-minority example to show how another jurisdiction can make dissent credible.
- Silicon Valley practice: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] cites startups, forks, non-compete limits, venture disruption, and the browser back button as exit machinery.
- Technology extension: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] lists Bitcoin, 3D printing, telepresence, quantified self, drones, and smart property as ways to reduce lock-in.
- Consumer-software contingency: [[evernote-is-what-happens-when-you-mix-vc-with-a-notes-app]] urges note-service users to plan for IPO, acquisition, financing difficulty, service decline, or abandonment rather than assume indefinite alignment.
- Alternative formation: [[evernote-is-what-happens-when-you-mix-vc-with-a-notes-app]] presents [[StandardNotes]] as an open-source, private, cross-platform exit from [[Evernote]], though the source does not test the alternative's durability or migration quality.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Both sources strongly favor exit. Srinivasan treats institutions such as Washington, D.C. through an adversarial frame and says little about people who lack resources to leave, collective goods that cannot be duplicated, or harms caused by fragmented governance. The Evernote essay is written by the builder of the promoted alternative and assumes that financing paths will harm users without demonstrating causality or comparing migration costs. The thesis should therefore be paired with [[PrivacyPovertyDivide]], [[PlatformStickiness]], and [[WebCentralization]] when evaluating who can actually opt out, and with explicit tests for export completeness, interoperability, learning cost, and destination durability.

## What Changed
- Extended the concept to data-bearing consumer software, where credible exit requires advance planning and preservation of user-held information.
- Added the qualification that naming an alternative does not establish low switching cost or long-term destination durability.
