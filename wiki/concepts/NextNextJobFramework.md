## Definition
[[NextNextJobFramework]] is [[AndrewChen]]'s career-decision method of choosing the next role by first naming the role after it, diagnosing why that future role is not available yet, and working backward to pick opportunities that close the gap.

## Current Synthesis
The framework shifts career planning from immediate offer comparison to option-building. Chen argues that people facing many possible jobs should define a small set of plausible next-next roles, study people already in those roles, and ask what gaps repeatedly appear. A current job then becomes valuable when it supplies missing skills, networks, experiences, mentors, ideas, or a differentiating "superpower" that can make the future role attainable. The method also has a shortcut check: if the desired future role is already possible, more credential accumulation may be risk aversion rather than preparation.

## Key Claims
- Current job options should be evaluated by how well they make a desired future role attainable.
- The central diagnostic is why the desired next-next job cannot be obtained now.
- Gap analysis should include skills, network, experiences, mentors, and idea exposure, not only credentials.
- Career research should continue until conversations with people already in the target role start producing repeated answers.
- A decisive superpower can outweigh an imperfect checklist of gaps.
- Some people over-prepare for roles they could already attempt, especially when risk aversion or imposter syndrome is present.

## Evidence
- Core prompt: [[andrew-chen-the-next-next-job]] asks what the next next job is and why it cannot be obtained right now.
- Future-role buckets: [[andrew-chen-the-next-next-job]] suggests defining 2-3 plausible futures such as investing, founding another company, joining a high-growth startup as an executive, switching industries, or entering product/design/engineering work.
- Research loop: [[andrew-chen-the-next-next-job]] recommends meeting people who already have the target role and asking which gaps must be filled.
- Gap categories: [[andrew-chen-the-next-next-job]] lists skills, network, experiences, mentors, and ideas as possible blockers.
- Superpower emphasis: [[andrew-chen-the-next-next-job]] argues that aspiring investors may need founder dealflow more than generic analytical polish.
- Personal case: [[andrew-chen-the-next-next-job]] says Chen chose [[Uber]] because it could expose him to future founders, scale problems, and startup-investing relevance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is reflective practitioner advice, not outcome research across career transitions. It assumes a person can form plausible future-role buckets and access enough mentors or role models to test them. It also warns that some choices remain emotional: manager fit, work enthusiasm, culture, and compensation still matter, even if the framework gives a better comparison structure.
