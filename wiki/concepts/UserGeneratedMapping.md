## Definition
[[UserGeneratedMapping]] is the use of public, customer, volunteer, or community contributions to create, correct, enrich, and update maps.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats user-generated mapping as both a freshness advantage and a governance problem. Public photos, panoramas, corrections, traffic data, local places, and open-data volunteer work can make digital maps more current and locally detailed than centralized surveys alone. But because maps are trusted infrastructure, user input requires moderation, anti-vandalism controls, source judgment, and choices about how much detail to show.

## Key Claims
- Public contributions can update maps quickly through photos, corrections, panoramas, ratings, and place information.
- Moderation is essential because malicious or inappropriate edits can become visible map data.
- Crowdsourced traffic and local data can improve navigation and commercial discovery.
- Volunteer mapping can democratize geographic knowledge but still reflects contributor interests and biases.
- Mapmaking still requires editorial selection because more contributed data is not automatically a better map.

## Evidence
- Public uploads: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] describes Google Maps users adding photos, corrections, and panoramas that feed moderated place databases.
- Moderation failure: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] discusses the Android-versus-Apple vandalism incident and Google's response to make moderation more robust.
- Waze traffic: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] notes that Google incorporated crowdsourced traffic data from Waze into Maps.
- OpenStreetMap contrast: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] presents [[OpenStreetMap]] as volunteer-maintained mapping with open data.
- Cartographic restraint: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] quotes Parsons saying cartography involves what to remove, not only what to add.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not give a full taxonomy of moderation systems, contributor incentives, or accuracy rates. Its critique of OpenStreetMap is partly from Google's perspective and should be read as one interested assessment.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to connect Google Maps contributions, Waze traffic, and OpenStreetMap volunteer data under one mapping-governance pattern.
