## Definition
[[CrossDomainWisdom]] is explanatory understanding that transfers across fields because it captures WHY-level patterns rather than only domain-specific facts.

## Current Synthesis
The systematic-learning source presents cross-domain wisdom as the most valuable outcome of learning. In the article's DIKW framing, knowledge usually belongs to a specific domain, while some wisdom can travel across domains because it explains underlying patterns. The indirect approach strategy is offered as a principle that can appear in military history, trade conflict, investing, enterprise competition, and careers; entropy is offered as a concept spanning thermodynamics, information theory, cryptography, management, politics, history, and cosmology.

Whitwell's 2017 learning list adds the discovery side of the same pattern. It does not present a finished theory, but it shows how [[CrossDomainScanning]] can feed wisdom by collecting grounded observations from startups, markets, AI translation, medical self-hacking, legacy infrastructure, climate systems, poverty research, media, and customer behavior. The synthesis is therefore two-stage: broad scanning produces leads and analogies; systematic learning tests whether those leads become durable WHY-level understanding.

## Key Claims
- Wisdom is more valuable when it transfers beyond one domain.
- WHY-level questions help distinguish transferable understanding from local information.
- Strategic principles can move across military, business, investing, and career contexts.
- Concepts such as entropy can connect scientific, technical, organizational, and political explanations.
- Cross-domain use requires abstraction, but the source still grounds examples in concrete domains.
- Curiosity-driven scanning can supply raw material for cross-domain wisdom, but the scanned facts need validation before becoming general principles.

- Validation boundary: [[52-things-i-learned-in-2017-fluxx-studio-notes-medium]] is a dated list of sourced facts rather than a causal model, making it useful as a prompt for follow-up rather than as final synthesis.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources celebrate cross-domain transfer and broad curiosity but do not solve the false-analogy problem. A principle that travels well still needs domain-specific constraints; otherwise abstraction can become superficial pattern matching. Whitwell's list also depends on linked underlying sources that the wiki has not separately ingested, so its role here is discovery evidence rather than complete validation.
