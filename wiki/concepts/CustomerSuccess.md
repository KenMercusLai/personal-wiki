## Definition
[[CustomerSuccess]] is the SaaS operating practice of proactively helping customers understand, adopt, and receive value from the product after they buy or trial it.

## Current Synthesis
The CB Insights source frames customer success as a missing bridge between product development and retained customer value. A strong retention rate did not remove the need for someone to help customers notice and use new capabilities as product development accelerated. The lesson is that customer value is not self-executing: even useful features may fail commercially if customers are expected to discover and translate them on their own.

## Key Claims
- Customer success becomes more important when product development accelerates.
- Retention can look healthy while customers still miss new value in the product.
- Customers should not be expected to figure out how a product can help them.
- Customer success connects product adoption, retention, and expansion potential.

## Evidence
- Product-adoption gap: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] says CB Insights had good retention but still suffered when product development outpaced customer awareness.
- Responsibility shift: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] argues that it is the company's job, not the customer's job, to show how new capabilities create value.
- Negative churn context: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] links customer success to the idea of negative churn, implying that adoption work can support expansion as well as retention.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This page currently rests on one founder retrospective. It establishes customer success as an operating lesson for a growing SaaS company, but it does not yet specify team structure, playbooks, health scoring, account management boundaries, or quantitative churn outcomes.
