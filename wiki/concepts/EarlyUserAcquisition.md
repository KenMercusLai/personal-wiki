## Definition
[[EarlyUserAcquisition]] is the stage-sensitive practice of recruiting a startup's first users through direct observation, existing relationships, audience-matched communities, credible conversion infrastructure, and small channel experiments before repeatable scaled distribution exists.

## Current Synthesis
The Penny case presents the first 500 users as a sequence of changing jobs. For the first ten, learning quality mattered more than volume: close contacts and enthusiastic research participants were onboarded gradually and, where possible, in person so the founders could watch failure and repair it. From ten to fifty, trust already present in the founders' personal network lowered the cost of asking people to try an unfinished product. Beyond fifty, acquisition required a credible marketing destination and a portfolio of external channels whose value varied sharply.

The source also distinguishes channels by what they can reveal. Show HN produced a visible funnel from about 700 visitors to 100 download clicks and roughly 40 signups; Reddit required prior participation and useful niche content; app-aggregation submissions were mostly weak but created an incidental press path; and blogger outreach prioritized expert feedback over promotion. Early acquisition is therefore both distribution and research, but signup counts remain incomplete unless teams also measure activation, retention, use quality, payment, and channel cost.

## Key Claims
- The first users are best recruited where founders can observe behavior and repair onboarding friction quickly.
- Pre-product interviewees who showed strong interest can form a more relevant initial cohort than an undifferentiated audience.
- Personal networks can bridge a product from a handful of observed users to a larger test cohort when outreach remains specific and respectful.
- A credible landing page becomes important before external traffic is pursued because it concentrates the product explanation and conversion action.
- Community channels require audience fit and norm-aware participation; attention alone does not guarantee qualified adoption.
- A portfolio of small experiments can reveal high-variance opportunities, but exposure, clicks, signups, activation, retention, and payment are different outcomes.
- Expert outreach can be valuable as a feedback channel even when it does not directly produce growth.

## Evidence
- Observed first cohort: [[mitchell-lee-your-first-500-users]] says the first user encountered roughly 12 bugs during in-person onboarding and that users two through ten were added gradually from interested research participants.
- Network bridge: [[mitchell-lee-your-first-500-users]] attributes growth toward 50 users to one Facebook post and one large group-chat message.
- Conversion infrastructure: [[mitchell-lee-your-first-500-users]] calls a good marketing page a prerequisite for wider traffic generation.
- Community funnel: [[mitchell-lee-your-first-500-users]] reports approximately 700 Show HN visitors, 100 download clicks, and 40 signups, plus roughly 30 Reddit users after community participation.
- High-variance discovery: [[mitchell-lee-your-first-500-users]] says most aggregator submissions had little effect but an Android Police roundup produced almost 200 signups in two days.
- Feedback-first outreach: [[mitchell-lee-your-first-500-users]] describes personal-finance bloggers primarily as domain experts from whom the founders requested product feedback.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept is initially grounded in one founder's retrospective about a pre-revenue, US-only consumer finance app. The article does not provide cohort definitions, activation, retention, referral, revenue, cost, demographic, or counterfactual data, and several channels overlapped with product and landing-page changes. Founder relationships, audience, category, geography, regulation, and timing affect whether the sequence transfers. The strongest general lesson is to match acquisition methods to the current learning problem and measure the next user behavior, not to copy the exact channel order or expect 500 signups to demonstrate product-market fit.

## What Changed
- Created a staged model separating observed first-user learning, personal-network seeding, and external channel experiments.
- Distinguished traffic, clicks, signups, activation, retention, and payment as progressively different evidence.
- Added expert outreach as a learning channel whose value need not depend on direct acquisition.
