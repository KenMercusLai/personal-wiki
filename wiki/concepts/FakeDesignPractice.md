## Definition
[[FakeDesignPractice]] is self-directed design work for fictional or unsolicited scenarios, undertaken to build skill, test capability, and create evidence before a real client or product supplies the opportunity.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents fake design as a permissionless learning environment. High-volume personal work supplies repetitions, while ambitious simulations let a learner encounter unfamiliar technical and product problems before someone is willing to hire them. A functioning fictional project can then serve as portfolio evidence that converts practice into real opportunity.

The useful version is not unconstrained visual polish. It retains realistic data, copy, error recovery, advertising, and organizational pressure, and it becomes stronger when representative users test it. The ethical boundary is control and consent: a self-chosen simulation or freely volunteered contribution differs from unpaid speculative work extracted by someone who may benefit commercially.

## Key Claims
- Repeated fictional projects can build craft through volume before paid work is available.
- A simulation can extend practice into work the learner is not yet trusted to perform professionally.
- Working fictional products can provide portfolio evidence and lead to real commissions.
- Realistic constraints make simulated work more educational than idealized screen decoration.
- Representative-user testing is stronger validation than the creator's judgment or peer applause.
- Self-directed practice, voluntary contribution, and client-requested speculative work have materially different consent and benefit structures.

## Evidence
- Repetition and visible improvement: [[fake-designs-yield-real-results-gv-library]] reports that the author's final ten of one hundred album covers were much better than the first ten.
- Capability expansion: [[fake-designs-yield-real-results-gv-library]] describes the working Coffee Cartel mock store as a way to learn e-commerce design and technical implementation before the team had a real commerce client.
- Portfolio conversion: [[fake-designs-yield-real-results-gv-library]] says Coffee Cartel remained in the studio portfolio and helped the team win a Canadian retailer's e-commerce and inventory-system project.
- Constraint discovery: [[fake-designs-yield-real-results-gv-library]] says the Verizon redesign exposed difficulty in phone-number input and the likely organizational role of an advertising unit the author initially wanted to remove.
- Validation standard: [[fake-designs-yield-real-results-gv-library]] recommends realistic data and copy, recovery flows, and one-on-one user studies in a design-test-redesign loop.
- Ethical boundary: [[fake-designs-yield-real-results-gv-library]] distinguishes coerced spec work from the team's self-initiated volunteer contribution to Mozilla's Firefox branding.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a retrospective practitioner essay, not comparative evidence that fake projects cause career success. The album-cover practice had no external critique, the Verizon redesign was not tested with users, and its claimed improvement was the author's own estimate. Portfolio outcomes are also subject to selection and survivorship effects: the essay does not describe fake projects that failed to teach, impress, or convert into work. Simulations cannot fully reproduce accountability to real users, accessibility needs, production systems, budgets, stakeholders, maintenance, or harmful failure consequences.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish realistic, self-directed simulation from both unconstrained portfolio decoration and client-extracted speculative work.
