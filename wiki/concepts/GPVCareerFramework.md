## Definition

The G+P+V career framework is a self-assessment heuristic that seeks career direction at the intersection of gifts, passion, and values.

## Current Synthesis

The framework asks three distinct questions: what a person does well, what they enjoy, and what work is consistent with their values and conduct. Their conjunction is presented as a promising direction for a career, but the current evidence also shows that personal fit alone does not establish that an activity is economically viable or that its day-to-day working conditions will be acceptable.

## Key Claims

- Gifts identify strengths and advantages that a career can use.
- Passion identifies activities a person genuinely enjoys rather than merely performs well.
- Values test whether a career direction is consistent with the person's worldview and ethical commitments.
- Alignment among all three is a useful prompt for generating career options, not a sufficient guarantee of a viable or satisfying profession.

## Evidence

- Three-part model - [[shi-yong-gpv-si-kao-ni-de-zhi-ye-sheng-ya]] defines G, P, and V as gifts, passion, and values, and represents career as their combination.
- Practical limitation - [[shi-yong-gpv-si-kao-ni-de-zhi-ye-sheng-ya]] includes a cookery example in which enjoyment of an activity does not imply enjoyment of customers or professional conditions.
- Option generation - [[shi-yong-gpv-si-kao-ni-de-zhi-ye-sheng-ya]] suggests teaching or writing as alternative ways to apply the same interests and abilities.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The current corpus contains only a brief secondary account and an anecdotal discussion. The formula does not explicitly test labor demand, income requirements, access barriers, skill-development costs, health constraints, or whether professionalizing an interest will diminish its enjoyment.

## What Changed

- Established gifts, passion, and values as three separate dimensions of career fit.
- Qualified the framework as an option-generating heuristic rather than a complete career decision model.
