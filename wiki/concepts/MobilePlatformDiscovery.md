## Definition
[[MobilePlatformDiscovery]] is how users find, choose, and return to services when app stores, search, feeds, relationships, notifications, operating systems, messaging products, advertising, and platform owners shape attention.

## Current Synthesis
Mobile discovery is not only search. Search retrieves known intent, while feeds, social graphs, colleagues, close ties, stores, charts, notifications, maps, and assistants can create awareness or push useful material before a user asks. Platform owners are therefore not neutral distributors: they choose rankings, placements, interface surfaces, and access rules. The Inside Intercom essay makes this push-versus-pull distinction explicit by calling Facebook a social browser, Slack a work browser, and WhatsApp a close-tie browser.

Attention is concentrated but unstable. The source's retained comScore chart assigns 50% of smartphone app time to the leading app and 78% to the top three, which explains the strategic value of building inside an existing high-attention surface. Yet Sensor Tower's historical sample shows that even reaching No. 1 in the U.S. iPhone download chart rarely guaranteed durable placement. Discovery strategy must therefore distinguish borrowed exposure from recurring demand and should test whether native installation, web access, messaging entry, or another surface matches the user's actual return behavior.

## Key Claims
- Search handles known intent better than awareness and demand generation.
- Mobile discovery is distributed across app stores, operating systems, feeds, messaging, maps, assistants, notifications, and advertising surfaces.
- Relationship context can turn discovery from deliberate pull into socially or organizationally filtered push.
- Attention concentration makes placement inside a leading app strategically valuable.
- Platform-controlled discovery creates dependency because rankings, interfaces, and distribution rules can change.
- App-store rank is volatile and does not by itself prove durable demand.
- The app-versus-web-versus-hosted decision should follow the task and return pattern, not an assumed distribution benefit from installation.

## Evidence
- **Intent versus awareness.** [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] says search can return what users already know they want but does not replace older demand-generation and filtering systems.
- **Contextual push.** [[browsers-not-apps-are-the-future-of-mobile-inside-intercom]] describes Facebook, Slack, and WhatsApp as surfaces where interests, colleagues, and close ties push content to users.
- **Concentrated attention.** [[browsers-not-apps-are-the-future-of-mobile-inside-intercom]] retains a comScore chart showing 50%, 18%, and 10% of smartphone app time in the first-, second-, and third-ranked apps.
- **Purchasable discovery.** [[advertising-models-in-mobile-messaging-apps-mobile-dev-memo]] documents promoted chats, Discover icons, Moments placements, sponsored content, and sticker stores inside messaging products.
- **Volatile chart exposure.** [[74-of-no-1-iphone-apps-dont-last-a-month-in-the-top-25]] reports that 74% of 53 first-time No. 1 U.S. iPhone apps in 2015-2016 left the top 25 within 30 days and only 8% remained after three months.
- **Product-surface test.** [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] asks whether users want a service's icon on the home screen; [[browsers-not-apps-are-the-future-of-mobile-inside-intercom]] warns startups not to assume native installation creates distribution.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is historical and source-scoped. The comScore chart does not visibly state its date, sample, collection method, or whether its ranking is per person or aggregated; it measures app time, not the whole mobile web. Sensor Tower covers first-time No. 1 U.S. iPhone download-chart apps in 2015-2016, not all stores, countries, categories, revenue charts, or later eras. The sources predate later recommendation feeds, short video, privacy controls, app-store regulation, mature PWAs, and LLM assistants. Push discovery can feel useful, manipulative, noisy, or untrustworthy depending on incentives and user control.

## What Changed
- Added relationship-filtered push as a discovery mechanism distinct from search and app-store pull.
- Added historical evidence that app attention can be highly concentrated even while chart placement remains fragile.
- Reframed native installation as a task-and-return-pattern decision rather than an automatic acquisition channel.
