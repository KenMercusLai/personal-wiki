## Definition
[[MobilePlatformDiscovery]] is the problem of how users find, choose, and return to services when traffic, curation, search, app stores, notifications, operating systems, and social graphs all shape attention.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats discovery as one of mobile's unresolved strategic bottlenecks. Search lets users retrieve things they already know they want, while print, retail, and portals once provided demand generation and filtering. In mobile, that function is fragmented across app stores, OS-level surfaces, notifications, messaging, maps, social feeds, and platform owners. The strategic question becomes who has traffic, who can route it, and what kinds of curation or AI can create awareness.

## Key Claims
- Search solves intent retrieval better than demand generation or awareness.
- Mobile platforms are not neutral because operating systems and app stores can shape what services users discover.
- User acquisition depends on traffic routing as much as on service quality.
- New runtimes such as messaging, assistants, maps, and notifications are partly attempts to own discovery.
- The app-versus-web decision has a product-discovery test: whether users want a service on their home screen.

## Evidence
- Search limits: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] says search can only give users what they already knew they wanted.
- Lost filters: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] contrasts older print and retail discovery with the internet's abundance problem.
- Platform power: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] argues OS control matters because the OS can shape service discovery more than the desktop web did.
- Runtime search: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] frames Siri, Google Now, messaging, maps, and notifications as possible new discovery models.
- App test: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] says brands and publishers should ask whether users want their icon on the home screen.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source identifies discovery as strategically central but does not settle which discovery model wins. It also predates later shifts in recommendation feeds, short video, AI assistants, privacy controls, and app-store regulation.
