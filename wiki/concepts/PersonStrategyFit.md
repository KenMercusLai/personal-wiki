## Definition
[[PersonStrategyFit]] is the degree to which a method, goal, environment, or benchmark matches the personality, skills, values, tolerances, and desired outcomes of the person expected to sustain it.

## Current Synthesis
The Collaborative Fund essay treats strategy as person-dependent rather than universally rankable. Its writer example shows how a method that succeeds for an institution can suppress the voice and intuition that made an individual effective. Its financial examples make the same point more directly: two reasonable investment or spending choices can suit different people, and copying another person's plan can be harmful when temperament, skill, or goals differ. The proposed discipline is neither isolation nor relativism. A person can learn humbly from others while testing advice against self-knowledge, measuring progress with personal benchmarks, and judging success by movement toward the life they actually want.

## Key Claims
- A strategy can be generally reasonable yet fail because it does not fit the person applying it.
- Standardization can reduce performance when distinctive judgment, voice, or intuition is part of the person's advantage.
- Differences in investing or spending do not by themselves prove that one person is correct and another mistaken.
- Personal benchmarks help separate development toward one's own potential from imitation of someone else's goals.
- Learning from other people remains valuable when advice is filtered through one's own goals, capabilities, and tolerances.

## Evidence
- Creative fit: [[do-it-your-way]] describes two independent writers who lost relevance after large media companies replaced their individual voice and intuition with house style and editorial direction.
- Financial fit: [[do-it-your-way]] argues that a reasonable investment plan can still be damaging when it conflicts with an investor's personality or skill set, and that spending differences often reflect preference rather than error.
- Evaluation: [[do-it-your-way]] recommends personal benchmarks and defines success by whether a person gets what they want from life.
- Learning boundary: [[do-it-your-way]] explicitly combines humility toward others with choosing the strategy most aligned with one's own personality and skills.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a short persuasive essay built from two anonymized writer anecdotes and hypothetical financial contrasts; it provides no comparative outcomes, causal evidence, or procedure for measuring fit. Person-strategy fit does not make every preference prudent: legal, ethical, financial, health, competence, and evidence constraints still apply. Comfort can also reflect avoidance rather than genuine alignment, while institutions may need shared standards for coordination, quality, safety, and accountability.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish a generally reasonable strategy from one a particular person can sustainably and effectively use.
