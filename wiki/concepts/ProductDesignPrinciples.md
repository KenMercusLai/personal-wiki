## Definition
[[ProductDesignPrinciples]] are concise, product-specific commitments that help teams choose among plausible design directions by stating what the experience should feel like, whom it prioritizes, and which tradeoffs express its brand and purpose.

## Current Synthesis
The Muzli compilation separates product principles from three neighboring layers. General design principles describe qualities of good visual work; process principles describe how a team researches and builds; system principles create a coherent language across products and platforms. Product principles instead distinguish one product's intended behavior and character. Their main value appears when reasonable options conflict, such as consistency versus contextual fit, automation versus visible control, speed versus accuracy, or expressiveness versus unobtrusiveness.

Across the collected organizations, the strongest principles translate abstract values into behavioral tests. Inclusion changes who must be served from the start; control implies predictable actions, cancellation, and reversibility; trust implies transparency and feedback; focus can justify removing configuration; and brand character can justify delight or visual distinctiveness. A short slogan is therefore insufficient unless teams can connect it to real decisions and examples.

## Key Claims
- Product principles should differentiate the intended experience rather than repeat baseline expectations such as clarity, simplicity, or usability without further meaning.
- Useful principles are concise, illustrated by real examples, applicable to difficult decisions, and connected to brand or product purpose.
- Principles create shared judgment and faster dispute resolution but cannot replace research, implementation quality, operations, or iteration.
- Product principles and design-system principles solve related but different problems: differentiation versus cross-platform coherence.
- Recurring product-level commitments include inclusion, agency, trust, feedback, reversibility, focus, speed, contextual fit, and distinctive character.
- A principle becomes operational only when its implications and tradeoffs are specific enough to rule some plausible choices out.

## Evidence
- Scope distinction: [[design-principles-behind-great-products-muzli-design-inspiration]] separates good-design rules, process rules, product principles, and system principles.
- Decision quality: [[design-principles-behind-great-products-muzli-design-inspiration]] says good principles should be simple, use real examples, guide decisions, and reflect the brand.
- Differentiation: [[design-principles-behind-great-products-muzli-design-inspiration]] rejects generic slogans unless they identify a meaningful product direction.
- Product and system layers: [[design-principles-behind-great-products-muzli-design-inspiration]] argues that cross-platform products can need a unifying design system in addition to product-specific principles.
- Tradeoffs: [[design-principles-behind-great-products-muzli-design-inspiration]] contrasts Medium's contextual appropriateness with strict consistency, Firefox's agency with minimalism as an end, and Asana's speed with accuracy.
- Operational values: [[design-principles-behind-great-products-muzli-design-inspiration]] grounds trust in transparency and reciprocal effort, user control in predictable and reversible actions, and inclusion in designing for difficult-to-serve users from the beginning.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is a 2017 curated compilation of public, organization-authored statements rather than a study of design practice or outcomes. Published principles may be aspirational, selectively remembered, inconsistently applied, or obsolete. Principles can also become vague slogans, post-hoc rationalizations, or centralized constraints that hide customer evidence and local context. Teams still need user research, accessibility work, technical judgment, delivery quality, and explicit mechanisms for revising principles when the product or audience changes.

## What Changed
- Created a product-specific framework that distinguishes decision principles from generic design rules, process guidance, and design-system standards.
