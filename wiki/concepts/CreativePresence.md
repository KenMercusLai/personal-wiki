## Definition
[[CreativePresence]] is the state of receptive, relaxed attention in which a creator prepares the conditions for inspiration to arrive and turns it into a complete work before its momentum dissipates.

## Current Synthesis
The sources treat creation as a field and system problem rather than a pure act of command. Ideas are described as visitors or signals, and the creator's task is to keep the inner room open enough to receive them. The practice is paradoxical: absorb strong influences and train taste, but release the demand to produce greatness on command; stay sensitive, but finish imperfect drafts while the initial life of the idea is present. The creator-anxiety source adds a practical layer: inspiration is easier to use when captured quickly, fed by strong inputs, and supported by activities that relax attention. Wibowo's retrospective adds a repair function: creative technical projects can help recover the sense of magic in computing after burnout and exclusion.

## Key Claims
- Creation often feels less like ownership than like receiving and translating a signal.
- Sensitivity can make creators more vulnerable while also giving them a finer antenna for meaning.
- Creative practice requires both skill cultivation and non-grasping openness.
- Completing an imperfect whole can preserve more creative DNA than polishing fragments.
- Regular presence makes inspiration more likely to find a usable channel.
- Inspiration also needs capture systems and rich inputs so fleeting ideas can become future work.
- Creative work can restore motivation when hostile environments have made a field feel unsafe or joyless.

## Evidence
- Reception metaphor: [[chang]] draws on creativity writing to present ideas as visitors and creators as translators rather than owners.
- Sensitivity and pain: [[chang]] uses Shi Tiesheng and other creator examples to argue that deeper vulnerability can increase receptivity to hidden meanings.
- Paradoxical practice: [[chang]] says creators should absorb good material, shape taste, keep beginner's mind, and cultivate a quiet environment while loosening anxiety about greatness.
- Draft completion: [[chang]] argues that the first spark has momentum and that a complete imperfect draft is often more useful than a perfect fragment.
- Regular presence: [[chang]] recommends showing up to the field daily rather than waiting passively for inspiration.
- Capture and inputs: [[xuan-ti-zhuan-ye-xing-wan-mei-zhu-yi-shu-ju-he-ling-gan-zhe-xie-chuang-zuo-jiao-lv-gai-ru-he-ying-dui]] uses a centralized idea database, shortcuts, quality sources, comments, conversations, walks, showers, and exercise to make inspiration more available.
- Repair through making: [[a-career-retrospective-10-years-working-in-tech-sailor-mercury-medium]] says art-computing projects, knitting-machine hacking, illustrated circuits, and zines were attempts to bring back the magic of computers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The field source uses metaphysical language about signals, the universe, and ideas choosing creators; these should be read as creative phenomenology rather than verified causation. The creator-anxiety source is more operational but remains a personal workflow, not proof that a specific tool stack produces inspiration. The sources may fit essayistic, artistic, and personal technical-creative work better than domains where creation depends mainly on collaboration, deadlines, or formal constraints. Wibowo's source also shows that creative recovery does not erase the structural harms that made recovery necessary.

## What Changed
- Added idea capture, high-quality inputs, and relaxed activities as operational supports for inspiration.
- Added creative technical projects as a way to recover motivation after burnout and exclusion.
