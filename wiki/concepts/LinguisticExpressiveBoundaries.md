## Definition
[[LinguisticExpressiveBoundaries]] is the qualified idea that available symbols, learned distinctions, and conceptual vocabulary constrain what a person or model can readily formulate, without strictly determining everything they can think, infer, or compose.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses a modern language-model analogy to connect learning and expression. A model receives tokenized training material and learns patterns over those representations; a person likewise acquires words and concepts that make distinctions easier to state, share, and develop. On this view, scientific discovery and concept formation expand a community's expressive resources by naming a new object, relation, or problem-solving distinction.

The analogy is useful only in qualified form. A model vocabulary contains encoding units, not one semantic slot per word or concept: subword or byte-level units can compose strings that were never stored as single tokens. Likewise, people can notice, demonstrate, or reason about something before agreeing on a compact name. Language and prior learning shape the cost and precision of expression, but the source does not establish a hard identity between vocabulary, knowledge, expression, and thought.

## Key Claims
- Learned language and concepts make some distinctions easier to formulate and communicate.
- Training coverage and representation constrain a language model's reliable outputs without exhaustively fixing them.
- Naming a new discovery or abstraction can make a previously diffuse distinction reusable in research and coordination.
- A token vocabulary is an encoding inventory, not a one-to-one inventory of words, concepts, or knowledge.
- Human thought and model generation should not be treated as equivalent merely because both can be discussed through language.

## Evidence
- Learning-expression analogy: [[yu-yan-de-bian-jie-jiu-shi-si-wei-de-bian-jie]] argues that what a model or person has learned bounds what they can express.
- Concept expansion: [[yu-yan-de-bian-jie-jiu-shi-si-wei-de-bian-jie]] treats naming new elements and proposing new problem-solving concepts as additions to a shared intellectual vocabulary.
- Model mechanism: [[yu-yan-de-bian-jie-jiu-shi-si-wei-de-bian-jie]] sketches tokenization, vector representation, matrix computation, and next-token generation as the basis for its analogy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a short philosophical-technical essay, not a cognitive study or model evaluation. It conflates words with tokens and concepts: subword tokenizers can spell unseen words from several units, while possessing a token does not imply possessing the associated concept. Models also show limited compositional generalization, and people can reason through perception, imagery, action, analogy, or paraphrase before a stable term exists. The essay invokes Wittgenstein through its title but does not analyze the original philosophical context, so its claim should be read as the author's contemporary analogy rather than an interpretation of Wittgenstein.

## What Changed
- Created a qualified concept linking learned distinctions to the practical ease and precision of expression.
- Separated token inventories from words, concepts, knowledge, and hard limits on thought.
