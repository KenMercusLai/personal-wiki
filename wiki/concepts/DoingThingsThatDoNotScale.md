## Definition
[[DoingThingsThatDoNotScale]] is the startup practice of using manual, high-touch, or inefficient work to learn what users need and create intense early love before automating or expanding.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents unscalable work as a path to scale, not a contradiction of scale. [[BrianChesky]] describes Airbnb founders visiting hosts, living with them, writing early reviews, and helping with photos because making 100 people love the product was more valuable than chasing weak mass adoption. The Nextdoor example extends the point: manual verification, door-to-door work, and direct calls helped test and seed neighborhood engagement before automation.

## Key Claims
- A small number of users who love a product can be more valuable than a large number who only mildly like it.
- Manual customer work exposes product, trust, and engagement details that broad metrics can hide.
- High-touch workflows can later become technology-supported processes.
- Unscalable work is most useful before teams scale neighborhoods, markets, or headcount broadly.

## Evidence
- Airbnb host work: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] describes founders meeting hosts, living with them, writing reviews, and helping take photos.
- Customer-love threshold: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] cites the contrast between 100 loving customers and one million indifferent users.
- Nextdoor manual seeding: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] describes asking for licenses, HOA rosters, door-to-door outreach, phone calls to small neighborhoods, and babysitter-demand walks.
- Automation transition: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] says Nextdoor later systematized manual processes into technology.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Manual work can reveal demand, but it can also hide unit-economics, compliance, safety, or operational costs that emerge only at scale. The source supplies successful examples rather than a failure set, so the concept should be treated as a discovery method rather than proof that every high-touch behavior can scale.
