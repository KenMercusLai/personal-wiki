## Definition
[[SocialProof]] is the persuasive signal people infer from seeing whether other relevant people trust, use, approve of, share, or ignore a product, page, or creator.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats social proof as an unavoidable interpretation layer on landing pages and posts. Testimonials, follower counts, ratings, comments, reviews, embedded tweets, and visible customer names are not automatically positive; visitors ask what the evidence says about who already trusts the offer and whether those people resemble them. That means an empty share count, missing testimonial, stale dated post with few comments, robotic praise, or overfamous association can become negative social proof. Good social proof is therefore curated for relevance, specificity, and timing, while weak signals should often be hidden until the product or audience has enough traction.

## Key Claims
- Social proof exists even when a site does not intentionally add testimonials or share counters.
- Missing or visibly weak approval can become negative social proof.
- Testimonials need specificity, natural language, and audience fit to create trust.
- Relevance often matters more than fame because prospects need to see themselves in the evidence.
- Low public activity metrics should be hidden, delayed, or replaced until they can support the desired perception.
- Deliberately negative proof can work only when an established persona makes rejection part of the brand meaning.

## Evidence
- Unavoidable signal: [[3-ways-to-make-your-landing-page-social-proof-prove-something]] argues that the use or absence of approval indicators always proves something to visitors.
- Negative absence: [[3-ways-to-make-your-landing-page-social-proof-prove-something]] uses the [[Microsoft]] Windows 8 student landing-page screenshot to show product imagery without relatable testimonials.
- Weak wording: [[3-ways-to-make-your-landing-page-social-proof-prove-something]] uses the Avaya example to criticize generic, jargon-heavy endorsement copy.
- Relevance over fame: [[3-ways-to-make-your-landing-page-social-proof-prove-something]] contrasts Marie Forleo's Oprah-style celebrity adjacency with Unbounce, QuickSprout, and Help Scout examples that show relatable or admired customer fit.
- Metric hiding: [[3-ways-to-make-your-landing-page-social-proof-prove-something]] recommends removing share buttons from low-traction posts or non-shareable lead-generation pages, hiding small subscriber numbers, removing dates, and delaying comment totals.
- Contrarian persona: [[3-ways-to-make-your-landing-page-social-proof-prove-something]] uses Jack White's anti-testimonial page as a special case where criticism can reinforce an already strong rock-star posture.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a practitioner copywriting argument rather than a controlled conversion study. It gives persuasive examples but does not isolate social proof effects from audience, design, offer, traffic source, product strength, brand familiarity, or the 2013 social-sharing context. Some current platforms also hide share counts by default, which changes how often this specific negative signal appears.
