## Definition
[[AlgorithmicDecisionOpacity]] is the accountability problem created when automated or data-driven systems make consequential decisions without giving affected people intelligible reasons or contestable evidence.

## Current Synthesis
The source frames opacity as a practical legal barrier for marginalized people. When algorithms deny welfare benefits, loans, jobs, housing, or other resources, lawyers and applicants may not be able to inspect the inputs, logic, or reasons behind the outcome. The article therefore treats explanation rights as more than procedural nicety: for people whose survival depends on bureaucratic or market decisions, an unexplained algorithm can make a denial functionally unchallengeable.

## Key Claims
- Opaque algorithms make consequential denials harder to contest than human decisions or inspectable records.
- Low-income people face especially concrete harms when automated decisions affect benefits, credit, employment, or housing.
- Data errors and broker records can compound algorithmic opacity by feeding hidden or stale inputs into decisions.
- Explanation rights could improve accountability by requiring at least a reason for denial.
- Individual self-defense is insufficient when even experts struggle to navigate the personal-data ecosystem.

## Evidence
- Benefits denials: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] quotes [[MicheleGilman]] describing clients denied benefits by algorithms that cannot be explained.
- Contestability problem: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] includes Gilman's line that she cannot cross-examine an algorithm.
- Opportunity scope: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] connects personal data to welfare benefits, loans, jobs, housing, and predatory targeting.
- Explanation rights: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] discusses GDPR-style right-to-explanation ideas as potentially useful for marginalized people in the U.S.
- System accountability: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] quotes [[MaryMadden]] arguing for policy measures that hold companies accountable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source advocates explanation and accountability but does not technically analyze the algorithms, model types, vendors, or administrative appeals processes involved. It also does not resolve how explanation rights should work for complex machine-learning systems.
