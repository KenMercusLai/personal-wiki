## Definition
[[BusinessModelValidation]] is the practice of testing whether a company's chosen customer segments, value proposition, channels, relationships, resources, costs, revenue model, and pricing tactics form a repeatable and scalable economic system.

## Current Synthesis
The concept extends product and funnel experiments beyond acquisition or stated preference. Traffic, activation, and requested features can all improve while the business remains unvalidated if users do not retain, the assumed payer never pays, or the cost and scale requirements do not fit the chosen market structure.

Validation therefore starts with an explicit model. In single-sided freemium, some users are expected to become payers; in a multi-sided model, one group may use the product while another pays for access, attention, or complementary value. Experiments should identify which proposition is being tested, measure behavior relevant to it, and turn results into insight that changes the next hypothesis. Customer selection is part of that loop because serving an off-model segment can improve local satisfaction while weakening repeatability or economics.

## Key Claims
- Acquisition and activation validate only part of a business model.
- Retention and payment behavior are stronger commercial evidence than feature requests or free signups alone.
- Revenue model describes how cash is generated from segments, while pricing is a set of tactics used to execute that strategy.
- User and payer identity must be explicit, especially when choosing between single-sided freemium and multi-sided models.
- Experiment results become useful when they revise a connected model rather than merely optimize an isolated metric.
- Declining a customer segment can be rational when its needs, costs, or payment behavior conflict with the intended model.

## Evidence
- Partial-funnel failure: [[steve-blank-your-job-is-not-to-make-every-possible-customer-happy]] describes a startup that attracted and activated free users but saw attrition and no paid upgrades.
- Model coverage: [[steve-blank-your-job-is-not-to-make-every-possible-customer-happy]] uses a canvas linking customers and value to channels, relationships, resources, partners, costs, and revenue.
- User-payer structure: [[steve-blank-your-job-is-not-to-make-every-possible-customer-happy]] distinguishes a freemium path in which users become payers from a multi-sided market with a separate payer group.
- Experiment interpretation: [[steve-blank-your-job-is-not-to-make-every-possible-customer-happy]] says tests should yield insight about repeatability, scalability, and money rather than data alone.
- Segment discipline: [[steve-blank-your-job-is-not-to-make-every-possible-customer-happy]] argues for selecting customers that serve the company's short- and long-term needs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers a conceptual case, not validated thresholds for retention, conversion, scale, or profitability. Business models can evolve, cross-subsidize segments, monetize indirectly, or prioritize network formation before revenue, so failure to charge early is not by itself disproof. Conversely, early payment does not establish durable demand, favorable unit economics, operational feasibility, or defensibility. Canvas completeness can also create false confidence unless each important relationship is tested with evidence appropriate to the decision.

## What Changed
- Created a system-level validation concept joining customer evidence to market structure, revenue, pricing, cost, retention, and scale.
- Distinguished partial funnel success from a validated business model.
