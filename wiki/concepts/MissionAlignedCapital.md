## Definition
[[MissionAlignedCapital]] is financing whose return expectations, time horizon, ownership terms, risk model, and governance demands are compatible with a company's stated social purpose and sustainable operating path.

## Current Synthesis
The zebra-company essay frames capital as part of product and institutional design rather than as neutral fuel. A financing model that requires rapid scale and a large exit can redirect culture, strategy, customer treatment, and power even when the founding mission is socially beneficial. Mission alignment therefore means more than placing a company inside an impact-labeled sector: the instrument, investor expectations, legal form, tax treatment, and governance relationship must let profit and purpose coexist over time.

The source also describes a market-formation problem. Investors hesitate to back unfamiliar hybrid models without proof, but founders cannot produce proof without experiments, patient adoption work, and early capital. In the authors' 2017 account, impact investment was narrow and risk-averse, hybrid structures consumed legal expense and founder time, and tax categories recognized only conventional for-profit or nonprofit logic. Their proposed response is an ecosystem that can test instruments such as dividends or equity crowdfunding, broaden eligible sectors, share learning, and connect aligned founders, philanthropists, and investors. These are proposals rather than validated return mechanisms.

The 2016 [[IndieVC]] release adds a concrete, commercially oriented experiment without requiring an explicit social-impact mission. It links investor return to capped company distributions, delays equity conversion until a later financing or acquisition, reduces that conversion after fast repayment, and denies the investor authority to hire or fire the founder. This broadens mission alignment into trajectory alignment: capital can fit a company's intended pace, ownership, customer-revenue model, and definition of success even when its mission is ordinary commercial service. Yet the release reports only early first-party cohort status, so it shows term design rather than validated alignment or returns.

## Key Claims
- Capital terms can shape company behavior, not merely finance an independently chosen strategy.
- Alignment requires matching return expectations and governance to a company's mission, pace, and durable economics.
- Purpose-and-profit companies face a proof gap because unfamiliar models need experiments before cautious investors will trust them.
- Legal and tax categories can impose meaningful search, transaction, and time costs on hybrid organizations.
- Sector-restricted impact mandates can leave socially important fields without suitable finance.
- A functioning market needs shared experiments, investor literacy, founder networks, and evidence about both returns and impact.
- Alignment can be encoded in distribution caps, conversion triggers, governance limits, and rewards for early repayment rather than expressed only through an impact label.

## Evidence
- Behavioral transmission: [[zebras-fix-what-unicorns-break-jennifer-mara-astrid-aniyia-medium]] argues that business models flow into culture, strategy, user experience, and society.
- Investor-company fit: [[zebras-fix-what-unicorns-break-jennifer-mara-astrid-aniyia-medium]] describes founders spending months searching for investors comfortable with alternative purpose-and-profit structures.
- Proof gap: [[zebras-fix-what-unicorns-break-jennifer-mara-astrid-aniyia-medium]] says investors avoid unproven models even though companies need funding to test those models.
- Structural cost: [[zebras-fix-what-unicorns-break-jennifer-mara-astrid-aniyia-medium]] names hybrid entities, public-benefit corporations, B Corps, and L3Cs while emphasizing legal fees, founder time, and binary tax treatment.
- Mandate breadth: [[zebras-fix-what-unicorns-break-jennifer-mara-astrid-aniyia-medium]] says much 2017 impact capital was restricted to clean technology, microfinance, or global health while journalism and education remained underserved.
- Ecosystem response: [[zebras-fix-what-unicorns-break-jennifer-mara-astrid-aniyia-medium]] calls for founders, investors, foundations, corporations, and advocates to pool resources and operating patterns.
- Term-level alignment: [[indie-vc-v-2-release-notes-strong-words-medium]] describes a 5× distribution cap, equity conversion only on financing or acquisition, and a 50% conversion reduction when the cap is reached within four years.
- Governance boundary: [[indie-vc-v-2-release-notes-strong-words-medium]] says the investor cannot hire or fire founders, while advice remains optional and quarterly retreats supply peer and expert support.
- Trajectory fit: [[indie-vc-v-2-release-notes-strong-words-medium]] targets companies with at least $10,000 in monthly revenue that seek profit and independence rather than the next fundable milestone.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not define a general test for alignment, quantify how particular instruments change behavior, or demonstrate that dividends, capped distributions, equity crowdfunding, or hybrid entities produce stronger financial or social outcomes. Investor and founder claims can diverge from actual governance, while social impact remains difficult to measure and can be used as branding. Patient capital can reduce exit pressure but may also accept weak accountability or prolong ineffective organizations.

The zebra article's $36 billion impact-investment figure and sector and demographic funding shares are historical and methodologically undocumented in the captured text. The Indie.vc release supplies a small, selected, one-year pilot with no comparison group, audited financials, complete legal terms, or realized investor-return evidence. Legal and tax details are also jurisdiction- and time-specific. The concept should therefore guide diligence about incentives and control, not imply that one label, instrument, or funding source is inherently aligned.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish capital-labeling from concrete alignment of incentives, time horizon, terms, and governance.
- Added the proof gap and ecosystem-building problem around unfamiliar purpose-and-profit models.
- Qualified the article's proposed instruments as experiments rather than established solutions.
- Added Indie.vc as a term-level experiment in capped returns, contingent conversion, founder control, and revenue-led company selection.
- Broadened alignment from social mission alone to fit with a company's intended trajectory and success definition.
