## Definition
[[FounderVisionAlignment]] is the degree to which founders and senior leaders share a lived commitment to the same company-building path, including growth pace, funding strategy, culture, product quality, management structure, decision rights, conflict norms, and personal development expectations.

## Current Synthesis
The Buffer source shows vision alignment as an operating constraint, not a sentimental ideal. [[JoelGascoigne]], [[LeoWidrich]], and [[SunilSadasivan]] could all value [[Buffer]], product quality, customers, and culture while still wanting different versions of the company. The split emerged around concrete scaling choices: sustainable growth versus more aggressive growth, slow hiring versus team expansion, product reliability versus shipping frequency, culture investment versus immediate performance bars, and whether to pursue more funding, senior leaders, and a traditional board setup.

Tan's TechCrunch source adds the interpersonal layer beneath those strategic forks. He describes resigning from [[Posterous]] after he could no longer agree with his co-founder over the future of the company, then argues that founders must maintain the relationship before the hard disagreement arrives. Alignment is therefore not only choosing the same strategy; it is also preserving enough trust, directness, and role clarity to argue through uncertainty without either avoiding conflict or debating every decision forever.

The strongest lesson is that alignment has to include excitement, personal growth, and live conflict-processing capacity, not only verbal agreement. Gascoigne says the July 2016 offsite produced a vision everyone agreed to, yet later decisions revealed that Widrich and Sadasivan could not fully inhabit that path. Tan adds the preventive version: founders with shared history can still drift if they stop speaking honestly, stop spending time together, or leave decision ownership vague. In this frame, a respectful leadership departure can be the outcome of clarity, but earlier relationship maintenance may keep disagreement from becoming company-ending.

## Key Claims
- Founders and executives can share values while disagreeing about the company-building path those values imply.
- Vision alignment becomes more concrete during scaling because decisions about funding, hiring pace, board structure, product quality, culture, and performance standards must be made.
- Agreement at an offsite or planning moment is weaker than sustained excitement through later decisions.
- Co-founder alignment requires maintained relationship trust and conflict habits, not only shared history or complementary functions.
- Unclear decision ownership can turn ordinary disagreement into chronic strategic conflict.
- Misalignment can be handled as a respectful transition rather than a moral ranking of one vision over another.

## Evidence
- Values-plus-divergence: [[change-at-buffer-the-next-phase-and-why-our-co-founder-and-our-cto-are-moving-on]] says Widrich and Sadasivan deeply shaped Buffer but were leaving because of differing visions.
- Concrete decision points: [[change-at-buffer-the-next-phase-and-why-our-co-founder-and-our-cto-are-moving-on]] names senior-leader timing, further fundraising, product versus culture balance, board setup, performance expectations, hiring pace, and growth model as areas of divergence.
- Weak initial agreement: [[change-at-buffer-the-next-phase-and-why-our-co-founder-and-our-cto-are-moving-on]] says the July 2016 offsite vision was agreed to after debate, but Sunil was unsure he could fully get excited and Leo hoped he could grow into it.
- Transition outcome: [[change-at-buffer-the-next-phase-and-why-our-co-founder-and-our-cto-are-moving-on]] says Widrich would remain a non-executive board member and advisor, while both departing leaders planned new explorations.
- Transparency: [[change-at-buffer-the-next-phase-and-why-our-co-founder-and-our-cto-are-moving-on]] publicly details the January and February 2017 communication timeline for the team and investors.
- Founder-conflict trigger: [[co-founder-conflict-techcrunch]] says Tan resigned after reaching a point where he could not agree with his co-founder over the future of the company.
- Relationship maintenance: [[co-founder-conflict-techcrunch]] says Tan had known his co-founder for more than eight years, but history was not enough because the relationship still had to be maintained.
- Decision ownership: [[co-founder-conflict-techcrunch]] recommends assigning owners to business areas so founders can debate, decide, and move on rather than fight about everything.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence remains founder-authored and retrospective. The Buffer account is strongest as Gascoigne's explanation of Buffer's chosen narrative and weaker as independent evidence about every person's private motivations, the full board context, or whether the new path later produced better outcomes. Tan's account is similarly strongest as a practitioner model of conflict dynamics and weaker as proof that every co-founder dispute can be solved through coaching, role boundaries, or more direct conversation.

## What Changed
- Added Tan's Posterous experience to show that future-direction alignment also depends on maintained conflict habits and decision ownership.
- Preserved the Buffer transition as the main example of respectful misalignment after a company chooses a coherent path.
