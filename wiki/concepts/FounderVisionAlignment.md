## Definition
[[FounderVisionAlignment]] is the degree to which founders and senior leaders share a lived commitment to the same company-building path, including growth pace, funding strategy, culture, product quality, management structure, and personal development expectations.

## Current Synthesis
The Buffer source shows vision alignment as an operating constraint, not a sentimental ideal. [[JoelGascoigne]], [[LeoWidrich]], and [[SunilSadasivan]] could all value [[Buffer]], product quality, customers, and culture while still wanting different versions of the company. The split emerged around concrete scaling choices: sustainable growth versus more aggressive growth, slow hiring versus team expansion, product reliability versus shipping frequency, culture investment versus immediate performance bars, and whether to pursue more funding, senior leaders, and a traditional board setup.

The strongest lesson is that alignment has to include excitement and personal growth, not only verbal agreement. Gascoigne says the July 2016 offsite produced a vision everyone agreed to, yet later decisions revealed that Widrich and Sadasivan could not fully inhabit that path. In this frame, a respectful leadership departure can be the outcome of clarity: once the company chooses one coherent path, leaders whose ambitions point elsewhere may need different roles or new ventures.

## Key Claims
- Founders and executives can share values while disagreeing about the company-building path those values imply.
- Vision alignment becomes more concrete during scaling because decisions about funding, hiring pace, board structure, product quality, culture, and performance standards must be made.
- Agreement at an offsite or planning moment is weaker than sustained excitement through later decisions.
- Misalignment can be handled as a respectful transition rather than a moral ranking of one vision over another.
- Public transparency around leadership transitions can turn internal strategy clarity into external trust, while also exposing sensitive company tensions.

## Evidence
- Values-plus-divergence: [[change-at-buffer-the-next-phase-and-why-our-co-founder-and-our-cto-are-moving-on]] says Widrich and Sadasivan deeply shaped Buffer but were leaving because of differing visions.
- Concrete decision points: [[change-at-buffer-the-next-phase-and-why-our-co-founder-and-our-cto-are-moving-on]] names senior-leader timing, further fundraising, product versus culture balance, board setup, performance expectations, hiring pace, and growth model as areas of divergence.
- Weak initial agreement: [[change-at-buffer-the-next-phase-and-why-our-co-founder-and-our-cto-are-moving-on]] says the July 2016 offsite vision was agreed to after debate, but Sunil was unsure he could fully get excited and Leo hoped he could grow into it.
- Transition outcome: [[change-at-buffer-the-next-phase-and-why-our-co-founder-and-our-cto-are-moving-on]] says Widrich would remain a non-executive board member and advisor, while both departing leaders planned new explorations.
- Transparency: [[change-at-buffer-the-next-phase-and-why-our-co-founder-and-our-cto-are-moving-on]] publicly details the January and February 2017 communication timeline for the team and investors.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is a single company-authored account by one of the involved founders. It is strongest as a first-person explanation of Buffer's chosen narrative and weaker as independent evidence about every person's private motivations, the full board context, or whether the new path later produced better outcomes.
