## Definition
[[DeveloperEconomySegmentation]] is the practice of separating developer-ecosystem analysis into meaningful country or market groups so that blended global averages do not hide distinct technology patterns.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that global Stack Overflow data should often be split into high-income countries and the rest of the world. That division is borrowed from World Bank income classification and is defended as more meaningful for technology-visit patterns than some geographic splits. In the 2017 data, high-income countries accounted for 63.7% of Stack Overflow traffic, while the largest non-high-income traffic sources included India, Brazil, Russia, and China, making the global picture a blend of two different software-industry contexts.

## Key Claims
- Global developer data can hide different technology patterns when high-income and non-high-income countries are blended together.
- World Bank high-income status can be a useful segmentation frame for software-industry analysis.
- High-income countries supplied about two-thirds of Stack Overflow traffic in the source's measured period.
- Non-high-income traffic was heavily shaped by large countries, especially India.
- Audience needs differ by segment, so recruiters, students, and investors may need different technology interpretations.

## Evidence
- Classification basis: [[a-tale-of-two-industries-how-programming-languages-differ-between-wealthy-and-developing-countries-stack-overflow-blog]] uses World Bank GNI-per-capita income categories.
- Traffic split: [[a-tale-of-two-industries-how-programming-languages-differ-between-wealthy-and-developing-countries-stack-overflow-blog]] says high-income countries produce 63.7% of Stack Overflow traffic.
- Country concentration: [[a-tale-of-two-industries-how-programming-languages-differ-between-wealthy-and-developing-countries-stack-overflow-blog]] identifies India, Brazil, Russia, and China as major non-high-income contributors.
- Audience implication: [[a-tale-of-two-industries-how-programming-languages-differ-between-wealthy-and-developing-countries-stack-overflow-blog]] says an American recruiter, Indian student, and investor studying Kenya may need different answers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Income categories compress large internal differences within countries and do not directly measure developer salaries, city-level ecosystems, outsourcing relationships, or language communities. The source also treats "non-high-income" as one broad group, which can obscure important differences among upper-middle, lower-middle, and low-income economies.

## What Changed
- Created the segmentation concept for interpreting global developer data by country-income context.
