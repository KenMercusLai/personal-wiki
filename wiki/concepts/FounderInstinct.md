## Definition
[[FounderInstinct]] is the context-rich judgment founders build by selling to early customers, doing many company jobs themselves, and repeatedly seeing how the product, market, and team actually behave.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats founder instinct as neither mystical nor automatically correct. It is earned through close contact with customers and operations, then becomes useful when founders make high-stakes calls, filter outside advice, avoid overbenchmarking, and onboard executives. The key management challenge is transmission: executives need enough founder context to make autonomous decisions without severing themselves from the company's accumulated learning.

## Key Claims
- Founder instinct comes from direct early customer work and firsthand operating exposure.
- Outside experts, investors, and benchmarks can inform founder judgment but should not replace it.
- Founder instinct matters especially when a company is choosing strategy, evaluating executive autonomy, or handling irreversible decisions.
- Transmitting founder instinct may require temporary micromanagement during executive onboarding.
- Advice quality depends on context, so founders should ask for that context before applying advice.

## Evidence
- Customer grounding: [[9-most-useful-pieces-of-advice-ive-received-mathilde-collin-medium]] says Front knew its market better than outside experts because the team had talked to customers directly.
- Growth evidence: [[9-most-useful-pieces-of-advice-ive-received-mathilde-collin-medium]] presents "follow your growth" as a way to let customer and usage signal guide strategy.
- Executive onboarding: [[9-most-useful-pieces-of-advice-ive-received-mathilde-collin-medium]] says Collin now spends two months closely onboarding executives to transmit founder instinct before granting autonomy.
- Benchmark restraint: [[9-most-useful-pieces-of-advice-ive-received-mathilde-collin-medium]] says startup benchmarks often provide limited signal because startups are trying to do things differently.
- Advice filtering: [[9-most-useful-pieces-of-advice-ive-received-mathilde-collin-medium]] says advice should be treated as one data point and interrogated for context.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a founder's retrospective advice list, not a controlled study of founder judgment. Founder instinct can become overconfidence if it ignores new evidence, specialist expertise, or changes in company scale. The useful claim is bounded: firsthand context should be preserved and transmitted, while still being tested against customers, growth, reversibility, and the specific context behind outside advice.
