## Definition
[[FounderInstinct]] is the context-rich judgment founders build by selling to early customers, doing many company jobs themselves, and repeatedly seeing how the product, market, and team actually behave.

## Current Synthesis
The sources treat founder instinct as neither mystical nor automatically correct. It is earned through close contact with customers, audiences, and operations, then becomes useful when founders make high-stakes calls, filter outside advice, avoid overbenchmarking, and onboard executives. [[DanielleWeisberg]] and [[CarlyZakin]] add an origin-stage case: their confidence in [[TheSkimm]] came from newsroom experience and repeated observation of busy friends, even though their formal market research was little more than an audience search. That decision produced reported early traction, but personal-network seeding and a rapid celebrity endorsement prevent the source from proving intuition alone found the market. [[EvanWilliams]] adds a failure case with two simultaneous lessons: organizational commitments can drown out a founder's unease, but a founder who does not share or closely study the target behavior can also have poorly grounded product judgment. The key management challenge is therefore to preserve and transmit context without confusing an internal feeling with user evidence.

## Key Claims
- Founder instinct comes from direct customer or audience observation and firsthand operating exposure, not from intuition detached from evidence.
- Outside experts, investors, and benchmarks can inform founder judgment but should not replace it.
- Founder instinct matters especially when a company is choosing strategy, evaluating executive autonomy, or handling irreversible decisions.
- Capital, hiring, boards, and publicity can make it harder to act on a founder's warning signals once a strategy feels institutionally committed.
- Transmitting founder instinct may require temporary micromanagement during executive onboarding.
- Advice and intuition both depend on context, so founders should test them against customers, behavior, growth, and reversibility.

## Evidence
- Customer grounding: [[9-most-useful-pieces-of-advice-ive-received-mathilde-collin-medium]] says Front knew its market better than outside experts because the team had talked to customers directly.
- Growth evidence: [[9-most-useful-pieces-of-advice-ive-received-mathilde-collin-medium]] presents "follow your growth" as a way to let customer and usage signal guide strategy.
- Executive onboarding: [[9-most-useful-pieces-of-advice-ive-received-mathilde-collin-medium]] says Collin now spends two months closely onboarding executives to transmit founder instinct before granting autonomy.
- Benchmark restraint: [[9-most-useful-pieces-of-advice-ive-received-mathilde-collin-medium]] says startup benchmarks often provide limited signal because startups are trying to do things differently.
- Advice filtering: [[9-most-useful-pieces-of-advice-ive-received-mathilde-collin-medium]] says advice should be treated as one data point and interrogated for context.
- Origin-stage judgment: [[get-that-life-how-two-friends-started-the-skimm-from-their-apartment]] says Weisberg and Zakin recognized a recurring news-consumption problem among their friends and chose morning email around observed phone routines despite doing almost no formal market research.
- Organizational muting: [[evan-williams-how-odeo-screwed-up]] says money, hiring, board discussion, and press attention drowned out Williams's unease about Odeo's direction.
- User-fit limit: [[evan-williams-how-odeo-screwed-up]] also says Williams did not podcast and that Odeo's browser-based recording tools were too simplistic for creators.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are founder retrospectives, not controlled studies of founder judgment. TheSkimm account is especially vulnerable to selection and attribution bias: it reports a few thousand initial signups but supplies no cohorts or retention, and its personal network, five-friend asks, Today show mention, and later press confound the effect of the founders' intuition. The Odeo report likewise cannot show that following Williams's gut would have produced a viable company, and his distance from podcast creation warns that intuition can be weakly grounded. Founder instinct can become overconfidence if it ignores new evidence, specialist expertise, or changes in company scale. The useful claim is bounded: firsthand context should be preserved and transmitted while still being tested against customers, growth, reversibility, and the specific context behind outside advice.

## What Changed
- Broadened founder instinct from accumulated operating knowledge to include origin-stage judgment grounded in repeated audience observation.
- Added an attribution limit: early traction after an intuitive decision does not isolate intuition from network access, referral prompts, or earned media.
- Added Odeo's paired lesson that organizational commitments can mute founder judgment while weak founder-user fit can make that judgment unreliable.
