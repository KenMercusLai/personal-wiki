## Definition
[[ProductCommoditization]] is the pressure that makes once-distinct products or task-level outputs easier to substitute, cheaper to produce, and harder to defend through features or supplier identity alone.

## Current Synthesis
The Groove source argues that most startup products either already are commodities or will become commodities as building software gets cheaper and easier. Startups face copying from below by newer entrants and from above by larger companies with more cash. The practical response is to build business assets that are harder to copy, especially brand and culture.

Thompson applies a stricter commodity-market model to AI. Tokens are not fungible when models need different token counts and serving resources to reach the same answer; a bounded unit of useful intelligence can be. If several models reliably complete the same job, market power shifts toward the supplier's marginal cost per correct outcome and toward differentiation in product experience, distribution, data feedback, and workflow integration. That transition is conditional rather than complete: frontier capability remains differentiated, equal-quality tasks are hard to specify, and current compute scarcity can keep price above marginal cost.

The Anthropic essay develops the strategic consequence. If model differentiation is fleeting because released capability can be distilled or matched, frontier labs have an incentive to become the user's primary work surface rather than remain an interchangeable input to other software. Workflow ownership can create switching costs and feed real-world use back into model improvement, but it can also increase privacy, competition, and governance risks when access and safety policy remain controlled by the same supplier.

## Key Claims
- Falling build cost makes feature-level product advantages less durable over time.
- Validated products invite copying from startups below and larger companies above.
- Sophisticated technology or defensible IP can slow commoditization but rarely eliminate it forever.
- A healthy business, brand, and culture are harder to reproduce than a product artifact even when features can be copied.
- AI commoditization should be measured at equal task quality rather than by model availability or per-token price.
- When useful intelligence becomes substitutable, model footprint, inference efficiency, memory use, serving utilization, and token efficiency determine cost advantage.
- Product integration, customer relationships, distribution, usage data, and ownership of the primary workflow can remain differentiated above a commodity-like model layer, while concentrating data and policy control at the integrated provider.

## Evidence
- Build-cost pressure: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] argues that cloud computing and many smaller technical advances make products easier and cheaper to build each year.
- Copying directions: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] says successful products risk copying by startups and cash-rich incumbents.
- Defensible business layer: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] says founders should defend brand and culture because building a healthy business remains hard.
- Unit of comparison: [[whos-afraid-of-chinese-models]] distinguishes non-fungible tokens from functionally equivalent task outcomes.
- AI cost structure: [[whos-afraid-of-chinese-models]] identifies footprint, inference, memory, serving, and token efficiency as contributors to cost per unit of intelligence.
- Remaining differentiation: [[whos-afraid-of-chinese-models]] argues that integrated agent products and inference-generated learning can preserve advantage above a substitutable model layer.
- Touchpoint strategy: [[anthropics-safety-superpower]] argues that frontier labs respond to fleeting model differentiation by moving into direct workflows, seeking lock-in and feedback data instead of remaining commodity suppliers to software companies.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The startup source acknowledges technically sophisticated products and defensible intellectual property as slower to commoditize, not permanently safe. The AI sources are strategy analysis rather than measured markets: they supply no equal-quality cost curves, demand elasticity, utilization data, or proof that intelligence is broadly fungible, that labs will replace software, or that retained usage traces will improve models enough to create a moat. Compute scarcity, frontier quality, regulation, trust, data access, latency, and switching costs can sustain differentiation even when a benchmark task appears interchangeable. Integration may defend a supplier while reducing customer portability or control.

## What Changed
- Added direct workflow ownership as a frontier lab's proposed response to fleeting model differentiation.
- Connected workflow lock-in and feedback data to both competitive advantage and customer-governance risk.
- Preserved the unmeasured scope of model substitutability and usage-driven improvement.
