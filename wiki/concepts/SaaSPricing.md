## Definition
[[SaaSPricing]] is the strategic choice of how a subscription software product charges, communicates value, and manages price changes, discounts, and plan incentives over time.

## Current Synthesis
The sources frame SaaS pricing as a patient value-setting and packaging process. Price is not only a number used to close a deal; it teaches customers and sales teams what the product is worth. Discounting can therefore damage pricing power when it lowers perceived value, makes promotions predictable, or separates acquisition success from retention and payback performance. The [[CBInsights]] case adds a positioning warning: competing on cheapness and cute plan names can attract the wrong comparison set, while value-based pricing can force clearer customer focus and product ambition. [[JeremyABoyd]] adds a plan-design tactic: use a conspicuous premium tier to establish an anchor, then make lower tiers genuinely different in support, service, integrations, or account capacity. That recommendation is a testable practitioner hypothesis, not proof that a deliberately extreme anchor improves retained revenue or customer trust.

## Key Claims
- Pricing work should not be undone by using discounts as a quick fix for acquisition.
- Price communicates value to customers, so lowering price can lower perceived quality or importance.
- SaaS promotions differ from retail scarcity because software supply is not naturally limited.
- Pricing choices should be evaluated across retention, churn, CAC recovery, and LTV, not only signups.
- Discount policy should include rules for segment, duration, scope, and offer variation.
- Low prices and playful packaging can weaken positioning when the product needs to sell institutional value.
- Tiered plans should communicate materially different service and capability bundles, while anchors and highlighted plans require testing against retained revenue and trust.

## Evidence
- Patient pricing process: [[a-comprehensive-data-guide-to-why-you-shouldnt-discount]] says companies should avoid underselling after doing pricing work.
- Value communication: [[a-comprehensive-data-guide-to-why-you-shouldnt-discount]] argues that discounting trains customers and teams to devalue the product.
- Retail contrast: [[a-comprehensive-data-guide-to-why-you-shouldnt-discount]] distinguishes SaaS promotions from retail discounts constrained by supply.
- Unit-economics evaluation: [[a-comprehensive-data-guide-to-why-you-shouldnt-discount]] connects discounting to churn, LTV, and CAC recovery.
- Discount rules: [[a-comprehensive-data-guide-to-why-you-shouldnt-discount]] gives constraints for when discounts are used.
- Cheap-positioning warning: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] says CB Insights initially tried to win on price and later raised pricing by more than 10x after moving toward value-based pricing.
- Packaging evidence: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] includes an inspected pricing screenshot with Limo, Town Car, and Cab plans, low monthly prices, usage limits, annual-license CTA copy, and "Our best value!" positioning.
- Customer-focus effect: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] says value-based pricing helped CB Insights focus on the right customers and product capabilities.
- Tier and anchor design: [[jeremy-a-boyd-tricks-to-monetize-your-side-project]] recommends a high-priced unlimited tier, differentiated support and service levels, a comparison chart, and a highlighted best-value or popular plan.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are operator/practitioner advice rather than a general pricing model. They do not yet cover value metrics, usage-based pricing, enterprise negotiation, or willingness-to-pay research in depth. The CB Insights example is a single company retrospective, so its 10x pricing increase should be treated as case evidence for positioning and value capture rather than a universal rule. Boyd reports no Duet experiment or pricing outcome; an intentionally unreasonable anchor and “best value” cue can clarify comparison, manipulate attention, or reduce trust depending on audience and execution.

## What Changed
- Added premium-tier anchoring and materially differentiated plan entitlements as a testable packaging hypothesis.
- Made customer trust and retained revenue explicit countermetrics for comparison-chart tactics.
