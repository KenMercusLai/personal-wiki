## Definition
[[SaaSPricing]] is the strategic choice of how a subscription software product charges, communicates value, and manages price changes, discounts, and plan incentives over time.

## Current Synthesis
The source frames SaaS pricing as a patient value-setting process. Price is not only a number used to close a deal; it teaches customers and sales teams what the product is worth. Discounting can therefore damage pricing power when it lowers perceived value, makes promotions predictable, or separates acquisition success from retention and payback performance.

## Key Claims
- Pricing work should not be undone by using discounts as a quick fix for acquisition.
- Price communicates value to customers, so lowering price can lower perceived quality or importance.
- SaaS promotions differ from retail scarcity because software supply is not naturally limited.
- Pricing choices should be evaluated across retention, churn, CAC recovery, and LTV, not only signups.
- Discount policy should include rules for segment, duration, scope, and offer variation.
