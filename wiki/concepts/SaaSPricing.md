## Definition
[[SaaSPricing]] is the strategic choice of how a subscription software product charges, communicates value, and manages price changes, discounts, and plan incentives over time.

## Current Synthesis
The sources frame SaaS pricing as a patient value-setting process. Price is not only a number used to close a deal; it teaches customers and sales teams what the product is worth. Discounting can therefore damage pricing power when it lowers perceived value, makes promotions predictable, or separates acquisition success from retention and payback performance. The [[CBInsights]] case adds a packaging and positioning warning: competing on cheapness and cute plan names can attract the wrong comparison set, while value-based pricing can force clearer customer focus and product ambition.

## Key Claims
- Pricing work should not be undone by using discounts as a quick fix for acquisition.
- Price communicates value to customers, so lowering price can lower perceived quality or importance.
- SaaS promotions differ from retail scarcity because software supply is not naturally limited.
- Pricing choices should be evaluated across retention, churn, CAC recovery, and LTV, not only signups.
- Discount policy should include rules for segment, duration, scope, and offer variation.
- Low prices and playful packaging can weaken positioning when the product needs to sell institutional value.
- Value-based pricing can clarify which customers and capabilities the SaaS company should prioritize.

## Evidence
- Patient pricing process: [[a-comprehensive-data-guide-to-why-you-shouldnt-discount]] says companies should avoid underselling after doing pricing work.
- Value communication: [[a-comprehensive-data-guide-to-why-you-shouldnt-discount]] argues that discounting trains customers and teams to devalue the product.
- Retail contrast: [[a-comprehensive-data-guide-to-why-you-shouldnt-discount]] distinguishes SaaS promotions from retail discounts constrained by supply.
- Unit-economics evaluation: [[a-comprehensive-data-guide-to-why-you-shouldnt-discount]] connects discounting to churn, LTV, and CAC recovery.
- Discount rules: [[a-comprehensive-data-guide-to-why-you-shouldnt-discount]] gives constraints for when discounts are used.
- Cheap-positioning warning: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] says CB Insights initially tried to win on price and later raised pricing by more than 10x after moving toward value-based pricing.
- Packaging evidence: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] includes an inspected pricing screenshot with Limo, Town Car, and Cab plans, low monthly prices, usage limits, annual-license CTA copy, and "Our best value!" positioning.
- Customer-focus effect: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] says value-based pricing helped CB Insights focus on the right customers and product capabilities.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are operator/practitioner advice rather than a general pricing model. They do not yet cover value metrics, usage-based pricing, enterprise negotiation, or willingness-to-pay research in depth. The CB Insights example is a single company retrospective, so its 10x pricing increase should be treated as case evidence for positioning and value capture rather than a universal rule.
