## Definition
[[BusinessAsArt]] is the view that building and running a business can be a creative practice, with room for personality, experimentation, taste, and unconventional choices across product, marketing, support, and customer relationships.

## Current Synthesis
The source frames business not only as monetization but as a creative medium. [[Hawstein]] argues that product development, marketing emails, campaigns, gifts, reviews, customer conversations, and support choices all leave room for invention. This view helps explain why the author prefers an independent, one-person SaaS path: it lets the business reflect personal taste rather than only growth conventions.

## Key Claims
- Business can involve creative choices across every operating area.
- Borrowing, recombining, and breaking convention are legitimate business moves.
- Marketing and customer support can carry personality instead of relying only on templates.
- The business-as-art stance depends on actually enjoying business activity.
- Independent and micro-company structures can preserve more room for personal expression.

## Evidence
- Creative scope: [[yi-ge-du-li-chuang-zao-zhe-de-wu-nian]] compares business choices to creative work in writing, painting, sculpture, or poetry.
- Tactics with personality: [[yi-ge-du-li-chuang-zao-zhe-de-wu-nian]] mentions unusual marketing emails, campaigns, gifts, customer friendships, and paid review requests as examples of creative latitude.
- Enjoyment condition: [[yi-ge-du-li-chuang-zao-zhe-de-wu-nian]] says business is only enlivening if one likes doing it.
- Independent fit: [[yi-ge-du-li-chuang-zao-zhe-de-wu-nian]] connects creation, autonomy, customers, and interesting people as part of the author's chosen life pattern.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's framing is philosophical and experiential rather than a general business theory. Some markets, regulated domains, or brand contexts leave less room for playful experimentation.
