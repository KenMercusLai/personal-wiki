## Definition
[[AttentionEconomy]] is the media and platform incentive system in which human attention is measured, commodified, and rewarded as a route to revenue.

## Current Synthesis
[[EvWilliams]] argues that when attention converts into money, publishers and recommendation systems can profit from whatever captures people most cheaply, regardless of quality, context, truth, or deliberate choice. This turns compulsion into an ambiguous behavioral signal: an inability to look away may be interpreted as demand for more of the same. The proposed countermodel is not attention elimination but incentive realignment through direct reader payment, ad-free service, and contributor rewards tied to depth and member value.

## Key Claims
- Attention-based revenue rewards capture independently of whether attention was earned through informed, conscious choice.
- Systems that infer preference from observed behavior can confuse compulsion, outrage, or morbid curiosity with genuine desire.
- Volume and speed become economically favored when revenue per reader falls and each additional click remains monetizable.
- Direct reader payment can reduce the conflict between maximizing advertiser attention and serving readers, but only if payment actually rewards quality and value.

## Evidence
- Reward mechanism: [[words-still-matter-3-min-read]] summarizes the prevailing online content incentive as "attention equals money."
- Preference ambiguity: [[words-still-matter-3-min-read]] uses car-crash and junk-food analogies to show systems supplying more of behavior users may not consciously endorse.
- Production pressure: [[words-still-matter-3-min-read]] links ad-funded economics to quantity, speed, clicks, and reduced room for research, fact-checking, nuance, and polish.
- Proposed alternative: [[words-still-matter-3-min-read]] presents Medium subscriptions and member-value-based partner earnings as an attempt to reward depth instead of fleeting attention.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers a platform founder's diagnosis and strategy rather than measured causal evidence. Direct payment does not inherently produce truthful, nuanced, or high-quality work, and subscription systems can still optimize engagement, personalize compulsively, or reward popularity. The article provides no before-and-after data showing that Medium's alternative improved content or user understanding.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish monetized attention from attention as a personal cognitive resource.
- Added compulsion-versus-preference ambiguity as a core recommendation-system risk.
- Added direct reader payment as a proposed, qualified incentive countermodel.
