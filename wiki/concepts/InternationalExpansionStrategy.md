## Definition
[[InternationalExpansionStrategy]] is the staged choice, testing, activation, and adaptation of foreign markets, distribution, product experience, operating capabilities, and local presence so that international demand can become supportable and monetizable growth.

## Current Synthesis
The Dropbox and Trello cases begin with readiness and demonstrated demand, not a desired list of countries. Once a company has [[ProductMarketFit]], international expansion should compete with adjacent products, platforms, and customer groups for scarce time and capital. Organic foreign use is useful evidence, but its meaning depends on product universality, regulation, sales motion, community engagement, and cross-border network effects; both companies could follow existing users in ways that other products should not assume.

Market attractiveness must separate use from monetization and apparent growth from removable friction. Dropbox favored deeper penetration where it already had awareness and network effects, estimated reachable users from connectivity, and treated GDP per capita as a rough monetization signal. Trello used signup, browser, device-language, and country data to select languages, then tested Brazil, Spain, Germany, and France before scaling. Country choice therefore needs an inspectable model of reach, penetration, willingness or ability to pay, and the product, distribution, marketing, and operating work required to close local gaps.

Execution spans the whole customer and company system. Dropbox needed language and time-zone support, local trust, retail partners, currency and payment adaptation, privacy reassurance, a capability-matched office, and a culture-carrying landing team. Trello needed translation tooling, volunteer governance, professional fallback, internationalized strings, reviewers, local content, PR, events, ambassadors, and launch communication because translated availability alone produced little movement in Spain. Sequencing should match scope: Dropbox warns that parallel country operations overload legal, finance, HR, engineering, and executive capacity, while Trello shows that a bounded digital translation program can parallelize many languages after staged experiments when shared tooling, community labor, and fallbacks exist.

## Key Claims
- International expansion should be compared with other adjacent growth options after product-market fit, using opportunity, execution difficulty, team capability, and existing demand.
- Organic international use is a demand signal, not proof that localization, monetization, support, trust, or regulation will transfer.
- Country ranking should distinguish reachable users and penetration from monetizable value and the structural reasons for weak adoption.
- Bounded country experiments can test whether translation, marketing activation, or the production model changes adoption before a larger rollout.
- The first foreign office should solve a concrete capability problem and be chosen for relevant talent rather than copied from another company's footprint.
- Localization includes discovery, distribution, language, marketing, service, data expectations, currency, payment methods, engineering, and organizational presence—not only interface translation.
- Rollout concurrency should follow shared-load and reversibility analysis: sequential market operations protect cross-functional capacity, while standardized language work may be parallelizable after pilots.

## Evidence
- Readiness and comparison: [[dropboxs-playbook-for-international-expansion-with-chenli-wang-reforge]] places the decision after product-market fit and compares it with new platforms, products, or customer bases.
- Demand and country economics: [[dropboxs-playbook-for-international-expansion-with-chenli-wang-reforge]] reports 30–40% international use before Dropbox's 2011 localization, contrasts mature Nordic markets with Thailand, and links monetization to GDP per capita.
- Demand and experiments: [[heres-how-trello-nailed-localization-and-global-marketing]] says Trello selected markets from existing usage and device data, compared launch tactics in three countries, and tested crowdsourcing in France before scaling.
- Capability-led presence: [[dropboxs-playbook-for-international-expansion-with-chenli-wang-reforge]] traces the foreign-office trigger to language and time-zone support and describes blinded candidate review favoring Dublin.
- Trust and landing team: [[dropboxs-playbook-for-international-expansion-with-chenli-wang-reforge]] reports stronger customer receptivity after a visible Dublin presence and describes experienced employees carrying core values into the new office.
- Whole-journey localization: [[dropboxs-playbook-for-international-expansion-with-chenli-wang-reforge]] describes Japanese boxed subscription codes, European data concerns, local currencies, French cards, German direct debit, and their engineering and finance consequences.
- Translation and activation: [[heres-how-trello-nailed-localization-and-global-marketing]] reports little signup change in translated-only Spain, doubled launch-period signups in marketing-intensive Brazil, and triple launch-period signups plus a 25% post-launch lift in France.
- Scope-sensitive sequencing: [[dropboxs-playbook-for-international-expansion-with-chenli-wang-reforge]] warns that parallel country operations overload shared functions, while [[heres-how-trello-nailed-localization-and-global-marketing]] reports a 16-language parallel rollout after staged pilots using shared tools and 520 volunteers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This synthesis comes from two retrospective operator interviews about unusually viral, relatively homogeneous digital products with substantial international use, not comparative evidence across successes and failures. Neither source supplies a full cost model, controlled causal analysis, long-term retention or revenue outcomes, or later audit. Dropbox's GDP-per-capita proxy can hide inequality, purchasing power, pricing, competition, payment access, and segment differences. Trello's country comparisons changed marketing intensity and translation model alongside localization, so the reported signup differences do not isolate causes; its volunteer “free” labor also carried tooling, management, review, engineering, incentive, fairness, and opportunity costs. Local offices add fixed cost, while remote, partner-led, or translation-led models may fit different businesses. All country, channel, payment, privacy, language, and platform examples are historically bounded and require current verification.

## What Changed
- Added country experiments as a bridge from organic demand to broad language investment.
- Expanded whole-journey localization to include launch discovery, local content, community translation operations, and internationalization engineering.
- Qualified sequential rollout with Trello's bounded, tool-supported parallel language launch.
- Added the need to separate translation availability from marketing activation when interpreting growth.
