## Definition
[[PassiveInvesting]] is an investing mode where the investor primarily supplies capital without taking a lead, operating, governance, or control role in the asset or company, often relying on liquidity, diversification, and low-cost market exposure instead of direct influence.

## Current Synthesis
Wilson does not dismiss passive investing; he argues that its best fit is liquid, diversified exposure where the investor can exit when the thesis deteriorates. Wulc's Zhang Xiaoyu course summaries add the individual-investor version: ordinary investors usually lack professional access and control, but they also avoid institutional pressure to look busy, chase bubbles, or justify short-term underperformance. Passive-first investing then becomes a practical principle rather than an ideology: use diversification and low-cost exposure, avoid pretending to have active control, inspect fees and local market context, make decisions around knowable constraints rather than constant market forecasts, and use rebalancing or broad index exposure to keep the strategy systematic.

## Key Claims
- Passive investing is defined by limited investor involvement after capital is committed.
- Liquid markets are better suited to passive investing because exit can replace control.
- Passive investing can make money, especially over long periods.
- Beating the market is difficult when investing passively.
- Passive investing pairs more naturally with diversification than with concentrated control, helping ordinary investors avoid asset-preference bias, market-timing pressure, and professional-style overactivity.
- Passive-first implementation still needs cost, valuation, and market-context discipline.
- Passive products can implement model allocations, but factor, industry, and strategy indexes are less passive in exposure choice than broad market-cap-weighted funds.

## Evidence
- Limited involvement: [[active-vs-passive-investing-avc]] lists ordinary public-stock purchases, REIT purchases, treasury bills, and non-led angel checks as passive examples.
- Liquidity fit: [[active-vs-passive-investing-avc]] says passive investors should prefer liquid markets so they can get out when things are not going well.
- Return qualification: [[active-vs-passive-investing-avc]] says passive investing can still make good money, particularly over a long period.
- Market-beating difficulty: [[active-vs-passive-investing-avc]] says it is hard to beat the market with passive investing.
- Diversification fit: [[active-vs-passive-investing-avc]] concludes that investors should be passive and diversified or active and concentrated.
- Individual-investor fit: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-1-shi-chang-gui-lu]] argues that ordinary investors should avoid obvious mistakes, diversify across asset classes and geography, and focus on knowable implementation details such as costs and allocation.
- Passive-first principle: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-2-tou-zi-gong-ju-yu-zi-wo-ju-xian]] lists "passive first" alongside diversification, lower costs, patience, and not investing in what one does not understand.
- Discipline qualification: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-2-tou-zi-gong-ju-yu-zi-wo-ju-xian]] cautions that index-fund advice can vary by market context and that fees and purchase price still matter.
- Portfolio implementation: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-3-tou-zi-zu-he-gou-jian]] recommends products from large fund companies that are low cost and passively track markets rather than depend on active fund-manager intervention.
- Exposure-choice qualification: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-3-tou-zi-zu-he-gou-jian]] distinguishes broad indexes from industry and strategy indexes, which can supplement a core but require understanding of the exposure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are not a full theory of factor investing, public-market efficiency, tax-aware portfolio construction, or retirement planning. Wilson's claim is narrower: passive capital without active work should be paired with liquidity and diversification. Wulc's sources add practical personal-investor advice and example portfolio weights, but still caution against turning passive index investing into a mechanical rule across markets, industries, factors, or valuation environments.

## What Changed
- Added passive-first implementation as one of Zhang Xiaoyu's ordinary-investor principles.
- Qualified passive implementation with cost, valuation, and market-context discipline.
- Added low-cost passive product selection and the distinction between broad passive exposure and narrower strategy or industry indexes.
