## Definition
[[CodingBootcampEducation]] is accelerated vocational programming instruction intended to turn concentrated study, guided practice, projects, and career support into employable software-development capability.

## Current Synthesis
The Hack Reactor retrospective makes bootcamp value a multi-part question rather than a placement-rate slogan. A program can cover many technologies quickly while still offering limited instructor contact, shallow time per topic, inexperienced support, or too little opportunity to build durable debugging and design judgment. Projects and hiring preparation may be valuable, but prospective students need to distinguish those services from expert-led technical instruction when comparing tuition with self-study or lower-cost options.

Employment percentages are interpretable only with the denominator, exclusions, search window, job definition, prior student qualifications, and comparison baseline. The source alleges that Hack Reactor lengthened its placement window while serving students who were already strong candidates, but it provides neither audited methodology nor counterfactual evidence. Its strongest contribution is therefore an evaluation framework—teaching intensity, mentor expertise, curriculum breadth, project quality, career support, total cost, and transparent outcomes—not a general verdict on all bootcamps.

## Key Claims
- Curriculum breadth is not the same as instructional depth when many topics are compressed into short sprints.
- Mentor experience matters because beginners need feedback on debugging, design, and professional practice, not only assignment completion.
- Team projects and career coaching should be valued separately from direct technical teaching.
- A single-language curriculum can teach a coherent stack, but may narrow exposure to other paradigms and employer expectations.
- Placement claims require transparent denominators, time windows, job definitions, exclusions, and student-entry characteristics.
- Tuition value depends on the program's incremental benefit over self-study, prior qualifications, and cheaper educational paths.

## Evidence
- Compressed instruction: [[confessions-of-a-20k-bootcamp-graduate-hack-reactor]] describes hour-long lectures followed by paired two-day sprints and solution videos across a broad JavaScript curriculum.
- Support expertise: [[confessions-of-a-20k-bootcamp-graduate-hack-reactor]] says students often queued for help from graduates of the immediately preceding cohort.
- Service mix: [[confessions-of-a-20k-bootcamp-graduate-hack-reactor]] divides the program into about six technical weeks and six weeks dominated by thesis projects, resumes, hiring lectures, and career search.
- Breadth concern: [[confessions-of-a-20k-bootcamp-graduate-hack-reactor]] argues that JavaScript alone left graduates less competitive than exposure to another language or paradigm would have.
- Outcome interpretation: [[confessions-of-a-20k-bootcamp-graduate-hack-reactor]] challenges a longer placement window and the effect of selecting applicants who already held strong qualifications.
- Cost comparison: [[confessions-of-a-20k-bootcamp-graduate-hack-reactor]] contrasts reported US$19,780 tuition with free self-study and cheaper bootcamps.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is currently grounded in one dissatisfied graduate's pseudonymous 2016 account. The source does not compare cohorts, measure learning gains, audit employment data, or observe students who chose self-study instead. Intensive pacing, peer programming, projects, alumni services, and career structure may benefit some learners even when classroom hours are limited. A program's value can also differ by prior experience, available time, need for accountability, local labor demand, and access to mentors or professional networks.

## What Changed
- Established a bootcamp-evaluation framework spanning instruction, support, curriculum, projects, career services, cost, selection, and outcome transparency.
- Separated the historical Hack Reactor critique from claims about bootcamps in general.
