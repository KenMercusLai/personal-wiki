## Definition
[[WorkplaceSexismInTech]] is the pattern of gendered harm, exclusion, credibility denial, harassment, and role stereotyping that makes technical work harder or less safe for women and other marginalized people.

## Current Synthesis
The source shows sexism operating at several levels. In research, an advisor tied mentorship to private dinners, escalated unwanted attention, and made the author feel unsafe, while institutional investigation still placed some blame on her behavior. In startup engineering, blame for outages, ignored suggestions, git-blame reversals, being the only woman engineer, and advice to pursue fashion instead of tech undermined her confidence. The essay's broader claim is that dramatic incidents sit on top of many smaller acts of underestimation and exclusion.

## Key Claims
- Harassment can convert a research environment from intellectually enabling to physically unsafe.
- Gatekeeping mentorship through romantic or sexual attention damages technical development.
- Credibility denial can appear through ignored suggestions, gendered blame, and mistaken assumptions about who wrote code.
- Gendered role stereotypes can push women away from engineering even when they have technical evidence of competence.
- Institutional responses can improve policy while still reproducing victim-blaming narratives.
- The cumulative effect can erode the joy and confidence that drew someone to computing.

## Evidence
- Research harassment: [[a-career-retrospective-10-years-working-in-tech-sailor-mercury-medium]] says the advisor refused advice outside private dinners, repeatedly asked the author out, followed her, and tried to kiss her.
- Mentorship loss: [[a-career-retrospective-10-years-working-in-tech-sailor-mercury-medium]] says she completed the project without real advice because she refused unsafe private conditions.
- Startup credibility denial: [[a-career-retrospective-10-years-working-in-tech-sailor-mercury-medium]] describes being blamed for outages, having suggestions accepted only after men repeated them, and finding criticized changes had been made by her manager.
- Stereotyping: [[a-career-retrospective-10-years-working-in-tech-sailor-mercury-medium]] says founders told her to leave tech for fashion at an exit interview.
- Cumulative cost: [[a-career-retrospective-10-years-working-in-tech-sailor-mercury-medium]] says repeated sexism made the magic of computers disappear for her.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This page is grounded in one autobiographical source. It should not be treated as a statistical account of the tech industry, though the source explicitly frames the examples as part of broader patterns faced by marginalized people in computing.
