## Definition
[[AIEraCreativeStandards]] are the quality expectations creators need when AI makes acceptable average output cheap, abundant, and easy to imitate.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that AI's main empowerment is also a competitive filter. Coding assistants and image generators lower the cost of making apps, images, and writing-like artifacts, but this means 60-point work becomes nearly free. In that environment, creators cannot rely on novelty, low price, or "it works" alone for long. They need strong inputs, the ability to recognize excellence, and a habit of noticing product flaws or unmet needs that can become the next creative starting point.

## Key Claims
- AI lowers the skill threshold for entering creative and technical fields.
- Cheap average output makes mediocrity less defensible as a strategy.
- High standards become a survival line, not just a competitive advantage.
- Quality input is necessary because people cannot make good work if they cannot perceive good work.
- Studying excellent examples trains creators to notice market pain and weak products.

## Evidence
- Threshold drop: [[blog-limboy-make-good-new-things]] says AI coding assistants let ordinary people build credible products after learning the tools.
- Expanded media: [[blog-limboy-make-good-new-things]] also cites AI image tools as making visual creation more reachable.
- Average flood: [[blog-limboy-make-good-new-things]] argues that AI makes 60-point acceptable products nearly zero-cost, flooding the world with mediocre output.
- Survival line: [[blog-limboy-make-good-new-things]] says the winners after the threshold falls are products that are not merely usable but genuinely good.
- Input quality: [[blog-limboy-make-good-new-things]] argues that creators must study excellent inputs because seeing what is good precedes making what is good.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source assumes that markets or audiences are saturated enough for quality to dominate. Some domains still reward speed, distribution, regulatory access, price, network effects, or timing more than craft quality. "High standards" can also become perfectionism if it prevents shipping, testing, or learning from real users.
