## Definition
[[AgileFluencyModel]] is the Shore-Larsen model Fowler cites for describing different levels of team skill in applying Agile approaches.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses the Agile Fluency Model to clarify that agile adoption is not a simple yes-or-no state. A team can gain some value from basic project-management practices that focus attention on customer needs, but that does not automatically unlock the productivity and reliability associated with deeper agile practice.

The model's role in this wiki is therefore diagnostic: it separates surface process adoption from capability growth. Fowler uses it to argue that technical skill is necessary for higher agile fluency.

## Key Claims
- Agile skill develops in distinguishable levels rather than appearing all at once.
- Basic Scrum-style project management can create some customer-focus value.
- Higher productivity and reliability require technical skills beyond basic process adoption.
- The model supports Fowler's critique of shallow mainstream "agile."
- The model is connected to [[JamesShore]] and [[DianaLarsen]] as co-creators.

## Evidence
- Creation: [[blog-martin-fowler-foreword-to-the-art-of-agile-development]] says Shore and Larsen created the Agile Fluency Model from their experiences.
- Skill levels: [[blog-martin-fowler-foreword-to-the-art-of-agile-development]] says the model captures different ways people develop skill with Agile approaches.
- Basic adoption limit: [[blog-martin-fowler-foreword-to-the-art-of-agile-development]] says a basic Scrum approach can provide value but lacks technical skills needed for high productivity and reliability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The current source describes the model only at a high level. It does not enumerate the model's full zones, assessment method, or later revisions.
