## Definition
[[BlueOceanStrategy]] is a market-creation strategy that seeks uncontested demand by rethinking value delivery so differentiation and lower cost can reinforce each other.

## Current Synthesis
The source contrasts red oceans, where mature markets are crowded and commoditized, with blue oceans, where companies create new demand in less contested space. Unlike traditional differentiation, which often adds cost and charges a premium, blue ocean strategy aims for value innovation: removing or reducing some costly category assumptions while raising or creating other sources of customer value. The approach shares the differentiation article's customer-centered warning: copying competitors keeps a firm in the red ocean, while rethinking what customers actually value can open new demand.

## Key Claims
- Red oceans are crowded markets where direct competition pushes firms toward commoditization and lower margins.
- Blue oceans seek new or underdeveloped demand rather than fighting only for existing demand.
- Value innovation tries to combine differentiation with lower cost by changing what the market offers.
- Blue oceans are not permanent; successful spaces eventually attract competitors and become redder.
- The strategy requires attention to customer value rather than competitor imitation.

## Evidence
- Red-ocean pressure: [[cha-yi-hua-zhan-lue-zhi-nan-what-why-yu-how-36kr]] describes mature markets as commoditized, competitive, and margin-compressing.
- New demand: [[cha-yi-hua-zhan-lue-zhi-nan-what-why-yu-how-36kr]] presents blue oceans as undeveloped spaces where demand must be created.
- Value innovation: [[cha-yi-hua-zhan-lue-zhi-nan-what-why-yu-how-36kr]] explains that rethinking what consumers value can allow difference and low cost together.
- Temporality: [[cha-yi-hua-zhan-lue-zhi-nan-what-why-yu-how-36kr]] notes that every blue ocean can become a red ocean as competitors enter.
- Customer focus: [[cha-yi-hua-zhan-lue-zhi-nan-what-why-yu-how-36kr]] says the point is to stop obsessing over competitors and focus on customer needs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source treats blue ocean strategy as related to but distinct from traditional differentiation. It does not provide a detailed method for discovering blue oceans or evidence that any given company can create demand cheaply enough to justify the risk.
