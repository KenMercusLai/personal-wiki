## Definition
[[ProductShippingCredibility]] is the trust a company earns when users, developers, investors, and observers can see it consistently launch meaningful product improvements and explain why they matter.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that shipping is not merely internal execution. For a stalled or scrutinized platform such as [[Twitter]], visible launches become evidence that the company can learn, iterate, and solve harder problems. Small tests and private improvements may be real, but if enthusiastic users cannot perceive meaningful progress, then the company loses the credibility needed for abuse response, metric reframing, user tooling, and developer-platform repair.

## Key Claims
- Public product cadence can determine whether outsiders believe a platform can recover.
- Feature launches need user-facing explanation, not only silent experimentation.
- Perception matters alongside internal metrics when engaged users feel neglected.
- Shipping credibility is upstream of other turnaround claims because it proves the organization can act.

## Evidence
- Turnaround prerequisite: [[a-billion-dollar-gift-for-twitter-startup-grind-medium]] says everything depends on Twitter showing it can ship consistently.
- Explanation requirement: [[a-billion-dollar-gift-for-twitter-startup-grind-medium]] says Twitter must launch features, tell users what they are, and explain why they were made.
- Perception gap: [[a-billion-dollar-gift-for-twitter-startup-grind-medium]] argues that outside users could not tell whether Twitter had meaningful improvements in progress.
- Comparative pressure: [[a-billion-dollar-gift-for-twitter-startup-grind-medium]] contrasts Twitter's perceived stagnation with Instagram, Facebook, and Google appearing to ship often.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Visible shipping can become theater if launches do not solve real problems, and some infrastructure, safety, or experimentation work may be valuable before it is visible. The source is an outside critique of perception and does not audit Twitter's internal roadmap or actual experiment velocity.
