## Definition
[[EngineeringOrganizationEvolution]] is the stage-sensitive redesign of engineering hiring, specialization, management, and team boundaries as a startup moves from a small generalist group toward platform and product organizations.

## Current Synthesis
[[MikeKrieger]]'s [[Instagram]] retrospective describes a sequence rather than one ideal structure. A small team first needs curious generalists who cross disciplines, stay tied to a few company goals, and distinguish necessary problem solving from technical detours. Growth then creates demands for specialist depth, common standards, technical leadership, one-on-ones, recruiting ownership, and dedicated management. Platform teams can turn that specialization into quality standards and durable engineering identity. When product managers must negotiate separately with several functional managers and everyone makes global tradeoffs from local information, product teams become a better boundary for shared goals and execution.

The transitions preserve useful residue from earlier stages. Broad end-to-end awareness remains leverage after specialization, flatness does not excuse absent manager behavior, and product teams still need platform quality and expertise. Diversity is also path-dependent organizational infrastructure: postponing representation makes later recruiting harder because candidates evaluate the team that exists, not only its stated intent.

## Key Claims
- Early-stage fit favors pragmatic generalists who learn across boundaries and allocate scarce engineering time toward product goals.
- Specialist hiring becomes warranted when platform limits, market-specific performance, or codebase scale exceeds generalist capacity.
- Common standards and platform identity can improve onboarding, quality, and preparation for later scale without erasing end-to-end thinking.
- One-on-ones and other manager behavior should begin before formal hierarchy, while founder overload indicates missing management work rather than proof that it is unnecessary.
- Internal and external managers require different sourcing, but both need explicit role interest, credibility, support, and a candid relationship with the team and founder.
- Product teams become valuable when functional allocation creates repeated staffing negotiation, adversarial manager relationships, and local rather than global prioritization.
- Early representation affects later hiring feasibility, making inclusion a compounding organizational choice rather than a promise that can be postponed without cost.

## Evidence
- Generalist stage: [[instagram-co-founder-mike-krieger-on-engineering-team-growth-first-round-review]] describes cross-disciplinary task completion, stopping rules for yak shaving, practical work trials, and a rolling goal-themed task document.
- Specialist and platform stage: [[instagram-co-founder-mike-krieger-on-engineering-team-growth-first-round-review]] ties specialist need to platform invention, emerging-market performance, and codebase scale, then treats platform teams as a place to establish standards and quality culture.
- Management stage: [[instagram-co-founder-mike-krieger-on-engineering-team-growth-first-round-review]] says delayed one-on-ones exposed hidden concerns and that dedicated managers revealed recruiting, planning, and future-oriented work that had gone undone.
- Product-team stage: [[instagram-co-founder-mike-krieger-on-engineering-team-growth-first-round-review]] describes cross-platform staffing as turning managers into resource allocators and reports that Instagram's move near 150 engineers occurred too late.
- Inclusion path: [[instagram-co-founder-mike-krieger-on-engineering-team-growth-first-round-review]] says an established all-male team raised the barrier to later hiring, while visible participation by women improved subsequent recruiting.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The model comes from one leader's retrospective inside an unusually successful consumer company. Facebook's recruiting pipeline, coaching program, experienced managers, brand, capital, and scale limit transfer to independent startups. The source offers no counterfactual organization, employee sample, causal metric, or evidence that the sequence must be linear. Platform teams may remain appropriate for shared infrastructure, product teams can duplicate expertise, generalists can become overloaded, and management layers can add delay. The stated switch near 150 engineers is a late-case observation, not a universal threshold.
