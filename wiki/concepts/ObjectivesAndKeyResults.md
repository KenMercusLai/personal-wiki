## Definition
[[ObjectivesAndKeyResults]] is a goal-setting framework that pairs a qualitative direction, the objective, with measurable indicators of progress, the key results, so an organization can coordinate movement toward a desired change.

## Current Synthesis
The source distinguishes OKRs from KPIs by purpose rather than format. KPIs monitor whether existing operations remain healthy; OKRs identify where the organization wants to go next and make progress toward that direction observable. In this account, ambitious OKRs may be intentionally difficult, but partial attainment is meaningful only when the system supports learning rather than treating every shortfall as failed performance.

The distinction is also an incentive boundary. When ambitious OKR completion directly controls ratings or bonuses, employees can rationally protect themselves by proposing safer objectives and optimizing reported completion. The organization then loses the exploratory information the framework was meant to create. Conversely, applying a relaxed aspiration threshold to minimum operational KPIs weakens standards that exist precisely as floors. A credible system therefore separates directional aspiration from operating health and makes the evaluation consequences explicit.

## Key Claims
- Objectives express a desired direction while key results make progress toward it measurable.
- KPIs and OKRs can use similar-looking metrics while serving different decision purposes: operational health versus directional change.
- Ambitious goals require room for partial attainment and honest learning if employees are expected to take uncertainty seriously.
- Directly tying stretch-goal completion to compensation or ratings encourages target lowering, gaming, and self-protection.
- Minimum operating standards should not inherit a stretch-goal convention that treats substantial non-completion as success.
- Trust depends on employees knowing whether a number is an aspiration, a diagnostic, or an evaluated commitment.

## Evidence
- Direction versus health: [[jian-dan-yi-dong-de-you-du-zhi-chang-lian-cheng-shu-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] distinguishes OKRs that orient future change from KPIs that monitor established business operation.
- Incentive distortion: [[jian-dan-yi-dong-de-you-du-zhi-chang-lian-cheng-shu-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] argues that performance-linked OKRs reward safe targets over ambitious ones.
- Category boundary: [[jian-dan-yi-dong-de-you-du-zhi-chang-lian-cheng-shu-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] uses service availability to show why a minimum KPI cannot simply adopt a partial-completion norm.
- Trust condition: [[jian-dan-yi-dong-de-you-du-zhi-chang-lian-cheng-shu-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] says mixed standards leave workers guessing which rule management will apply.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one uncited practitioner essay, not a comparative study of OKR and KPI systems. Its “70 percent” language is a popular stretch-goal heuristic rather than a universal property of every OKR implementation, and organizations can combine goals, operating measures, and evaluation in many ways. Separation alone does not prevent gaming: poorly chosen key results can still displace unmeasured value, hide dependencies, or reward local optimization. Some commitments legitimately affect evaluation, but the intended risk, decision use, ownership, and consequences should be stated rather than inferred from a label.

## What Changed
- Established the distinction between directional change goals and operating-health indicators.
- Added performance linkage as a mechanism that can turn ambitious goal-setting into defensive target selection.
- Added explicit metric purpose and consequence as conditions for organizational trust.
