## Definition
[[InternalCoDesign]] is a product-research practice where teammates with relevant product or customer context actively participate in redesign exercises rather than only reacting to finished concepts.

## Current Synthesis
The Crashlytics source presents internal co-design as a way to convert long-running team knowledge into redesign evidence. Instead of showing concepts and asking for feedback, the designer ran individual paper-cutout sessions where teammates rearranged dashboard pieces and explained their reasoning aloud. The individual format reduced group bias, and the repeated themes were actionable: everyone focused on filters and information hierarchy, while developer relations added context about features users struggled to use or find.

## Key Claims
- Internal teammates can hold valuable product and customer context, especially on long-lived products.
- Co-design sessions can be more generative than asking teammates to critique finished designs.
- Independent sessions reduce bias and make repeated pain themes easier to detect.
- Physical cutouts can help digital product teams reason concretely about dashboard structure.
- Internal evidence is strongest when combined with user, support, developer-relations, and research inputs.
- Sharing findings in an ongoing deck and weekly check-ins turns co-design into team alignment.

## Evidence
- Motivation: [[advocating-for-a-complete-product-redesign-google-design-medium]] says the designer wanted a more collaborative process than concept sharing and feedback.
- Method: [[advocating-for-a-complete-product-redesign-google-design-medium]] describes printing and cutting out the dashboard, then asking teammates to rearrange pieces and think aloud.
- Bias control: [[advocating-for-a-complete-product-redesign-google-design-medium]] says individual sessions made it easier to see each person's pain points without influence from others.
- Repeated themes: [[advocating-for-a-complete-product-redesign-google-design-medium]] says every participant focused on filters and information hierarchy.
- Alignment artifacts: [[advocating-for-a-complete-product-redesign-google-design-medium]] describes an ongoing redesign deck and weekly design check-ins.
- Image evidence: [[advocating-for-a-complete-product-redesign-google-design-medium]] includes inspected photos of paper-cutout sessions and redesigned dashboards with post-it additions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Internal co-design can overrepresent team assumptions if it is disconnected from actual users. In this source, that risk is mitigated but not eliminated by direct user conversations, developer-relations evidence, support anecdotes, and user researchers.
