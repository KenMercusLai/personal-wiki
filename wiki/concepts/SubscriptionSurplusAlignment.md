## Definition
[[SubscriptionSurplusAlignment]] is the pricing pattern in which recurring payments let producer revenue rise as user value grows, reducing the mismatch created by a single upfront packaged-software price.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses economic-surplus reasoning and two diagrams to compare packaged software with subscriptions. In the packaged-software diagram, price is flat while consumer value rises with time and expertise, leaving late-stage heavy users with large consumer surplus and early casual users facing a painful upfront price. In the subscription diagram, payments rise stepwise over time while consumer value still grows, giving the producer more surplus during the product relationship and lowering the starting commitment for users who are not yet sure the app is worth it.

## Key Claims
- Packaged software captures value poorly when users differ sharply in willingness to pay and intensity of use.
- Heavy professional users can receive career-scale value after paying only a one-time license.
- Beginners or casual users may be blocked by upfront price before they discover whether the product is useful.
- Subscriptions can convert a static price into an ongoing relationship that tracks user value more closely.
- Better surplus alignment also changes producer incentives because reducing churn requires maintenance and improvement.

## Evidence
- Packaged-software diagram: [[adobes-subscription-model-why-platform-owners-should-care-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] shows flat producer revenue against rising consumer value, with consumer surplus expanding later in time.
- Subscription diagram: [[adobes-subscription-model-why-platform-owners-should-care-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] shows a stepwise payment path where producer surplus accumulates as consumer value grows.
- User examples: [[adobes-subscription-model-why-platform-owners-should-care-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] uses casual, professional, and student Photoshop users to illustrate different surplus outcomes.
- Maintenance incentive: [[adobes-subscription-model-why-platform-owners-should-care-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] says Adobe is incentivized to maintain the app to reduce churn.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's diagrams are conceptual rather than measured revenue or utility curves. The argument also depends on the subscription being priced and maintained well; subscriptions can still overcharge light users or frustrate customers who prefer ownership.
