## Definition
[[EmergentLayerTheory]] is [[AlexDanco]]'s interpretive model for growth that occurs when a scalable abstraction turns a constrained resource from scarce to abundant, enables customers to reach an underserved higher-order job directly, and shifts advantage to a newly scarce layer.

## Current Synthesis
The model joins two transitions. On the supply side, businesses initially optimize and monetize friction around a scarce resource; an abstraction later makes that resource scalable and abundant, weakening the old performance measures and profit points. On the demand side, competition can overserve customers at the immediate job level with features, saturation, or expense while constraints prevent them from reaching the higher-order purpose for which they use the product. When the abstraction removes that block, a new entrant can serve the higher-order job directly and experience unusually steep growth.

The cycle does not end in abundance. A new scarce resource emerges around the new layer—such as network effects, a standard, routing and liquidity, intent data, an application ecosystem, or integrated infrastructure—and power accrues to organizations that handle both the now-abundant input and the new bottleneck. The article uses WhatsApp, Tesla, Uber, Medium, Intel, Google, the iPhone, and Amazon as illustrations. Amazon is the most systematic case: capabilities built for its own operation become primitives and external services, then can support marketplaces and platforms. This is a useful hypothesis-generating map, but the source does not operationalize the layer boundaries, compare failures, or test the theory prospectively.

## Key Claims
- Scarce resources create constraints, performance measures, and points of friction from which incumbents earn profit.
- Competition at the constrained layer can overserve an immediate job while a higher-order customer purpose remains underserved.
- Scalable abstraction can remove the constraint and let a product serve the higher-order job directly.
- Incumbent incentives may preserve the old profit pool or push firms upmarket while entrants reorganize value around the new layer.
- Abundance relocates rather than ends scarcity, creating a new bottleneck that can become a standard, network, ecosystem, data advantage, or platform moat.
- The transition may be accidental or deliberate, and the framework is diagnostic rather than deterministic.

## Evidence
- Full cycle: [[emergent-layers-chapter-3-explosive-growth-the-startup-medium]] explicitly moves from scarce input, through over-service and abstraction, to higher-order service and a new scarce resource.
- Messaging and mobility: [[emergent-layers-chapter-3-explosive-growth-the-startup-medium]] uses WhatsApp, Uber, and the iPhone to connect mobile data, trust, and mobility abstractions with new network, liquidity, and ecosystem control points.
- Computation and information: [[emergent-layers-chapter-3-explosive-growth-the-startup-medium]] presents programmable chips and PageRank as abstractions that widen access while making architecture standards and search intent newly strategic.
- Social and discovery jobs: [[emergent-layers-chapter-3-explosive-growth-the-startup-medium]] interprets Tesla through identity signaling and Medium through writer-reader discovery rather than only product specifications.
- Deliberate capability conversion: [[emergent-layers-chapter-3-explosive-growth-the-startup-medium]] describes Amazon turning books, publishing, fulfillment, cloud infrastructure, Prime, media, and voice interfaces into successive service and platform layers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is a single 2016 strategy essay built from selected successful companies. It offers no failed or negative cases, comparison group, measurable definition of “higher-order,” method for locating levels, or prospective prediction whose failure would falsify the model. Its examples may also be explained by capital, regulation, timing, subsidies, distribution, execution, or ordinary network effects. Steep adoption does not establish sustainable unit economics, social benefit, permanent monopoly, or causal proof that one abstraction produced the outcome. Company and competitive claims are historical snapshots, and the source's tiny retained diagram cannot independently substantiate text that is not legible at its supplied resolution.
