## Definition
[[EmergentLayerTheory]] is [[AlexDanco]]'s interpretive model in which technology or scalable abstraction makes a constrained resource abundant, unlocks a higher-order customer purpose, weakens value tied to the old friction, and shifts advantage to a newly scarce layer.

## Current Synthesis
The later framework joins supply and demand. Businesses initially optimize and monetize a scarce constraint; competition may overserve the immediate job while the constraint blocks a higher-order [[JobsToBeDone|job]]. An abstraction removes that block, but abundance does not end scarcity: value moves to a new network, standard, ecosystem, data asset, marketplace, presence layer, or integrated capability.

The media essay supplies a concrete application and a useful distinction between access and capture. The web made content and distribution abundant, allowing pull and push to become strategic. Front cameras and ephemerality then widened access to temporary self-expression, but Danco argues that products exploiting that access did not necessarily own the next bottleneck of situated presence. This strengthens the theory's relocation mechanism while remaining a retrospective, non-falsified interpretation rather than a prediction engine.

## Key Claims
- Scarce resources create constraints, performance measures, and profit pools.
- Competition can overserve an immediate job while a higher-order purpose remains blocked.
- Scalable abstraction or enabling technology can remove the constraint and reorganize demand.
- Incumbents may preserve old profit pools while entrants align around the newly accessible job.
- Abundance relocates rather than eliminates scarcity, creating a new strategic bottleneck.
- Access to an old scarce resource does not guarantee ownership of the next scarce layer.
- The framework is diagnostic and retrospective rather than deterministic.

## Evidence
- Full model: [[emergent-layers-chapter-3-explosive-growth-the-startup-medium]] connects scarce inputs, overserved jobs, abstraction, higher-order service, and new bottlenecks.
- Company cases: [[emergent-layers-chapter-3-explosive-growth-the-startup-medium]] uses WhatsApp, Tesla, Uber, Medium, Intel, Google, iPhone, and Amazon as illustrations.
- Media sequence: [[from-pull-and-push-to-here-and-now-the-grand-bargain-of-facebook-and-the-feed-is-unraveling-what-comes-next]] moves from content and distribution through pull and push to expressive variance and presence.
- Access-versus-capture boundary: [[from-pull-and-push-to-here-and-now-the-grand-bargain-of-facebook-and-the-feed-is-unraveling-what-comes-next]] says camera and ephemeral products make expression more abundant without necessarily owning the “here and now” layer.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence consists of two 2016 strategy essays built from selected visible companies and emerging products. The framework has no operational layer boundaries, comparison group, failed cases, prospective falsification test, or method for separating its mechanism from capital, regulation, subsidies, distribution, execution, timing, and ordinary network effects. “Higher-order,” “scarce,” and “presence” can become flexible retrospective labels. Rapid adoption does not establish durable economics, welfare gains, or control of the next bottleneck.

## What Changed
- Added the earlier media application as evidence for the scarcity-relocation mechanism.
- Added an explicit distinction between expanding access and capturing the next strategic layer.
- Extended the examples from functional jobs and platforms to expression, time, place, and participation.
- Preserved the framework's non-deterministic and retrospective boundary.
