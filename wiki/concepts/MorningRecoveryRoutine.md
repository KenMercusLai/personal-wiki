## Definition
[[MorningRecoveryRoutine]] is a start-of-day practice that uses sleep, technology boundaries, reflection, movement, meditation, and simple repeated actions to center attention before work.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats a morning routine as a recovery-to-work bridge rather than a hustle ritual. Huffington's version begins with enough sleep to wake without an alarm, avoids electronic devices, includes gratitude or intention-setting, uses yoga or cycling and meditation when time allows, and then moves through showering, dressing, coffee, and work. The routine is flexible: travel or schedule pressure can shorten or skip parts, but the underlying purpose is to center attention and set intentions.

## Key Claims
- A recovery-oriented morning begins before waking, with sufficient sleep.
- Avoiding electronic devices helps keep the morning from being captured by outside demands.
- Gratitude and intention-setting turn routine into attention direction.
- Movement and meditation are used to regulate energy and attention before work.
- Flexibility matters because travel and schedule pressure can make the full routine impossible.

## Evidence
- Sleep foundation: [[arianna-huffington-on-sleep-mornings-and-work-the-early-hour]] says Huffington usually gets eight hours and wakes naturally.
- Device boundary: [[arianna-huffington-on-sleep-mornings-and-work-the-early-hour]] lists no electronic devices as part of her morning routine.
- Reflection and intention: [[arianna-huffington-on-sleep-mornings-and-work-the-early-hour]] includes gratitude and intentions.
- Movement and meditation: [[arianna-huffington-on-sleep-mornings-and-work-the-early-hour]] includes yoga or an exercise bike and meditation, with shorter sessions or skipped exercise when travel or schedule demands interfere.
- Work transition: [[arianna-huffington-on-sleep-mornings-and-work-the-early-hour]] ends the routine with shower, dressing, coffee, and going into the day.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The routine is a personal practice from one interview subject, not an evaluated protocol. It assumes enough schedule control, privacy, and health to protect morning time; the source itself acknowledges that travel and schedule pressure can shrink the routine.
