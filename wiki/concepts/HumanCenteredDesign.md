## Definition
[[HumanCenteredDesign]] is an iterative design practice that investigates people's needs, contexts, interactions, and possible outcomes, then uses those observations to shape and revise products, services, and systems.

## Current Synthesis
Applied to AI, human-centered design widens the design surface beyond an interface. Choices about which data exists, how it is collected, what an algorithm optimizes, how its outputs enter a workflow, and what happens when it is wrong all affect people. The article's strongest contribution is therefore not a claim that designers can purify AI, but that algorithmic systems embody design decisions and should be investigated as sociotechnical systems before and after deployment.

Lilly's remembrance of Steve Jobs adds a cultural and economic layer. Human-centered design can make technology feel desirable, enable people to create valuable work of their own, and become a commercial advantage rather than an aesthetic afterthought. That achievement does not make an organization human-centered in every respect: Lilly's account separates humane products from leadership behavior he describes as dehumanizing and personally destructive.

The method remains limited by who participates, who controls the objective, which harms can be observed in advance, and whether an organization can act on what it learns. Systemic inequality cannot be reduced to individual user preference, and empathic research cannot substitute for domain expertise, statistical validation, legal safeguards, institutional change, or power-sharing.

## Key Claims
- Algorithms and data pipelines are designed systems even when their choices are expressed mathematically or implemented by engineers.
- Human inquiry should influence data collection, objectives, interaction design, failure analysis, and later iteration.
- Exploring possible negative outcomes before deployment can reveal harms that narrow performance testing misses.
- Designers and data scientists need shared ownership because neither discipline alone covers the full sociotechnical system.
- Human-centered design can improve systems without guaranteeing fairness or solving structural inequality.
- Human-centered products can create cultural and economic value without proving that the organization or its leadership treats employees humanely.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The IDEO source is a leader-driven acquisition narrative and offers no comparative outcome data. User research can miss people outside the selected sample, convert structural conflict into usability questions, or optimize an institution's harmful objective more effectively. Bias mitigation also requires technical testing, representative data, domain expertise, governance, transparency, appeal, and sometimes a decision not to deploy. The article's confidence that the method improves AI “in the general case” is a forecast, not measured evidence. Lilly's source is a personal elegy, not product-performance or workplace-outcome research; it supports a distinction between humane product experience and humane management more strongly than any causal claim about design's commercial return.

## What Changed
- Added cultural desirability, creative enablement, and economic value to the concept's possible outcomes.
- Distinguished human-centered product design from humane employee treatment and organizational practice.
- Preserved the new claims as source-scoped judgments rather than measured causal effects.
