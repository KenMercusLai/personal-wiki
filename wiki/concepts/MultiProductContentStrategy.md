## Definition
[[MultiProductContentStrategy]] is a planning method that maps a company's purchasable products, target audiences, or use cases against buyer-funnel stages so each strategically important segment receives appropriate content coverage.

## Current Synthesis
The source frames multi-product content planning as a two-dimensional matrix. The first dimension follows the business model: use product columns when several products serve essentially the same team, as in [[Zendesk]], and audience columns when a similar offer serves materially different teams, as in [[Clearbit]]. The second dimension follows the buyer journey from awareness through consideration to purchase. Tagging the existing inventory against both dimensions exposes gaps, but the matrix is a diagnostic aid rather than a quota system: priorities can vary with demand, keyword difficulty, writing quality, product maturity, and the fact that posts may cross stages or audiences.

## Key Claims
- Content strategy should remain anchored to product use cases that connect editorial work to an offer customers can buy.
- The business model determines whether the primary segmentation unit should be a product, an audience, or a distinct use case.
- Each important segment needs content across awareness, consideration, and purchase rather than only one funnel stage.
- A repeatable segment-by-funnel matrix makes existing coverage and missing cells visible.
- Tagging an inventory by segment and funnel stage supports prioritization without requiring equal volume in every cell.
- Classification remains approximate because one asset may address several audiences or funnel stages.

## Evidence
- Offer anchor and template: [[content-marketing-strategy-for-multi-product-companies]] says the strategy should begin with payable use cases and repeat a full-funnel template for each target group.
- Product-based segmentation: [[content-marketing-strategy-for-multi-product-companies]] uses [[Zendesk]] to illustrate several support products marketed separately to a similar team.
- Audience-based segmentation: [[content-marketing-strategy-for-multi-product-companies]] uses [[Clearbit]] to illustrate one flexible data-enrichment offer adapted to sales, marketing, and developer audiences.
- Inventory audit: [[content-marketing-strategy-for-multi-product-companies]] recommends tagging posts in [[Airtable]] by product or audience and by funnel depth to reveal gaps.
- Allocation and classification limits: [[content-marketing-strategy-for-multi-product-companies]] says coverage need not be even and acknowledges that stage and audience boundaries can overlap.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework comes from a single 2018 practitioner article built around two illustrative company profiles. It supplies no conversion, revenue, production-cost, or comparative outcome evidence, and the supplied Markdown omits the diagrams to which the prose refers. Product and audience segmentation may also interact rather than form an either-or choice, while account-based buying, retention content, channel differences, search demand, and product dependencies can require additional dimensions.

## What Changed
- Created a business-model-sensitive matrix for organizing multi-product content by product or audience and funnel depth.
- Preserved uneven allocation and overlapping classification as explicit limits rather than treating the matrix as a publishing quota.
