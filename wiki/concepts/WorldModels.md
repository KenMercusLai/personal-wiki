## Definition
[[WorldModels]] are internal representations of entities, dynamics, constraints, and possible outcomes that let an agent predict or reason about how a world behaves beyond the surface statistics of one observation stream.

## Current Synthesis
The source proposes physically consistent multimodal world models as a bridge between linguistic pattern competence and scientific invention grounded in experience. The intended contrast is not simply text versus images: a useful model would integrate multiple modalities, preserve physical relationships, and support counterfactual or imagined interaction from which new explanatory hypotheses could emerge.

Within the supplied evidence, this remains a research direction rather than a demonstrated solution. The source gives no architecture, objective, embodiment requirement, physical-consistency test, or experiment showing that a world model produces Einstein-like abduction. World models may improve grounding and prediction while still failing to select a novel, simple, and scientifically productive axiom system.

## Key Claims
- Scientific reasoning may require representations of physical entities, relations, and dynamics beyond linguistic co-occurrence.
- Multimodality is useful only if a system integrates modalities into behaviorally and physically coherent structure.
- Prediction and counterfactual simulation could support hypothesis generation from sparse observations.
- Possessing a world model does not by itself establish novelty, explanatory quality, or abductive invention.

## Evidence
- Proposed bridge: [[llms-cant-jump]] names physically consistent multimodal world models as the route it considers most promising for scientific invention.
- Grounding motivation: [[llms-cant-jump]] attributes Einstein's equivalence-principle insight to thought experiments grounded in physical experience rather than words or symbols alone.
- Capability boundary: [[llms-cant-jump]] contrasts current pattern recognition and logical proof with the proposed experience-to-axiom jump.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers a programmatic proposal rather than an implemented system or comparative result. It does not distinguish learned simulators, latent predictive models, embodied agents, causal models, and explicit symbolic theories, all of which can be called world models. Nor does it show that linguistic models lack implicit physical structure, that multimodal grounding is necessary, or that physical consistency is sufficient for scientific creativity. Any stronger conclusion needs operational definitions and evaluations that separate memorization, recombination, deduction, hypothesis generation, and empirical success.

## What Changed
- Created a qualified concept for physically coherent internal models rather than treating multimodality alone as grounding.
- Separated the proposed mechanism from evidence that it actually produces scientific invention.
