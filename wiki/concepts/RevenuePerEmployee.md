## Definition
[[RevenuePerEmployee]] is a productivity ratio that divides company revenue by employee count; when revenue is private, it can also be inverted into a rough scale estimate by multiplying observed headcount by a calibrated revenue-per-employee benchmark.

## Current Synthesis
The available source proposes the ratio as a rapid [[CompetitiveIntelligence]] shortcut for private SaaS and web-services companies after they reach a few million dollars in ARR. Its baseline is $150,000 per employee for well-funded companies and $200,000 for modestly funded companies, with a wider $100,000-$300,000 range for unusually fast-growing, heavily funded, self-funded, or freemium businesses. The useful output is an order-of-magnitude range, not a precise revenue figure. Reliability depends on the headcount source, date alignment, business-model comparability, capital intensity, growth, outsourcing, and consistent revenue definitions; the article's selected examples and mature-tech charts do not constitute a systematic validation study.

## Key Claims
- Public headcount can provide a fast first estimate of a private company's revenue scale when better financial evidence is unavailable.
- Funding, growth stage, and monetization model materially change the appropriate multiplier.
- The ratio is most defensible as a range for companies above the very earliest revenue stage.
- ARR and GAAP revenue are not interchangeable and should not be compared without labeling the measurement basis and period.
- Cross-company benchmarks require comparable sectors, operating models, dates, and workforce boundaries.
- Selected examples can test plausibility but cannot establish an estimator's general accuracy or error distribution.

## Evidence
- Baseline multipliers: [[jason-lemkin-how-to-figure-out-your-competitors-revenues-in-about-70-seconds]] proposes $150,000 per employee for well-funded companies and $200,000 for modestly funded companies.
- Range adjustments: [[jason-lemkin-how-to-figure-out-your-competitors-revenues-in-about-70-seconds]] lowers the estimate toward $100,000 for heavily funded fast growth and raises it toward $300,000 for self-funded or mainly freemium businesses.
- Worked checks: [[jason-lemkin-how-to-figure-out-your-competitors-revenues-in-about-70-seconds]] compares estimates for Marketo, [[HubSpot]], and YouSendIt with cited historical figures.
- Benchmark dispersion: [[jason-lemkin-how-to-figure-out-your-competitors-revenues-in-about-70-seconds]] includes two charts showing a wide revenue-per-employee spread among selected mature technology companies.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a short practitioner heuristic from 2012, not a model fitted and evaluated on a declared dataset. Its worked examples are selected, its multiplier changes after observing company characteristics, and one comparison moves between GAAP revenue and ARR. LinkedIn headcount may omit employees, include stale profiles, or differ from full-time-equivalent and contractor boundaries; revenue and headcount must also be aligned to the same period. The charts compare mature companies across different business models and therefore demonstrate dispersion more clearly than they validate a private-SaaS multiplier.

## What Changed
- Created the concept as a qualified headcount-based estimator and operating-productivity ratio.
- Recorded the article's $100,000-$300,000 calibrated range and $150,000/$200,000 baselines.
- Added comparability, measurement-basis, selection, and validation limits.
