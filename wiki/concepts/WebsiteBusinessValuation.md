## Definition
[[WebsiteBusinessValuation]] is the estimation of a transferable online business's sale value from normalized earnings and a market multiple adjusted for growth, durability, concentration risk, operating burden, buyer upside, and defensibility.

## Current Synthesis
The source's historical baseline is simple: multiply rolling 12-month average net profit by a monthly multiple. The substantive work lies in judging both terms. Profit can require defensible add-backs, while a multiple expresses buyer confidence in how long earnings may persist and how readily the buyer can take control.

That confidence rises in the source with stable or growing history, transferable systems, low owner workload, diversified traffic and revenue, several viable products or suppliers, a proven audience that produces sales, and a hard-to-copy brand or commercial position. It falls with declining earnings, dependence on one platform or counterparty, manual owner-bound operations, and assets that a competitor can reproduce quickly. Expense reduction is therefore not automatically value creation: removing fulfillment, staff, or systems can improve reported profit while making the business less transferable.

Organic search illustrates the framework's central tension. Persistent search traffic may attract more buyers than traffic requiring daily ad management, but SEO does not automatically raise intrinsic value and exclusive Google dependence creates a critical failure point. Valuation should therefore distinguish channel appeal from channel resilience and distinguish raw traffic or followers from evidence that they produce durable profit.

## Key Claims
- Rolling normalized profit provides a valuation base, while the multiple reflects buyer judgments about future durability and effort.
- Earnings history and direction matter because a longer stable or growing record reduces uncertainty relative to a short or declining one.
- Valid add-backs can normalize discretionary expenses, but removing necessary operating capacity can reduce transferability even when profit rises.
- Concentration in one traffic source, platform, monetization method, supplier, or product increases downside risk.
- Systems, teams, third-party operations, and SOPs create value when they reduce dependence on the seller.
- High traffic, email, or social reach supports value only when it produces revenue, useful buyer upside, or resilient direct demand.
- Defensibility can justify a stronger multiple when brand, niche authority, unique products, community, or transferable terms make imitation harder.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The synthesis rests on one broker-authored 2018 article and its comment thread, not a disclosed transaction dataset or independent valuation study. The reported 20-50x healthy-business range, 12-18x declining-business range, 24-35x comment-thread norm, brokerage fees, and individual high-multiple examples are historical, partly promotional, and unsupported by distributional detail. Business model, scale, margins, financing, tax, legal rights, customer concentration, churn, working capital, intellectual property, geography, and market conditions can require materially different methods. Add-backs are contestable, a buyer's operating skill can alter perceived upside, and a high multiple can still produce a poor return. The source largely excludes agencies and does not establish that any recommended preparation step causally raises sale price.

## What Changed
- Created a valuation model that separates normalized earnings from the buyer-confidence factors expressed through the multiple.
- Preserved the tension between SEO's buyer appeal and the fragility of single-platform dependence.
- Distinguished cost cutting from transferability-enhancing operational design.
