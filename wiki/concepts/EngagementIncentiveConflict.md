## Definition
[[EngagementIncentiveConflict]] is the tension that arises when a product's revenue or valuation benefits from more frequent or longer use while user welfare may require less compulsive, more bounded engagement.

## Current Synthesis
Newport's Facebook case separates two kinds of remediation. A platform can change political-content rules, ranking, or debate practices while continuing to maximize overall usage through other material. Removing features that drive compulsive checking is harder when time spent supplies advertising inventory and engagement is a primary success metric. This does not prove harmful intent or make all engagement valueless: Facebook Groups can create real support and community, and a high-frequency product can be useful. The conflict instead warns that activity metrics and user benefit may diverge, so reform claims should be tested against changes to the revenue-linked behavior itself.

## Key Claims
- Engagement-funded products can face a direct economic cost when safety or wellbeing changes reduce time spent.
- A company may prefer remedies that change the content of engagement without reducing its total volume.
- Public debate about one tractable harm can coexist with avoidance of a deeper incentive conflict.
- Valuable product functions do not justify every mechanism used to increase frequency or duration.
- Engagement metrics require countermetrics for deliberate value, wellbeing, or avoided harm.

## Evidence
Revenue and usage:
- [[facebooks-desperate-smoke-screen-study-hacks-cal-newport]] argues that Facebook's advertising economics made average time spent commercially important and made reductions in compulsive use costly.

Selective remediation:
- [[facebooks-desperate-smoke-screen-study-hacks-cal-newport]] interprets Facebook's democracy blog series as compatible with continued engagement because other content can replace political outrage as an attention source.

Useful-use qualification:
- [[facebooks-desperate-smoke-screen-study-hacks-cal-newport]] includes a commenter describing valuable support and hobby Groups and Newport's reply that bounded use can preserve that value without continuous checking.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is grounded here in one external 2018 argument, not Facebook financial records, product experiments, clinical addiction measures, or causal evidence about particular features. Newport infers strategic distraction; the source does not establish internal intent. Some frequent use reflects communication, support, work, or genuine community value, and reducing aggregate minutes is not automatically a sufficient welfare measure. Political manipulation and compulsive engagement may also be mutually reinforcing rather than separable problems.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish engagement-preserving reform from changes that directly reduce revenue-linked compulsive use.
