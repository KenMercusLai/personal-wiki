## Definition
[[CreativeTechnicalCareer]] is a career pattern where technical work, artistic practice, craft, education, and product-building are integrated into one evolving path rather than separated into opposing identities.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents a creative technical career as something discovered through experiments, not planned as a clean ladder. [[AmyWibowo]] begins by feeling forced to choose between art and engineering, but her actual path joins machine-learning notes drawn like art, emotion-aware robotics, garment-pattern interfaces, web engineering, Lego murals, knitting-machine hacking, and illustrated CS zines. The pattern depends on environments that validate unusual combinations, but it is also vulnerable to workplace cultures that punish marginality, femininity, or nonstandard technical identity.

## Key Claims
- Creative technical work can be a coherent career thread even when it looks like a sequence of tangents.
- Art and computation can reinforce each other through physical artifacts, interfaces, illustrations, and education.
- Supportive peers and labs can legitimize unusual research questions and projects.
- Repeated experimentation can discover career fit better than a fixed ladder.
- Workplace exclusion can make creative recovery and self-directed work necessary, not merely optional.

## Evidence
- Early split pressure: [[a-career-retrospective-10-years-working-in-tech-sailor-mercury-medium]] says Wibowo felt forced to choose between art and engineering before MIT.
- Research combination: [[a-career-retrospective-10-years-working-in-tech-sailor-mercury-medium]] connects machine learning, robotics, emotion recognition, sewing geometry, mannequin interaction, and pattern flattening.
- Project artifacts: [[a-career-retrospective-10-years-working-in-tech-sailor-mercury-medium]] describes [[Legoizer]], knitting-machine hacking, and [[BubblesortZines]] as art-computing projects.
- Supportive environment: [[a-career-retrospective-10-years-working-in-tech-sailor-mercury-medium]] says surrounding oneself with people with unusual ideas can support creative projects.
- Harm and recovery: [[a-career-retrospective-10-years-working-in-tech-sailor-mercury-medium]] says sexism and burnout damaged the magic of computing, while art-computing projects tried to bring it back.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is autobiographical and does not prove that every art-tech career can become sustainable. It also shows that creative technical paths can be blocked or distorted by harassment, sexism, lack of mentorship, and credibility denial.
