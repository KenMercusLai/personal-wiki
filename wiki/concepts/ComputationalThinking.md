## Definition
[[ComputationalThinking]] is a problem-solving frame built around decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, and algorithm design.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats computational thinking as a valuable but incomplete foundation for AI-era education. It helps learners break complex systems into parts, identify reusable structures, and design procedures, but it can look too clean when moved into the real world. RORIRI therefore extends it with statistical model thinking for uncertainty and academic writing for problem definition and evidence.

## Key Claims
- Computational thinking names a durable capability for understanding powerful tools rather than merely learning one current tool.
- Its core operation is abstraction from concrete cases into reusable structure.
- Narrow CT assumes clean inputs and defined tasks more readily than messy real-world inquiry allows.
- AI-era education should preserve CT's execution layer while adding epistemic and problem-definition layers.

## Evidence
- Durable capability: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] argues that fast tool turnover makes learning one specific tool less important than understanding the boundaries of powerful tools.
- Core operations: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] defines CT through decomposition, pattern recognition, abstraction, and algorithm design.
- Real-world gap: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] says CT underhandles noisy data, lossy descriptions, task origins, boundaries, and evidence standards.
- Extended frame: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] combines abstraction/programming, statistical model thinking, and academic writing into a wider CT.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a formative essay rather than a curriculum trial, so the proposed extension is conceptual. It also explicitly says the three abilities are an adaptable heuristic rather than a natural or final list.
