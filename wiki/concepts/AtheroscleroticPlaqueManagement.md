## Definition
[[AtheroscleroticPlaqueManagement]] is the long-term effort to stabilize, slow, or potentially regress atherosclerotic plaque and reduce cardiovascular events through lifestyle change, risk-factor control, medication, and reassessment, as distinct from procedures used to treat an obstruction or acute thrombotic event.

## Current Synthesis
The available source organizes management around five linked goals: reducing plaque volume, changing plaque composition, lowering rupture risk, slowing progression, and preventing adverse cardiovascular events. It presents lipid management as the core intervention within a broader program that also addresses lifestyle, blood pressure, glucose, and selected inflammatory or thrombotic risk. Its distinction between disease modification and stenting or thrombolysis is conceptually useful because opening an artery or dissolving a clot does not by itself correct the underlying plaque process. The post's claim that intensive lipid lowering may stabilize plaque within about 30 days but that regression usually takes one to two years supports sustained treatment, although neither timeline is referenced. Imaging is positioned as feedback for maintaining or adjusting care, but the source does not establish which test, interval, or change threshold improves clinical outcomes.

## Key Claims
- Plaque management should target composition, stability, progression, volume, and cardiovascular outcomes rather than plaque size alone.
- Lifestyle change and coordinated control of lipids, blood pressure, and glucose form the long-term management base.
- Lipid lowering is presented as the central disease-modifying treatment, with stabilization expected earlier than structural regression.
- Anti-inflammatory and antithrombotic treatment are conditional additions rather than universal substitutes for risk-factor management.
- Stenting and thrombolysis treat important manifestations or complications but are not equivalent to modifying atherosclerosis itself.
- Serial imaging may inform follow-up, but an imaging change is not automatically the same as improved patient outcomes.

## Evidence
- Goals and scope: [[dong-mai-zhou-yang-ying-hua-ban-kuai-gan-yu-yu-zhi-liao]] lists plaque volume, composition, rupture, progression, and adverse cardiovascular events as intervention targets.
- Core management: [[dong-mai-zhou-yang-ying-hua-ban-kuai-gan-yu-yu-zhi-liao]] centers dyslipidemia while also naming lifestyle, blood pressure, glucose, inflammation, and thrombosis.
- Time horizon: [[dong-mai-zhou-yang-ying-hua-ban-kuai-gan-yu-yu-zhi-liao]] states that stabilization can occur within roughly 30 days of intensive lipid lowering and regression after one to two years.
- Treatment boundary: [[dong-mai-zhou-yang-ying-hua-ban-kuai-gan-yu-yu-zhi-liao]] excludes stenting and thrombolysis from direct treatment of atherosclerosis itself.
- Follow-up: [[dong-mai-zhou-yang-ying-hua-ban-kuai-gan-yu-yu-zhi-liao]] recommends repeat imaging to assess plaque morphology and composition and adjust treatment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence base is one concise social-media post with no guideline, trial, review, patient definition, drug regimen, target value, imaging protocol, or outcome estimate. Its timelines should therefore be treated as source claims rather than universal expectations. The appended reply proposes a different cholesterol theory and structural improvement within seven to 15 days, but provides no clinical study or reproducible method and does not overturn the main account. Imaging markers can be informative without proving that a patient will avoid infarction, stroke, bleeding, or treatment adverse effects. Medication choice, procedural care, follow-up, and interpretation of plaque findings require individualized clinical assessment; this page is not a treatment plan.
