## Definition
[[PrototypeFirstProductDiscovery]] is an approach that makes a plausible product assumption tangible early, then learns from intended users interacting with it before investing heavily in research documentation or implementation.

## Current Synthesis
The approach begins from the claim that both research-first and prototype-first teams still hold assumptions until users encounter something concrete. Interviews and personas may orient a team, but they cannot fully predict comprehension, behavior, or rejection in use. A realistic prototype shortens the interval between forming an assumption and receiving behavioral evidence. The SVPG source shows prototypes revealing technical possibility internally through an engineer-built Alexa demonstration. The MSN Messenger case adds a release path: Glasser coupled a new communication mechanism to a rough UI, the team self-hosted it, and collaborators then built the polished interface. This is a sequencing argument, not an absolute anti-research rule: exploratory research may still be necessary when the problem, population, context, or stakes are unclear.

## Key Claims
- Research artifacts do not eliminate product assumptions.
- Tangible interaction exposes comprehension, behavior, and rejection that documentation cannot directly demonstrate.
- The learning cycle improves when teams shorten the time between assumption and behavioral test.
- Personas, empathy maps, and interviews become waste when they cease to influence decisions.
- Problem ambiguity and consequence determine how much discovery should precede prototyping.
- Engineering prototypes can communicate a newly practical solution space to the team as well as test behavior with customers.
- Internal self-hosting can test an interaction mechanism before investing in final interface polish.

## Evidence
- Artifact decay: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] says AJ&Smart's personas and documentation often gathered dust after design and testing began.
- Behavioral evidence: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] says the first user tests produced the useful data in the firm's projects.
- Compressed cycle: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] reports a five-day sprint outperforming the firm's prior package in perceived progress.
- Research exception: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] identifies unclear problem spaces such as emergency-room improvement as cases for up-front research.
- Technical possibility: [[customer-inspired-technology-enabled-silicon-valley-product-group]] uses an engineer-built Alexa prototype to show how a tangible artifact can expand a team's product vision.
- Waste reduction: [[customer-inspired-technology-enabled-silicon-valley-product-group]] argues that involving engineers for a few hours in discovery can prevent weeks or months of implementation waste.
- Mechanism-to-interface path: [[danny-glasser-is-typing-danny-glasser]] describes a coded typing-activity mechanism, rough proof-of-concept UI, internal self-hosting, and later shipping-interface work.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Prototype tests condition participants on one proposed solution and can narrow discovery prematurely. Internal self-hosting is useful for functional and team-level learning but does not substitute for representative external users. Generative research can reveal unmet needs, environmental constraints, vulnerable populations, power relations, and alternative problem framings that no chosen prototype expresses. All three sources make experiential, selected claims rather than measured comparisons, so the strongest synthesis is to choose the first evidence-producing artifact according to uncertainty: prototype when solution behavior or technical possibility is the main unknown; research when the problem and context are.

## What Changed
- Added internal self-hosting as a mechanism-validation step between rough proof of concept and polished release.
- Preserved the boundary that employee use does not replace representative customer evidence.
