## Definition
[[ForcingFunctions]] are deliberately designed tasks, commitments, constraints, deadlines, or events that change the cost of inaction and make a target behavior or result more likely.

## Current Synthesis
[[BenjaminHardy]], citing [[DanMartell]], treats motivation as partly situational rather than purely internal. Short timelines, public deadlines, promised delivery, immediate feedback, and consequential commitments can close the distance between intention and action. The useful design principle is to alter the environment around behavior, but pressure is not equivalent to correctness: a forcing function can accelerate the wrong goal, encourage metric gaming, or turn accountability into coercion unless stakes are proportional, consented to, reversible, and paired with feedback about quality.

## Key Claims
- Environmental constraints can influence action even when intention remains unchanged.
- Short deadlines and public commitments can increase immediacy and reduce deferral.
- Faster feedback makes the consequence of behavior easier to perceive and learn from.
- External accountability can supplement unreliable moment-to-moment motivation.
- Safe forcing functions need bounded stakes, quality checks, revision paths, and protection against gaming or coercion.

## Evidence
- Definition: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] attributes to Martell the definition of a forcing function as an activity or event that compels action and a result.
- Deadline examples: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] recommends a short writing timer, earlier-than-expected delivery promises, and public goals.
- Situational motivation: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] uses Martell's imagined high-stakes story to argue that perceived necessity can change confidence and effort.
- Feedback link: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] connects immediate consequences with adaptation and claimed flow.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supplies examples and rhetoric rather than comparative outcome evidence. Its violent hypothetical should not be normalized as a literal intervention. Excessive stakes can narrow attention, suppress reporting, reward shortcuts, intensify anxiety, or make a person persist with a mistaken objective. Deadlines also trade speed against reflection and quality. A forcing function is therefore an environmental design tool whose value depends on goal validity, proportionality, agency, measurement, and recovery.
