## Definition
[[MultiplePathsToYes]] is Bezos's Amazon decision pattern for letting reversible ideas find internal sponsorship through many executives instead of dying when one manager says no.

## Current Synthesis
The Forbes profile contrasts startup-like permission with traditional corporate hierarchy. In a hierarchy, every manager in a chain can veto a junior executive's idea; in a startup, one investor saying yes can be enough. Bezos says Amazon tries to preserve that entrepreneurial path by letting hundreds of executives green-light two-way-door experiments that can be reversed if wrong. For one-way-door decisions that change company direction, Bezos slows the process and applies stricter tests: originality, scale, and returns on capital.

## Key Claims
- Innovation can be killed by veto chains where any single "no" blocks an idea.
- Reversible two-way-door decisions should have many possible sponsors and low senior-review burden.
- Some failures should be expected and even welcomed when they come from experiments.
- Irreversible one-way-door decisions require slower central judgment.
- Large strategic bets should be differentiated, big enough to matter to Amazon, and capable of good returns on capital.

## Evidence
- Veto-chain diagnosis: [[bezos-unbound-exclusive-interview-with-the-amazon-founder-on-what-he-plans-to-conquer-next]] quotes Bezos explaining that a junior executive's idea can die at any "no" in the management chain.
- Startup comparison: [[bezos-unbound-exclusive-interview-with-the-amazon-founder-on-what-he-plans-to-conquer-next]] says startups can proceed when the twentieth venture capitalist says yes.
- Two-way doors: [[bezos-unbound-exclusive-interview-with-the-amazon-founder-on-what-he-plans-to-conquer-next]] says hundreds of Amazon executives can green-light reversible ideas that employees shop internally.
- Failure tolerance: [[bezos-unbound-exclusive-interview-with-the-amazon-founder-on-what-he-plans-to-conquer-next]] quotes [[JeffWilke]] saying Amazon wants some failures to occur throughout the company without Bezos or Wilke reviewing them.
- One-way doors: [[bezos-unbound-exclusive-interview-with-the-amazon-founder-on-what-he-plans-to-conquer-next]] says Bezos plays "chief slowdown officer" for larger ideas and looks for differentiation, scale, and returns on capital.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source gives Amazon's internal account rather than an independent audit of decision quality. Multiple paths to yes can increase experimentation, but the article does not quantify failure rates, opportunity costs, employee autonomy, or cases where decentralized yes created confusion or duplicated effort.
