## Definition
[[ArtificialGeneralIntelligence]] (AGI) is a disputed category for AI with sufficiently broad capability or adaptive learning to mark a deeper transition than improvements to a specific product such as [[ChatGPT]].

## Current Synthesis
The sources agree that AGI lacks a crisp, universally accepted boundary, but they use that ambiguity differently. In a February 2023 interview, [[SamAltman]] expects a slow takeoff—a gradual capability transition over which observers will disagree about when AGI was reached. That framing makes governance an ongoing problem rather than a decision deferred until a universally recognized milestone.

The same account links AGI to abundance and to possible institutional discontinuity. Altman argues that sufficiently capable AI could break important assumptions of capitalism, so the distribution of profits, access, and governing power cannot be treated as ordinary company-policy questions. His preferred direction is plural: multiple AGIs, no single-company ownership of the AI universe, bounded investor returns, and mission-preserving safety powers. These are proposals and forecasts from an interested builder, not evidence that AGI exists or that the proposed arrangements will work.

[[BenThompson]] supplies a narrower personal definition: AGI is AI that learns continuously. On that criterion, a frozen frontier model does not qualify merely because it is highly capable, and a harness that restores state from Markdown only simulates continuity around unchanged weights. This definition makes adaptation central but remains one author's threshold rather than a field-wide test; it also leaves open whether learning must occur in model weights or can be implemented through durable external memory and tools.

## Key Claims
- AGI may emerge through a gradual, disputed transition rather than one objectively identifiable breakthrough moment.
- Competing AGI definitions can emphasize broad capability, continuous learning, or system-level adaptation and therefore classify the same model differently.
- File-backed memory and agent harnesses can simulate continuity without proving that the underlying model learns continuously.
- Multiple AGIs are preferable to control by one company because capability concentration raises access and governance concerns.
- Full AGI could disrupt capitalism's normal assumptions about ownership, returns, labor, and scarcity.
- Profit sharing, access, and governance are distinct distribution problems that require institutional design before the transition is complete.

## Evidence
- Transition shape: [[exclusive-interview-openais-sam-altman-talks-chatgpt-and-how-artificial-general-intelligence-can-break-capitalism]] records Altman's expectation of slow takeoff and disagreement about the moment AGI arrives.
- Plurality and concentration: [[exclusive-interview-openais-sam-altman-talks-chatgpt-and-how-artificial-general-intelligence-can-break-capitalism]] says the field is too large for one company and expresses hope for multiple AGIs.
- Economic discontinuity: [[exclusive-interview-openais-sam-altman-talks-chatgpt-and-how-artificial-general-intelligence-can-break-capitalism]] says full AGI could break capitalism and names profit distribution, access, and governance as separate unresolved questions.
- Institutional preparation: [[exclusive-interview-openais-sam-altman-talks-chatgpt-and-how-artificial-general-intelligence-can-break-capitalism]] presents capped returns and safety-override provisions in the [[Microsoft]] relationship as attempts to preserve mission under a nonstandard outcome.
- Broader framework: [[exclusive-interview-openais-sam-altman-talks-chatgpt-and-how-artificial-general-intelligence-can-break-capitalism]] records Altman saying AGI drives his actions and connects the project to energy and a world of abundance.
- Continuous-learning threshold: [[write-things-down]] rejects a frontier-model AGI declaration because the model does not update its learned weights continuously.
- Harness distinction: [[write-things-down]] treats Claude Code's Markdown-backed cross-session continuity as a crude memory system and possible “pseudo-AGI,” not decisive evidence that AGI exists.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Neither source defines a validated measurable threshold, establishes a timeline, or demonstrates that gradual takeoff is more likely than discontinuous progress. Altman's economic and governance claims are speculative and come from OpenAI's CEO while discussing his own organization; Thompson's continuous-learning criterion is explicitly personal. Frozen weights do not imply a wholly static deployed system when retrieval, tools, external memory, online feedback, or other components can change behavior, while system-level adaptation does not necessarily mean that the base model itself learns. Multiple systems also need not imply distributed power if compute, capital, data, or deployment channels remain concentrated, and capped returns or safety overrides do not establish public accountability by themselves.

## What Changed
- Added continuous learning as a competing personal AGI threshold.
- Distinguished model-weight learning from continuity simulated through files, memory, tools, and a harness.
- Preserved the absence of a validated field-wide threshold and the forecast status of governance claims.
