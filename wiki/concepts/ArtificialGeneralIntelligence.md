## Definition
[[ArtificialGeneralIntelligence]] (AGI) is the source's term for broadly capable AI whose arrival would represent a deeper transition than improvements to a specific product such as [[ChatGPT]].

## Current Synthesis
The wiki currently has one direct AGI source: a February 2023 interview in which [[SamAltman]] rejects both an imminent, crisp threshold and the idea that one organization should control the outcome. He expects a slow takeoff—a gradual capability transition over which observers will disagree about when AGI was reached. That framing makes governance an ongoing problem rather than a decision deferred until a universally recognized milestone.

The same account links AGI to abundance and to possible institutional discontinuity. Altman argues that sufficiently capable AI could break important assumptions of capitalism, so the distribution of profits, access, and governing power cannot be treated as ordinary company-policy questions. His preferred direction is plural: multiple AGIs, no single-company ownership of the AI universe, bounded investor returns, and mission-preserving safety powers. These are proposals and forecasts from an interested builder, not evidence that AGI exists or that the proposed arrangements will work.

## Key Claims
- AGI may emerge through a gradual, disputed transition rather than one objectively identifiable breakthrough moment.
- Multiple AGIs are preferable to control by one company because capability concentration raises access and governance concerns.
- Full AGI could disrupt capitalism's normal assumptions about ownership, returns, labor, and scarcity.
- Profit sharing, access, and governance are distinct distribution problems that require institutional design before the transition is complete.
- Energy and an abundance-oriented economy are presented as supporting conditions in Altman's broader AGI framework.

## Evidence
- Transition shape: [[exclusive-interview-openais-sam-altman-talks-chatgpt-and-how-artificial-general-intelligence-can-break-capitalism]] records Altman's expectation of slow takeoff and disagreement about the moment AGI arrives.
- Plurality and concentration: [[exclusive-interview-openais-sam-altman-talks-chatgpt-and-how-artificial-general-intelligence-can-break-capitalism]] says the field is too large for one company and expresses hope for multiple AGIs.
- Economic discontinuity: [[exclusive-interview-openais-sam-altman-talks-chatgpt-and-how-artificial-general-intelligence-can-break-capitalism]] says full AGI could break capitalism and names profit distribution, access, and governance as separate unresolved questions.
- Institutional preparation: [[exclusive-interview-openais-sam-altman-talks-chatgpt-and-how-artificial-general-intelligence-can-break-capitalism]] presents capped returns and safety-override provisions in the [[Microsoft]] relationship as attempts to preserve mission under a nonstandard outcome.
- Broader framework: [[exclusive-interview-openais-sam-altman-talks-chatgpt-and-how-artificial-general-intelligence-can-break-capitalism]] records Altman saying AGI drives his actions and connects the project to energy and a world of abundance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not define a measurable AGI threshold, provide capability evidence, establish a timeline, or demonstrate that slow takeoff is more likely than discontinuous progress. Its economic and governance claims are speculative and come from OpenAI's CEO while discussing his own organization's structure. Multiple systems need not imply meaningfully distributed power if compute, capital, data, or deployment channels remain concentrated, and capped returns or safety overrides do not by themselves establish public accountability.

## What Changed
- Created the concept with a source-scoped slow-takeoff interpretation of AGI.
- Separated the transition question from the three distribution questions of profits, access, and governance.
- Preserved the forecast status of claims about capitalism, abundance, and institutional safeguards.
