## Definition
[[PlatformSpecificEditorialStrategy]] is the practice of adapting editorial goals, formats, voice, offers, and ownership to each distribution platform because the same audience may bring different intentions, expectations, and behaviors to different contexts.

## Current Synthesis
The sources agree that distributed publishing cannot simply syndicate the same message everywhere. [[BleacherReport]] operationalizes this through platform mission statements and managing editors: Instagram calls for visually impressive content, while Twitter rewards breaking news and sharper commentary. [[GaryVaynerchuk]] adds a marketing priority rule: infer why the audience is on the platform, create for that immediate intent rather than the marketer's taste, and place the sales agenda after platform and audience fit. Channel-specific execution should still ladder to a coherent brand goal, so adaptation changes the expression without abandoning the underlying story.

## Key Claims
- Platform-native publishing needs explicit editorial missions by channel.
- Different platforms can serve the same person in different consumption mindsets.
- Editorial ownership by platform can make distributed publishing more deliberate.
- Format and voice should follow user expectation rather than a generic brand template.
- Platform differentiation can make distributed content feel intentional instead of merely reposted.
- Audience intent should outrank the marketer's preferred message or immediate sales agenda when choosing a platform treatment.
- Channel adaptation should preserve a stable brand goal and personality across individual posts.

## Evidence
- Mission statements: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says Bleacher Report's editorial leaders were developing mission statements for each platform.
- Managing-editor ownership: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says Bleacher Report identified managing editors assigned to specific platforms such as Instagram.
- Mindset difference: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] contrasts Instagram users wanting to be wowed with Twitter users wanting breaking news and snark.
- Experience design: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says differentiated platform experiences can serve the same person in different contexts.
- Intent contrast: [[content-is-king-but-context-is-god-gary-vaynerchuk-medium]] says the same person may use Pinterest with shopping intent and Facebook to keep up with her world, requiring different storytelling.
- Priority order: [[content-is-king-but-context-is-god-gary-vaynerchuk-medium]] places respect for platform and audience before the marketer's agenda.
- Brand boundary: [[content-is-king-but-context-is-god-gary-vaynerchuk-medium]] says each post should return to a consistent company north star, core story, and personality.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Neither source includes comparative campaign performance, editorial costs, or controlled evidence that platform tailoring improves conversion, retention, or brand lift. Bleacher Report supports the organizational logic more than a detailed creative playbook, while Vaynerchuk's Facebook/Pinterest contrast is a 2016 practitioner assumption that may not generalize across people, cultures, or later platform behavior. Platform-native work can also blur commercial intent, fragment brand identity, or increase production overhead; coherence and disclosure remain constraints.

## What Changed
- Extended the concept from publisher organization to audience-intent and marketing-message decisions.
- Added a coherence boundary: adapt expression by channel while preserving the underlying brand goal.
