Side projects as hiring evidence means treating a project as proof of job-relevant capability, not just as an extra line on a resume. Hack Career argues that this matters especially for candidates from non-traditional backgrounds: a project can help answer concerns about missing experience, but only if it makes the candidate's abilities easier to evaluate.

The concept depends on fit. A relevant project should address the role being pursued, such as using a shipped iPhone app to demonstrate technical learning for a product role, or using a niche blog with an engaged audience to support a content marketing application. The project is strongest when it fills a specific evidentiary gap rather than merely showing general busyness.

Depth is the second signal. The source frames a side project as a visible representation of work quality: it should function, show thought about user experience, and handle enough edge cases to make the effort credible. A course or practice exercise does not carry the same weight unless it produces something shipped that reviewers can inspect.

Iteration is the third signal. After an initial release, the candidate should show that they sought feedback, improved the work, or built a meaningful body of output. This distinguishes a serious project from resume filler and gives recruiters or interviewers a reason to spend extra time looking at it.

The source also adds a visibility requirement. If the project is meant to help in a job search, it should be prominent on the resume, described with concrete credibility indicators when available, and documented publicly through a post that explains the process, lessons, challenges, and project evidence such as screenshots or demos.
