## Definition
[[Sharenting]] is the parent or carer practice of sharing a child's photos, videos, milestones, identity details, or daily life online, thereby creating data about someone who may be unable to understand or consent to the exposure.

## Current Synthesis
The source frames sharenting as cumulative profile creation, not merely an audience-choice question. A celebratory post can expose direct facts such as a birth date, home, school, street, interests, relatives, pets, or device location, while platforms and brokers can infer further attributes by joining posts with purchases, browsing, contacts, and third-party records. The child's profile can therefore begin before birth, outlast the original post, contain errors, and become useful for advertising, identity fraud, or decisions in credit, insurance, education, and criminal justice.

The appropriate control depends on risk and purpose. The author adopts a strong precautionary default—do not share—but also distinguishes public-interest analysis from commercial profiling. The retained guidance supports more granular safeguards as well: avoid posts that reveal personal information, secure connected devices, install updates, and ask schools, online services, and loyalty programs what they collect and why.

## Key Claims
- Sharenting creates a child's digital history through decisions made by adults before the child can give meaningful consent.
- Ordinary milestone posts can jointly reveal identity, birth date, home, school, routines, interests, relationships, and security-question answers.
- Volunteered content is only one input; metadata, browsing, purchases, devices, social graphs, and third-party records can produce additional inferred data.
- Data aggregation makes small disclosures cumulative and can preserve or amplify inaccurate interpretations.
- Downstream harms range from unwanted targeting and embarrassment to identity fraud and opaque high-stakes decisions.
- Children's data can support beneficial interventions, so purpose, proportionality, security, governance, and recourse matter alongside minimization.
- A defensible family default is to minimize public sharing and treat every disclosed detail as potentially joinable with other records.

## Evidence
- Consent and accumulation: [[dont-post-your-kid-online]] argues that a child's profile may form before birth from parents' searches, purchases, reading, contacts, and later social posts.
- Direct disclosure: [[dont-post-your-kid-online]] identifies birthdays, tagged homes, school logos, street signs, interests, names, pets, vehicles, and family details as joinable identity clues.
- Inference mechanism: [[dont-post-your-kid-online]] distinguishes explicitly supplied data from “given off” and inferred data, and its retained Acxiom diagram shows multi-channel signals being unified, enhanced, activated, and recirculated.
- Profile breadth: [[dont-post-your-kid-online]] retains a Cracked Labs comparison depicting demographic, household, financial, health, purchase, search, political, and behavioral attributes in broker profiles.
- Consequential use: [[dont-post-your-kid-online]] says profiles may inform advertising and decisions concerning mortgages, insurance, education, credit, or bail, while noting that some examples are risks rather than traced cases.
- Practical safeguards: [[dont-post-your-kid-online]] retains the Children's Commissioner recommendations to limit revealing posts, change default device passwords, buy genuine devices, install updates, and question data-holding organizations.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence comes from one 2018 journalistic article interpreting a UK Children's Commissioner report and several broker materials. It does not follow a specific child's social post into a verified adverse institutional decision, measure the prevalence of fraud caused by sharenting, compare private and public sharing settings, or establish that abstention always outperforms selective sharing with consent and controls. Beneficial child-protection analytics also show that not every collection purpose is equivalent, although good intent does not by itself establish accuracy, proportionality, security, or fair recourse.

## What Changed
- Created the concept as a cumulative consent, identity, and profiling problem rather than only a social-media etiquette issue.
- Added a qualified distinction between commercial profiling, public-interest analysis, and risk-sensitive family safeguards.
