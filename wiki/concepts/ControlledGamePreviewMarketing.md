## Definition
[[ControlledGamePreviewMarketing]] is the use of selectively granted, developer-framed early access to generate coverage and expectation while limiting the public's ability to inspect the same build independently.

## Current Synthesis
The source distinguishes invitation-only previews from public demos by who can inspect the product, under what conditions, and with what incentives. A curated event can concentrate creator output, preserve message control, and turn travel, hospitality, gifts, embargo privileges, and future access into soft influence without an explicit payment-for-praise agreement. A downloadable demo distributes verification more broadly and lets players test performance, controls, difficulty, and genre fit, although it can impose packaging, security, support, and representativeness costs that this source does not measure.

The central risk is an evidence substitution: frequency and polish of preview coverage can be mistaken for development completeness or final quality. [[WuchangFallenFeathers]] is presented as the retrospective expectation-backlash case and [[PhantomBladeZero]] as a prospective warning. Both remain source-scoped interpretations rather than a comparative result across games or proof that invited participants acted dishonestly.

## Key Claims
- Selective access can coordinate a burst of favorable attention while keeping build access and framing under developer control.
- Benefits tied to attendance or future access may create editorial pressure even when there is no explicit instruction or direct payment for praise.
- Preview frequency is evidence of marketing activity, not by itself evidence of production completion, launch stability, or final quality.
- Public demos provide stronger direct evidence of hardware performance, interaction feel, difficulty, and audience fit for participating players.
- Controlled hype can create an expectation gap that turns ordinary shortcomings or genre mismatch into disproportionate launch backlash.
- The relative costs and outcomes of curated previews and public demos require comparative evidence absent from the source.

## Evidence
- Access mechanism: [[plumz-14418]] describes scarce invitations, dedicated venues, subsidized attendance, gifts, embargo-like privileges, and coordinated creator coverage.
- Retrospective case: [[plumz-14418]] argues that [[WuchangFallenFeathers]] accumulated expectations its launch could not satisfy despite being a competent Soulslike.
- Prospective case: [[plumz-14418]] says years of [[PhantomBladeZero]] previews encouraged schedule confidence that a later delay undermined.
- Verification contrast: [[plumz-14418]] argues that a public demo would let players inspect system performance, controls, and genre fit directly.
- Incentive illustration: [[plumz-14418]] includes an AI-generated image of a developer handing money and a review guide to media; it communicates the accusation but does not document a real transaction.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sole source is a first-person polemic, not a study of preview coverage, demo economics, or launch outcomes. It does not identify contracts, quantify sentiment, compare otherwise similar releases, verify that invited reviewers withheld concerns, or measure whether event feedback affected development. Public demos can be unrepresentative vertical slices and can add packaging, support, security, and schedule costs. Invitation-only access can also produce legitimate reporting and feedback. The framework therefore identifies incentives and verification differences, not corruption or product failure by default.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from a critique of Chinese single-player game preview events.
- Separated controlled publicity, direct player verification, and final product quality as distinct evidence layers.
