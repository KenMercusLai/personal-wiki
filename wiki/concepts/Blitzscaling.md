## Definition
[[Blitzscaling]] is a startup-growth strategy that accepts operational fragility, high burn, and efficiency losses in order to grow faster than competitors when timing and market capture matter more than near-term stability.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents blitzscaling as a deliberate bet, not a default startup virtue. [[ReidHoffman]] analogizes it to outrunning the supply chain: a company carries enough resources to move quickly, but if the bet is wrong the absence of stable support systems can be fatal. [[BrianChesky]]'s Airbnb example shows why founders might take that risk: once competitors and regulators could see the opportunity, fast international expansion became a survival move rather than cosmetic growth.

## Key Claims
- Blitzscaling prioritizes speed over efficiency and operational comfort.
- The strategy is risky because higher burn and weak support systems can kill the company if the market bet is wrong.
- Competition is a major trigger because visible opportunity starts a race for users, markets, and legitimacy.
- Blitzscaling should come after evidence that the product is working, not immediately after an idea.
- The practice forces organizational systems to change quickly, especially hiring, communication, and leadership.

## Evidence
- Core definition: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] describes blitzscaling as fast growth that raises burn and can collapse the company if wrong.
- Competitive trigger: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] says competition is one principal reason to blitzscale and cites Airbnb's rapid international expansion after copycat pressure.
- Timing boundary: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] warns that using afterburners before readiness is a way to die.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a course-note synthesis of operator claims, not a controlled comparison of companies that scaled fast versus slowly. It emphasizes venture-backed network-effect contexts and does not show that blitzscaling is appropriate for bootstrapped, regulated, or operationally fragile businesses without similar competitive pressure.
