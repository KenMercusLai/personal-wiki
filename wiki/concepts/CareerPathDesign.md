## Definition

Career path design treats a career as an evolving route through roles and work arrangements rather than as a single job choice or fixed endpoint.

## Current Synthesis

A broad career direction can be expressed through multiple jobs, customer relationships, and business models. This makes adaptation part of career planning: a person may preserve valued activities while changing the parts of work that create poor fit, and may use interim roles to build toward a longer-term aim. The approach expands the option set but does not make every passion professionally feasible.

## Key Claims

- A career is broader and longer-lived than any one job within it.
- Unwanted features of a role can sometimes be avoided by changing role, customer type, or business model within the same field.
- Interim work can build skills or access needed for a later target role.
- Career paths remain constrained by feasibility; some interests may not support a sustainable profession.

## Evidence

- Role flexibility - [[shi-yong-gpv-si-kao-ni-de-zhi-ye-sheng-ya]] describes a restaurant owner moving into supplying food to cafés to avoid difficult front-of-house interactions.
- Alternative expressions - [[shi-yong-gpv-si-kao-ni-de-zhi-ye-sheng-ya]] proposes teaching cookery or writing about food as different roles built on similar interests and strengths.
- Sequenced progress - [[shi-yong-gpv-si-kao-ni-de-zhi-ye-sheng-ya]] uses working as a hotel cook before becoming a teacher as an example of an intermediate step toward a goal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

The examples are illustrative anecdotes rather than evidence that role redesign is always available. Market demand, credentials, finances, caregiving obligations, geography, and opportunity costs can restrict transitions, while the skeptical comment explicitly notes that some hobbies cannot become careers.

## What Changed

- Established the distinction between a career path and an individual job.
- Added role redesign and interim work as two mechanisms for moving toward a preferred career.
- Preserved feasibility as a constraint on passion-led path design.
