## Definition
[[ProductBundling]] is the merchandising practice of presenting multiple items as one coordinated purchase opportunity while preserving either a combined action, an offer-level incentive, or both.

## Current Synthesis
The source organizes ecommerce bundles around the customer's likely next purchase. When useful complements exist, pair them with the anchor product. When buyers may want several instances or variants, make that multi-item choice easy. When the product is usually purchased once and has no natural complement, pair it with store credit that can support a gift or later purchase. Best Buy's cited widget keeps the offer editable and provides one action to add the selected items, so the intervention combines recommendation relevance with lower cart-building effort.

The article also separates curated bundles from a site-wide threshold incentive. Sumo's promoted tool reacts to an add-to-cart event and discounts additional spend without maintaining a product-specific bundle catalog. That reduces configuration but weakens product-level curation and introduces discount cost. In either form, the defensible result is not the sum of displayed prices; it is incremental contribution after acceptance, conversion, substitution, fulfillment, returns, discounting, and customer effects.

## Key Claims
- Bundle composition should reflect complementarity, plausible multi-unit demand, or a credible gift use.
- Selectable items preserve customer control and can reduce resistance to a preassembled offer.
- A single multi-add action reduces the navigation and cart work of purchasing several products.
- Site-wide spend thresholds trade product-specific relevance for simpler configuration and broader reach.
- Bundle success requires incremental economic evidence, not only a larger displayed cart total.

## Evidence
- Complementary pattern: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] groups Beats headphones with a speaker, portable charger, and iTunes gift card.
- Multi-variant pattern: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] groups four Fingerlings Baby Monkey variants.
- Gift-value pattern: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] pairs a hair straightener with a same-value Sephora gift card.
- Interaction design: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] says shoppers can deselect products and add the remaining set in one action.
- Threshold alternative: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] describes Sumo notifications that reward additional cart spend across the site.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a vendor-authored tactical article and does not report actual bundle take rates, controlled comparisons, margins, substitutions, returns, repeat purchase, or customer response. Its three-pattern taxonomy is useful for generating tests but is not exhaustive, and equal-value gift cards create future liabilities and possible cannibalization rather than free incremental value. Discounts can also train price sensitivity or attract weak-fit demand.

## What Changed
- Established a three-pattern bundle taxonomy and distinguished curated bundles from site-wide spend thresholds.
