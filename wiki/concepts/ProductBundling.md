## Definition
[[ProductBundling]] is the merchandising practice of presenting multiple items as one coordinated purchase opportunity while preserving either a combined action, an offer-level incentive, or both.

## Current Synthesis
The sources organize bundles around either the customer's likely next purchase or a broader learning outcome. When useful complements exist, pair them with the anchor product. When buyers may want several instances or variants, make that multi-item choice easy. When the product is usually purchased once and has no natural complement, pair it with store credit that can support a gift or later purchase. Best Buy's cited widget keeps the offer editable and provides one action to add the selected items, so the intervention combines recommendation relevance with lower cart-building effort.

For digital knowledge products, every tier can preserve a core artifact while added exercises, screencasts, and working code change both the learning modality and the category against which buyers evaluate the offer. In the [[RefactoringToCollections]] case, the reported $67.84 average selling price exceeded twice the $29 book-only price, but the result does not isolate packaging from demand, launch discounting, audience quality, or selection among tiers.

The ecommerce source also separates curated bundles from a site-wide threshold incentive. Sumo's promoted tool reacts to an add-to-cart event and discounts additional spend without maintaining a product-specific bundle catalog. That reduces configuration but weakens product-level curation and introduces discount cost. In every form, the defensible result is not the sum of displayed prices; it is incremental contribution after acceptance, conversion, substitution, fulfillment, support, returns, discounting, and customer effects.

## Key Claims
- Bundle composition should reflect complementarity, plausible multi-unit demand, or a credible gift use.
- Selectable items preserve customer control and can reduce resistance to a preassembled offer.
- A single multi-add action reduces the navigation and cart work of purchasing several products.
- Digital bundles can combine modalities, exercises, and working examples so higher tiers represent different learning outcomes rather than only more copies or superficial extras.
- Site-wide spend thresholds trade product-specific relevance for simpler configuration and broader reach.
- Bundle success requires incremental economic evidence, not only a larger displayed cart total.

## Evidence
- Complementary pattern: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] groups Beats headphones with a speaker, portable charger, and iTunes gift card.
- Multi-variant pattern: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] groups four Fingerlings Baby Monkey variants.
- Gift-value pattern: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] pairs a hair straightener with a same-value Sephora gift card.
- Interaction design: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] says shoppers can deselect products and add the remaining set in one action.
- Threshold alternative: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] describes Sumo notifications that reward additional cart spend across the site.
- Knowledge-product tiers: [[the-61-392-book-launch-that-let-me-quit-my-job]] describes $29, $59, and $135 packages that successively add screencasts and a production Laravel project to the book and exercises.
- Reported value capture: [[the-61-392-book-launch-that-let-me-quit-my-job]] reports 905 sales, $61,392 in package revenue, and only $26,245 in the counterfactual where every buyer paid the book-only price.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence consists of one vendor-authored tactical article and one creator-authored launch retrospective. The ecommerce source does not report actual bundle take rates, controlled comparisons, margins, substitutions, returns, repeat purchase, or customer response. Its three-pattern taxonomy is useful for generating tests but is not exhaustive, and equal-value gift cards create future liabilities and possible cannibalization rather than free incremental value. The book-launch source reports tier units and revenue but no randomized book-only comparison, costs, refunds, profit, or customer outcomes; its hypothetical $29-only total assumes the same 905 people would have purchased without higher-value packages. Discounts can also train price sensitivity or attract weak-fit demand.

## What Changed
- Expanded bundling from physical-product complementarity into multi-format knowledge products with differentiated outcomes.
- Added a reported tier-mix case while preserving the missing counterfactual, cost, and customer-outcome boundaries.
