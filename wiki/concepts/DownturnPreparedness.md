## Definition
[[DownturnPreparedness]] is the practice of preserving enough liquidity, manageable commitments, employability, and advance decision rules to respond deliberately if weaker industry conditions combine with a broader shock.

## Current Synthesis
[[KentBeck]] frames preparedness as a response to changing relative values rather than a confident crash prediction. When jobs appear plentiful and future income feels dependable, cash reserves and conservative commitments can look unnecessarily cautious. When valuations fall, layoffs rise, or hiring becomes geographically narrower, a current job and liquid cash gain option value because the next role, equity payout, investment price, or home value may no longer be available on familiar terms.

The practical aim is to avoid choices made in panic after flexibility has already disappeared. Beck's proposed defenses are deliberately incremental: build roughly six months of cash when possible, delay or reduce large expense streams, work sustainably enough to protect current performance, and make small learning investments across emerging technical areas. Scenario questions—what if another job is not immediately available, equity loses most of its value, or housing falls sharply—are used to precompute responses, not to assert that those events will occur.

The source also separates vulnerability from trigger. A technology-sector dip may remain contained, and an external shock may be absorbed, but their interaction can create a reinforcing decline. Preparedness therefore concerns compound risk and response capacity rather than point forecasting.

## Key Claims
- Downturn resilience depends partly on avoiding panic-driven decisions after income, asset, or employment options narrow.
- Cash reserves become more valuable when future earnings and rapid re-employment become less dependable.
- Lower recurring expenses preserve room to adapt without requiring a prediction that housing, equity, or employment will collapse.
- Sustainable work can protect the value of a current role when replacement opportunities become scarcer.
- Small, diversified learning bets can preserve career optionality without committing heavily to one forecast.
- Scenario planning is useful when it defines responses to plausible losses rather than pretending to predict their timing.

## Evidence
- Experienced downside: [[not-saying-winter-is-coming-but-wheres-your-coat-facebook]] says Beck moved from exceptional XP consulting demand to depression, depleted savings, and poor decisions after work disappeared.
- Compound-shock model: [[not-saying-winter-is-coming-but-wheres-your-coat-facebook]] distinguishes existing technology-sector vulnerability from an external event that could activate a reinforcing decline.
- Liquidity and commitments: [[not-saying-winter-is-coming-but-wheres-your-coat-facebook]] recommends six months of cash where feasible and delaying or reducing major expense streams.
- Employment resilience: [[not-saying-winter-is-coming-but-wheres-your-coat-facebook]] recommends valuing the current job more highly and learning to work sustainably.
- Option-building: [[not-saying-winter-is-coming-but-wheres-your-coat-facebook]] recommends small, convex learning investments across several emerging technical domains.
- Response rehearsal: [[not-saying-winter-is-coming-but-wheres-your-coat-facebook]] asks readers to plan for delayed re-employment, a 90% RSU loss, or a 40% housing decline without claiming those outcomes are imminent.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept currently rests on one first-person practitioner essay, not labor-market, household-finance, or macroeconomic outcome evidence. The page metadata supplies an update date but not a clearly established original Facebook publication date, and the named technologies and geopolitical scenarios reflect the source's historical context. Six months of cash is a rough heuristic, may be unattainable for financially precarious households, and may be insufficient in a prolonged downturn. Deferring housing, transport, or other spending can carry its own costs; the article's exception for having children is a normative aside rather than financial evidence. Broad learning does not guarantee employability, sustainable work is not fully under an employee's control, and individual preparation cannot eliminate correlated layoffs, medical costs, benefit loss, discrimination, or systemic financial shocks.

## What Changed
- Created the concept around preserving decision quality and options before a possible downturn.
- Distinguished scenario planning from forecasting and vulnerability from an external trigger.
- Qualified the six-month reserve and personal-preparation advice by feasibility, historical scope, and systemic limits.
