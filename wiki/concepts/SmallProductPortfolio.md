## Definition
[[SmallProductPortfolio]] is an independent-creator strategy of building several narrow, low-maintenance paid products so income and learning do not depend on a single product bet.

## Current Synthesis
Zhang Xiaoji's source presents a small product portfolio as a risk-management and learning strategy for an indie programmer after leaving employment. [[TableToFigma]] supplies the strongest revenue and customer-learning base, [[FitCurve]] tests a one-week niche plugin opportunity, and [[GlimmerCard]] validates a consumer-facing design tool through social demand. Rozen's source provides an explicit counterweight: when products are unrelated, diversification can force the founder to repeat the hardest zero-to-early-revenue phase instead of compounding what already works. The portfolio model works only when each product has a clear user problem, manageable maintenance cost, and a plausible distribution channel; otherwise, focus may be the better risk control.

## Key Claims
- Multiple small products can improve tolerance for uncertainty after leaving employment.
- Product ideas should pass a fit test around personal skill, real demand, low maintenance, and quick validation.
- A small portfolio lets creators reuse learning across product, design, marketing, documentation, and overseas distribution.
- Passive-income language can hide the need for ongoing feedback, support, iteration, and marketing.
- Platform selection is part of portfolio design because marketplace exposure and user payment habits affect viability.
- Portfolio diversification can backfire when unrelated products prevent learning, customer memory, and product maturity from compounding in one place.

## Evidence
- Uncertainty tolerance: [[zhang-xiaoji-jian-ru-jia-jing-xie-gang-cheng-xu-yuan-de-shu-zi-you-min-zhuan-xing-zhi-lu]] frames three income-generating products as the concrete result of the author's transition.
- Fit test: [[zhang-xiaoji-jian-ru-jia-jing-xie-gang-cheng-xu-yuan-de-shu-zi-you-min-zhuan-xing-zhi-lu]] says [[FitCurve]] matched low development cost, quick launch, and passive-income potential.
- Reused learning: [[zhang-xiaoji-jian-ru-jia-jing-xie-gang-cheng-xu-yuan-de-shu-zi-you-min-zhuan-xing-zhi-lu]] connects failed video production, Midjourney document translation, and affiliate data to later product documentation and overseas-market decisions.
- Ongoing work: [[zhang-xiaoji-jian-ru-jia-jing-xie-gang-cheng-xu-yuan-de-shu-zi-you-min-zhuan-xing-zhi-lu]] stresses that sustainable revenue requires product, development, design, interaction, project management, and marketing to be good enough.
- Platform choice: [[zhang-xiaoji-jian-ru-jia-jing-xie-gang-cheng-xu-yuan-de-shu-zi-you-min-zhuan-xing-zhi-lu]] chooses Figma for growth tailwinds and iOS for payment willingness and limited development resources.
- Focus critique: [[2023-focusing-on-a-single-product-pays-off]] argues that moving from zero to early MRR is harder than later revenue growth, so unrelated small bets can repeat the most difficult stage too often.
- Compounding alternative: [[2023-focusing-on-a-single-product-pays-off]] says [[OnlineOrNot]] benefited from old prospects returning, early users converting, and operating fixes improving adoption over time.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are successful retrospectives, not general base-rate studies. A portfolio can also fragment attention, delay deeper product work, and increase support obligations unless the products are genuinely small and maintainable. Rozen's critique should not be universalized either: unrelated small bets may still make sense when the founder is searching for demand, the products are deliberately tiny, or the goal is learning and optionality rather than compounding one proven product.
