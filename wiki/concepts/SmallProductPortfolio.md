## Definition
[[SmallProductPortfolio]] is an independent-creator strategy of building several narrow, low-maintenance paid products so income and learning do not depend on a single product bet.

## Current Synthesis
Zhang Xiaoji's source presents a small product portfolio as a risk-management and learning strategy for an indie programmer after leaving employment. [[TableToFigma]] supplies the strongest revenue and customer-learning base, [[FitCurve]] tests a one-week niche plugin opportunity, and [[GlimmerCard]] validates a consumer-facing design tool through social demand. [[AndreyAzimov]] adds a distinct pre-fit use: launch several products within a fixed runway, abandon ideas without paying demand, and let the portfolio emerge from search rather than from a plan to maintain permanent diversification. [[TonyDinh]] adds a post-fit operating case: [[DevUtils]], [[BlackMagic]], [[Xnapper]], and [[TypingMind]] supplied revenue diversification, creative variety, and recurring material for public distribution, while one employee and three freelancers reduced maintenance work. Black Magic's forced sale after a Twitter API-pricing change makes portfolio resilience concrete because another product had already begun replacing the threatened revenue. Rozen provides the counterweight: once a product works, unrelated bets can force the founder to repeat the hardest zero-to-early-revenue phase instead of compounding customer memory and operating learning. A portfolio is therefore most defensible when it either searches cheaply for fit or diversifies maintainable products without starving an already promising one.

## Key Claims
- Multiple small products can improve tolerance for uncertainty after leaving employment and reduce the impact of one product or platform failure.
- Product ideas should pass a fit test around personal skill, real demand, low maintenance, and quick validation.
- A small portfolio lets creators reuse learning across product, design, marketing, documentation, and overseas distribution.
- Passive-income language can hide the need for ongoing feedback, support, iteration, and marketing.
- Platform selection is part of portfolio design because marketplace exposure and user payment habits affect viability.
- Portfolio diversification can backfire when unrelated products prevent learning, customer memory, and product maturity from compounding in one place or multiply support burden.
- Rapid multi-product launching can function as a temporary product-discovery strategy, but launch count is not evidence that the resulting portfolio is profitable, maintainable, or preferable to later focus.

## Evidence
- Uncertainty tolerance: [[zhang-xiaoji-jian-ru-jia-jing-xie-gang-cheng-xu-yuan-de-shu-zi-you-min-zhuan-xing-zhi-lu]] frames three income-generating products as the concrete result of the author's transition.
- Fit test: [[zhang-xiaoji-jian-ru-jia-jing-xie-gang-cheng-xu-yuan-de-shu-zi-you-min-zhuan-xing-zhi-lu]] says [[FitCurve]] matched low development cost, quick launch, and passive-income potential.
- Reused learning: [[zhang-xiaoji-jian-ru-jia-jing-xie-gang-cheng-xu-yuan-de-shu-zi-you-min-zhuan-xing-zhi-lu]] connects failed video production, Midjourney document translation, and affiliate data to later product documentation and overseas-market decisions.
- Ongoing work: [[zhang-xiaoji-jian-ru-jia-jing-xie-gang-cheng-xu-yuan-de-shu-zi-you-min-zhuan-xing-zhi-lu]] stresses that sustainable revenue requires product, development, design, interaction, project management, and marketing to be good enough.
- Platform choice: [[zhang-xiaoji-jian-ru-jia-jing-xie-gang-cheng-xu-yuan-de-shu-zi-you-min-zhuan-xing-zhi-lu]] chooses Figma for growth tailwinds and iOS for payment willingness and limited development resources.
- Focus critique: [[2023-focusing-on-a-single-product-pays-off]] argues that moving from zero to early MRR is harder than later revenue growth, so unrelated small bets can repeat the most difficult stage too often.
- Compounding alternative: [[2023-focusing-on-a-single-product-pays-off]] says [[OnlineOrNot]] benefited from old prospects returning, early users converting, and operating fixes improving adoption over time.
- Pre-fit search: [[i-quit-my-job-and-i-have-one-year-to-get-to-profitability]] says Azimov planned to move beyond Sheet 2 Site if customers did not want it and later reported launching seven apps while reaching his monthly revenue goal.
- Creative and distribution reuse: [[tony-dinh-my-solopreneur-story-zero-to-45kmo-in-2-years]] says switching products reduced boredom and supplied fresh build-in-public material for Dinh's audience.
- Revenue and platform diversification: [[tony-dinh-my-solopreneur-story-zero-to-45kmo-in-2-years]] reports revenue from DevUtils, Black Magic, Xnapper, and Typing Mind, with Typing Mind growing as Twitter API pricing threatened Black Magic.
- Delegated maintenance: [[tony-dinh-my-solopreneur-story-zero-to-45kmo-in-2-years]] reports hiring one employee and three freelancers so products could keep moving without all work remaining founder-bound.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are successful retrospectives, not general base-rate studies. A portfolio can fragment attention, delay deeper product work, and increase support obligations unless the products are genuinely small and maintainable. Dinh's reported combined revenue does not disclose product-level margins, time allocation, churn, shared infrastructure, or the counterfactual return from focusing on one winner; his ability to hire help also limits comparison with a strictly solo operator. Rozen's critique should not be universalized either: unrelated small bets may still make sense when the founder is searching for demand, the products are deliberately tiny, or the goal is learning and optionality rather than compounding one proven product. Azimov's update gives no product-level revenue, maintenance burden, shutdown count, or sequence showing whether seven apps remained a portfolio or whether one winner produced the target, so it supports rapid search more strongly than durable diversification.

## What Changed
- Added a post-fit portfolio case where products provide creative variety, audience content, and protection from a platform-specific shock.
- Added delegation as one condition that can make multi-product maintenance more viable.
- Added missing product-level time, margin, churn, and focus-counterfactual evidence as explicit limits.
