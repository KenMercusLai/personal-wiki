## Definition
[[TeamProductivity]] is the net useful output of a group after accounting for coordination, rework, maintenance, decision quality, and each member's effect on everyone else's ability to contribute.

## Current Synthesis
[[ParkerThompson]] argues that the team, not the conspicuous individual developer, is the useful unit of engineering productivity. A person can appear exceptionally fast by shipping large amounts of code or intervening dramatically at night while imposing review, cleanup, incompatibility, and coordination costs on colleagues. Individual activity is therefore weak evidence unless the resulting system and surrounding team become more effective.

The management implication is diagnostic as well as cultural. Leaders should collect perspectives across the team, examine downstream work, and reward collaboration rather than hero narratives. This does not deny differences in individual ability; it changes the outcome being optimized from one person's visible production to durable collective delivery.

## Key Claims
- Engineering productivity should be evaluated at team level rather than inferred from one person's visible code output.
- Rework, cleanup, technical rigidity, and coordination costs can reverse the apparent value of a highly active individual.
- Hero narratives are partly incentive failures because leaders can reward emergency intervention while overlooking the work it creates for others.
- Team-wide evidence and team-oriented rewards are better aligned with durable collective output.
- Individual skill remains valuable when it increases the capability and decision quality of the surrounding team.

## Evidence
- Unit of output: [[10x-ing-your-team-the-end-of-superstar-developer-culture]] quotes Thompson saying that the unit of productivity is the team.
- Hidden downstream cost: [[10x-ing-your-team-the-end-of-superstar-developer-culture]] describes supposedly exceptional engineers whose late-night code leaves others rewriting or working around it.
- Incentive mechanism: [[10x-ing-your-team-the-end-of-superstar-developer-culture]] says non-technical leaders may reward visible hero behavior and recommends gathering performance evidence from the whole team.
- Collaboration and craft: [[10x-ing-your-team-the-end-of-superstar-developer-culture]] favors collaborative, problem-oriented craftspeople over rigid technology identity.
- Visual framing: [[10x-ing-your-team-the-end-of-superstar-developer-culture]] contrasts a stressed lone nighttime developer with a larger, happier daytime team.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is one interview and supplies no longitudinal productivity measures, controlled comparisons, or operational metric design. Team-level measurement can also hide individual underperformance or unfairly distribute credit unless responsibilities and evidence remain visible. The useful correction is to include downstream and collaborative effects, not to pretend individual contribution cannot be distinguished.
