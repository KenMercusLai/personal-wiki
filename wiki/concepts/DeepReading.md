## Definition
[[DeepReading]] is sustained, patient engagement with an extended text that allows cumulative argument, style, ambiguity, emotion, and meaning to develop beyond immediate fact extraction or utility.

## Current Synthesis
Harris frames deep reading as a learned attentional mode rather than the automatic result of looking at words. In this mode, a reader stays with one work long enough to be absorbed by an experience larger than an immediate goal. The contrast is not simply print versus screen: it is between patient continuity and a fragmented, novelty-seeking style organized around useful facts, shareable links, comments, points, and the next available stimulus.

The source argues that repeated media habits can travel across formats. A reader trained to expect interruption may bring scanning and impatience to a paperback, while a creator who consumes only compressed, provocative, useful fragments may begin writing to the same criteria. This makes reading quality—not merely the number of words encountered—the relevant concern. The retained illustration condenses that claim by layering app icons and notification badges over an open novel.

Recovery, in Harris's account, means practicing a different relationship to time: reading patiently, slowly, and sometimes “uselessly,” without requiring every passage to yield a tip or excerpt. Books then function as access to a less interrupted mental frame. This is a reflective prescription, not evidence that print is inherently deep, screens inherently shallow, or deliberate practice alone can reverse every attention difficulty.

Chongxi adds an interface-side complement: a screen can be deliberately shaped for continuity through readable type, wide line spacing, paragraph breathing room, a paper-like background, and removal of surprise or floating elements. Author details, licensing, and recommendations remain available but appear only after the article. This supports the distinction between medium and reading mode, although the source reports a design intention rather than measured comprehension, completion, or immersion.

## Key Claims
- Deep reading depends on sustained attention, tolerance for delay, and willingness to let meaning accumulate across an extended work.
- Reading volume and reading quality are distinct; a person can encounter abundant text while mostly scanning, extracting, and switching.
- Repeated novelty and interruption may train expectations that persist when the reader moves from a device to a book.
- Instrumental reading can feed back into instrumental writing optimized for brevity, provocation, shareability, and explicit usefulness.
- Deep reading is learned and effortful, which makes it vulnerable to disuse but also available for deliberate practice.
- Patient, non-instrumental book reading can serve as a counter-practice, though the source does not measure its effects.
- Digital article design can reduce avoidable attention competition through typography, spacing, restraint, and deferred secondary material.

## Evidence
- Phenomenology of lost continuity: [[i-have-forgotten-how-to-read-the-globe-and-mail]] describes paragraphs and sentences failing to cohere during an attempted uninterrupted chapter.
- Mode distinction: [[i-have-forgotten-how-to-read-the-globe-and-mail]] contrasts immersive book reading with online searching for useful facts, links, comments, and immediate stimuli.
- Quantity-versus-quality claim: [[i-have-forgotten-how-to-read-the-globe-and-mail]] argues that a text-saturated society can read more while exercising sustained attention less.
- Cross-format carryover: [[i-have-forgotten-how-to-read-the-globe-and-mail]] reports that screen-trained impatience follows Harris into paperback reading.
- Creation feedback: [[i-have-forgotten-how-to-read-the-globe-and-mail]] connects his changed reading habits to pressure for shorter, more provocative, less demanding prose.
- Counter-practice: [[i-have-forgotten-how-to-read-the-globe-and-mail]] proposes a changed reading diet and slow, patient reading without immediate utility.
- Interface support: [[my-blog-design]] removes pop-ups, floating sharing controls, progress indicators, and unrelated components while using spacious typography and placing secondary material after the article.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence consists of two first-person essays, not controlled or longitudinal studies. The cited researchers and public figures are summarized without enough study detail to assess effect size, causality, population scope, or competing explanations, while Chongxi supplies no usability, accessibility, comprehension, or completion measures. Print can be skimmed and screens can support long-form concentration; genre, purpose, interface design, literacy, disability, stress, sleep, and prior knowledge may matter as much as medium. Some readers may benefit from progress, navigation, or sharing aids that others find distracting. Goal-directed extraction is valuable for lookup and much practical nonfiction, while openness and continuity matter more for literature and cumulative arguments. “Deep” should therefore name a reading mode, not a moral ranking of every text or reader.

## What Changed
- Added digital interface design as a potential support for continuity rather than treating print as the only suitable environment.
- Preserved the distinction between intended calm and measured reading outcomes.
