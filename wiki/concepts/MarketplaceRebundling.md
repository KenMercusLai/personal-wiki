## Definition
[[MarketplaceRebundling]] is the consolidation of narrowly separated marketplace use cases into a broader category platform when adjacent transactions share users, liquidity, trust, or attention.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that Craigslist's apparent unbundling into dozens of specialized startups overstated the durability of very narrow verticals. A marketplace must generate enough repeated activity or enough value per transaction to sustain acquisition, liquidity, and monetization. When users naturally cross adjacent tasks—selling furniture and cars, hiring painters and handymen, applying across job functions, or discussing several topics—a broader category platform can reuse attention and participation across those jobs. Rebundling therefore describes a middle layer between one universal classifieds site and one app per micro-category: broad community, commerce, services, housing, or work platforms.

## Key Claims
- Category decomposition reveals possible entry wedges but does not by itself establish a sustainable standalone marketplace.
- Low transaction frequency can prevent a narrow marketplace from building a large recurring network.
- Low transaction value can constrain monetization even when a narrow service has some demand.
- Adjacent user jobs can make category breadth more convenient and can pool attention and marketplace activity.
- A large or valuable segment can remain a successful vertical when its economics are sufficient, with Airbnb offered as the source's main exception.

## Evidence
Entry wedges versus durable platforms:
- [[a-crowded-space-the-rebundling-of-craigslist]] compares the original Craigslist unbundling map with a later diagram marking many narrow entrants as dead, dying, or pivoted.

Frequency, value, and attention:
- [[a-crowded-space-the-rebundling-of-craigslist]] attributes narrow-marketplace failures to insufficient usage frequency or monetization potential and argues that users do not want dozens of separate apps for adjacent needs.

Category-level breadth:
- [[a-crowded-space-the-rebundling-of-craigslist]] presents OfferUp, Thumbtack, Reddit, Indeed, and Upwork as platforms spanning broad categories rather than individual Craigslist rows.

Vertical exception:
- [[a-crowded-space-the-rebundling-of-craigslist]] treats Airbnb as evidence that a sufficiently large, high-value section can support a major standalone company.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a short 2017 investor essay, not a systematic cohort study. Its failure annotations have no stated criteria, its proposed winners mix products with different business models and geographic scopes, and it supplies no data on liquidity, retention, acquisition cost, revenue, profitability, or later outcomes. The author's portfolio included OfferUp and Upwork, creating an incentive to foreground those examples. Rebundling is therefore a strategic hypothesis about marketplace breadth, not a rule that narrow entry is mistaken or that every category becomes a duopoly.

## What Changed
- Added a middle-layer model between universal horizontal marketplaces and single-use verticals.
- Separated the usefulness of a narrow entry wedge from the sustainability of staying narrow.
- Made transaction frequency, transaction value, and shared user attention explicit category-breadth tests.
