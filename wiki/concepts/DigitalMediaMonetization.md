## Definition
[[DigitalMediaMonetization]] is the problem of turning digital audience attention, content formats, brands, and platform distribution into durable revenue.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues against the search for one perfect digital media business model. BuzzFeed's preferred answer is a multi-revenue model: keep advertising, but add platform revenue, commerce, studio development, licensing, merchandising, subscriptions, and brand partnerships. The strategic claim is that digital media companies should use their data, audience focus, efficiency, and global reach to build diversified businesses rather than depending on one ad product or one platform.

## Key Claims
- Digital media companies should not expect one business model to solve the industry's economics.
- Direct-sold advertising is no longer enough for a mature digital media company.
- Platform revenue can grow, but publishers should not wait passively for platforms to pay fairly.
- Commerce, studio development, licensing, merchandising, and brand partnerships can turn media brands into multi-sided businesses.
- Revenue diversification requires organizational alignment, not just new sales experiments.

## Evidence
- Model critique: [[9-boxes]] lists native advertising, programmatic advertising, commerce, pivot-to-video, TV development, scale, and subscription niches as overhyped single-answer candidates.
- Revenue mix target: [[9-boxes]] says about a quarter of 2017 revenue would come from outside direct-sold advertising, rising to about one third in 2018 and around half in 2019.
- Platform revenue growth: [[9-boxes]] includes a chart showing direct revenue from Facebook, Amazon, Netflix, and Google rising from $7 million in 2014 to $52 million in 2017.
- Tasty example: [[9-boxes]] shows Tasty earning or seeking value through partnerships, books, apps, social distribution, products, and shows.
- Organization link: [[9-boxes]] ties content engines to advertising, commerce, and studio opportunities in the nine-box matrix.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The memo states targets and strategic direction but does not provide profit margins, cost structures, cash-flow evidence, or later outcome data. It also treats diversification as maturity, while a broader analysis would need to test whether new revenue lines reduce platform dependence or add operational complexity.
