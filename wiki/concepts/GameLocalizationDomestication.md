## Definition

Game localization domestication adapts names, setting cues, language, and cultural references toward the target culture so that translated players encounter functions and reactions closer to those intended in the source.

## Current Synthesis

For a game whose jokes permeate dialogue and character names, the source argues that preserving Japanese-looking names while inserting Chinese puns would create a hybrid effect unlike the original. Its Chinese localization therefore gives characters target-language names that preserve layered meanings and shifts the setting toward China. This is functional equivalence at the experience level, but it can strain continuity and cannot guarantee identical audience reactions.

## Key Claims

- Name translation may need to preserve semantic and phonetic joke functions, not just pronunciation or orthography.
- Widespread wordplay can make setting, naming, and dialogue mutually dependent localization decisions.
- Full domestication may produce a more coherent target-language experience than isolated pun substitutions.
- Audience reaction can provide useful qualitative feedback but does not prove equivalence across player populations.

## Evidence

### Layered name adaptation

- [[wei-jie-ru-he-yong-bao-li-ji-suan-fan-yi-xie-yin-geng-nu-xing-jiao-liu-fan-yi-bi-ji]] explains how 小栗久梨子 became 殷蒂娜 to retain a Chinese surname-plus-Western-given-name structure and the original sexual wordplay.

### Whole-setting rationale

- [[wei-jie-ru-he-yong-bao-li-ji-suan-fan-yi-xie-yin-geng-nu-xing-jiao-liu-fan-yi-bi-ji]] cites the American localization of Ace Attorney as precedent and reports similar streamer reactions across Japanese and Chinese versions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications

- The assessment relies on the localizer's interpretation and selected livestream observations, not systematic audience research.
- Moving the setting can create continuity problems or cultural incongruities elsewhere in a series.
- Players may prefer preservation of source-culture identity even when it weakens individual puns.
- A strategy suitable for a pun-saturated comedy may be inappropriate for historically or culturally anchored works.

- Added an experience-level account of why names and setting may need coordinated adaptation.
- Made explicit the tradeoff between functional wordplay and source-setting continuity.
