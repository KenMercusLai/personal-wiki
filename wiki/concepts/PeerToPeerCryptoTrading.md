## Definition
[[PeerToPeerCryptoTrading]] is direct cryptocurrency exchange between marketplace participants who negotiate price and settle through payment instruments such as gift-card codes rather than a conventional exchange order book.

## Current Synthesis
The documented broker used [[Paxful]] to connect international demand for [[Bitcoin]] with U.S. retail infrastructure. Buyers paid a bitcoin premium using gift-card codes; the broker redeemed those balances for fresh, internationally desirable gaming cards; and he resold the new codes to acquire more bitcoin. His income was the residual spread after marketplace fees and the premium charged by the next bitcoin seller.

The model combines access, informal work, and regulatory arbitrage with substantial trust and provenance risk. Platform history, dispute resolution, immediate in-store spending, and delayed bitcoin release helped manage the broker's exposure, but retailer policy changes could compress or close the conversion path. The same flexibility that serves buyers with few access options also makes the market usable for stolen-value laundering.

## Key Claims
- Peer-to-peer markets can connect global cryptocurrency demand with locally available payment and retail rails.
- Brokers earn a spread between the premium charged to buyers and the premium paid to replenish inventory.
- Gift-card liquidity depends on brand, denomination, geography, and downstream resale demand.
- Reputation, dispute resolution, delayed release, and immediate redemption are complementary risk controls.
- Retailer policy is part of the trading infrastructure because it determines whether received value can be converted safely.
- The model is dual-use: it can support lawful access and self-employment while also absorbing fraudulently obtained value.

## Evidence
- Cross-border access: [[inside-the-wild-west-world-of-gift-card-bitcoin-brokering]] follows a U.S. broker negotiating a $400 gift-card trade with a buyer he believed was in Kenya.
- Spread economics: [[inside-the-wild-west-world-of-gift-card-bitcoin-brokering]] reports a 28 percent bitcoin sale premium, a 25 percent repurchase premium, and a $12 residual margin.
- Inventory loop: [[inside-the-wild-west-world-of-gift-card-bitcoin-brokering]] describes received cards becoming PlayStation and Steam cards that were resold for bitcoin.
- Liquidity selection: [[inside-the-wild-west-world-of-gift-card-bitcoin-brokering]] says brands and denominations were chosen for overseas street and digital resale demand.
- Risk controls: [[inside-the-wild-west-world-of-gift-card-bitcoin-brokering]] documents transaction-history checks, delayed bitcoin release, complete balance redemption, and marketplace disputes.
- Policy dependence: [[inside-the-wild-west-world-of-gift-card-bitcoin-brokering]] shows Target and Best Buy restrictions shifting activity toward Walmart and Walmart's later rules threatening the broker's livelihood.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source profiles one anonymous broker and one observed transaction, so its markup, volume, income, and estimate of thousands of participants are not representative measurements. The buyer's location was not verified, neither card provenance nor downstream resale was independently audited, and the article predates later market, platform, regulatory, and retailer changes.

## What Changed
- Created a market-structure account of gift-card-settled peer-to-peer bitcoin trading.
- Distinguished gross bitcoin markups from the broker's much smaller residual spread.

## Related Concepts

- [[MarketplaceTrust]] - reputation and adjudication substitute partly for institutional counterparty knowledge.
- [[CryptoArbitrage]] - related spread-seeking practice, though this case carries payment-instrument and provenance exposure rather than only price exposure.
- [[CryptocurrencyMerchantAdoption]] - peer-to-peer access routes compensate when conventional purchase and payment rails are limited.
