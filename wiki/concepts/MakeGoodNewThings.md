## Definition
[[MakeGoodNewThings]] is a creative-life ethic that treats making valuable new artifacts as a way to express care, learn deeply, help others, and leave a meaningful trace of one's life.

## Current Synthesis
The source combines [[PaulGraham]]'s "make good new things" with [[SteveJobs]]' "make something wonderful" into a broad practice of creation. The point is not limited to startups or software: a creator might write essays, build apps, make visual work, craft physical objects, or produce small tools that solve a personal problem. The article's distinctive move is to treat creation as both intrinsically rewarding and economically asymmetric: a failed project still returns learning, system-building, solved pain, and satisfaction, while a successful project can help strangers and attract people with similar concerns.

## Key Claims
- Creation can be a lifelong orientation rather than a single project outcome.
- Good artifacts transmit care to people the creator may never meet.
- Writing remains valuable because personal experience, subtle feeling, and sustained inquiry are not replaced by generic AI text.
- Small tools and other projects can be worthwhile even when they do not find an audience.
- Creating after effort makes later rest and consumption feel different from pure passive consumption.
- Finished artifacts help people remember their lives and stay less captured by news and social feeds.

## Evidence
- Ethic source: [[blog-limboy-make-good-new-things]] uses Graham's phrase as the central thesis and connects it to Jobs' view that making something wonderful expresses appreciation to humanity.
- Form breadth: [[blog-limboy-make-good-new-things]] lists physical craft, laws of nature, blog posts, apps, and AI-assisted visual work as possible creative outputs.
- Writing value: [[blog-limboy-make-good-new-things]] says blogs can preserve travel, essays, technical records, reading reflections, fleeting ideas, and deep topic exploration.
- Asymmetric return: [[blog-limboy-make-good-new-things]] argues that even an ignored tool returns learning, solved personal pain, and achievement, while upside may include helping others and meeting peers.
- Presence and memory: [[blog-limboy-make-good-new-things]] says creation keeps attention in the present and leaves memorable knots along the rope of life.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a reflective argument, not a comparative study of creator well-being or project outcomes. Its value-investing analogy may fit low-cost personal projects better than work requiring large capital, team coordination, health risk, or high opportunity cost. The claim that AI cannot replace personal experience is strongest for reflective writing and weaker for standardized content.

## What Changed
- Created this concept to capture Limboy's synthesis of creation as care, learning, asymmetric return, and presence.
