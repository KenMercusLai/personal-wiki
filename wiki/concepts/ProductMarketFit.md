## Definition
[[ProductMarketFit]] is the point at which a startup has enough evidence that a real market wants its product to justify shifting from discovery and manual learning toward growth, hiring, revenue scaling, and organization-building.

## Current Synthesis
The sources treat product-market fit as the major boundary around hiring, sales, CEO priorities, and strategic focus. Before it, teams should stay small, learn intensely, keep founders close to customers, and avoid organizational inertia. After it, the company faces a different set of problems: fast recruiting, communication, culture, revenue expansion, and new growth avenues. The Maderight failure case adds that accelerator acceptance, venture funding, paying service customers, and partial product adoption are not substitutes for a coherent fit signal.

## Key Claims
- Hiring too early can create burn, inertia, and career-process concerns before the company knows what works.
- Evidence of product love can matter more than broad but weak adoption.
- Product-market fit changes the CEO role toward hiring, strategy, culture, and growth direction.
- Revenue scaling and new verticals or geographies become important after fit.
- Pre-fit sales should preserve founder learning about problems, willingness to pay, and solution shape.
- Revenue from a service path can obscure whether the technology product has found its own market.

## Evidence
- Slow early hiring: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] cites [[SamAltman]], [[Airbnb]], [[Dropbox]], and [[Stripe]] on waiting months before first hires.
- Customer love: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] cites [[BrianChesky]]'s lesson that 100 people who love the product matter more than one million who merely like it.
- Post-fit CEO priorities: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] says Chesky focused on hiring, strategy, and culture after product-market fit.
- Growth expansion: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] cites [[MariamNaficy]] on revenue scaling and new verticals or geographies after fit.
- Failed-fit case: [[4-lessons-from-a-failed-startup-from-and-for-first-time-founders]] says [[Maderight]] joined [[YCombinator]] and [[StartX]], raised venture funding, operated for three years, and still shut down without product-market fit.
- Pre-fit sales learning: [[4-lessons-from-a-failed-startup-from-and-for-first-time-founders]] argues sales before fit is about customer conversations, problem identification, and solution testing, with revenue as a byproduct.
- Service/product ambiguity: [[4-lessons-from-a-failed-startup-from-and-for-first-time-founders]] says Maderight's existing service customers wanted high-touch work while new customers adopted the product, splitting focus.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources assume venture-style startups where fast growth becomes possible and desirable after fit. They do not define a quantitative threshold for product-market fit, and the right hiring pace may differ for regulated, enterprise, service-heavy, or capital-intensive companies. Maderight also shows that some customer demand may validate a service business while leaving the software product's fit unresolved.

## What Changed
- Added Maderight as a failure case showing that funding, accelerators, service revenue, and partial adoption do not by themselves prove fit.
