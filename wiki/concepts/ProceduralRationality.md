## Definition
[[ProceduralRationality]] is the use of orderly, rational-looking procedures as proof that work is being handled properly, even when the procedure's substantive contribution is unclear.

## Current Synthesis
Barnett applies [[MaxWeber]]'s procedural-versus-substantive rationality distinction to modern meeting culture. Scheduled meetings, committees, and recurring calendar blocks can signal that work is being managed rationally, but the article argues that the signal can detach from actual purpose when people schedule first and ask why later. Procedural rationality therefore explains why meetings persist as legitimate organizational behavior even when they lack a clear problem, agenda, decision, or output.

## Key Claims
- Procedures can symbolize rational work without guaranteeing useful work.
- Recurring meetings are a common organizational form of procedural rationality.
- Meeting-first behavior can turn procedure into a solution looking for a problem.
- Substantive rationality requires asking why the meeting exists before protecting it as a calendar norm.
- Informal interaction can avoid some procedural drag because it ends when participants stop finding value.

## Evidence
- Weber frame: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] says meetings exemplify Weber's procedural rationality.
- Symbolic work: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] argues meetings show that work is being done rationally whether or not they help get things done.
- Agenda inversion: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] says people often set meetings and then decide what should be on the agenda.
- Informal contrast: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] argues informal chats continue only while they matter and can stop when value disappears.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source treats meeting overload polemically and does not deny that some scheduled meetings create alignment, accountability, decisions, or coordination. The useful distinction is not formal versus informal in every case, but procedure that serves a known purpose versus procedure protected because it looks rational.
