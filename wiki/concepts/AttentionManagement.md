## Definition
[[AttentionManagement]] is the deliberate protection and allocation of focus so cognitively important work is less disrupted by multitasking, noisy inputs, and avoidable interruptions.

## Current Synthesis
The wiki now frames attention management as a work-efficiency practice, a learning-input filter, a creative-production constraint, and an agency boundary. One source recommends single-tasking, reducing information sources, batching communication, going offline when the internet becomes distracting, and doing avoided work early so unfinished tasks do not intrude mentally. The INDIGO source adds that attention sharpens when a learner selects current creative topics and uses focused reading to ignore irrelevant material while noticing high-value sources. The tutorial-writing source adds that selecting one article and limiting work in progress can prevent topic overload and context switching during complex output work. The Durov podcast essay adds a stronger self-control frame: attention can be captured before work even begins if morning phone use lets other people's messages and feeds decide what the mind will process. Mission.org adds that attention is tested in recurring small choices: spare minutes, alarms, creative work, and time with loved ones become places where distraction spends scarce time.

## Key Claims
- Single-tasking is presented as more effective and less error-prone than multitasking.
- Information inputs should be simplified to preserve attention for valuable material.
- Similar tasks should be batched so messages and notifications do not repeatedly interrupt workflow.
- Offline work can be useful when connectivity creates more distraction than benefit.
- Focused reading can improve attention by turning broad intake into topic-driven filtering.
- Doing disliked tasks early and avoiding morning phone capture can reduce mental interference before or during focused work.
- Creative output benefits when work in progress is limited to one main piece and attention choices keep entertainment, snoozing, and phone checking from consuming time meant for learning, creating, or relationships.

## Evidence
- Single-tasking: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] states that people do not handle multitasking like machines and should focus on one task at a time.
- Input simplification: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] recommends reducing information sources and keeping the most valuable ones.
- Batching and offline work: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] advises planned processing of messages and offline work when browsing becomes distracting.
- Topic-driven filtering: [[feynman-technique-in-practice-indigo-information-acquisition-knowledge-output-methodology]] describes focused reading as ignoring unrelated information once one to three creative themes have been selected.
- Procrastination drag: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] recommends doing the most resisted task first so it no longer occupies mental space.
- Morning boundary: [[zhi-you-zi-lv-cai-neng-kan-dao-zhen-zheng-de-zi-you-durov-podcast-sui-bi]] argues that checking a phone first thing in the morning can hand the day's mental agenda to incoming feeds, messages, and low-value information.
- Work-in-progress limit: [[ru-he-xie-de-gan-huo-man-man-you-tong-su-yi-dong-cong-yi-pian-wen-zhang-fen-xiang-wo-de-chuang-zuo-jing-yan]] says the author chose one trailer-structure article rather than parallel drafts because switching among tasks reduces mental efficiency.
- Moment-by-moment attention cost: [[anything-is-possible-if-you-pay-the-price-mission-org-medium]] asks whether spare minutes, alarm wakeups, creative time, and time with loved ones are given to distraction or to chosen aims.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The productivity and Durov sources do not fully distinguish harmful interruption from necessary responsiveness. Some work requires real-time collaboration, monitoring, logistics, or rapid context switching, so the advice is strongest for work that allows protected focus blocks. The focused-reading and tutorial-writing approaches also depend on choosing themes early enough; narrowing too soon can reduce serendipitous discovery or defer worthwhile adjacent projects. Mission.org's framing can make ordinary rest or entertainment look like failure unless the person also accounts for recovery, care duties, and realistic capacity.

## What Changed
- Expanded attention management from workplace focus habits to include topic-driven reading filters.
- Added morning phone avoidance as an attention boundary tied to self-directed agency.
- Added work-in-progress limits as a creative-output focus practice.
- Added Mission.org's small-moment tests of attention as opportunity-cost evidence.
