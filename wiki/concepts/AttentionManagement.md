## Definition
[[AttentionManagement]] is the deliberate protection and allocation of focus so cognitively important work is less disrupted by multitasking, noisy inputs, and avoidable interruptions.

## Current Synthesis
The wiki now frames attention management as both a work-efficiency practice and a learning-input filter. One source recommends single-tasking, reducing information sources, batching communication, going offline when the internet becomes distracting, and doing avoided work early so unfinished tasks do not intrude mentally. The INDIGO source adds that attention sharpens when a learner selects current creative topics and uses focused reading to ignore irrelevant material while noticing high-value sources.

## Key Claims
- Single-tasking is presented as more effective and less error-prone than multitasking.
- Information inputs should be simplified to preserve attention for valuable material.
- Similar tasks should be batched so messages and notifications do not repeatedly interrupt workflow.
- Offline work can be useful when connectivity creates more distraction than benefit.
- Focused reading can improve attention by turning broad intake into topic-driven filtering.
- Doing disliked tasks first can reduce recurring mental interference from procrastinated work.

## Evidence
- Single-tasking: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] states that people do not handle multitasking like machines and should focus on one task at a time.
- Input simplification: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] recommends reducing information sources and keeping the most valuable ones.
- Batching and offline work: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] advises planned processing of messages and offline work when browsing becomes distracting.
- Topic-driven filtering: [[feynman-technique-in-practice-indigo-information-acquisition-knowledge-output-methodology]] describes focused reading as ignoring unrelated information once one to three creative themes have been selected.
- Procrastination drag: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] recommends doing the most resisted task first so it no longer occupies mental space.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The productivity source does not distinguish between harmful interruption and necessary responsiveness. Some work requires real-time collaboration, monitoring, or rapid context switching, so the advice is strongest for work that allows protected focus blocks. The focused-reading approach also depends on choosing themes early enough; narrowing too soon can reduce serendipitous discovery.

## What Changed
- Expanded attention management from workplace focus habits to include topic-driven reading filters.
