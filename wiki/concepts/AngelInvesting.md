## Definition
[[AngelInvesting]] is direct early-stage startup funding by individuals who put their own personal money into a company, in contrast to venture capitalists who deploy capital pooled from limited partners.

## Current Synthesis
TechCrunch reduces the angel-versus-venture-capital distinction to a single question: whose money is it? An angel invests personal capital. A VC manages a fund supplied by LPs, with a typical ten-year life and an expectation of returns through exits. Both are equity investors who take ownership stakes and lose their money if the company fails, so the difference is not risk appetite in kind but the capital source, the time horizon, and the set of people the investor answers to.

The distinction matters to founders because the same early round can bring in either kind of investor, and the source notes that investors also specialize by stage, presenting themselves as seed-stage or late-stage funds. The article does not argue that one is better. It treats angels and VCs as two suppliers of the same product - ownership capital for a growing company - with different structures behind the check, which is why the wiki keeps the fund-level structure in [[VentureCapitalFundStructure]] and the round-level event in [[StartupFundingRound]].

## Key Claims
- The defining difference is the capital source: angels invest their own personal money, while VCs manage a fund.
- Both are equity investors who receive ownership rather than a repayment obligation, so they share the loser's downside if the startup fails.
- VC capital comes with fund-level obligations, including LP money and a typical ten-year fund life with returns expected at the end.
- Investors commonly specialize by stage, labeling themselves seed-stage or late-stage funds rather than covering every round.
- Because the capital sources differ, founders raising an early round may be choosing between individual investors and fund managers with different constraints and horizons.

## Evidence
- Core distinction: [[wtf-is-a-funding-round-techcrunch]] says angels invest their own personal money and VCs manage a fund.
- Shared equity risk: [[wtf-is-a-funding-round-techcrunch]] says investors who fund a startup receive an ownership stake and lose out if the company goes bust.
- Fund-level obligations: [[wtf-is-a-funding-round-techcrunch]] describes LP-funded venture funds with ten-year life cycles and returns expected from exits.
- Stage specialization: [[wtf-is-a-funding-round-techcrunch]] says many investors specialize in different stages and label themselves "seed-stage funds" or "late-stage funds".

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The definition is a single sentence in a general explainer, and it says nothing about how angels actually operate: typical check sizes, syndicates and rolling funds, accreditation rules, the use of SAFEs or convertible notes, tax treatment, or how angels differ from one another in involvement and expertise. The article also implies a clean angel-versus-VC divide that in practice blurs when angels invest in funds, when VCs write personal checks, or when a seed round includes both. The ten-year fund life and carried-interest description belongs to the fund structure rather than to angel investing, and the source provides no performance comparison between the two.
