## Definition
[[HiringSystemDesign]] is the deliberate construction of hiring as a measurable, candidate-facing organizational system: define the team outcome, turn it into evidence criteria and interactions, assign decisions, observe results, and revise the process over time.

## Current Synthesis
[[DanPupius]] frames hiring as two coupled products: the process candidates experience and the team it produces. The design starts with a specific account of the whole team's needed traits, values, skills, and composition. Those needs become a rubric of defined attributes and observable behaviors, not a license to reward pedigree, affinity, or an interviewer's unsupported impression.

The process then needs plural ways for candidates to show relevant ability, advance disclosure of the rubric, calibrated interviewers, and a single accountable decision-maker who uses the panel's evidence without requiring consensus. Measurement operates on different time horizons: funnel movement and candidate feedback reveal immediate friction; offer acceptance and employee sentiment provide intermediate signals; retention is a delayed and confounded outcome. The system should remain documented and change through focused retrospectives before repeatable parts are automated.

## Key Claims
- Hiring design should begin with the team system the organization wants, not a collection of individually impressive candidates.
- Criteria should be defined in behavioral terms, while pedigree and other weak or bias-inducing signals are explicitly excluded.
- Fair evaluation requires transparent expectations, multiple job-relevant demonstration modes, and calibrated interviewers.
- Decision authority should be explicit so evidence can inform a timely decision without forcing panel consensus.
- Candidate experience is a product outcome even for rejected applicants and should feed process improvement.
- Metrics and themed iteration can make the process learn, but no single measure proves hiring quality or fairness.

## Evidence
- Product frame and team vision: [[develop-your-hiring-system-like-a-product-to-eliminate-bias-and-boost-retention-first-round-review]] treats the process and team as coupled products and asks founders to define complementary team needs.
- Evidence discipline: [[develop-your-hiring-system-like-a-product-to-eliminate-bias-and-boost-retention-first-round-review]] requires judgments to cite candidate behavior against a rubric and recommends excluding schools, grades, prior employers, and connections at Range.
- Candidate access: [[develop-your-hiring-system-like-a-product-to-eliminate-bias-and-boost-retention-first-round-review]] recommends sharing the rubric and allowing several relevant ways to demonstrate aptitude.
- Interviewer reliability: [[develop-your-hiring-system-like-a-product-to-eliminate-bias-and-boost-retention-first-round-review]] describes two shadows followed by two reverse shadows before independent interviewing at Medium.
- Governance: [[develop-your-hiring-system-like-a-product-to-eliminate-bias-and-boost-retention-first-round-review]] assigns the hiring manager the final decision and asks dissenting panelists to commit to the hire's success.
- Feedback loop: [[develop-your-hiring-system-like-a-product-to-eliminate-bias-and-boost-retention-first-round-review]] combines funnel analytics, demographic patterns, candidate surveys, employee pulse checks, retention, and quarterly themed retrospectives.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework comes from one operator interview about Medium and early-stage Range, not a controlled comparison. The source reports positive candidate response and process changes but gives no selection-rate, performance, retention, demographic, or causal outcome data. Behavioral rubrics can still encode disputed values, observable behavior can be interpreted unevenly, alternative exercises can differ in burden and validity, and interviewer shadowing can reproduce a shared bias as easily as a shared standard. Offer acceptance reflects compensation and labor-market conditions as well as screening quality, while retention can reflect constrained mobility or exclusion as well as fit. Informal backchannels also raise fairness, privacy, and legal questions the article does not address.

## What Changed
- Hiring is modeled as a coupled process-and-team product with explicit feedback loops.
- Candidate experience and fairness become measured system outcomes, not recruiting polish.
- Transparency, multiple demonstration modes, and interviewer calibration become core reliability controls.
