## Definition
[[EmployeeTermination]] is the end-to-end process by which an organization prepares for, decides, communicates, executes, and follows through on an involuntary employment departure.

## Current Synthesis
[[ZachHolman]] argues that termination quality cannot be reduced to decision speed. Before dismissal, clear recurring feedback and a good-faith performance improvement process can expose expectations, permit recovery, and reduce surprise. At the decision point, the power and experience asymmetry matters: the company may run the process repeatedly while the employee is shocked and poorly positioned to evaluate legal, financial, or equity terms immediately.

Execution combines dignity with operational control. The employer can revoke access and rotate credentials promptly without humiliation, but must understand dependencies involving personal files, corporate email, healthcare, payroll, stock administration, and other services. Communication to remaining staff should preserve legitimate privacy while accurately distinguishing a unilateral decision from a mutual departure. The process continues through written separation terms, severance, benefits, tax documents, equity deadlines, a durable contact path, coworker support, and alumni relations.

The source's strongest contribution is systemic: treatment of departing people affects not only the manager and employee but also trust among remaining staff, security, recruiting, alumni advocacy, and the culture carried into later organizations. Its prescriptions nevertheless require jurisdiction-specific employment, tax, benefits, discrimination, accommodation, and collective-representation safeguards that the article does not supply.

## Key Claims
- Clear expectations and a genuine opportunity to improve can reduce harmful surprise without guaranteeing continued employment.
- The employee's emotional and informational disadvantage makes immediate signatures or commitments especially risky.
- Secure offboarding should revoke access promptly while accounting for personal data and benefit, payroll, and equity dependencies.
- Truthful internal communication must balance privacy with the trust cost of falsely presenting unilateral decisions as mutual.
- Separation terms, benefits, taxes, equity, and post-employment contact are core process elements, not cleanup tasks.
- Coworkers and alumni networks can support recovery, recruiting, knowledge continuity, and cultural reputation after departure.

## Evidence
- Warning and recovery: [[firing-people]] recommends clear expectations and a good-faith performance improvement plan as protection against surprise dismissal.
- Decision asymmetry: [[firing-people]] advises dismissed employees to pause, avoid immediate commitments, obtain terms in writing, and consult independent advisers because management is usually more practiced and less distressed.
- Operational offboarding: [[firing-people]] recommends automated credential rotation and access removal while tracing effects on personal data and employment services.
- Organizational trust: [[firing-people]] argues that misleading internal departure language poisons confidence in later management communication.
- Continuing obligations: [[firing-people]] identifies severance, healthcare, taxes, stock, follow-up communication, and an `alumni@` contact as parts of responsible separation.
- Relationship continuity: [[firing-people]] describes coworker outreach and the self-organized GitHub alumni network as sources of emotional support, networking, and future recruiting value.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is Holman's retrospective first-person account plus stories he says he heard from others, not a comparative study of termination practices or outcomes. His own firing rationale remained unclear, so the account cannot establish why GitHub acted or which intervention would have changed the decision. The prescriptions primarily reflect a California technology-startup setting in 2015-2016; employment law, notice, representation, benefits, stock taxation, and required procedure vary materially across jurisdictions and contracts.

The article's “don't talk to HR” maxim is better read as a warning about incentive alignment than a universal rule. Internal reporting may be necessary, and employees may also need a union representative, lawyer, regulator, ombuds function, or other protected channel. Performance improvement plans can clarify expectations and constrain bias when genuine, but can also become pretextual documentation for a predetermined outcome. Prompt access revocation is prudent security practice only when implemented proportionately and without destroying personal property or blocking essential records and benefits.

## What Changed
- Created an end-to-end model joining performance clarity, the termination meeting, secure access removal, communication, separation terms, recovery, and alumni relations.
- Added the employee's emotional and informational disadvantage as a procedural design constraint.
- Distinguished truthful privacy-preserving communication from euphemisms that misrepresent a unilateral departure.
