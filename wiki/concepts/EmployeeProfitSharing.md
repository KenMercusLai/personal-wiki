## Definition
[[EmployeeProfitSharing]] is compensation that distributes a defined portion of company profit to employees, linking payouts to realized operating economics rather than only salary or the future sale value of equity.

## Current Synthesis
The Wistia case presents profit sharing as a compensation fit for an independent company that no longer plans around a near-term acquisition or IPO. Replacing employee options with profit sharing made the economic connection more concrete: an infrastructure team's proposed two-to-three-point gross-margin improvement could increase a regularly paid pool for everyone. That immediacy may make operating finance more legible, but the source does not specify the formula, distribution rules, payout history, or how employees valued the lost equity upside.

## Key Claims
- Profit sharing can provide recurring realized value when employee options lack a credible near-term liquidity path.
- A visible formula can connect revenue, cost, margin, and operational improvements to employee compensation.
- Company-wide sharing can make financial performance relevant beyond executives and shareholders.
- Profit-based payouts are variable and can fall when the company reinvests, margins contract, or accounting definitions change.
- Replacing options trades uncertain exit upside for nearer-term participation and need not be better for every employee or outcome.

## Evidence
- Compensation reset: [[heres-how-and-why-entrepreneurs-are-getting-venture-investors-out-of-their-companies-inc-com]] reports that Wistia replaced employee stock options with a profit-sharing plan after its investor buyout.
- Liquidity contrast: [[heres-how-and-why-entrepreneurs-are-getting-venture-investors-out-of-their-companies-inc-com]] says employees struggled to value their options and expected a long wait before any payout, while profit sharing was more concrete and intended to pay regularly.
- Operating response: [[heres-how-and-why-entrepreneurs-are-getting-venture-investors-out-of-their-companies-inc-com]] reports broad excitement when an infrastructure team identified a possible two-to-three-point gross-margin improvement that could enlarge profit-sharing value.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is a founder's anecdote about one company shortly after the change. The source does not provide plan documents, eligibility, allocation, payout size, employee interviews, retention effects, accounting definitions, or comparison with the surrendered option value. Profit sharing can encourage useful cost awareness, but it can also make employees sensitive to short-term accounting choices or discourage investments whose benefits arrive later. Its fairness depends on transparent formulas and on whether employees can influence the measured profit.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Wistia's shift from illiquid option upside to more immediate participation in operating profit.
