## Definition
[[Metaknowledge]] is knowledge about knowledge: awareness of what one knows or does not know, and of how one's knowledge compares with other people's knowledge.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents metaknowledge as a measurable signal for improving surveys and crowd forecasts. Instead of trusting raw confidence, researchers can ask respondents for their own answer and for a prediction of how others will answer. Respondents whose predictions better match the group's actual distribution often have better knowledge, better awareness of disagreement, or more honest access to hidden behavior in their community.

## Key Claims
- Metaknowledge can be measured indirectly by comparing predictions about others with actual response distributions.
- Good metaknowledge can identify expertise because experts often know which facts others are likely to miss.
- Metaknowledge can expose hidden or socially undesirable behavior through differences between self-reporting and predictions about peers.
- Metaknowledge can create incentives for honest survey answers when rewards depend on collective truthfulness.
- Surprise at widespread disagreement can indicate novice status or weak domain calibration.

## Evidence
- Measurement procedure: [[a-mathematical-bs-detector-can-boost-the-wisdom-of-crowds-aeon-essays]] describes asking respondents for a response and a prediction of agreement.
- Expertise signal: [[a-mathematical-bs-detector-can-boost-the-wisdom-of-crowds-aeon-essays]] says students who knew Springfield was Illinois's capital also better predicted that many classmates would choose Chicago.
- Hidden behavior: [[a-mathematical-bs-detector-can-boost-the-wisdom-of-crowds-aeon-essays]] describes survey logic where people who deny an activity but expect peers to admit it reveal useful information.
- Honesty incentive: [[a-mathematical-bs-detector-can-boost-the-wisdom-of-crowds-aeon-essays]] reports a research-malfeasance survey where metaknowledge-based incentives increased admissions of data fabrication.
- Calibration warning: [[a-mathematical-bs-detector-can-boost-the-wisdom-of-crowds-aeon-essays]] quotes Prelec's advice that being surprised by disagreement can show novice-level metaknowledge.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Metaknowledge is not perfect and cannot directly verify any single respondent's truthfulness or factual accuracy. The source also warns that strong consensus remains important; metaknowledge is most useful where naive aggregation is distorted or where disagreement contains hidden information.
