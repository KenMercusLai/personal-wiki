## Definition
[[CreativeOccupationCriteria]] are Nesta's five heuristic tests for whether an occupation uses cognitive skill to produce novel or significantly improved outputs whose final form is not fully specified in advance.

## Current Synthesis
The framework tests whether work uses novel processes, resists complete mechanization, produces non-repetitive or non-uniform outputs, contributes creativity independently of its industry, and requires interpretation rather than direct transformation. The five criteria are meant to classify the intrinsic character of work instead of assuming that every role inside a creative industry is creative.

Applied to data science, the article awards full points for novel process, variable outputs, cross-industry creative contribution, and interpretation, plus half a point for disputed resistance to automation. The resulting 4.5/5 exceeds the prior research's baseline threshold of four, but the authors explicitly treat occupational scoring as subjective and robustness-sensitive.

## Key Claims
- Novel process asks whether a role repeatedly develops or selects new approaches.
- Mechanization resistance asks whether technology augments the work or can wholly replace it.
- Non-repetitiveness asks whether learning, context, and judgment make project outputs hard to predict.
- Creative contribution asks whether novelty belongs to the work rather than merely its industry.
- Interpretation asks whether translation from question to output leaves meaningful discretion.
- A threshold score is a classification aid, not an objective measurement of creative value.

## Evidence
- Framework definition: [[data-science-work-is-creative-work-p1]] states the five criteria and the broader definition of creative occupations.
- Data-science application: [[data-science-work-is-creative-work-p1]] maps exploration and lateral transfer to novel process, new projects to non-uniformity, cross-sector use to independent contribution, and research design to interpretation.
- Threshold and uncertainty: [[data-science-work-is-creative-work-p1]] reports a baseline creative threshold of four and a provisional data-science score of 4.5 because mechanization receives only half a point.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework is heuristic and its scores depend on judgment about occupational descriptions. Mechanization resistance can change with technology, broad criteria may produce false positives across knowledge work, and occupation-level labels can hide variation among tasks, seniority levels, organizations, and specialties. The source does not test inter-rater reliability, predictive validity, or whether classification improves labor-market, education, or management decisions.
