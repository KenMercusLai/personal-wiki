## Definition
[[VentureCapitalPortfolioSizing]] is the practice of choosing how many companies a VC fund should back so its exposure matches startup attrition, power-law return concentration, reserve policy, and the fund's ability to capture rare large winners.

## Current Synthesis
McClure frames VC portfolio size as a statistical exposure problem. In his model, most startups fail or return little, a modest share produce small exits, a few become $100M-plus "centaurs," and only about 1-2% become billion-dollar "unicorns." Because those rare outcomes dominate returns, a small portfolio can be less a demonstration of investor conviction than a high-variance bet that may simply miss every outlier. The source therefore recommends much larger portfolios than many traditional VC funds use, especially at seed stage where attrition is higher.

## Key Claims
- VC portfolio construction should start from the frequency of large outcomes, not only from partner attention or board-seat capacity.
- Small portfolios can produce excellent returns when they happen to include a unicorn, but they can also miss all outlier winners.
- With unicorn rates around 1-2%, fewer than 50-100 investments gives a fund a weak statistical shot unless it has exceptional selection ability.
- Early-stage portfolios need more companies than later-stage portfolios because company-level attrition is higher before traction and financing are proven.
- Follow-on reserves, fees, deployment timing, and recycling matter, but they do not remove the basic rare-winner exposure problem.

## Evidence
- Outcome distribution: [[99-vc-problems-but-a-batch-aint-one-why-portfolio-size-matters-for-returns]] says 500 Startups expected 50-80% of investments to return less than 1X, 15-25% to return 2-5X, 5-10% to become $100M-plus centaurs, and 1-2% to become unicorns.
- Size recommendation: [[99-vc-problems-but-a-batch-aint-one-why-portfolio-size-matters-for-returns]] recommends 50-100 companies for later-stage funds and at least 100-200, possibly 500, for early-stage funds.
- Concentration risk: [[99-vc-problems-but-a-batch-aint-one-why-portfolio-size-matters-for-returns]] argues that portfolios under 50-100 companies are gambling with LP money if unicorn hit rates are only 1-2%.
- Model qualification: [[99-vc-problems-but-a-batch-aint-one-why-portfolio-size-matters-for-returns]] explicitly excludes management fees, expenses, follow-on capital, deployment timing, recycling, and other reserve mechanics from its simple model.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is an opinionated simple model based partly on 500 Startups' experience and does not prove that every firm can maintain selection quality, founder support, pro-rata access, or governance quality across hundreds of investments. It also acknowledges that follow-on strategy and fund economics are omitted, so the numerical thresholds should be treated as a power-law exposure heuristic rather than a complete fund model.
