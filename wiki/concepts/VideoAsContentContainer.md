## Definition
[[VideoAsContentContainer]] is the use of video, GIF-like media, or a platform-controlled audiovisual stream as a portable wrapper for text, animation, sound, live action, interface-like sequences, and advertising rather than as a single conventional genre.

## Current Synthesis
[[video-is-the-new-html-benedict-evans]] argues that “video” became comparable to HTML when social and mobile platforms used it to package many kinds of rich content into units that could autoplay, travel through feeds, and render consistently inside proprietary surfaces. The strategic change is larger than richer presentation: the container joins editorial format to distribution, discovery, analytics, advertising, and platform control. Snapchat Discover, Facebook video, AMP, Instant Articles, and Instant Apps use different technologies, but all illustrate movement away from publisher-controlled plain HTML toward optimized platform runtimes.

## Key Claims
- Video containers can combine motion, sound, text, animation, live action, and advertising in one shareable unit.
- A file labeled “video” may function more like a Flash experience or content card than like television footage.
- Platform-native containers tie the creative format to a particular feed, curation model, runtime, metric system, and revenue model.
- Portable audiovisual units can reduce dependence on conventional webpages while increasing dependence on the platform that renders and measures them.
- The relevant strategic comparison is not simply video versus text, but competing containers for delivering complete experiences on mobile screens.

## Evidence
- Rich container: [[video-is-the-new-html-benedict-evans]] describes Snapchat Discover content as a mixture of sound, motion, animation, text, and occasional live action delivered through video files.
- Portable card: [[video-is-the-new-html-benedict-evans]] says GIFs and video can encapsulate many kinds of content, travel through social feeds, and include advertising.
- Runtime comparison: [[video-is-the-new-html-benedict-evans]] compares video delivery with Instant Apps, AMP, and Instant Articles as attempts to provide richer or faster experiences than ordinary HTML and JavaScript.
- Distribution coupling: [[video-is-the-new-html-benedict-evans]] uses BuzzFeed's Q3 2015 view mix to show that content format and acquisition channel were already intertwined across direct, Facebook, Snapchat, YouTube, and search surfaces.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept comes from one 2016 strategy essay and describes a historical transition rather than a stable technical standard. Video is less semantic, searchable, accessible, and interactive than HTML unless a platform adds supporting structure, while large audiovisual payloads can increase bandwidth and production costs. The BuzzFeed chart is one publisher's Q3 2015 snapshot, and later product closures, algorithm changes, web standards, short-form video systems, and creator tools may alter the balance between open documents and proprietary containers.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish video as a delivery container from video as a live-action genre.
- Connected portable rich media to platform-controlled distribution, measurement, and monetization.
