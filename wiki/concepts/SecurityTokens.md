## Definition
[[SecurityTokens]] are blockchain-based tokens that represent investable assets such as private-company equity, real estate, art, collectibles, or other securities-like claims.

## Current Synthesis
The Fortune article presents security tokens as a possible bridge between crypto infrastructure and conventional finance. For [[Coinbase]], they offered a diversification path beyond consumer trading fees: if ownership claims could be digitized, transferred, and compliance-checked on-chain, Coinbase could help operate markets for alternative assets that are currently illiquid and paperwork-heavy. The opportunity depends less on speculative coin trading than on whether token rails can lower record-keeping, compliance, and transaction costs while satisfying securities regulation.

## Key Claims
- Security tokens shift blockchain use from native cryptocurrencies toward tokenized claims on external assets.
- Tokenization is pitched as a liquidity mechanism for private companies, real estate, art, and collectibles.
- Compliance-aware code and regulated intermediaries are central because these tokens represent securities-like assets.
- Coinbase's interest in security tokens was partly a revenue-diversification response to falling trading volume and fee pressure.
- The concept remained early and speculative in the article, with mainstream investors waiting for clearer regulation.

## Evidence
- Asset scope: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] says security tokens could represent privately held companies, art, collectibles, and real estate.
- Coinbase opportunity: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] names security tokens as Coinbase's most promising project for creating an alternative investment market.
- Real-estate example: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] cites David Sacks on tokenizing U.S. real estate and the St. Regis Aspen owners selling a 19% hotel stake as tokens.
- Compliance claim: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] cites Harbor as writing code to keep tokens compliant with securities laws and Preston Byrne on streamlining compliance and record-keeping.
- Regulatory caution: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] reports that mainstream fund managers were waiting for a stronger regulatory framework before investing.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source describes a 2018 opportunity thesis, not proof that tokenized securities became a large liquid market. It also relies heavily on advocates and consultants; it gives less evidence about investor demand, settlement risk, custody risk, securities-law interpretation, secondary-market depth, or whether blockchains outperform conventional databases for these assets.
