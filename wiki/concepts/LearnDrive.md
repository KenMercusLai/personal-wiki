## Definition
[[LearnDrive]] is the intrinsic drive to learn, powered by curiosity, existing knowledge, perceived value, and the learner's real-time valuation of available information.

## Current Synthesis
The sources treat learn drive as the fragile center of good learning. Curiosity can push a mature mind very far without external discipline, but young learners often need goals, dreams, small acts of exploratory study, and room to form their own questions so institutional pressure or rote demands do not suppress interest. Knowledge also compounds: the more meaningful knowledge someone has, the more additional knowledge they tend to want. This qualifies any simple opposition between curiosity and knowledge; the stronger pattern is a feedback loop in which curiosity directs learning and accumulated understanding enables better questions.

The newer knowledge-valuation source makes learn drive more dynamic. It describes learn drive as a matching process between the brain's current semantic network and real-time valuation signals from available information. In this view, [[FreeLearning]] protects learn drive because the learner can follow high-value, coherent, and applicable information, while [[CoerciveLearning]] and [[ReverseLearning]] can drain curiosity by repeatedly pairing study with low value, false rewards, mild punishment, or helpless passivity.

The curiosity essay adds an action layer. Questions can accelerate learning by prompting observation, estimation, cross-role inquiry, conceptual exploration, and market-facing experiments. Its strongest anti-knowledge and anti-schooling language remains source-scoped: ignorance can remove inhibition, but it can also conceal important constraints, and formal learning can support inquiry when it protects autonomy and supplies needed foundations.

## Key Claims
- Curiosity and learn drive can produce high achievement without explicit discipline, especially when questions lead to observation, experiments, building, and feedback.
- Goals can protect learn drive by connecting study to personally valued knowledge.
- Learn drive is vulnerable to coercive schooling, credential competition, and rote memorization.
- Small amounts of exploratory reading can seed interest and rebuild momentum, while stopping study entirely can weaken drive by breaking habits.
- Accumulated knowledge can become self-propelling because knowledge increases the desire for more knowledge.
- Learn drive operates by matching current knowledge structures with valuation signals from available information.
- Free learning and healthy developmental conditions protect learn drive, while coercive schooling and reverse learning can suppress it.

## Evidence
- Curiosity as force: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] says curiosity and learn drive can carry human thought to exceptional heights.
- Goal connection: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] says goals give knowledge a value center from which love of learning can grow.
- Institutional risk: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] warns that young minds are often constrained by institutions and environments.
- Exploratory reading: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] proposes five to ten minutes of inspired reading as a way to find seeds of interest.
- Habit continuity: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] says stopping learning can reduce drive and break study habits rather than clearing the mind.
- Knowledge compounding: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] compares knowledge to a self-propelled vehicle: the more one knows, the more one wants to know.
- Real-time valuation: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] says learn drive matches the current semantic network with real-time input from the knowledge valuation network.
- Free-learning protection: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] argues that play, broad behavioral space, low stress, and [[FreeLearning]] help develop healthy valuation networks.
- Coercive-learning risk: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] says one-size-fits-all schooling, false rewards, and mild punishment can shut down knowledge valuation and drain learn drive.
- Question-led action: [[curiosity-is-better-than-knowledge-mission-org-medium]] connects curiosity to fast learning through business-estimation games, cross-role investigation, abstract inquiry, and building [[Praxis]].
- Assigned-question risk: [[curiosity-is-better-than-knowledge-mission-org-medium]] argues that punishment, recitation, and limits on permissible exploration can suppress curiosity.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources offer motivational theory and examples, not a controlled account of how to measure learn drive. They also distinguish between learners: established experts with deep knowledge may need fewer explicit supports than students whose motivation is still forming. The stronger anti-schooling claims are source-scoped and do not by themselves prove that every formal learning environment suppresses learn drive. The claim that knowledge kills action is rhetorical and conflicts with the other sources' account of knowledge as a basis for valuation and further curiosity; expertise can also prevent unsafe or wasteful experimentation.

## What Changed
- Added question-led observation, experimentation, and building as ways curiosity becomes rapid learning.
- Reconciled the new essay's curiosity-versus-knowledge slogan with the existing knowledge-curiosity feedback loop.
- Expanded the schooling qualification to distinguish coercive question control from formal learning generally.
