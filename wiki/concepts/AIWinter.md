## Definition
[[AIWinter]] is a period of reduced confidence, funding, and practical expectation for artificial-intelligence research after a wave of claims outruns demonstrated capability.

## Current Synthesis
The sources place AI winter inside a recurring expectation cycle. The 2016 Economist excerpt looks backward: a 1956 proposal expected major progress from one summer, subsequent disappointment made researchers prefer narrower labels such as expert systems or neural networks, and the 2012 ImageNet result helped rehabilitate “AI.” [[FilipPiekniewski]] then applies the same pattern prospectively in 2018. Conferences, corporate press releases, executive promises, and high-profile slogans still projected acceleration, but autonomous-driving progress, DeepMind's practical uncertainty, OpenAI's quieter media cycle, and research-community sentiment suggested to him that deep-learning expectations were weakening. AI winter is therefore best treated as a mismatch-and-reputation cycle rather than a precisely dated technical state.

## Key Claims
- AI winter can begin as a narrative fracture before institutions openly admit disappointment.
- The reputational cycle can alter the field's vocabulary, pushing researchers toward narrower labels and later restoring a broader “AI” identity after a visible success.
- The clearest stress test is a real-world application where public promises meet safety, physics, and unpredictable environments.
- Corporate and celebrity incentives can sustain optimistic messaging after technical progress has slowed.
- A crash in expectations is hard to time precisely, but signs can accumulate in visible failures, quieter research sentiment, and retreat from application claims.

## Evidence
- Narrative fracture: [[ai-winter-is-well-on-its-way-piekniewskis-blog]] contrasts ongoing AI press enthusiasm with signs that the 2014-2016 deep-learning acceleration story had weakened by mid-2018.
- Earlier expectation cycle: [[from-not-working-to-neural-networking-technology]] contrasts the optimism of a 1956 summer-research proposal with decades of disappointment and avoidance of the “AI” label.
- Reputational recovery: [[from-not-working-to-neural-networking-technology]] links renewed use of “AI” to the visible 2012 ImageNet performance jump.
- Real-world stress test: [[ai-winter-is-well-on-its-way-piekniewskis-blog]] identifies autonomous driving as the place where cracks are most visible because it forces AI systems into open-world perception and action.
- Messaging inertia: [[ai-winter-is-well-on-its-way-piekniewskis-blog]] cites continued self-driving promises and "AI is bigger than electricity" style slogans as examples of narrative strength despite application problems.
- Timing uncertainty: [[ai-winter-is-well-on-its-way-piekniewskis-blog]] compares predicting AI winter to predicting a market crash: signs can be clear while timing and depth remain unknowable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Piekniewski's source is an opinionated 2018 forecast, not an empirical funding-cycle study. Its interpretation depends heavily on autonomous-driving evidence and public sentiment signals, so it should not be treated as a complete account of all AI subfields or later LLM-era developments. The Economist capture is only a two-paragraph historical excerpt, and its attribution of AI's rehabilitation to one contest compresses concurrent changes in data, compute, institutions, and investment. Neither source supplies a consistent quantitative definition for when an AI winter begins or ends.
