## Definition
[[HardwareAfterlives]] are the useful, social, or expressive roles a physical product acquires after its intended commercial lifecycle through repair, customization, accessories, software, and creative reinterpretation.

## Current Synthesis
The original [[GameBoy]] persists not only through nostalgia or collection but through active modification and use. Owners replace or paint shells, add backlights and controls, alter audio circuitry, install synthesizer hardware, run cartridge-based music tools, and make photographs with the Game Boy Camera and Printer. These activities preserve some constraints while removing others: a player may improve screen visibility, a musician may extend the audio path, and a photographer may deliberately retain the Camera's low resolution. A hardware afterlife is therefore selective continuity, in which communities decide which limitations are defects, which are identity, and which can become new creative affordances.

## Key Claims
- Repair can be the entry point to deeper technical knowledge and product customization.
- Aftermarket parts and documented modifications turn discontinued hardware into an adaptable platform.
- Creative reuse extends a device by changing its purpose, not merely restoring its original function.
- Technical limitations can become valued aesthetic signatures when users choose to preserve them.
- Continued use depends on surviving devices, parts, software, knowledge, and communities rather than nostalgia alone.

## Evidence
- Repair-to-making path: [[game-boys-have-infinite-lives-at-30-they-live-on-in-unexpected-ways-los-angeles-times]] describes Guillermo Hernandez learning to fix a failed modified unit before designing and selling customized systems.
- Visual customization: [[game-boys-have-infinite-lives-at-30-they-live-on-in-unexpected-ways-los-angeles-times]] documents painted shells, artwork, buttons, and backlit displays.
- Functional repurposing: [[game-boys-have-infinite-lives-at-30-they-live-on-in-unexpected-ways-los-angeles-times]] describes a Game Boy containing a synthesizer kit and performance setups using music cartridges and MIDI control.
- Deliberate constraint: [[game-boys-have-infinite-lives-at-30-they-live-on-in-unexpected-ways-los-angeles-times]] describes Maxwell Scheller keeping the Game Boy Camera in its classic state while making and distributing low-resolution portraits.
- Broader experimentation: [[game-boys-have-infinite-lives-at-30-they-live-on-in-unexpected-ways-los-angeles-times]] notes telephoto-lens and color-photo experiments by other Camera enthusiasts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The article profiles selected enthusiasts and does not measure how widespread, durable, affordable, legally safe, or environmentally beneficial these practices are. Modification can damage scarce hardware, depend on newly manufactured parts, or erase qualities other users value. Longevity also does not show that the original product was designed for repair or that every constraint should be preserved.

## What Changed
- Created the concept and distinguished restoration, improvement, repurposing, and deliberate preservation of constraints.
