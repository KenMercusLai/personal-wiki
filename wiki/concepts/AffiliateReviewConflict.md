## Definition
[[AffiliateReviewConflict]] is the risk that referral payments, sponsorship, approval conditions, or access to commercial programs influence—or reasonably appear to influence—how a publisher ranks and describes products.

## Current Synthesis
The NordVPN article identifies a real structural concern but provides weak product-specific proof. Review publishers can earn more when readers select high-converting, high-commission products, and a provider could intensify that pressure by making affiliate approval conditional on favorable placement. Those incentives justify disclosure, editorial-commercial separation, reproducible testing, visible scoring rules, correction history, and comparison against non-affiliate alternatives.

The conflict should not be confused with demonstrated corruption. Similar rankings may result from shared criteria, product quality, herd behavior, search incentives, or commercial influence. High advertising spend proves promotion, not payment to a named reviewer. In this source, the strongest direct counterevidence is a commenter identifying himself as PCMag reviewer Max Eddy, who denies payment and says he alone made the ranking from testing; the strongest supporting affiliate claim is anonymous and does not identify the provider or site.

## Key Claims
- Affiliate revenue creates a financial incentive that can diverge from a reader's need for independent comparison.
- Conditional affiliate approval would be more direct evidence of ranking pressure than the mere existence of a referral link.
- Sponsorship, referral terms, test methods, scoring rules, and ownership should be disclosed separately and clearly.
- Agreement among review sites is a signal to investigate, not proof that each ranking was purchased.
- Product quality and reviewer independence require evidence beyond user-rating disagreement, advertising spend, or commission size.
- Reproducible tests and editorial-commercial controls can reduce conflict but do not eliminate judgment, selection, or undisclosed influence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is an openly hostile article, not an audit of review-site finances or editorial workflows. Its screenshot evidence is unavailable because seven files saved as PNGs are actually expired domain-parking HTML. Comment identities are not authenticated, the final affiliate anecdote names neither party, and the source does not distinguish disclosed affiliate relationships from covert pay-for-rank arrangements. A financial conflict can bias work even when no explicit instruction exists, but alleging bribery or fabricated testing requires evidence about the particular reviewer and outlet.

## What Changed
- Created the concept around the difference between structural affiliate incentives and proven editorial corruption.
- Added disclosure, reproducibility, and editorial-commercial separation as concrete evaluation controls.
