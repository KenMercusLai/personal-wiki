## Definition
[[BehaviorDesign]] is the practice of deliberately shaping conditions so people are more likely to perform beneficial target behaviors.

## Current Synthesis
Within this wiki, behavior design is introduced through [[BJFogg]]'s model-centered account. The practice begins by naming the target behavior and then designing around the conditions that make it happen: motivation, ability, and prompts. This makes behavior design more concrete than generic persuasion because it gives the designer multiple levers. If the desired action is not happening, the designer can ask whether users care enough, whether the action is simple enough, and whether the prompt arrives at the right time.

The source frames behavior design as an applied discipline for products and professional work rather than as a purely academic theory. Its ethical orientation is stated as changing people's behavior "for the better," but the source does not deeply define who decides what counts as better. That makes behavior design powerful but in need of explicit user-benefit boundaries.

## Key Claims
- Behavior design treats behavior change as a designed outcome rather than only as persuasion.
- Target behaviors can be diagnosed through motivation, ability, and prompts.
- Simplicity is a central design lever because easier behaviors require less motivation.
- Professional behavior design aims to create beneficial change.
- Ethical evaluation matters because behavior-change tools can influence users toward designer-chosen outcomes.

## Evidence
- Applied system: [[behavior-model]] says the Fogg Behavior Model is part of a larger system that helps people design for behavior change.
- Model levers: [[behavior-model]] names core motivators, simplicity factors, and prompt types as subcomponents of the framework.
- Practical setting: [[behavior-model]] promotes professional training for people creating products that change behavior for the better.
- Simplicity lever: [[behavior-model]] explains ability through simplicity factors in the context of a prompt.
- Ethical claim: [[behavior-model]] explicitly frames the work as changing people's behavior for the better.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source comes from the model's author and includes promotional material for training, so it should be read as both explanation and positioning. The phrase "for the better" is underspecified; behavior design can support user goals, but it can also become manipulative if product goals replace user welfare.
