## Definition
[[OnlineDatingSocialIntegration]] is the hypothesis that dating platforms create ties between people outside one another's existing social networks, changing who can meet and potentially reducing group separation in marriage.

## Current Synthesis
The model summarized by MIT Technology Review begins from clustered networks in which potential partners usually arrive through friends, family, work, school, church, or neighborhood ties. [[JosueOrtega]] and [[PhilippHergovich]] argue that online dating introduces "absent ties" between complete strangers. In their stylized simulation, even a small number of such cross-group links produces much more interracial marriage and stronger modeled marriages.

The historical evidence is suggestive rather than causal. U.S. interracial-marriage growth accelerated around the spread of dating websites and again near Tinder's rise, and separate research reportedly found lower breakup rates among couples who met online. The retained meeting-channel chart clearly shows online introductions becoming common, especially among same-sex couples, but neither the chart nor temporal coincidence demonstrates that platforms caused the marriage outcomes.

## Key Claims
- Partner choice embedded in clustered offline networks can reproduce racial and social separation even without an explicit rule requiring within-group marriage.
- Dating platforms can create links between strangers whose existing networks do not overlap.
- In the cited model, a small number of new cross-group links is enough to produce a large increase in interracial matching.
- The model also predicts stronger marriages, though that result depends on its operational definition of strength and simplified assumptions.
- Observed U.S. timing and reported breakup patterns are consistent with the hypothesis but are not causal identification.
- Online meeting became especially prominent for same-sex couples in the retained historical chart.

## Evidence
- Network mechanism: [[first-evidence-that-online-dating-is-changing-the-nature-of-society-mit-technology-review]] contrasts network-embedded offline introductions with online meetings between complete strangers.
- Model results: [[first-evidence-that-online-dating-is-changing-the-nature-of-society-mit-technology-review]] reports near-complete racial integration and stronger modeled marriages after adding a small number of random cross-group links.
- Historical consistency: [[first-evidence-that-online-dating-is-changing-the-nature-of-society-mit-technology-review]] aligns accelerations in U.S. interracial marriage with the adoption of dating websites and Tinder, while explicitly denying that this proves causation.
- Meeting-channel shift: [[first-evidence-that-online-dating-is-changing-the-nature-of-society-mit-technology-review]] includes a chart in which online meeting rises to roughly one-fifth of heterosexual couples and around two-thirds of same-sex couples by the late 2000s.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The model simplifies race, gender, sexual orientation, preferences, partner availability, and marriage into a constrained matching system; its same-sex meeting evidence is therefore broader than its opposite-sex marriage assumptions. Historical timing can be confounded by changing attitudes, laws, demographics, geography, platform selection, and other social changes. The article gives insufficient detail to evaluate the separate breakup-rate study, and its claim that demographic composition cannot explain the increase does not establish online dating as the remaining cause. Platform-mediated contact may also expose people to discrimination or reproduce preferences rather than consistently weakening [[Homophily]].

## What Changed
- Added a mechanism connecting digitally created absent ties to population-level integration.
- Distinguished clear evidence that meeting channels changed from uncertain evidence about interracial-marriage and stability effects.
- Added the disproportionately large online-meeting shift among same-sex couples as an important scope qualification.

## Related Concepts
- [[Homophily]] - clustered similarity in existing networks is the separation mechanism the model attempts to disrupt.
- [[MarketplaceTrust]] - online dating likewise requires interaction between strangers, though trust infrastructure is not tested by this model.
- [[SocialProof]] - reputation or visible social evidence may shape stranger selection even when the initial tie is created online.
