## Definition
[[SmallProductTeamBalance]] is the idea that a very small product team needs enough people, clear ownership, and accountable decision domains to divide labor without becoming adversarial or ambiguous.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that three people can be a useful minimum team size because the group remains small enough to align quickly but large enough to split work and avoid a two-person offense-defense dynamic. The deeper claim is not only headcount. Each team member should own tasks and also hold accountability for a major product dimension, including the deciding vote when tradeoffs arise. In this frame, team balance means clarity of role, motivation to do good work, and enough distributed authority that product quality is not dependent on one overloaded person.

## Key Claims
- Two-person product teams can work, but their dynamic can become polarized.
- A third team member can create balance and improve labor division.
- Small teams should stay agile by keeping alignment costs low.
- Accountability should attach to major product aspects, not only task completion.
- Deciding authority helps prevent unclear ownership when product tradeoffs appear.

## Evidence
- Team-size claim: [[7-ways-to-use-the-rule-of-threes-to-build-great-products]] says a team is at least three people and that a third person creates balance after the author's experience with two-person teams.
- Labor division: [[7-ways-to-use-the-rule-of-threes-to-build-great-products]] says three people are small enough to get aligned quickly and large enough to divide work effectively.
- Accountability: [[7-ways-to-use-the-rule-of-threes-to-build-great-products]] argues that each member should be accountable for a major product aspect and receive the deciding vote on it.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not prove that three people are always better than two or that every product needs the same role split. Some projects may work with one or two people when scope is narrow, or need larger cross-functional teams when design, engineering, data, research, compliance, sales, or operations are substantial. The useful part is the balance among role clarity, motivation, and accountability.
