## Definition
[[SalaryTransparency]] is the practice of making compensation rules, ranges, levels, or individual pay visible enough for workers to compare outcomes, understand advancement, and challenge perceived unfairness.

## Current Synthesis
The [[JetCom]] case combines unusually strong disclosure with compensation standardization. Employees reportedly knew one another's salaries; 13 levels set cash pay from $40,000 to $300,000; people within a level received the same equity amount; bonuses were absent; and promotion was the only route to a raise. This design can reduce the bargaining advantage created by private negotiation and make disparities easier to question.

Visibility does not make compensation fair by itself. Jet's model moves discretion from individual bargaining into level assignment, promotion, job classification, market calibration, and equity valuation. The source provides [[MarcLore]]'s theory that transparency builds trust and reduces counteroffer behavior, but no employee perspectives, demographic audit, retention data, productivity measures, or comparison with another compensation system.

## Key Claims
- Salary visibility can expose differences that employees and managers would otherwise struggle to identify or contest.
- Fixed pay and equity within disclosed levels can reduce variation caused by individual negotiation.
- Promotion-only raises make the advancement rule legible but increase the stakes of level definitions and promotion governance.
- Removing bonuses and counteroffers can reduce some forms of managerial discretion while also limiting responses to exceptional contribution or market change.
- Transparency shifts rather than eliminates fairness work: classification, promotion, equity terms, market updates, and appeal processes remain consequential.

## Evidence
- Inspectability: [[jet-employees-know-each-others-salaries-business-insider]] reports that Jet employees knew salaries and could raise perceived unfairness with managers.
- Standardized architecture: [[jet-employees-know-each-others-salaries-business-insider]] describes 13 salary levels, equal equity within a level, no bonuses, and promotion-only raises.
- Negotiation mechanism: [[jet-employees-know-each-others-salaries-business-insider]] attributes to Lore the claim that opaque pay and counteroffers reward complaint or outside interviewing rather than a consistent rule.
- Cultural outcome claim: [[jet-employees-know-each-others-salaries-business-insider]] records Lore's belief that transparency supported trust, empowerment, ownership, and effort.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
One company interview does not establish that salary transparency causes trust, fairness, retention, or performance. The source contains no employee testimony, workforce demographics, pay-gap analysis, promotion distribution, external salary benchmark, equity valuation, or outcome comparison. Full individual disclosure can also create privacy and contextual-interpretation concerns. Equal pay inside a band may conceal unequal band placement, and rigid bands can become stale or encourage title inflation unless roles, calibration, review cadence, appeals, and promotion standards are explicit.

## What Changed
- Created a model separating pay visibility and standardization from the unresolved fairness of classification and promotion.
- Added Jet's 13-level, no-bonus, promotion-only system as a source-scoped implementation case.
- Preserved the absence of employee-side and outcome evidence.
