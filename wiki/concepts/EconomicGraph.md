## Definition
[[EconomicGraph]] is LinkedIn's platform vision for digitally representing the global labor market: workers, companies, jobs, skills, educational institutions, and publishing knowledge so economic opportunity can be matched and expanded.

## Current Synthesis
The source frames the economic graph as LinkedIn's way to operationalize a broad vision of creating economic opportunity for the global workforce. [[JeffWeiner]] describes the target as profiles for every worker and company, digital representations of every available job and required skill, educational institutions that can teach those skills, and a publishing platform where participants can share knowledge.

The concept matters because it turns a mission statement into platform surface area. LinkedIn's original mission of connecting knowledge workers becomes more ambitious as member growth passes the midpoint toward the estimated knowledge-worker market. The economic graph expands the system from professional identity into jobs, skills, learning, company presence, and publishing. The [[LyndaCom]] acquisition is presented as part of that skills and education layer, while the growth from thousands of job postings toward millions makes the vision feel less like science fiction to Weiner.

## Key Claims
- The economic graph operationalizes a workforce-opportunity vision rather than remaining an abstract mission statement.
- A complete graph would represent workers, companies, jobs, skills, learning institutions, and published knowledge.
- LinkedIn's member growth gave the company confidence to move from knowledge workers toward the broader global workforce.
- Job inventory expansion from thousands toward millions is used as evidence that a much larger labor-market graph could become real.
- Acquiring [[LyndaCom]] connected LinkedIn's skills layer with education and learning supply.
- A platform approach would let companies distribute their own learning material through Lynda-style infrastructure.

## Evidence
- Full graph scope: [[cs183c-session-19-jeff-weiner-linkedin]] says the vision includes profiles for all workers and companies, all available jobs, required skills, educational institutions, and a publishing platform.
- Mission extension: [[cs183c-session-19-jeff-weiner-linkedin]] says LinkedIn moved from connecting knowledge workers toward helping the global workforce after reaching roughly 400 million members.
- Jobs trajectory: [[cs183c-session-19-jeff-weiner-linkedin]] says LinkedIn moved from 6,000 to 8,000 jobs when Weiner joined toward millions of jobs after setting a goal of representing all jobs.
- Acquisition evidence: [[cs183c-session-19-jeff-weiner-linkedin]] says Bright helped the jobs layer and [[LyndaCom]] helped the skills layer.
- Learning platform: [[cs183c-session-19-jeff-weiner-linkedin]] says Lynda could become a platform where companies offer learning materials to employees, partners, students, or broader users.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source presents the economic graph as executive vision and progress narrative, not as independent validation that the full global labor-market representation is complete, equitable, accurate, or accessible. It does not analyze privacy, labor-market power, certification quality, data accuracy, bias, or the difference between representing opportunity and actually distributing it.
