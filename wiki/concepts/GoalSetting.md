## Definition
[[GoalSetting]] is the practice of choosing explicit aims that focus attention, motivate thought, guide strategy selection, and turn a desired direction into staged action.

## Current Synthesis
The wiki now treats goal setting as a bridge between motivation and path design. Wozniak's source presents goals as especially useful for learners whose interests are not yet self-sustaining: goals help attach value to knowledge, turn required study into a possible source of curiosity, and protect students from purely external tracks of grades, exams, and credentials. The 褪墨 career source adds that goals also matter when a desired career cannot be reached immediately; a person may need interim work, adjacent roles, or a broader path toward better-fit work.

The combined view is qualified rather than rigid. Goals should strengthen [[LearnDrive]] and clarify [[CareerPlanning]], not become coercive status targets or simplistic commands to monetize every passion.

## Key Claims
- High goals can focus attention and motivate thinking.
- Learning improves when goals and study plans roughly align.
- Goals can seed love of learning by making knowledge feel valuable.
- Goal setting is more important for young or constrained learners than for mature experts with strong curiosity.
- Goals should emerge through small non-painful decisions rather than coercive imposition.
- External status targets can become harmful when they replace personally meaningful aims.
- Career goals can require staged paths through interim roles rather than direct jumps to an ideal job.

## Evidence
- Focus and strategy: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] says high goals help people focus attention, energize thought, and choose better strategies.
- Learning alignment: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] argues that clear goals improve learning quality when they roughly coincide with study plans.
- Motivation: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] connects goals to a knowledge valuation center that can generate love of learning.
- Developmental qualification: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] contrasts established scientists with young students who face institutional constraints and fragile interests.
- Small decisions: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] recommends five to ten minutes of exploratory reading rather than forcing large painful commitments.
- Status-track risk: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] uses [[PeterThiel]] as an example of escaping credential competition.
- Career staging: [[tuimo-shi-yong-gpv-si-kao-ni-de-zhi-ye-sheng-ya]] says a career is a path and may require earlier roles before reaching the preferred work.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The Wozniak source explicitly rejects a universal rule that genius requires goals or self-discipline. The career source also resists simple passion advice by noting that hobbies may not work as direct jobs. Across both sources, the support is theoretical and illustrative rather than experimental.

## What Changed
- Broadened goal setting from learning motivation to include career-path staging.
- Added a qualification that career goals should not assume every hobby can become a direct job.
