## Definition
[[StaffMeetings]] are recurring forums in which the relevant leadership group represents a team, product, company, or problem and coordinates through shared metrics, jointly supplied topics, open questions, and recorded follow-up.

## Current Synthesis
A staff meeting is warranted by coordination demand rather than managerial convention. The source's practical triggers include at least three direct reports, substantial work among them, managers reporting to managers, rapid growth, repeated problems that better peer communication could have prevented, or a recent organizational disruption. Those triggers make the meeting a response to interdependence and lost shared context, but they do not guarantee that recurrence remains useful.

The proposed operating system separates facilitation from memory. A meeting runner owns the agenda, manages flow, and stops threads when they cease producing enough value. A different meeting historian records major themes, decisions, action items, relevant thoughts, and the meeting's narrative. After personal and clearly confidential material is removed, those notes are broadcast beyond the room. This makes the record both communication infrastructure and a governance test: preparing it forces the group to ask whether its work is valuable and limits the status and speculation created by a closed recurring forum.

The agenda has three persistent layers. A minimal set of metrics should tell a story and provoke questions rather than merely report stable numbers. A rolling team-sourced agenda shifts ownership from the leader toward participants, with unsolicited items and useful detours acting as early signs that the forum is becoming real work rather than a presentation. A final “Gossip, Rumors, and Lies” segment gives participants a safe place to surface and correct uncertain hallway or chat claims during organizational change. None of these replaces execution: discussion matters only when it produces follow-up work that addresses the underlying problem.

## Key Claims
- Recurring staff meetings should begin only when team size, interdependence, growth, management layers, coordination failures, or organizational shocks create a concrete shared need.
- Facilitation and historical capture are separate jobs: one person curates flow, while another records themes, decisions, and actions.
- Broadly shared notes reduce information asymmetry, meeting-based status, and speculative narratives outside the room.
- Story-producing metrics, team-owned agenda items, and explicit rumor correction give the meeting complementary operating functions.
- Participant-led detours and unsolicited topics show that the group is taking ownership; a leader who dominates the room prevents that transition.
- A recurring meeting should be reconsidered when its metrics never raise questions, its agenda lacks meaningful work, or its original reason has disappeared.
- Conversation can help people interpret change, but only follow-up action can resolve the underlying organizational problem.

## Evidence
- Start conditions: [[gossip-rumors-and-lies-rands-in-repose]] proposes team size, interdependence, management layers, rapid growth, avoidable coordination failures, and organizational disruption as reasons to convene.
- Role separation: [[gossip-rumors-and-lies-rands-in-repose]] assigns agenda and flow to the meeting runner and narrative capture to a different meeting historian.
- Transparency mechanism: [[gossip-rumors-and-lies-rands-in-repose]] recommends company-wide distribution of notes after removing personal and clearly confidential information.
- Metrics test: [[gossip-rumors-and-lies-rands-in-repose]] says useful metrics frame the meeting by telling a story and creating questions; prolonged absence of questions should trigger reassessment.
- Participant ownership: [[gossip-rumors-and-lies-rands-in-repose]] treats useful conversational detours and unsolicited agenda items as early health signals and urges leaders to stop monopolizing discussion.
- Error correction: [[gossip-rumors-and-lies-rands-in-repose]] reserves the final minutes for surfacing uncertain stories and constructing a healthier response.
- Action boundary: [[gossip-rumors-and-lies-rands-in-repose]] distinguishes the relief of talking about change from progress on the problem that made the meeting necessary.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a practitioner essay, not comparative evidence that this agenda improves decisions, execution, trust, or political behavior. Its numerical heuristics—three or more direct reports, efficiency decline beyond seven participants, a sixty-minute meeting, and five to ten minutes for rumor correction—are not established as universal thresholds. Broadcasting notes can reduce information gaps but can also suppress candor, create performative records, expose sensitive strategy, or endanger people in low-trust environments; deciding what is “clearly confidential” also gives the meeting runner substantial editorial power. A safe agenda label does not itself create psychological safety, and rumor correction can reproduce false claims if the group lacks evidence or affected perspectives. The design should therefore be judged by decision quality, useful follow-up, inclusion, and whether the original coordination problem persists, not by faithful repetition of the ritual.

## What Changed
- Created a staff-meeting model organized around start conditions, distinct roles, a three-part agenda, broad notes, participant ownership, and continuing value tests.
- Made explicit that meetings interpret organizational change but do not substitute for work on its root cause.
