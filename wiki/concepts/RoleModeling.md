## Definition
[[RoleModeling]] is the influence created when visible behavior teaches norms more forcefully than explicit instructions or explanations.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses smartphone parenting as its main example: adults who tell children not to use phones while constantly using phones themselves undermine the rule they are trying to teach. In this frame, discipline is communicative; the model's behavior is part of the lesson.

## Key Claims
- Visible behavior can override verbal instruction.
- Inconsistent smartphone behavior weakens adults' authority when asking children to avoid screens.
- Discipline must be embodied if it is meant to persuade others.

## Evidence
- Copying behavior: [[zhi-you-zi-lv-cai-neng-kan-dao-zhen-zheng-de-zi-you-durov-podcast-sui-bi]] quotes the interview claim that children copy a visible mistake quickly even if adults usually do the right thing.
- Smartphone inconsistency: [[zhi-you-zi-lv-cai-neng-kan-dao-zhen-zheng-de-zi-you-durov-podcast-sui-bi]] argues that parental explanations fail when parents are visibly absorbed in phones.
- Embodied discipline: [[zhi-you-zi-lv-cai-neng-kan-dao-zhen-zheng-de-zi-you-durov-podcast-sui-bi]] concludes that example-setting matters more than coercive restriction.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source discusses role modeling through ordinary observation and moral reflection, not developmental evidence. Some rules may still require explicit boundaries, safety limits, or age-specific explanation beyond example alone.
