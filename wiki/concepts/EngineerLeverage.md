## Definition
[[EngineerLeverage]] is the use of code, writing, independent products, AI-assisted output, and public sharing to make an engineer's expertise reusable beyond one-off labor.

## Current Synthesis
The source distinguishes labor leverage, capital leverage, and knowledge leverage, then argues that knowledge leverage is usually the most accessible route for ordinary engineers. Labor leverage requires hiring and management, while capital leverage can magnify losses unless judgment is strong. Knowledge leverage is more engineer-native: code can package expertise into software products, writing can clarify thought and build trust, and sharing can attract peers, users, sponsors, or customers. The inspected slides make the mechanism explicit: pain points become automation, automation becomes product, and product plus operation can become cloud-based revenue.

## Key Claims
- Leverage means making effort reusable, but different leverage types carry different risk and accessibility.
- Labor leverage can scale work but requires management and carries high cost.
- Capital leverage can amplify returns but easily creates losses when judgment is weak.
- Knowledge leverage through code, writing, products, and public media has low replication cost.
- Code is most valuable when it packages hard-to-copy expertise into products with rigid demand, scalability, and low maintenance.
- Writing and sharing can become trust assets that improve judgment, attract like-minded people, and productize content.

## Evidence
- Leverage taxonomy: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] contrasts labor, capital, and knowledge leverage and labels knowledge leverage as easiest for ordinary people.
- Capital warning: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] says using other people's money requires good judgment and can easily lose money.
- Code as product: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] describes turning repeated pain points into automated solutions, packaged products, operations, and cloud collection.
- Product quality: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] says valuable code needs rigid demand, scalability, and low maintenance.
- Writing and sharing: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] says writing is a leverage on thought and sharing is a way to scale personal value.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source gives examples and heuristics rather than a full business model. Code products, creator income, sponsorships, and writing distribution still depend on demand, quality, trust, marketing, maintenance, platform dynamics, and persistence.
