## Definition
[[EngineerLeverage]] is the use of code, writing, independent products, AI-assisted output, and public sharing to make an engineer's expertise reusable beyond one-off labor.

## Current Synthesis
The sources distinguish labor, capital, and knowledge leverage, then argue that reusable code and communication are unusually accessible forms of leverage for engineers. Labor leverage requires hiring and management, while capital leverage can magnify losses unless judgment is strong. Knowledge leverage is more engineer-native: code can package expertise into software products, writing can clarify thought and build trust, and sharing can attract peers, users, sponsors, or customers. The inspected investment slides make one mechanism explicit: pain points become automation, automation becomes product, and product plus operation can become cloud-based revenue.

Meyerson adds an ownership and infrastructure argument. Cheap cloud capacity and modular services for distribution, contracting, payments, and collaboration can let an engineer build and deploy without the resources of a large company. This expands the possible scope of one person's work, but it does not establish that independent products reliably outperform employment or that builders capture all the value they create.

## Key Claims
- Leverage means making effort reusable, but different leverage types carry different risk and accessibility.
- Labor leverage can scale work but requires management and carries high cost.
- Capital leverage can amplify returns but easily creates losses when judgment is weak.
- Knowledge leverage through code, writing, products, and public media has low replication cost.
- Code is most valuable when it packages hard-to-copy expertise into products with rigid demand, scalability, and low maintenance.
- Writing and sharing can become trust assets that improve judgment, attract like-minded people, and productize content.
- Cheap infrastructure and modular business services can lower the organizational threshold for an engineer to ship an owned product.

## Evidence
- Leverage taxonomy: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] contrasts labor, capital, and knowledge leverage and labels knowledge leverage as easiest for ordinary people.
- Capital warning: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] says using other people's money requires good judgment and can easily lose money.
- Code as product: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] describes turning repeated pain points into automated solutions, packaged products, operations, and cloud collection.
- Product quality: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] says valuable code needs rigid demand, scalability, and low maintenance.
- Writing and sharing: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] says writing is a leverage on thought and sharing is a way to scale personal value.
- Infrastructure leverage: [[you-are-not-a-commodity-dev-community]] links cloud, distribution, contracting, payments, and collaboration services to individual product-building capacity.
- Ownership thesis: [[you-are-not-a-commodity-dev-community]] argues that independent invention increases creative control and the builder's share of value.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources give examples and heuristics rather than comparative career or business evidence. Code products, creator income, sponsorships, and writing distribution still depend on demand, quality, trust, marketing, maintenance, platform dynamics, runway, and persistence. Meyerson's claim that a product inventor captures all value ignores customers, collaborators, taxes, platforms, payment providers, and operating costs; his claim that failed independence is safely reversible also omits personal constraints and labor-market variation.

## What Changed
- Expanded leverage from reusable code and writing to the infrastructure and ownership conditions that can support independent product work.
