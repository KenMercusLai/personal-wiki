## Definition
[[DataScienceAsCreativeWork]] is the view that data science is creative because its questions, methods, interpretations, and outputs require novel judgment and are not fully specified in advance.

## Current Synthesis
The Nesta article locates creativity in the analytical process rather than in an industry's label or the visual appearance of an output. Exploratory analysis moves between generating possibilities and selecting promising models; lateral transfer adapts methods across domains; and even a supplied business question leaves substantial discretion over data acquisition, variables, research design, interpretation, and communication.

This framing makes uncertainty an operating property rather than only a defect. Projects often combine unfamiliar data, questions, and contexts, so managers cannot guarantee a useful result in advance. Pilots and timeboxes can bound risk, while communication, motivation, and learning from failure become part of managing analytical talent.

## Key Claims
- Creativity resides in the structure of data-science work, not only in creative-industry employment or visualization skill.
- Exploratory analysis combines divergent idea generation with convergent method selection.
- Cross-domain transfer of analytical techniques is a form of lateral thinking.
- New questions, datasets, and applications make outputs variable and partly unpredictable.
- Research design and interpretation preserve human discretion even when the initial question comes from elsewhere.
- Tool innovation can emerge as practitioners solve novel analytical and data-management problems.
- Managers should bound uncertain projects and support communication and learning from failure.

## Evidence
- Novel process: [[data-science-work-is-creative-work-p1]] describes exploratory analysis, visualization, method selection, and cross-domain transfer as repeated sites of creative judgment.
- Non-uniform outcomes: [[data-science-work-is-creative-work-p1]] says projects regularly involve new data, questions, and applications, making results uncertain.
- Interpretation: [[data-science-work-is-creative-work-p1]] identifies data acquisition, variable choice, modeling, interpretation, and communication as discretionary work.
- Practitioner innovation: [[data-science-work-is-creative-work-p1]] points to open-source data tools as products of users solving novel problems.
- Management consequence: [[data-science-work-is-creative-work-p1]] connects uncertainty to piloting, timeboxing, motivation, communication, and learning from failure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source applies a subjective five-part occupational heuristic to interview descriptions rather than observing or measuring creativity directly. Its mechanization criterion was already disputed in 2014, and later automation could change the division between routine execution and human judgment. The criteria may also classify many forms of knowledge work as creative, so the argument does not establish that data science is uniquely creative or that adopting the label improves training or management outcomes.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish intrinsic process creativity from employment in a nominally creative industry.
- Recorded project uncertainty and interpretation as management-relevant features rather than incidental exceptions.
