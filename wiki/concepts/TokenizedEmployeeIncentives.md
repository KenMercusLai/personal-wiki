## Definition
[[TokenizedEmployeeIncentives]] are compensation and workplace-reward arrangements that give or let employees acquire a tradeable project token whose value is expected to depend on product use, network activity, or market demand.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents tokens as more than a liquid substitute for stock options. A tradeable token could let employees sell vested compensation earlier, buy exposure without a new board grant, trade with colleagues, and pay one another for tasks or project contributions. The accompanying feedback loop proposes that ownership raises morale, morale strengthens product-building effort, and a better product attracts use that raises token value.

Those same properties alter the risk and time horizon. Continuous pricing can reward promotion before delivery, internal purchases can concentrate an employee's labor income and savings in one fragile project, and open trading introduces conduct and governance questions. The model is therefore conditional on genuine token utility, sufficient market depth, enforceable vesting or lockups, fair internal rules, and legal compliance; the source offers a design hypothesis, not evidence that token incentives outperform equity.

## Key Claims
- Active token trading can make vested employee incentives liquid earlier than conventional private-company options.
- Employees can voluntarily buy exposure after formal grant pools are exhausted, reducing the company's exclusive control over participation.
- Internal transfers can form a peer market for ownership, recognition, task coverage, and project rewards.
- Public pricing and early liquidity can shorten the incentive horizon and reward token marketing over sustainable product construction.
- Purchased tokens create stronger direct downside than unexercised options and may motivate through loss aversion, but they also concentrate employee financial risk.
- Traceable transfers can support oversight, yet identity, privacy, market manipulation, insider conduct, tax, and securities questions remain.

## Evidence
- Liquidity and access: [[employee-incentives-in-a-tokenized-world-token-economy]] contrasts long waits for stock-option liquidity with token trading after creation and proposes secondary purchases by later employees.
- Internal market: [[employee-incentives-in-a-tokenized-world-token-economy]] gives examples of tokens paid for tedious tasks, vacation coverage, team bonuses, and project staking.
- Motivation loop: [[employee-incentives-in-a-tokenized-world-token-economy]] diagrams ownership and profit raising morale, product motivation improving output, and adoption increasing token value.
- Horizon and concentration risk: [[employee-incentives-in-a-tokenized-world-token-economy]] warns that employees can profit from promotion before delivery and argues that purchased tokens expose them directly to company failure.
- Governance boundary: [[employee-incentives-in-a-tokenized-world-token-economy]] identifies liquidity uncertainty, employee insider trading, decentralized exchanges, privacy coins, holdings disclosure, and self-regulation as unresolved issues.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sole source is a speculative essay written during the 2017 token boom, assumes that tokens retain utility-linked value, and reports no comparative employee, retention, product, or company-performance data. A token can be listed without being liquid at a reliable price, and liquidity can increase both employee choice and speculative short-termism. Loss aversion does not establish that personally purchased tokens improve useful work, and concentrating employment plus investment exposure can reduce welfare. The article's legal discussion is historically bounded and is not current securities, employment, tax, market-abuse, or compensation advice.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to separate token-specific liquidity, internal exchange, and governance mechanisms from conventional employee equity.
