## Definition
[[DeveloperCustomerExposure]] is the practice of letting selected engineers directly observe and interact with users or customers so technical judgment, empathy, and motivation are grounded in firsthand product problems rather than only filtered requirements.

## Current Synthesis
The source explains developer enthusiasm as an exposure effect. Engineers readily improve developer tools because they repeatedly see colleagues' friction; when companies isolate them from customers, they remove the equivalent evidence for customer problems and can mistake the resulting distance for lack of interest. Direct observation of good, bad, and painful product experiences is presented as a way to make problems vivid enough to prompt both commitment and inventive technical responses.

The practice is selective rather than universal. Cagan says some engineers need direct contact, often the more senior ones, rather than requiring every developer to attend every interaction. Those representatives still need preparation for customer-facing conduct, especially around direct sales relationships. The source does not specify how learning should be sampled, documented, or shared with the rest of the team, so exposure should complement rather than replace product research, design judgment, support evidence, analytics, and representative testing.

## Key Claims
- Engineers tend to invest in problems they can observe regularly, whether those problems belong to developers or end users.
- Filtering all customer evidence through other roles can reduce both engineering motivation and access to solution-relevant detail.
- Firsthand observation can turn abstract requirements into concrete human and product problems.
- A representative subset of engineers can participate directly and carry learning back to the wider team.
- Customer-facing preparation and organizational boundaries remain necessary, particularly in sales-led environments.

## Evidence
- Exposure mechanism: [[developer-powered-innovation-silicon-valley-product-group]] argues that developers improve internal tools because colleagues are the users whose problems they see most often.
- Motivation and empathy: [[developer-powered-innovation-silicon-valley-product-group]] says witnessing real customer experiences, including painful failures, can create unexpectedly strong motivation to solve them.
- Selective participation: [[developer-powered-innovation-silicon-valley-product-group]] says not every developer needs customer contact and identifies senior engineers as common participants.
- Interaction boundary: [[developer-powered-innovation-silicon-valley-product-group]] recommends customer-facing etiquette training, especially where direct sales relationships are involved.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one practitioner argument and supplies no measured comparison between teams with and without direct engineer contact. Individual customer encounters can be unrepresentative, engineers can overfit vivid anecdotes or propose technically appealing answers, and customer-facing activity can displace focused implementation work. Direct exposure also needs consent, privacy, accessibility, research quality, support ownership, sales coordination, and a mechanism for sharing evidence with non-attending teammates.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to separate direct engineer-user contact from generic customer-led development and from engineering participation based only on secondhand context.
