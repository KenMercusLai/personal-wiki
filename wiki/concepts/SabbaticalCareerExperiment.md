## Definition
[[SabbaticalCareerExperiment]] is a deliberate break from ordinary employment used to search for meaningful work, test a creative or entrepreneurial path, and spend personal runway on a high-uncertainty career bet.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents a sabbatical career experiment as personally transformative but structurally unequal. [[AmirRajan]] leaves a lucrative but empty software career, downsizes, liquidates savings, and spends months recovering before [[ADarkRoom]] becomes his central creative project. The experiment helps him find "digital, evocative experiences" as a calling, yet the same story stresses that such experiments require savings, health luck, family support, no catastrophic shocks, and acceptance that even a large success may not permanently replace employment.

## Key Claims
- A sabbatical can create enough mental space to rediscover intrinsic motivation after burnout.
- Personal savings can function like startup runway for a career experiment, but that makes access deeply unequal.
- The productive phase may begin only after a recovery period, not immediately after quitting.
- Creative calling and financial sustainability are separate outcomes.
- A successful sabbatical can make returning to employment emotionally harder because it reveals a more meaningful alternative.
- The experiment should be interpreted as a risky roll, not as a replicable success formula.

## Evidence
- Burnout trigger: [[a-dark-room-from-sabbatical-year-to-800000]] says Rajan was well paid but empty in corporate America and chose between sanity, family, and job.
- Runway construction: [[a-dark-room-from-sabbatical-year-to-800000]] describes ten years of saving, low expenses, selling possessions, and liquidating 401k savings.
- Recovery period: [[a-dark-room-from-sabbatical-year-to-800000]] says it took about three months to figure out a routine before the game project began.
- Calling: [[a-dark-room-from-sabbatical-year-to-800000]] says Rajan found what he was supposed to do while building digital, evocative experiences.
- Sustainability limit: [[a-dark-room-from-sabbatical-year-to-800000]] says dwindling game revenue eventually led Rajan back to work despite the success of [[ADarkRoom]].
- Unequal access: [[a-dark-room-from-sabbatical-year-to-800000]] lists prerequisites such as high earnings, low expenses, no children, no sickness, no catastrophic events, and tolerance for going uninsured.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a single retrospective, not evidence that sabbaticals generally produce better careers. It also warns against treating Rajan's result as advice: his own interpretation emphasizes luck, privilege, and the probability that most people with similar desire and effort will not see the same outcome.
