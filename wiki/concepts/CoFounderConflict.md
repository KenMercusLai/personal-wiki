## Definition
[[CoFounderConflict]] is the unresolved tension between startup founders over company direction, roles, decisions, or working relationship health, especially when the team has too little direct conflict or too much unbounded conflict.

## Current Synthesis
[[GarryTan]] presents co-founder conflict as relationship infrastructure that founders have to maintain deliberately. The risk is not simply that founders disagree; the risk is that they either avoid disagreement until resentment and distance accumulate, or they fight constantly because ownership and decision boundaries are unclear. In the [[Posterous]] case, fast growth masked deeper relational weakness until growth flattened and the company needed the founders to work together under greater stress.

The source's practical model is a middle path: founders should spend more time resolving hard disagreements, speak directly without self-abandonment, listen before asserting, use data and shared company goals, and define ownership areas so every decision is not endlessly reopened. When those habits are already failing, outside support from trusted advisors, investors, mentors, or executive coaching becomes part of the operating toolkit rather than a last-resort embarrassment.

## Key Claims
- Startup success can hide co-founder relationship problems until a difficult phase exposes them.
- Too little conflict is dangerous when founders avoid contact, preserve surface harmony, or stop saying what they actually believe.
- Too much conflict often points to unclear ownership and decision rights rather than merely strong personalities.
- Healthy founder conflict requires directness, listening, shared goals, and a concrete resolution instead of either surrender or domination.
- Prior friendship and long history help but do not substitute for maintaining the current working relationship.
- External help can be legitimate startup infrastructure when unresolved conflict threatens the company.

## Evidence
- Masked relationship debt: [[co-founder-conflict-techcrunch]] says Posterous grew 10X yearly and became a top 200 Quantcast site before growth flatlined and unprepared founder conflict surfaced.
- Avoidance pattern: [[co-founder-conflict-techcrunch]] identifies reduced time together, minimal interaction, and route-changing avoidance as signals that conflict is being ducked rather than resolved.
- Relationship-maintenance warning: [[co-founder-conflict-techcrunch]] says Tan had known his co-founder for more than eight years, but history was not enough without ongoing maintenance.
- Decision-boundary remedy: [[co-founder-conflict-techcrunch]] advises founders who fight constantly to list business areas, assign an owner for each, hear each other out, and then let the owner decide.
- Fair-conflict norm: [[co-founder-conflict-techcrunch]] distinguishes speaking up from bulldozing, and frames fair conflict as collaborative, data-based, and anchored in being on the same team.
- Outside-support norm: [[co-founder-conflict-techcrunch]] recommends trusted friends, investors, mentors, individual help, group help, and executive coaching when conflict is company-threatening.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a practitioner essay grounded in Tan's experience at Posterous and advising startups, not a comparative study of founder-dispute outcomes. It argues strongly for resolving conflict, but some disputes may still require founder separation, role changes, legal intervention, or shutdown when trust, health, ethics, ownership, or strategy has already broken beyond repair.
