## Definition
[[DollosLaw]] is the evolutionary principle, as used in the source, that a lost trait is unlikely to reappear because the original path that produced it depended on complex conditions that will not recur exactly.

## Current Synthesis
Housel uses Dollo's Law as an analogy rather than as a biology lesson. The point is that some outcomes are not random luck, because they arose from real forces, but they are still not repeatable because the specific sequence of selection pressures, environment, competition, and time cannot be rerun. That makes the law a compact model for business and investing history: learn broad causal lessons without assuming the detailed path will return.

## Key Claims
- Complex outcomes can be caused without being repeatable.
- Path-dependent histories should not be copied as if the same conditions will recur.
- Evolutionary irreversibility supplies an analogy for business and investing history.

## Evidence
- Evolution analogy: [[collaborative-fund-lucky-vs-repeatable]] describes an animal losing horns and being unlikely to regain them because the original selection path was too complex to replicate.
- Luck distinction: [[collaborative-fund-lucky-vs-repeatable]] says such traits are not merely luck, because specific forces produced them, even though one cannot rely on those forces repeating exactly.
- Strategy analogy: [[collaborative-fund-lucky-vs-repeatable]] applies the same logic to business and investing, where historical details are usually not repeatable.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
