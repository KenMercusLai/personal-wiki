Startup pricing as positioning means the startup uses pricing to choose whom it serves, what value promise it makes, and what product capabilities it should build. In Anand Sanwal's CB Insights reflection, early cheap pricing looked like a way to win, but it pushed the company toward the wrong axis of competition. Later value-based pricing rose by more than an order of magnitude and helped the team focus on customers whose needs justified deeper product work.

Packaging language is part of the same positioning system. Sanwal cites an early pricing page with playful plan names as an example of being too clever. The problem was not only aesthetics; confusing language made it harder for institutional customers to understand what they were buying and why the product was serious enough for their work.

The source also warns against pricing for everyone. CB Insights initially tried to serve institutional customers and startups at the same time because more options felt safer. Choosing a market clarified the message and made the sales and product approach more coherent.

## Pricing Questions

- Does the price communicate the value of the product or only try to look cheaper than alternatives?
- Does packaging help the target buyer understand the offer quickly?
- Is the company using pricing to select a customer segment it can serve well?
- Are low-price plans attracting customers whose needs distract from the core market?
