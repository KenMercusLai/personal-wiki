## Definition
[[DesignSprint]] is a short, structured product-design process that turns assumptions into a realistic prototype and user test within roughly one working week.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents the Design Sprint as an alternative to front-loading a project with weeks of interviews, personas, empathy maps, benchmarking, and documentation. Its epistemic advantage is not that assumptions disappear, but that the team exposes them sooner through a tangible interaction. In AJ&Smart's reported trial, a new client moved further in five days than clients had under the firm's former six-to-eight-week package. The method is best treated as a fast evidence cycle for bounded product questions, not proof that exploratory research is unnecessary when the problem, users, environment, or consequences remain unclear.

## Key Claims
- A sprint can reach a high-fidelity prototype and user test in four to five days.
- Rapid tangibility makes assumptions observable sooner than a documentation-heavy process.
- Starting without a brief, benchmarking, or prior research can work when the product question is sufficiently bounded.
- Sprint evidence comes from behavior with the prototype rather than the existence of design artifacts.
- The method does not remove the need for broader discovery in ambiguous or high-consequence domains.

## Evidence
- Timebox and output: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] describes a four-to-five-day process ending in realistic prototype testing.
- Consulting comparison: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] reports that AJ&Smart's five-day client sprint advanced further than its prior six-to-eight-week package.
- Learning mechanism: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] argues that observed use reveals comprehension and rejection more directly than research documentation.
- Applicability boundary: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] preserves up-front research when the underlying problem is unclear.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is a single consultancy retrospective without matched projects, independent outcome measures, or long-term product results. A five-day test can reveal reactions to the chosen hypothesis but may miss whether the team chose the right problem, recruited the right users, represented a complex workflow, or accounted for accessibility, safety, organizational constraints, and rare failure modes. Prototype fidelity can also create false confidence if simulated behavior differs materially from production reality.

## What Changed
- Created the concept as a rapid assumption-to-prototype-to-test cycle with an explicit problem-ambiguity boundary.
