## Definition
[[VentureCapitalValueAdd]] is the founder-perceived usefulness of an investor beyond capital, including judgment, access, responsiveness, operating help, and relationship quality rather than the investor's own count of services or contacts.

## Current Synthesis
The survey exposes a measurement and expectation problem. VCs reported more contact, support, and impact than founders reported receiving or valuing: investors rated their impact at 7/10 versus founders' 5.3/10, claimed weekly contact at almost three times the founder-reported rate, and described recruiting and sales support that fewer than half of portfolio companies used. The gap does not establish that investors provide no value. It shows that service availability, activity, uptake, and founder-perceived impact are different measures. The same source suggests that the value proposition begins before the investment: personal chemistry led partner-selection criteria for both sides, founders valued decision speed much more than VCs expected, and preferences shifted by geography and financing stage. A credible value-add claim therefore needs founder-side evidence, a clear fit with the company's current needs, and separation of brand or service inventory from realized help.

## Key Claims
- Investor activity and founder-perceived impact are different measures; more contact or a longer service menu does not by itself establish useful value.
- A 7/10 VC self-rating versus a 5.3/10 founder rating indicates a material perception gap in the surveyed populations.
- Operational support should be assessed through relevant use and founder outcomes, not merely whether the fund says the service exists.
- Chemistry and relationship quality are part of investor value because financing creates a continuing working relationship.
- Decision speed can be valuable to founders facing a constrained fundraising timeline even when investors do not regard speed as differentiating.
- Investor value is contextual: geography, financing stage, company need, terms, network, experience, and brand can change which contribution matters.

## Evidence
- Impact gap: [[do-vcs-really-add-value-founders-say-sometimes-by-carl-fritjofsson]] reports average self-assessed VC impact of 7/10 and founder-assessed impact of 5.3/10.
- Contact gap: [[do-vcs-really-add-value-founders-say-sometimes-by-carl-fritjofsson]] says VCs reported weekly contact almost three times as often as founders reported receiving it.
- Service uptake: [[do-vcs-really-add-value-founders-say-sometimes-by-carl-fritjofsson]] reports that more than 60% of VCs offered recruiting and sales or business-development support, while fewer than half of portfolio companies used either service.
- Selection value: [[do-vcs-really-add-value-founders-say-sometimes-by-carl-fritjofsson]] places personal relationship and chemistry first for both groups and identifies a large mismatch over the importance of decision speed and brand.
- Context dependence: [[do-vcs-really-add-value-founders-say-sometimes-by-carl-fritjofsson]] reports relative differences between US and European founders and between early- and later-stage companies.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source reports perceptions, service use, and stated selection priorities rather than causal effects on company performance. Its 121 founders and 98 VCs were separate, unmatched samples recruited voluntarily through social media and direct contact, so individual investors cannot be compared with their own portfolio founders and population representativeness is unknown. The article does not publish uncertainty estimates, subgroup sizes, exact question wording, a response rate, or controls for stage, geography, firm quality, and company need. Low service uptake could reflect low need, poor fit, weak awareness, capacity constraints, substitution by other providers, or low service quality. The results support better measurement and expectation-setting, not the universal conclusion that VC support is ineffective.

## What Changed
- Created the concept around the distinction among offered services, investor activity, founder uptake, and founder-perceived impact.
- Added chemistry, decision speed, and context-specific needs as parts of the investor value proposition.
- Established the unmatched, voluntary survey design as the main evidence boundary.
