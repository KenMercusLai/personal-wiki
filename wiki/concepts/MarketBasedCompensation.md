## Definition
[[MarketBasedCompensation]] sets salary and equity by an employer's estimate of the external market value of a role and the individual's demonstrated skill level, rather than by automatic tenure increases alone.

## Current Synthesis
eShares' 2015 framework made marketability the stated basis of salary plus equity. It rejected automatic annual raises and manager-favor-based increases, proposed a market review at nine months and every year thereafter, allowed pay to move down as well as up, and targeted the 75th percentile rather than the top of market. The company paired that policy with a broader proposition: employees might trade some short-term pay optimization for learning and career capital in an environment that increased their future market value.

The model names a real external constraint but does not make pay objective by declaration. Role definitions, comparison groups, market datasets, equity valuation, skill assessment, negotiation power, and manager judgment all shape the result. Learning is also uncertain and should not become an unfalsifiable reason to underpay. A credible system therefore needs transparent bands, consistent leveling, bias review, pay-equity analysis, and a clear process for challenging assessments.

## Key Claims
- External role demand and individual skill can inform pay more directly than tenure-based increments.
- Periodic market review can adjust compensation as labor demand and capability change.
- Learning and career capital may be part of an employee value proposition but do not replace fair current pay.
- Market data still requires choices about role matching, geography, percentile, equity value, and skill evaluation.
- Transparency, consistency, bias controls, and appeal mechanisms are necessary if market pricing is meant to reduce favoritism.

## Evidence
- Pay basis: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] says eShares based salary and equity on the market for a person's skill set and the person's skill level.
- Review cadence: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] describes review at the nine-month anniversary and every twelve months afterward, including possible downward adjustment.
- Market position: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] reports a 75th-percentile target and argues that learning opportunities help attract people who are not optimizing only for immediate salary.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers policy rationale but no pay data, employee perspective, retention outcomes, equity valuation method, skill rubric, market dataset, or demographic fairness analysis. Downward salary adjustment may transfer market volatility to employees and can undermine trust if the employer controls both measurement and interpretation. The claim that the strongest people prioritize learning over pay risks moralizing different financial needs.
