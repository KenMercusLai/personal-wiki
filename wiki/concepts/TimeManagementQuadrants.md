## Definition
[[TimeManagementQuadrants]] are a priority framework that sorts tasks by whether they are urgent and whether they are important, producing four categories for deciding where attention should go.

## Current Synthesis
The article uses the quadrant framework to argue that non-urgent but important work deserves protected time. In this view, productivity is not just doing more tasks, but keeping meaningful work from being crowded out by urgent but less important demands.

## Key Claims
- The four categories are urgent-important, non-urgent-important, urgent-not-important, and non-urgent-not-important.
- The source gives special priority to non-urgent but important work.
- The framework supports [[PersonalProductivity]] by clarifying which tasks deserve deliberate time allocation.

## Evidence
- Quadrant categories: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] cites Steven Covey's four-part time-management model.
- Priority judgment: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] argues that important but non-urgent work should receive enough time rather than being rushed later.
- Productivity role: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] places the model inside a broader list of habits for doing important work efficiently.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source summarizes the framework briefly and does not explore edge cases, such as tasks that change quadrant over time or environments where urgency is externally imposed.
