## Definition
[[ActiveLearning]] is learning that requires the learner to produce, explain, teach, recreate, or otherwise use knowledge rather than only receive it passively.

## Current Synthesis
The sources treat active learning as the reason the [[FeynmanTechnique]] and creator practice matter. Explaining material to others, writing from understanding, and turning research into a course all force the learner to expose weak spots, build connections between concepts, and internalize the subject more deeply than passive reading alone. The tutorial-writing source adds that accumulated notes can feel reassuring while still leaving the learner in a comfort zone; writing for real readers, collecting examples, testing a draft, and publishing create the practice loop that passive study lacks.

## Key Claims
- Teaching-oriented explanation is presented as a strong active-learning mode.
- Active learning reveals gaps because failed output makes unclear understanding visible.
- Recreating, writing, and teaching can strengthen retention and conceptual connection.
- Active learning supports creativity by forcing links between unfamiliar concepts.
- Course creation represents a deeper active-learning stage than article writing because it requires simplified structure, examples, and visuals.
- Public writing can become active learning when it forces topic choice, concrete examples, feedback, and revision.

## Evidence
- Teaching and explanation: [[feynman-technique-in-practice-indigo-information-acquisition-knowledge-output-methodology]] frames learning by teaching as the core of the Feynman Technique.
- Gap discovery: [[feynman-technique-in-practice-indigo-information-acquisition-knowledge-output-methodology]] says the review step finds what the learner cannot explain.
- Retention support: [[feynman-technique-in-practice-indigo-information-acquisition-knowledge-output-methodology]] cites Dale's Cone of Experience to claim that recreating and teaching improve retention.
- Creativity and links: [[feynman-technique-in-practice-indigo-information-acquisition-knowledge-output-methodology]] argues that output encourages the brain to form connections between different concepts.
- Courses as internalization: [[feynman-technique-in-practice-indigo-information-acquisition-knowledge-output-methodology]] describes courses as a later stage that turns learned knowledge into a clear teachable system.
- Writing as practice: [[ru-he-xie-de-gan-huo-man-man-you-tong-su-yi-dong-cong-yi-pian-wen-zhang-fen-xiang-wo-de-chuang-zuo-jing-yan]] says the author left passive note accumulation by writing a tutorial article, choosing writing because it could start quickly and generate faster feedback.
- Feedback and revision: [[ru-he-xie-de-gan-huo-man-man-you-tong-su-yi-dong-cong-yi-pian-wen-zhang-fen-xiang-wo-de-chuang-zuo-jing-yan]] shows friends' draft feedback leading to revisions in opening length, context, video flow, examples, and summary visuals.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources offer strong practitioner cases for active learning but do not compare active-learning formats experimentally. Their retention claims are presented through Dale's Cone and creator retrospectives, so they should be treated as directional support rather than precise measurement. Public writing also requires enough prior knowledge, available examples, and feedback access to be useful rather than premature performance pressure.
