Value-first founder networking treats a network as a record of useful help given over time, not as a pile of business cards or shallow event contacts. The Groove source says relationships with smart founders, mentors, and advisors can save a startup during hard periods and create opportunities such as partnerships, interviews, and strategic decisions.

The mechanism is asymmetric at first. A founder looks for people they would eventually like to know, then finds a way to help that person or someone trusted by that person without demanding an immediate return. Useful help might be specific ideas, relevant expertise, an introduction, or another resource the recipient would genuinely value.

Warm introductions matter because busy people screen for trust. The source frames repeated value creation as the way to make future requests welcome rather than intrusive. Networking is therefore long-term business development and judgment support, but only when the founder keeps the first move focused on the other person's actual needs.

## Signals

- Outreach offers concrete help rather than a discount, a vague call, or a generic pitch.
- The founder can explain why the recipient would value the help.
- Requests for advice or introductions come after trust has been built.
- The network includes people whose judgment and relationships can matter during difficult decisions.
