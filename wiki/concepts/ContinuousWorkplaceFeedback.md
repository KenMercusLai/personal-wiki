## Definition
[[ContinuousWorkplaceFeedback]] is a recurring approach to employee development in which timely coaching, reflection, and support happen throughout the year rather than being concentrated in a single rated annual review.

## Current Synthesis
The Buffer account presents continuous feedback as a portfolio of relationships rather than one replacement meeting. Mentor-style one-on-ones occurred at least every two weeks and were largely driven by the teammate, who brought achievements, challenges, questions, and desired feedback. Optional peer masterminds used a more structured exchange—time to celebrate each person's achievements followed by time on current challenges—and could include personal as well as professional development.

The important distinction is relational. A team lead can provide mentorship, context, and signals about the organization, while a peer with overlapping work can offer vulnerability and experience from a similar position. Buffer briefly tried peer masterminds without one-on-ones during a self-management experiment, then restored one-on-ones after deciding that mentorship remained valuable. Across both formats, the proposed coaching discipline is to listen, ask questions, and help the speaker reach a self-authored solution instead of taking the problem away.

The eShares account broadens the model beyond the direct manager. Employees walked with their manager every two to three weeks, their manager's manager every four to six weeks, and the CEO every four to six months. Managers were instructed to listen, and employees could discuss whatever was on their minds. This strengthens the relationship-network case for recurring conversations, while leaving performance, confidentiality, escalation, and power-boundary questions unanswered.

## Key Claims
- Frequent conversations reduce the delay and recall burden built into annual reviews.
- Employee-owned agendas can surface achievements, challenges, and development needs that formal manager-led evaluation may miss.
- Mentor one-on-ones and peer masterminds provide different kinds of support and should not be treated as interchangeable.
- Regular coaching can give managers signals about workflow and structure while giving employees advice, reflection, and accountability.
- Question-led listening aims to strengthen the employee's judgment and motivation rather than create dependence on the coach's answer.
- Skip-level and executive one-on-ones can strengthen relationships across organizational layers, but their purpose and confidentiality need clarity.
- Continuous feedback is an evolving operating practice, not a complete substitute for every evaluation, pay, promotion, or accountability process.

## Evidence
- Review limitations: [[why-we-dont-have-performance-reviews]] argues that yearly recall is weak, numeric ratings can derail dialogue, negative feedback can dominate attention, and useful information arrives late.
- One-on-one cadence and ownership: [[why-we-dont-have-performance-reviews]] describes at-least-fortnightly, roughly hour-long sessions whose agenda is primarily set by the teammate.
- Complementary relationships: [[why-we-dont-have-performance-reviews]] distinguishes mentor-to-mentee one-on-ones from optional peer-to-peer masterminds and reports restoring one-on-ones after a self-management experiment.
- Reflective coaching: [[why-we-dont-have-performance-reviews]] says both formats prioritize listening and questions that help the speaker form a solution.
- Iterative practice: [[why-we-dont-have-performance-reviews]] repeatedly describes both formats as systems Buffer continued to revise.
- Multi-level cadence: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] describes manager, skip-level, and CEO one-on-one walks at progressively longer intervals.
- Listening rule: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] says managers were trained for one-on-ones and given the central instruction to listen while employees chose the topic.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence comes from two company-published practitioner accounts and relies on intended practice and operating experience rather than measured comparisons. Neither shows whether the approach improved performance, retention, promotion fairness, pay equity, or manager consistency. Employee-led agendas may leave avoided topics unspoken unless managers retain responsibility for clear expectations and candid feedback. Optional peer sessions require trust, overlap, and psychological safety; personal disclosure can become intrusive if expected. Skip-level and CEO conversations also create power, confidentiality, follow-through, and manager-bypass risks that the eShares source does not address.

## What Changed
- Expanded continuous feedback from direct-manager and peer coaching to a multi-level relationship network including skip-level and executive conversations.
- Added confidentiality, power-boundary, escalation, and manager-bypass qualifications for higher-level one-on-ones.
