## Definition
[[BrandPositioning]] is the deliberate association of a product or company with a clear idea relative to alternatives, expressed through what it is, the benefit it gives customers, and the personality of the relationship it seeks with them.

## Current Synthesis
[[GibsonBiddle]] treats positioning as a compact team model rather than a slogan-writing exercise. Teams describe the offer in plain language, state the customer benefit, and specify how the product should feel as a relationship. Brevity and sixth-grade language force prioritization, while the “one word to own” question tests whether the intended meaning is distinctive and memorable.

The framework is directional rather than fixed. Biddle says Netflix revised its positioning as the product moved from DVD rental toward streaming and original content. Positioning can therefore change with the offer and market even when the more emotional or aspirational layers of a [[BrandPyramid]] remain relatively stable.

## Key Claims
- Positioning is relative: it aims to place a specific idea in customers' minds compared with alternatives.
- The model combines category or company description, customer benefit, and relational personality.
- Clear, brief language is a strategic constraint because customers will not parse a dense internal formulation.
- Describing the brand as a person can help teams articulate how the product should relate to customers.
- A long-term “one word” test can reveal whether the intended position is focused enough to become memorable.
- Positioning should be revised as the offer and evidence change rather than treated as permanently static.

## Evidence
- Three-question model: [[gibson-biddle-branding-for-builders]] asks teams to define what the product is, how it benefits customers, and what personality it should have.
- Relative association: [[gibson-biddle-branding-for-builders]] uses Volvo's association with safety to explain owning an idea relative to competitors.
- Communication constraints: [[gibson-biddle-branding-for-builders]] recommends precise sixth-grade language and a small number of ideas.
- Evolution: [[gibson-biddle-branding-for-builders]] says Netflix updated its positioning continuously as product features and presentation changed.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The model is a practitioner heuristic supported by familiar examples, not comparative research proving that three questions or one-word ownership produce durable advantage. Customers may hold multiple, segment-specific, contradictory, or category-dependent associations, and a simple phrase can conceal important tradeoffs. The source does not specify research sampling, competitive analysis, or decision rules for choosing among candidate positions.

## What Changed
- Created the concept as a dynamic three-part model linking category description, customer benefit, and relational personality.
