## Definition
[[TeamFocus]] is the shared capacity of collaborators to sustain competent attention on a project without dragging the group into avoidable coordination, morale, or distraction costs.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats team performance as more than adding people to work. Drawing on the A-player/B-player discussion and the author's own project memories, it argues that unfocused or mismatched collaborators can demotivate stronger contributors, not only by increasing communication overhead but also by making deep work feel impossible beside someone who cannot sustain attention.

## Key Claims
- More people do not automatically make work faster because coordination and mismatch can slow the group.
- A distracted or underqualified collaborator can demotivate teammates who are trying to go deep.
- Some apparent laziness may instead be limited ability to focus on one task for a long period.
- Reframing distraction as capacity mismatch can reduce emotional waste and support clearer collaboration boundaries.

## Evidence
- Headcount caution: [[zhi-you-zi-lv-cai-neng-kan-dao-zhen-zheng-de-zi-you-durov-podcast-sui-bi]] connects the podcast's team discussion with the "Mythical Man-Month" idea that added people increase coordination cost.
- Morale cost: [[zhi-you-zi-lv-cai-neng-kan-dao-zhen-zheng-de-zi-you-durov-podcast-sui-bi]] emphasizes the demotivating effect of working beside someone who cannot contribute at the needed level.
- Sustained attention: [[zhi-you-zi-lv-cai-neng-kan-dao-zhen-zheng-de-zi-you-durov-podcast-sui-bi]] highlights the claim that many weak collaboration cases come from inability to focus deeply for extended periods.
- Boundary-setting: [[zhi-you-zi-lv-cai-neng-kan-dao-zhen-zheng-de-zi-you-durov-podcast-sui-bi]] says this reframing can free the author from useless resentment and support more neutral collaboration choices.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is based on personal reflection and interview excerpts, not a general team-design study. Its A-player/B-player framing risks oversimplifying differences in experience, role fit, management quality, and project structure.
