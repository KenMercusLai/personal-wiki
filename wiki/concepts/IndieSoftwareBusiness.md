## Definition
[[IndieSoftwareBusiness]] is an independent product operating model in which software development is one capability within a broader system of customer selection, differentiated inputs, pricing, distribution, supplier relationships, support, and continuing investment.

## Current Synthesis
The Pleco case separates building a polished app from building a durable business. A focused customer problem creates the market boundary, but code alone does not create the advantage: exclusive or difficult-to-obtain content, negotiated rights, specialized features, reputation, and cross-platform availability can make the position harder to copy. A free entry product can widen evaluation and distribution while modular purchases capture different levels of customer value, and pricing changes can be judged by net revenue rather than unit volume alone.

Durability also changes how apparently inefficient work is evaluated. Pleco's Android version reportedly returned less revenue per unit of development effort than iOS, yet it helped the product remain the single recommendation for learners on either platform. The relevant unit is therefore the whole business and category position, not each feature or platform in isolation.

## Key Claims
- Sustainable independent software requires a chosen customer problem and business system, not product polish alone.
- Differentiation can reside in licensed content, supplier agreements, reputation, and accumulated category knowledge as well as code and design.
- A useful free product can support paid modular upgrades when the free tier demonstrates quality and the add-ons match distinct customer needs.
- Pricing should be evaluated through captured value and net economics rather than download volume or the lowest acceptable sticker price.
- A lower-return platform can still be strategically useful when it preserves recommendation coverage and category ownership.
- App-store policy shapes available business models, but does not determine the outcome independently of product, customer, and operating choices.

## Evidence
- Business system: [[pleco-building-a-business-not-an-app]] attributes Pleco's position to customer understanding, publisher deals, pricing, free and paid investment, and platform maintenance in addition to development.
- Non-code differentiation: [[pleco-building-a-business-not-an-app]] identifies exclusive dictionaries and licensed handwriting recognition as early advantages.
- Free-to-paid architecture: [[pleco-building-a-business-not-an-app]] describes a strong free dictionary product with optional dictionaries, stroke order, fonts, document reading, and OCR.
- Pricing: [[pleco-building-a-business-not-an-app]] reports that lowering and unbundling the basic package increased net revenue.
- Portfolio logic: [[pleco-building-a-business-not-an-app]] says Android produced about one-third of iOS revenue while consuming more than one-third of development time, but was retained for cross-platform brand coverage.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Pleco is one favorable retrospective selected to rebut a pessimistic indie-app anecdote. It provides no base rate, audited accounts, customer cohorts, licensing-cost breakdown, competitor comparison, or counterfactual showing which decisions caused longevity. Early entry, exclusive rights, a specialized learning need, and accumulated reputation may make the case unusually defensible. Free with in-app purchase is one viable architecture, not a universal rule; paid-up-front, subscription, services, advertising, institutional sales, or hybrid models can fit different value and cost structures.

## What Changed
- Established independent software as a joined product, commercial, supplier, and platform operating system.
- Added brand coverage as a reason to maintain a platform whose direct development return is weaker.
- Preserved single-case and transferability limits around the Pleco example.
