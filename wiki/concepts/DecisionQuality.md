## Definition
[[DecisionQuality]] is the value of making consequential judgments well rather than maximizing the number or apparent speed of decisions made.

## Current Synthesis
[[JeffBezos]] argues that executive productivity should be evaluated by the quality of a small number of important decisions and interactions, not by daily decision count or hours awake. In his account, sacrificing sleep may add nominal working time while reducing the judgment that makes that time valuable. The defensible synthesis is a prioritization rule: when a role concentrates consequence in a few choices, recovery and attention can matter more than visible activity volume.

## Key Claims
- Decision count is a weak productivity proxy when a few choices carry disproportionate consequences.
- More available working time can create illusory productivity if fatigue lowers judgment or interaction quality.
- Recovery can be part of decision infrastructure rather than time outside performance.
- The relevant tradeoff is decision value against decision volume, not inactivity against activity.

## Evidence
- Consequential-choice priority: [[jeff-bezos-why-getting-8-hours-of-sleep-is-good-for-amazon-shareholders]] quotes Bezos saying that making a small number of key decisions well matters more than making many decisions.
- Sleep and judgment: [[jeff-bezos-why-getting-8-hours-of-sleep-is-good-for-amazon-shareholders]] says shortchanging sleep may produce extra nominal hours while making their productivity illusory.
- Interaction quality: [[jeff-bezos-why-getting-8-hours-of-sleep-is-good-for-amazon-shareholders]] extends the quality-over-quantity claim from decisions to interactions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one executive's brief self-report, not a measured decision framework or causal study. The source does not define decision quality, compare outcomes at different sleep durations, separate sleep from other conditions, or show that fewer decisions are always better. Some roles require timely high-volume triage, and delay itself can impose costs. The concept should therefore guide attention toward consequence and judgment without becoming a universal rule about pace, volume, or individual sleep needs.

## What Changed
- Created a source-scoped distinction between consequential judgment and raw decision volume.
- Added sleep and recovery as possible inputs to decision quality rather than losses of productive time.
