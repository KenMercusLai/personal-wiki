## Definition
[[VentureInvestorDevelopment]] is the selection, entry, and formation of venture investors through attributes, differentiated deal access, domain capability, mentorship, networks, repeated investment work, and experience across company and market cycles.

## Current Synthesis
The sources present investor development as both a selection problem and a long-duration learning problem. Candidate qualities include curiosity, relevant expertise, passion, focus, relationship building, persuasion, emotional intelligence, and conviction, with communication practices from journalism offering a concrete model for inquiry and synthesis. For an early-career hire, those qualities become useful when they create incremental investment access: credible emerging-domain judgment or trusted relationships that bring a firm companies it would otherwise miss. Financial modeling and delegated diligence alone are presented as weaker differentiators in early-stage practice.

A scalable program could combine software, education, institutional hiring, structured sourcing teams, brand, fellowships, syndicates, scouts, and peer networks. Direct practice matters alongside formal programs: candidates can research sectors, identify relevant companies, cultivate founder relationships, and use the recruiting process itself to demonstrate sourcing judgment. Firm fit and deal-flow quality shape the resulting apprenticeship, while hiring timing may loosely follow new funds but remains uncertain.

The hardest task is measuring intangibles without confusing existing access with future ability. One source's diversity goal therefore creates an important design test for the other's hiring advice: a system should broaden opportunity across gender, race, and geography, but reliance on elite schools, prestigious experience, network strength, social visibility, or incumbent validation may reproduce the barriers it intends to lower.

## Key Claims
- Strong investor selection requires several cognitive, interpersonal, commercial, and domain capabilities that produce differentiated investment access rather than a single background credential.
- Research and communication practices associated with journalism can support investor sourcing, inquiry, verification, and concise explanation.
- Apprenticeship, mentorship, repeated company problems, and exposure to market cycles are formative inputs that short assessments cannot easily replace.
- Structured sourcing roles and self-directed company discovery can provide early practice, while firm fit and deal-flow quality shape what the apprentice learns.
- Technology, education, brand, and network mechanisms can be combined into new investor-development models, but neither source establishes which pathways predict performance.
- Pipeline expansion is a substantive success criterion because capital, relationships, elite credentials, experience, and geography make venture entry unusually difficult.

## Evidence
- Multidimensional qualities: [[decoding-the-qualities-of-a-great-vc-by]] gives eight core attributes and records six additional community suggestions.
- Incremental access: [[getting-a-vc-job-better-everyday]] makes otherwise-missed investments the proposed test for an early-career hire and identifies emerging-domain expertise or trusted founder access as the main routes.
- Transferable inquiry skills: [[decoding-the-qualities-of-a-great-vc-by]] uses journalists' sourcing, questioning, validation, and communication practices as a model.
- Experiential formation: [[decoding-the-qualities-of-a-great-vc-by]] invokes mentorship, prior operating or writing experience, the dot-com cycle, and portfolio-company problems; [[getting-a-vc-job-better-everyday]] adds structured outbound sourcing, direct company discovery, and high-growth operating work.
- Recruiting as work sample: [[getting-a-vc-job-better-everyday]] recommends mapping firms, networking into them, cultivating long-cycle relationships, and selectively surfacing relevant companies as evidence of sourcing ability.
- Program design space: [[decoding-the-qualities-of-a-great-vc-by]] points to classes, fellowships, syndicates, scouts, internal training, self-funded learning, and a possible “YC for investors”; [[getting-a-vc-job-better-everyday]] identifies established analyst sourcing teams as a demanding training ground.
- Access objective and tension: [[decoding-the-qualities-of-a-great-vc-by]] seeks wider access across gender, race, geography, capital, networks, and experience, while [[getting-a-vc-job-better-everyday]] describes elite-school pipelines and network reach that may encode incumbent advantage.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The synthesis comes from two 2017 practitioner essays, including one partly crowdsourced trait exploration, not prospective studies of investor performance. Their qualities are broad, overlap conceptually, lack operational definitions and weights, and may be judged differently across cultures, geographies, stages, or investment strategies. No evidence shows that a course, ranking system, fellowship, scout program, analyst sourcing role, public sector thesis, or networking strategy predicts returns, improves founder outcomes, or produces more durable diversity. Selection based on elite schools, network strength, conventional experience, social ubiquity, or approval by established investors may encode incumbency and homophily rather than latent skill. Public-stock or cryptocurrency speculation is also a weak proxy for illiquid private-market sourcing and may create personal financial risk.

## What Changed
- Added differentiated deal access as the practical early-career hiring test.
- Added analyst sourcing, self-directed company discovery, and recruiting behavior as apprenticeship routes and work samples.
- Qualified network- and elite-school-based selection against the existing pipeline-diversity objective.
