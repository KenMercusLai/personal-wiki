## Definition
[[IntentionalLifeDesign]] is the practice of allocating finite time, attention, money, health, and relationships according to consciously chosen values while retaining enough freedom to act on worthwhile opportunities.

## Current Synthesis
The source frames a good life as a portfolio rather than a single maxim. Close relationships, health, learning, gratitude, generosity, and new experiences supply meaning; purposeful work, focus, talented peers, and strenuous effort support achievement; restrained consumption and reduced cognitive load preserve room to choose. Long-range goals give direction, but plans remain subordinate to rare opportunities and to actions whose omission would create lasting regret.

The organizing insight is temporal. Individual days can feel long even while decades disappear, so repeated conduct matters more than distant intention. The same principle qualifies ambition: work can be central without displacing every other source of value, and status without substance is neither durable nor satisfying.

## Key Claims
- Finite time makes relationships, health, meaningful activity, and direct action higher priorities than external expectations or low-value obligations.
- Goals at multiple horizons focus effort, but exceptional opportunities and regret-sensitive choices can justify revising the plan.
- Freedom depends partly on financial margin, restrained consumption, and protection from avoidable cognitive load.
- Meaningful achievement joins good problem selection, focus, self-belief, relationships, talent judgment, and sustained effort.
- Learning, novelty, empathy, gratitude, forgiveness, and helping others are recurring practices that keep a life adaptive and connected.

## Evidence
### Time and relationship priorities
- [[sam-altman-shi-nian-hen-duan-yi-ri-hen-chang]] prioritizes family, close friends, partners, health, and meaningful activity, closing with the observation that days feel long while decades pass quickly.

### Agency, goals, and opportunity
- [[sam-altman-shi-nian-hen-duan-yi-ri-hen-chang]] pairs daily, annual, and decade goals with seizing unusually good opportunities, asking directly for what one wants, and acting when omission is likely to produce regret.

### Freedom and attention
- [[sam-altman-shi-nian-hen-duan-yi-ri-hen-chang]] treats money chiefly as freedom from pressure, recommends controlling consumption, and urges reducing distraction and cognitive burden.

### Achievement and continued growth
- [[sam-altman-shi-nian-hen-duan-yi-ri-hen-chang]] connects achievement to problem selection, focus, effort, capable peers, long-form learning, experimentation, and direct action.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is a translated personal essay of aphorisms, not a longitudinal or comparative study. Its introduction itself argues that advice changes meaning with the speaker's and reader's context. Financial security, health, family obligations, geography, discrimination, and access to opportunity constrain how much risk, novelty, consumption control, or relationship pruning is feasible. The essay also leaves unresolved how to distinguish productive intensity from overwork and wise risk-taking from avoidable harm.

## What Changed
- Established a portfolio model linking relationships, health, work, learning, money, attention, and agency.
- Made time horizon the central reason repeated daily choices outrank distant intention.
- Preserved the tension between ambitious effort and a life not consumed by work.
- Bounded the framework as contextual practitioner advice rather than causal evidence.
