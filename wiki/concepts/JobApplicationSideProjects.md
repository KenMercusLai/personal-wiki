## Definition
[[JobApplicationSideProjects]] are shipped personal projects designed to make a candidate's ability, judgment, and fit more credible to resume screeners and interviewers.

## Current Synthesis
The Hack Career source treats side projects as evidence, not decoration. For a project to strengthen a job application, it should be relevant to the target role, deep enough to represent the quality of work the candidate would produce on the job, and iterated after launch or feedback. The article warns that viral side projects exist but are rare; most candidates should instead make ordinary projects useful by shipping something real, improving it, and presenting it clearly through resumes, metrics, screenshots, demo material, and public process notes.

## Key Claims
- A side project helps a job application only when it addresses a real concern or strengthens a relevant claim about the candidate.
- Viral attention is an exceptional path, not the normal standard for project usefulness.
- Relevance, depth, and iteration are the source's core tests for whether a project is worth employer attention.
- Skill learning becomes application evidence only when it produces a shipped artifact.
- Resume placement, metrics, screenshots, demos, and process writeups make project evidence visible to reviewers.

## Evidence
- Application fit: [[3-principles-to-make-your-side-project-stand-out-to-help-you-land-a-job-in-tech-hack-career]] says relevant projects can address concerns about a candidate's experience, such as technical background for product management or direct content experience for marketing.
- Exceptional path: [[3-principles-to-make-your-side-project-stand-out-to-help-you-land-a-job-in-tech-hack-career]] uses [[YouTubeInstant]] as a rare "Field of Dreams" case rather than the standard most projects must meet.
- Quality tests: [[3-principles-to-make-your-side-project-stand-out-to-help-you-land-a-job-in-tech-hack-career]] names relevance, depth, and at least two iterations as the criteria for a useful project.
- Shipped artifact: [[3-principles-to-make-your-side-project-stand-out-to-help-you-land-a-job-in-tech-hack-career]] explicitly says completing a course is not the same as having a side project.
- Visibility: [[3-principles-to-make-your-side-project-stand-out-to-help-you-land-a-job-in-tech-hack-career]] recommends prominent resume placement, careful descriptions, credibility indicators, public process documentation, screenshots, and demos.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is career advice rather than hiring-outcome research. It does not prove that any single project will change an employer's decision, and its recommendations depend on the target role, reviewer attention, applicant baseline experience, and whether the project can be evaluated quickly.
