## Definition
[[CuriosityDrivenAction]] is a practice of turning an unresolved question into observation, experiment, construction, and feedback before complete knowledge is available.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats curiosity as both motive and method. A sufficiently compelling question makes uncertainty something to investigate rather than a reason to wait: the learner estimates, asks across role boundaries, follows conceptual rabbit trails, builds a market-facing test, and uses the response to decide what to learn next. Knowledge remains useful, but should stay subordinate to inquiry rather than becoming a claim that the relevant possibilities are already known.

The Praxis anecdote supplies the strongest concrete loop. The author began with a question about moving young people into work they love at no cost within a year, then treated building and market response as the means of finding an answer. Smaller curiosity games apply the same posture to ordinary settings by moving from visible activity to revenue, costs, survival, and replacement hypotheses.

## Key Claims
- A compelling question can convert uncertainty into experiment and feedback.
- Acting before complete knowledge can expose information that prior analysis cannot supply.
- Cross-role and cross-domain inquiry can produce practical ideas beyond a person's assigned specialty.
- Curiosity can be cultivated through repeated observation, estimation, and follow-up questions.
- Gaining expertise should refine inquiry without eliminating naive possibilities.

## Evidence
- Venture experiment: [[curiosity-is-better-than-knowledge-mission-org-medium]] says a career-transition question led the author to launch [[Praxis]] and seek the answer in the market.
- Everyday practice: [[curiosity-is-better-than-knowledge-mission-org-medium]] proposes estimating a coffee shop's economics and likely successors as a game that generates nested questions.
- Workplace breadth: [[curiosity-is-better-than-knowledge-mission-org-medium]] attributes professional success and employee upside to exploring other roles, motivations, and processes.
- Cross-domain transfer: [[curiosity-is-better-than-knowledge-mission-org-medium]] says a curiosity-led path into rational choice theory improved the author's management, product, sales, and marketing work.
- Expertise boundary: [[curiosity-is-better-than-knowledge-mission-org-medium]] urges readers to retain questions and optimistic possibility even after inquiry produces knowledge.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is an unnamed author's retrospective and hiring experience, not a comparison of curious and knowledgeable people or ventures. Ignorance can enable action, but can also hide technical, ethical, financial, legal, or safety constraints; expertise often improves question quality and experiment design. The essay's contrast is therefore most useful as a warning against certainty and analysis paralysis, not as a general case for uninformed action or against domain knowledge.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish question-led experimentation from curiosity as motivation alone.
- Preserved expertise as a complement and safety boundary rather than accepting the essay's knowledge-versus-curiosity slogan literally.
