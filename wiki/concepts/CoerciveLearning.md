## Definition
[[CoerciveLearning]] is learning driven by imposed curriculum, pressure, punishment, false rewards, or institutional demands that override the learner's own valuation signals.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats coercive learning as the main educational threat to [[LearnDrive]]. When school repeatedly requires low-value material, the learner may associate learning contexts with negative value, boredom, or helpless compliance. This does not merely make study unpleasant; in Wozniak's theory, it can weaken the [[KnowledgeValuationNetwork]] that selects, consolidates, and enjoys knowledge.

Coercive learning is also presented as a social design problem. A one-size-fits-all school system can make all children acquire the same knowledge while ignoring the personal valuation networks that make learning efficient and creative.

## Key Claims
- Coercive learning can suppress curiosity by forcing low-value material onto learners.
- False rewards and mild punishments can damage knowledge valuation rather than strengthen it.
- Schooling can train passive habits when it repeatedly blocks personally meaningful goals.
- Coercive contexts may produce negative associations that inhibit memory consolidation.
- Educational systems should create space for individual development rather than standardized compliance.

## Evidence
- One-size-fits-all critique: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] says the education system's main problem is pushing the same knowledge through all children while ignoring learn drive.
- Negative valuation: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] argues that endless false rewards and mild punishment may shut down knowledge valuation.
- Passive habits: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] contrasts natural goal pursuit with passive schooling that can lead to learned helplessness.
- Personal case: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] says Wozniak's school repeatedly obstructed his most important SuperMemo-related learning goal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is polemical about schooling and relies heavily on Wozniak's theory and autobiography. It does not separate all possible formal education from coercive forms, so this page treats coercion, standardization, and valuation suppression as the central mechanism rather than assuming every school experience has the same effect.
