## Definition
[[TrustMinimizationTechnology]] is a product, protocol, device, or automation that makes transactions between unfamiliar parties safer or more enforceable without relying as heavily on interpersonal trust or a dominant platform's trust systems.

## Current Synthesis
Dellanna treats trust-minimization technology as the weaker point in aggregator defenses. Demand ownership is self-reinforcing, so directly replacing an incumbent aggregator is difficult. But part of an aggregator's profit comes from replacing the work users would otherwise spend on vetting, contracts, insurance, payment assurance, dispatch, and legal enforcement. If smart locks, smart contracts, self-driving cars, or other low-human-action systems make the underlying transaction safer, they may let suppliers charge premiums inside the existing aggregator, build parallel discovery surfaces, or eventually weaken the platform's trust advantage.

## Key Claims
- Trust minimization targets the trust-enablement part of aggregator value rather than the demand-ownership part.
- Hardware, protocols, or automated enforcement can reduce the need for parties to rely on platform reputation alone.
- Incumbent platforms may absorb the benefit when suppliers list safer offerings on the dominant marketplace.
- A challenger is more plausible when trust-minimizing infrastructure also creates a convenient alternate discovery layer.
- The concept does not imply that all transactions can become trustless; it shifts which risks require human or institutional trust.

## Evidence
- Profit source: [[aggregators-and-trust-luca-dellanna]] says aggregators capture value by replacing costs such as vetting, contracts, insurance, and lawyers.
- Smart-lock example: [[aggregators-and-trust-luca-dellanna]] imagines safer room rental through a lock that prevents a landlord from entering a rented part of a house.
- Likely absorption: [[aggregators-and-trust-luca-dellanna]] says the most probable scenario is that safer listings remain on [[Airbnb]] and collect a premium.
- Challenger path: [[aggregators-and-trust-luca-dellanna]] says a third party could list all compatible smart-lock houses and make parallel search convenient enough to challenge the incumbent.
- Technology set: [[aggregators-and-trust-luca-dellanna]] names smart contracts, machine-operated door locks, and self-driven cars as possible enablers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The article presents hypothetical scenarios rather than observed disruption. Trust-minimizing tools can introduce new risks around device reliability, standards, security, liability, governance, adoption, and user experience. Incumbent aggregators may also integrate the technology faster than entrants can aggregate demand.
