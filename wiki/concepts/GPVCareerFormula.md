## Definition
[[GPVCareerFormula]] is a career-reflection framework that combines gifts, passion, and values to identify work a person can do well, cares about, and regards as ethically or personally acceptable.

## Current Synthesis
The formula gives a compact prompt for career reflection: gifts ask what a person is strong at, passion asks what they like doing, and values ask whether the work fits their view of life and conduct. The source's important qualification is that the formula should be applied at the career-path level, not only to a current job title. When a direct job version of a passion is unattractive, the same ingredients may point toward a different role in the same broader field.

## Key Claims
- Gifts identify strengths and advantages that can support good work.
- Passion identifies activities or domains that generate interest and desire.
- Values test whether the work fits a person's life view and standards.
- Career fit emerges when the work combines what someone recognizes, does well, and loves.
- The formula works best when applied to a career path rather than a single job.

## Evidence
- Formula definition: [[tuimo-shi-yong-gpv-si-kao-ni-de-zhi-ye-sheng-ya]] defines gifts, passion, values, and career as the source's central career equation.
- Fit criterion: [[tuimo-shi-yong-gpv-si-kao-ni-de-zhi-ye-sheng-ya]] says career should combine what a person identifies with, is good at, and loves.
- Path-level use: [[tuimo-shi-yong-gpv-si-kao-ni-de-zhi-ye-sheng-ya]] argues that career is broader than job and can involve alternate routes in the same field.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source includes skepticism that some hobbies cannot become careers. Its answer is not that every passion should be monetized, but that the person should look for adjacent career forms and accept that some routes require staged progress.
