## Definition
[[FactorInvesting]] is an investing approach that seeks systematic exposure to characteristics associated with long-term differences in stock returns, such as size, value, low volatility, dividends, quality, or momentum.

## Current Synthesis
Wulc's summary of Zhang Xiaoyu's course presents factor investing as a middle path between plain passive index investing and active stock selection. The course introduces the Fama-style idea that stock returns can be partly explained by recurring factors, then translates that into ordinary-investor practice: instead of trying to copy a famous investor's whole skill, an investor can use low-cost factor funds to capture a narrow and systematic part of the pattern. The article keeps the claim qualified: factors can rotate over five- or ten-year windows, so the same diversification principle used across asset classes should also apply across factors.

## Key Claims
- Some stock-return differences can be partly explained by systematic factors rather than only manager genius or company stories.
- Size, value, low volatility, dividend, quality, and momentum are presented as the course's six widely accepted factors.
- Factor funds make systematic factor exposure easier for ordinary investors than hand-picking individual stocks.
- Factor investing is not pure passive market replication, but it also avoids some demands of discretionary active selection.
- Factors are cyclical, so concentration in one currently fashionable factor can underperform a plain broad-market index.

## Evidence
- Return explanation: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-3-tou-zi-zu-he-gou-jian]] introduces factor investing through market, size, and value explanations associated with Eugene Fama's three-factor model.
- Six-factor list: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-3-tou-zi-zu-he-gou-jian]] lists size, value, low volatility, dividend, quality, and momentum as repeatedly validated factors.
- Fund implementation: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-3-tou-zi-zu-he-gou-jian]] says large fund providers offer low-cost factor index funds and that domestic products include dividend, value, low-volatility, and quality-style funds.
- Active-passive middle: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-3-tou-zi-zu-he-gou-jian]] frames factor investing as a way to raise long-term returns somewhat without requiring deep discretionary expertise.
- Cyclicality: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-3-tou-zi-zu-he-gou-jian]] warns that different factors perform well in different periods and that a poorly timed factor can lag broad index exposure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a course summary, not a full empirical review of factor premia, implementation costs, tax effects, crowding, factor definitions, or factor decay. Its practical warning is that factors should not become another one-factor bet; diversification and market-context judgment still matter.
