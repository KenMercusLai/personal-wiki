## Definition
[[AppleRealWorldSocialNetwork]] is [[BenThompson]]'s frame for [[Apple]] using retail stores, [[TodayAtApple]] sessions, devices, Apple IDs, and controlled local programming to build real-world customer engagement rather than a traditional algorithmic social network.

## Current Synthesis
The Stratechery source argues that Apple's culture is poorly suited to building a conventional online social network: Apple prefers control, polish, and integrated products, while traditional social networks require constant iteration around user-generated variability and edge cases. Thompson therefore treats Today at Apple as a more Apple-like answer to engagement. The program brings customers into stores for creative sessions, product learning, local talent, and group activities, making Apple ownership feel like entry into a continuing social and educational relationship. The strategic analogy matters because Apple was also asking investors to think beyond device units toward Services, engagement, installed base, and loyalty. If Apple wants to be valued partly on ongoing monetization of an existing base, Thompson argues that it should disclose customer-level metrics closer to active users and revenue per user, not only active devices and Services revenue.

## Key Claims
- Apple is culturally mismatched with traditional social networks built on user-generated content, feeds, algorithms, and constant edge-case handling.
- [[TodayAtApple]] gives Apple a controlled, offline form of social engagement that fits its strengths in retail, curriculum, products, and experience design.
- The program reframes Apple products as access points to an ongoing relationship with Apple rather than isolated hardware purchases.
- Apple's engagement strategy is tied to its mature growth story: as unit growth slows, installed-base monetization and customer loyalty become more important.
- Active devices are useful but incomplete because investors also need active-customer and revenue-per-user views to understand the value of Apple's base.

## Evidence
- Social-network mismatch: [[apples-social-network-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] argues Apple's past social efforts failed because its culture differs from the iterative, variability-heavy culture of traditional social networks.
- Real-world social layer: [[apples-social-network-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] interprets Ahrendts' "real social way" language as evidence that Apple is building offline social engagement.
- Retail scale: [[apples-social-network-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] reports more than 18,000 weekly Today at Apple sessions across 505 retail locations.
- Relationship framing: [[apples-social-network-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] says Today at Apple implies customers are buying into an ongoing relationship with Apple.
- Metrics implication: [[apples-social-network-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] argues Apple's reporting should include active customers and revenue per user if growth comes from monetizing the existing base over time.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is source-scoped to Thompson's 2018 interpretation. The source does not prove that Today at Apple generated measurable retention, Services spending, hardware upgrades, or durable community formation. The analogy also differs from ad-supported social networks because Apple's economic model remains hardware and ecosystem monetization rather than advertising inventory.
