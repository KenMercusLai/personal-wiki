## Definition
Subject line optimization is the practice of using engagement data to improve email subject lines, including length, wording, clarity, and fit with recipient expectations.

## Current Synthesis
The SendGrid source presents subject-line optimization as a measurable email-marketing problem. In an early-November analysis, nine-word subject lines were the most common, but two-to-four-word subject lines had the highest engagement, suggesting that concise subject lines can outperform more typical longer copy. The article also uses an 80,000-subject-line word cloud to show how crowded and repetitive inbox language can become during retail campaigns.

## Key Claims
- Common subject-line practice may not match highest-engagement practice.
- Brevity can improve email engagement when short lines communicate the offer quickly.
- Large subject-line corpora reveal inbox-level competition among marketers.
- Subject-line findings should be treated as campaign-context evidence rather than universal copywriting law.

## Evidence
Common practice may not match highest-engagement practice:
- [[black-friday-and-cyber-monday-by-the-numbers-sendgrid]] says nine-word subject lines were most popular in the sample while shorter subject lines had higher engagement.

Brevity can improve engagement:
- [[black-friday-and-cyber-monday-by-the-numbers-sendgrid]] reports two-to-four-word subject lines as the highest-engagement group in the early-November analysis.

Large subject-line corpora reveal inbox-level competition:
- [[black-friday-and-cyber-monday-by-the-numbers-sendgrid]] describes a word cloud built from 80,000 English-language subject lines as the "sensory bombardment" of the inbox.

Subject-line findings are campaign-context evidence:
- [[black-friday-and-cyber-monday-by-the-numbers-sendgrid]] frames the observations around holiday marketing and early-November data rather than a controlled copywriting experiment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not publish the full dataset, segmentation, or causal controls. Shorter subject lines may correlate with different senders, offers, audiences, or campaign types rather than causing higher engagement by length alone.
