## Definition
[[FamilyOfOrigin]] is the early family field that teaches emotional reactions, relationship expectations, scarcity or safety assumptions, and default ways of handling conflict before a person can consciously evaluate them.

## Current Synthesis
The source frames family of origin as the first field a person inhabits. It shapes not only explicit beliefs but also bodily memory: how conflict feels, whether needs can be spoken, whether money feels safe, and what love looks like. Adulthood may require separating from that inherited field, building new routines, and becoming economically independent enough to meet parents from a more equal position.

## Key Claims
- Early family patterns become emotional reflexes rather than just remembered ideas.
- Returning to the family field can reactivate older roles even after adulthood.
- Self-reparenting means constructing a new field where different forms of care, speech, and daily ritual become possible.
- Spiritual separation from parents is not necessarily rejection; it can enable a more equal relationship.
- Economic independence is often the practical foundation for psychological independence.

## Evidence
- Emotional reflexes: [[chang]] describes children absorbing parents' conflict, problem-solving, silence, fear, and daily atmosphere like a sponge.
- Role reactivation: [[chang]] notes that adults can regress into old patterns when returning home or confronting familiar family scripts.
- New field building: [[chang]] presents self-reparenting through new friends, rituals, living spaces, and kinder self-talk.
- Spiritual separation: [[chang]] frames symbolic separation from parents as loyalty to one's own life rather than betrayal.
- Economic foundation: [[chang]] argues that earning and living independently gives a person the material basis to withstand parental pressure and make decisions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's account is reflective and may fit people whose family fields were limiting or harmful more than those whose families remain supportive. Economic independence is presented as crucial, but access to it varies with class, health, local housing markets, care duties, and broader labor conditions.
