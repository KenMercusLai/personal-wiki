## Definition
[[SearchPlatformDisintermediation]] is the shift from a search engine referring users to third-party sites toward answering, comparing, ranking, or transacting within its own result surface.

## Current Synthesis
The source attributes this shift to mobile constraints and platform incentives. Users with limited time and bandwidth prefer an immediate answer or a compact set of options, while a search platform that controls demand can improve that experience by ingesting supplier inventory and owning more of discovery. The former intermediary is not removed from the chain; it removes or weakens the specialized aggregator between itself and individual suppliers.

For third-party aggregators, the strategic change is twofold. Search policies against crawlable internal results and doorway pages narrow the space for query-shaped inventory pages, while richer vertical results make even successfully indexed category pages less central. Winters's proposed response is to make individual listings legible to the platform and to convert borrowed reach into loyal direct demand. That response trades page-level control for continued visibility and does not eliminate the risk that the platform will rank suppliers, own the user relationship, or move toward the transaction.

## Key Claims
- Mobile search can favor direct answers and option cards over lists of external links.
- A demand-controlling search platform can internalize aggregation by building, buying, or partnering for supply and data.
- Internal-search and doorway-page rules can reduce the indexable surface available to third-party inventory aggregators.
- Supplying individual listings may preserve visibility inside platform discovery while weakening the aggregator's control of comparison and customer relationships.
- Refusal to participate is difficult when competitors can cooperate, but direct audience loyalty and switching costs can preserve bargaining power.
- Aggregator result pages may still add comparison and conversion value that a generic platform surface does not reproduce.

## Evidence
- User-experience mechanism: [[googles-new-strategy-and-how-it-affects-aggregators-casey-accidental]] links mobile time and bandwidth constraints to immediate answers and discovery cards.
- Visible product example: [[googles-new-strategy-and-how-it-affects-aggregators-casey-accidental]] includes a mobile recipe result in which Google presents named recipe cards before conventional external results.
- Policy mechanism: [[googles-new-strategy-and-how-it-affects-aggregators-casey-accidental]] combines Google's internal-search and doorway-page guidance into a narrow indexability window for inventory pages.
- Entry modes: [[googles-new-strategy-and-how-it-affects-aggregators-casey-accidental]] uses Google Local, Google Flights, and Google Events as build, buy, and partner cases.
- Strategic response: [[googles-new-strategy-and-how-it-affects-aggregators-casey-accidental]] recommends distributing individual listings and developing direct demand rather than relying on query-category rankings.
- Value qualification: [[googles-new-strategy-and-how-it-affects-aggregators-casey-accidental]] preserves a reader's counterpoint that aggregator result pages can offer useful comparison and stronger conversion.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one 2018 practitioner essay and an appended reader comment, not a cross-category traffic study or causal evaluation of ranking changes. The article does not quantify click loss, conversion effects, supplier economics, or the relative welfare created by Google and specialist aggregators. Its claim about Chrome data use is unsupported in the source, its AMP recommendation is historically situated, and its ten-year forecast should not be treated as an observed outcome. Direct answers may improve user experience, specialist aggregators may still add selection or workflow value, and search policies can target low-value pages without implying that all inventory presentation is penalized.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish search referral from platform-owned answering and vertical discovery.
- Connected mobile experience, indexability policy, vertical entry, supplier participation, and direct-demand strategy in one mechanism.
- Preserved the countercase that specialist result pages may add comparison value and convert better than individual listings.
