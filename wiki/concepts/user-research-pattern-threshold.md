Source: [7 Ways to Use the Rule of Threes to Build Great Products]({{< relref "/wiki/sources/rule-of-threes-build-great-products.md" >}})

User research pattern threshold is a lightweight rule for deciding when repeated interview or usability-testing signals should change product work. The source recommends doing interviews heavily at the start of development and testing whenever a new prototype exists, but it also notes that both activities are expensive enough that teams need a stopping rule.

The proposed threshold separates signal strength into three levels. A finding heard once is an anecdote to note and continue testing. The same finding heard twice deserves attention but may still be coincidence. When the same finding appears three times, the team can treat it as a pattern and start building against that hypothesis.

The source limits this rule to research with the intended audience. Repetition from people outside the target users does not produce the same confidence because it may reflect the wrong needs, context, or constraints.
