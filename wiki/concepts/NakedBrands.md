## Definition
[[NakedBrands]] are [[DavidPerell]]'s proposed influencer-led brand archetype in which a visible founder or creator, frequent direct communication, perceived authenticity, and audience participation carry more identity and trust than conventional broadcast advertising alone.

## Current Synthesis
The concept is best understood as a change in brand evidence and distribution, not the disappearance of branding. Social platforms let a creator repeatedly expose daily work, struggles, lifestyle, product decisions, and company milestones; audiences can answer through comments, attendance, purchases, evangelism, and product suggestions. [[LoganPaul]] illustrates online identity becoming a physical merchandise ritual, [[CaseyNeistat]] illustrates company-building as serialized media, and [[EmilyWeiss]] with [[Glossier]] illustrates an audience preceding and participating in a consumer product brand.

The label overstates its contrast with “traditional” brands. Creator names, recurring aesthetics, merchandise, stories, symbols, and communities still accumulate [[BrandEquity]]. Transparency is also a perception that can be selectively produced, while dependence on a personality and platform introduces concentration, reputation, and distribution risk.

## Key Claims
- Changes in media distribution alter how brands repeatedly reach audiences and make claims credible.
- A creator's ongoing public persona can become a central brand asset and distribution channel.
- Fans may enact attachment through participation, rituals, merchandise, events, evangelism, and pride in early discovery.
- Audience-before-product and visible company-building can make customers feel involved in a brand's evolution.
- Reviews and searchable product information add trust signals beyond advertising, but they do not eliminate accumulated brand meaning.
- Perceived authenticity can strengthen attachment without proving disclosure completeness, product quality, or durable commercial performance.

## Evidence
- Creator-to-commerce conversion: [[naked-brands-adventures-in-consumer-technology-medium]] reports the long line for Logan Paul's temporary merchandise shop.
- Serialized company building: [[naked-brands-adventures-in-consumer-technology-medium]] describes Casey Neistat exposing Beme's launches and struggles through a daily vlog.
- Audience-first participation: [[naked-brands-adventures-in-consumer-technology-medium]] presents Emily Weiss's Into the Gloss audience and Glossier product crowdsourcing as a community-led sequence.
- Platform trust shift: [[naked-brands-adventures-in-consumer-technology-medium]] argues that reviews, keywords, ingredients, and use cases compete with advertising as purchase evidence on algorithmic marketplaces.
- Historical mechanism: [[naked-brands-adventures-in-consumer-technology-medium]] connects ownership marks, industrial quality signals, television's concentrated attention, and social media's direct access.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is grounded in one 2017 essay with selected creator examples, compressed history, generational generalization, and no comparison group. A crowded event, subscriber count, comments, or product suggestions do not establish retention, profitability, causality, representative preference, or resilience. Influencer communication can be strategic self-presentation rather than complete transparency; founder centrality can create key-person and controversy risk; and platform access remains governed by algorithms and intermediaries. The cases also preserve rather than abolish familiar brand mechanisms such as recognizable identity, symbolism, merchandise, aspiration, repetition, and social proof.

## What Changed
- Created the concept as a qualified shift in brand evidence, participation, and distribution rather than the end of traditional brand equity.
