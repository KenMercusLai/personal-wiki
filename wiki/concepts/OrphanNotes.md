## Definition
[[OrphanNotes]] are notes that are not referenced by other notes in a knowledge base, especially card notes whose missing inbound links prevent them from participating in a linked note network.

## Current Synthesis
The source defines orphan notes by inbound-link absence rather than by whether a note contains outgoing links. That distinction matters because a linked note system only becomes useful when other notes can discover, reuse, and contextualize a note. Orphans are not always mistakes: they can appear when reading strays beyond the current knowledge base, when a topic has not yet accumulated enough context, or when a bounded research project should live outside the main garden. The article's treatment model is deliberately plural: expand the local context through related reading and commentary, collect fragments into spaced-repetition review so random collisions generate associations, or leave difficult material dormant until future experience makes it meaningful.

## Key Claims
- Orphan notes are identified by missing incoming links, not merely by weak outgoing links.
- Precise semantic links are more demanding than tags or folders, so serious note systems naturally produce orphans for a time.
- Orphans can signal that a note needs neighboring notes, related literature, or external discussion before it can join the main graph.
- Standalone folders or databases can protect the main knowledge base when a one-off research cluster does not need durable integration.
- Randomized spaced repetition can turn orphaned excerpts into a creative engine by making distant fragments reappear together.
- Some orphans should remain dormant rather than be forced into premature or misleading links.

## Evidence
- Definition and diagnosis: [[wei-shen-me-yi-ji-ru-he-chu-li-gu-er-bi-ji]] defines orphan notes by lack of inbound links and shows Obsidian graph views where isolated nodes sit at the graph edge.
- Link precision: [[wei-shen-me-yi-ji-ru-he-chu-li-gu-er-bi-ji]] argues that semantic backlinks have much finer precision than tags and should not be added merely for graph aesthetics.
- Context expansion: [[wei-shen-me-yi-ji-ru-he-chu-li-gu-er-bi-ji]] recommends reading adjacent classics, commentary, or discussions so a single note can gain a small surrounding cluster.
- Standalone containment: [[wei-shen-me-yi-ji-ru-he-chu-li-gu-er-bi-ji]] uses a warmth-materials research database as an example of a useful cluster that may not belong in the main note garden.
- Spaced-repetition review: [[wei-shen-me-yi-ji-ru-he-chu-li-gu-er-bi-ji]] describes putting excerpts into Anki cloze cards so repeated random review can reveal unexpected relationships.
- Dormancy: [[wei-shen-me-yi-ji-ru-he-chu-li-gu-er-bi-ji]] argues that some topics are not yet ready for connection and should be left for future experience rather than forced.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a practitioner essay, not an empirical comparison of note-maintenance strategies. It also assumes a link-centered note system where inbound references matter; users who rely mainly on search, folders, tags, outlines, or project databases may experience fewer practical costs from orphaned notes. Its Anki strategy may work best for quote-like fragments and creative recombination, not for every kind of technical, legal, or project note.

## What Changed
- Created a canonical concept for orphan notes as a PKM maintenance problem.
- Added a three-part treatment model: contextual expansion, spaced-repetition recombination, and patient deferral.
- Added the qualification that some isolated clusters belong in standalone databases instead of the main note garden.
