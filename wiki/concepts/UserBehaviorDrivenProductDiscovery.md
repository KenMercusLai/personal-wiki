## Definition
[[UserBehaviorDrivenProductDiscovery]] is product discovery that starts from what users are actually doing with a product, especially when that behavior differs from the founder's original framing.

## Current Synthesis
The sources show discovery shifting from the founder's intended use case to behavior users were already demonstrating. [[DavidLieb]] says calling the top 100 [[Bump]] users revealed photo sharing, redirecting the team toward [[Flock]], Photoroll, and eventually [[GooglePhotos]]-related work. [[ClassPass]] adds a marketplace version: repeated Passport accounts looked fraudulent at first but revealed that variety, not one-time studio sampling, was the deeper value. [[Path]] adds a product-redesign case: users posted screenshots of music, notes, running maps, and health apps because they understood the private network as a life journal rather than only a photo-sharing tool. Path 2 formalized those behaviors as content types and reportedly grew from about 10,000 to more than 500,000 daily active users in two weeks. [[JustinTV]] supplies the slower segment-level case: gaming broadcasters remained a small but consistent group for years before the team recognized their strategic importance. [[MichaelSeibel]] provides the limiting rule across all four cases: unexpected behavior merits investigation, but only a recurring group with compatible economics, outcomes, and growth potential should redirect the company. The pattern therefore complements customer-led development without making every workaround authoritative.

## Key Claims
- Heavy users can reveal a product's real use case more clearly than the initial pitch.
- Direct calls can uncover behavior that analytics or founder intuition may not explain.
- Product pivots are stronger when they respond to observed use rather than abstract opportunity.
- A failed follow-on product can still preserve insight for later products.
- Workarounds and policy violations can contain product insight when they reveal motivation rather than mere misuse.
- Repeated behavior is more decision-relevant when it forms a coherent segment with compatible economics.
- Teams create leverage when they replace a recurring workaround with direct product support for the underlying job.

## Evidence
- Top-user calls: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says Lieb and Jake called Bump's top 100 users to learn why they used it.
- Behavior discovery: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says those calls revealed that users were sharing photos.
- Pivot attempt: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says the team showed Paul Graham the first version of [[Flock]], a semi-automated photo-sharing product.
- Later reuse: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says Photoroll was never released, but many ideas later returned in [[GooglePhotos]].
- Marketplace workaround: [[classpass-founder-on-how-marketplace-startups-can-achieve-product-market-fit-first-round-review]] says Passport users created new email addresses to keep accessing class variety.
- Subscription pivot: [[classpass-founder-on-how-marketplace-startups-can-achieve-product-market-fit-first-round-review]] says that behavior pushed [[ClassPass]] toward a subscription model where variety could work for customers and studios.
- Persistent adjacent behavior: [[users-you-dont-want]] says Justin.tv's gaming broadcasters were a small but consistent group whose importance took four to five years to recognize.
- Economic fit: [[users-you-dont-want]] says serving gaming streams did not materially change salary or bandwidth costs and created attractive advertising options.
- Workaround as product map: [[get-out-of-the-way-design-philosophy-medium]] says Path users posted screenshots to represent music, notes, running, and health activity that the photo-only product did not support directly.
- Formalized behavior: [[get-out-of-the-way-design-philosophy-medium]] says Path 2 added photos, music, locations, thoughts, health, and a Nike+ Running integration, then reports daily active users rising from about 10,000 to more than 500,000 in two weeks.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Top users may not represent the broader market, and Bump's source itself shows that discovering real behavior did not guarantee immediate growth for Flock. Workarounds can also harm suppliers, trust, compliance, safety, or economics if treated only as demand signals. Seibel's Justin.tv account is retrospective and offers no quantitative pivot threshold. The Path account is likewise a first-party retrospective: its 50-fold daily-active-user claim lacks retention, acquisition-channel, causal, and independent verification data. Behavior-driven discovery still needs a product that gives first users a reason to start, repeat, or spread it and a model that works for the company and other participants.

## What Changed
- Added Path as a case where screenshot workarounds exposed a broader journaling job and were replaced with direct product support.
- Distinguished a sharp post-redesign active-use increase from proof of causality or durable product-market fit.
