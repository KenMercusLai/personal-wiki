## Definition
[[FirstPrinciplesThinking]] is a reasoning method that decomposes a problem or thing through layers of abstraction into fundamental parts treated as true, then recombines those parts from a changed perspective.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses first-principles thinking as both a mental model and a demonstration of knowledge processing. A two-page fleeting note records definitions, assumptions, questions, evidence checks, alternative perspectives, consequences, and repeated "why" prompts. Chaperon then rewrites the essence in his own words as a permanent note and converts its logic into a directed concept map. The practical lesson is less a complete theory of first principles than a three-representation workflow: exploratory capture, compressed prose, and explicit relationships.

## Key Claims
- First-principles reasoning breaks a complicated subject into fundamental components before recombining them.
- Useful abstraction need not reach an imagined atomic bottom; it should go deep enough to reveal different purposes, ideas, or opportunities.
- The method contrasts with beginning from an inherited recipe and making only local adjustments.
- Socratic questions, assumption challenges, evidence checks, alternative perspectives, consequence analysis, and repeated why questions can guide decomposition.
- Rewriting and visualization can expose whether the reasoner understands the model and its relationships.

## Evidence
- Working definition: [[knowledge-processing-system-for-marketers-creators-and-knowledge-workers]] defines the method as breaking a problem into known-true fundamental parts and reassembling it.
- Fleeting-note evidence: [[knowledge-processing-system-for-marketers-creators-and-knowledge-workers]] shows a handwritten two-page exploration of assumptions, evidence, alternatives, consequences, and why questions.
- Permanent-note evidence: [[knowledge-processing-system-for-marketers-creators-and-knowledge-workers]] condenses the method into a short note written in the author's own words.
- Relationship evidence: [[knowledge-processing-system-for-marketers-creators-and-knowledge-workers]] maps directed links among the problem, abstraction, layers, fundamental parts, known truths, and scientific questioning.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is one practitioner's explanation assembled partly from secondary articles and analogy. It does not establish a test for which premises are truly fundamental, compare the method with analogical or incremental reasoning, or show that the note-and-map exercise improves decisions. Decomposition can also hide interactions, uncertainty, history, and system-level behavior if parts are treated as independent too quickly.
