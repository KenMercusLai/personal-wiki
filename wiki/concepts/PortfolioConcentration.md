## Definition
[[PortfolioConcentration]] is the choice to hold fewer investments so each position can receive more attention, judgment, influence, or follow-through.

## Current Synthesis
Wilson frames concentration as the natural partner of active investing. If an investor intends to help shape outcomes, the scarce resource is not only capital but time, energy, and intellect. Diversification remains viable, but in this source it belongs with passive investing because spreading capital across many positions is incompatible with meaningful active involvement in each one.

## Key Claims
- Concentration is a practical consequence of active investing's attention demands.
- Diversification is more compatible with passive investing than with hands-on investing.
- Attempting to scale an active posture across too many investments can create a mismatch between promise and actual support.
- The active/concentrated and passive/diversified choice is a strategic boundary rather than a claim that one model always wins.

## Evidence
- Attention demand: [[active-vs-passive-investing-avc]] says active investors must invest time, energy, and intellect as well as capital.
- Capacity limit: [[active-vs-passive-investing-avc]] says active investing is hard to scale and limits how many investments one can make.
- Diversification pairing: [[active-vs-passive-investing-avc]] says investors should choose active and concentrated or passive and diversified.
- Model neutrality: [[active-vs-passive-investing-avc]] says both models work.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not define an optimal number of investments or distinguish individual investors, firms, and funds with larger teams. Its concentration claim is best read as a capacity warning rather than a universal portfolio formula.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to capture the portfolio-shape consequence of Wilson's active-versus-passive distinction.
