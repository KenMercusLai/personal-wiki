## Definition
[[LivestreamCommerce]] is ecommerce conducted through live video streams where sellers, brands, or hosts demonstrate products, answer viewers, run launches or discounts, and turn attention into transactions.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents livestream commerce as a natural extension of social livestreaming because video reduces product uncertainty while live interaction creates urgency and attention. In China, ecommerce companies and brands used livestreams for cooking competitions, seller demonstrations, product reviews, limited-time discounts, battery-life stunts, and product launches. The pattern differs from pure social livestreaming because the primary goal is utility and sales, but it can still inherit gifts, personality, and entertainment from the broader livestreaming ecosystem.

## Key Claims
- Live video makes ecommerce more persuasive by showing products and sellers in motion.
- Commerce livestreams combine demonstration, promotion, entertainment, and real-time interaction.
- Platforms can use livestreaming both for GMV growth and for virtual-gift monetization.
- Brand campaigns can treat livestreaming as product placement or spectacle rather than ordinary advertising.
- Utility-centered livestreaming may monetize differently from social entertainment streams.

## Evidence
- JD Fresh case: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] says JD Fresh broadcast a lobster-cooking competition on Douyu and saw a significant GMV increase during the period.
- Taobao Live case: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] describes Taobao Live as a separate app where sellers stream product reviews, launches, and limited-time discounts.
- Taobao screenshot: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] includes a screenshot showing a Taobao Live shopping feed and a makeup product detail stream.
- Xiaomi case: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] says Xiaomi used a 19-hour Mi Max battery livestream and a Xiaomi Drone launch to attract large audiences.
- Brand experiments: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] cites Macy's, Oreo, and branded digital gifts as early promotional experiments.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source argues from early examples, not long-term conversion, retention, or margin data. It also warns that digital gifts have different appeal in education, news, and other utility-centered streams than in playful social entertainment.
