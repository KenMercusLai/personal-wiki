## Definition
[[DigitalCartography]] is the practice and politics of making maps for screens, platforms, sensors, and live data rather than for static paper artifacts.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that digital cartography changes both map technique and map power. Maps can now be personalized, zoom-dependent, A/B tested, legally localized, commercially altered, and updated from user contributions. That makes them more useful for navigation and local discovery, but it also concentrates editorial authority in platform operators and weakens older local cartographic conventions, national styles, and shared views of the world.

## Key Claims
- Screen maps are dynamic media rather than paper maps displayed on a device.
- Personalization puts the user at the center of the map while leaving platform owners in control of data, ranking, and presentation.
- Digital maps require cartographic abstraction because showing everything can make a map less useful.
- Utility-driven global conventions can reduce local cartographic aesthetics, geopolitical nuance, and shared map literacy.
- Legal, commercial, and product-testing choices can cause different users or countries to see different map realities.

## Evidence
- Medium shift: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] quotes Parsons arguing that moving from cave walls and paper to screens should require a different cartographic leap.
- Personalization: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] compares modern personal maps to billions of miniaturized Chonhado maps centered on the user.
- Dynamic editing: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] describes zoom-level label changes, A/B tests, country-specific map renderings, and local business highlighting.
- Abstraction: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] quotes Parsons and McClendon on the need to decide which information matters at each zoom level.
- Local-convention loss: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] reports critics' concern that Google's efficient, visually banal conventions flatten local cartographic traditions and geopolitical markers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The article strongly features Google's view of digital cartography, so the concept page should not treat Google's utility-first model as the only possible model. The source also predates later augmented-reality, privacy, and platform-governance debates in mapping.
