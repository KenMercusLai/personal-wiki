## Definition
[[StrategicSelfCannibalization]] is the deliberate launch or support of a new offer that may reduce demand for a firm's successful existing product in order to preserve customer relevance through a market transition.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents self-cannibalization as one of three ways to improve survival odds across successive product cycles, alongside building a product family and creating a platform. The Apple example is the strongest: integrating iPod functions into the iPhone threatened a product that the source says represented close to half of Apple's revenue, yet allowed Apple to move customer value onto the next computing platform.

Self-cannibalization depends on organization design as well as executive intent. The source argues that Apple's shared functional structure and single P&L reduced incentives for autonomous product divisions to protect their own revenue. The strategic unit becomes the customer's problem and the company's overall future, not the preservation of one product line.

Netflix adds the transition-execution boundary. DVD rental and streaming had different content costs, product needs, and increasingly distinct customer groups, so keeping streaming as a lightly priced addition to the DVD plan would underfund the successor. Separating plans, prices, and teams created focus, but [[NeilHunt]] says Netflix moved too boldly and treated established customers too harshly during the Qwikster period. Strategic necessity therefore does not excuse abrupt packaging, pricing, communication, or migration design.

## Key Claims
- A company may need to transfer demand away from its own cash cow before a competitor does.
- Cannibalization is rational when the successor solves the customer problem better in a changed context.
- A single P&L and functional organization can reduce product-division incentives to resist transition.
- Product families and platforms provide alternative ways to spread bets across successive curves.
- Continuous experimentation is safer than waiting until decline forces one company-saving launch.
- Self-cannibalization creates transition capacity but does not guarantee that the chosen successor will work.
- Separating successor economics and teams can protect focus, but transition design must preserve trust with customers who funded the incumbent.

## Evidence
- iPod to iPhone: [[why-you-should-kill-your-cash-cow-the-startup-medium]] says Apple integrated iPod functions into the iPhone despite iPod's major revenue contribution.
- Repeated precedent: [[why-you-should-kill-your-cash-cow-the-startup-medium]] quotes Tim Cook describing Macintosh replacing Apple II, iPhone absorbing iPod demand, and iPad potentially absorbing Mac demand.
- Organizational support: [[why-you-should-kill-your-cash-cow-the-startup-medium]] and its organization chart present Apple as one functional company with one P&L rather than separate product-business heads.
- Multiple bets: [[why-you-should-kill-your-cash-cow-the-startup-medium]] uses Amazon's experiments and platforms as a model for avoiding a single late rescue bet.
- Successor economics: [[neil-hunt-on-netflix-and-the-story-of-netflix-streaming-internet-history-podcast]] says streaming content costs could not remain funded as a small increment to the DVD plan.
- Organizational focus: [[neil-hunt-on-netflix-and-the-story-of-netflix-streaming-internet-history-podcast]] says separating DVD and streaming teams let each serve its own customers and capabilities.
- Transition cost: [[neil-hunt-on-netflix-and-the-story-of-netflix-streaming-internet-history-podcast]] calls the change necessary while acknowledging heavy-handed pricing and insufficient generosity toward existing customers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Both sources rely on successful retrospective examples and do not compare failed acts of self-disruption. Cannibalization can destroy profitable demand without creating an adequate successor, confuse positioning, strain operations, or accelerate a transition before customers are ready. A functional structure may support company-wide tradeoffs but can also create coordination bottlenecks; the Apple source does not test organization structure independently from leadership, ecosystem, timing, or execution. Hunt's participant account does not compare alternative Netflix pricing, packaging, branding, or separation sequences, so “necessary” should not be read as proof that Qwikster's form or timing was inevitable.

## What Changed
- Added separate economics and team focus as mechanisms for resourcing a successor business.
- Added customer trust, pricing, packaging, communication, and migration design as constraints on self-cannibalization.
- Distinguished the need to transition from the claim that one specific execution was inevitable.
