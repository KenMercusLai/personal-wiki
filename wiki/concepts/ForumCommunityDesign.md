## Definition
[[ForumCommunityDesign]] is an online-community architecture organized around specialized topics, durable threads, searchable archives, and identities that may remain separate from a person's public real-name profile.

## Current Synthesis
The sources present online community as a bundle of participation, identity, organization, retrieval, and governance choices rather than an audience-size feature. Forums are strongest when a community needs specific experiential knowledge, stable topical organization, repeated retrieval, and pseudonymous participation; these properties can turn past conversations into a reusable knowledge base and support sensitive discussion. [[Facebook]] Groups are stronger at low-friction recruitment, recommendations, sharing, and friendship through an existing social graph. [[Kinja]] adds a publication-centered case: comments and recommendation signals can become a valued product layer at very large scale, though activity totals do not establish knowledge quality or community health. Community value therefore depends on whether participation, identity rules, navigation, moderation, and retrieval fit the group's purpose.

## Key Claims
- Specialized scope helps members exchange information that broad blogs, news outlets, and social feeds may not provide.
- Durable threads and effective search convert conversation into reusable community memory.
- Pseudonymity can reduce participation risk in health, sexuality, and other stigmatized contexts.
- Shared identity and relationships can sustain contribution beyond a member's immediate question.
- Feed-first discovery is convenient for current activity but can bury accumulated knowledge as posting volume grows.
- Real-name social graphs improve sharing and interpersonal continuity while creating membership-visibility and context-collapse risks.
- Publication comments and recommendation signals can create a meaningful community layer, but volume alone does not demonstrate quality, retrieval value, or healthy governance.

## Evidence
- Specialized knowledge and identity: [[are-forums-still-relevant-in-a-facebook-world]] uses running-shoe advice and the WDWMAGIC Disney community to argue that narrow expertise attracts members while shared identity retains them.
- Retrieval and community memory: [[are-forums-still-relevant-in-a-facebook-world]] says large Facebook Groups make threads and comments hard to archive, navigate, and search, encouraging repeated questions.
- Pseudonymity and sensitive participation: [[are-forums-still-relevant-in-a-facebook-world]] contrasts forum anonymity with Facebook groups where names, profile pictures, or membership may remain visible.
- Distribution and moderation: [[are-forums-still-relevant-in-a-facebook-world]] credits Facebook with scheduled posts, membership tools, metrics, linked-group discovery, easy resharing, and an existing friendship network.
- Publication community: [[closing-the-book-on-gawker-com]] reports more than 16 million Gawker.com comments, names Kinja as a reason the author joined Gawker Media, and preserves a list of highly recommended comments.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The Threadloom source is a historical, commercially interested blog post, not a controlled comparison of retention, knowledge quality, wellbeing, civic engagement, or moderation outcomes. Its claimed forum-reading and contribution rates refer to reports without enough local bibliographic detail to verify their samples or dates. The Gawker source supplies first-party scale and sentiment but no active-user denominator, moderation history, architecture, retention data, or comparison between recommendation totals and contribution quality. Forum search is admitted to be imperfect, pseudonymity can reduce accountability, and durable archives can preserve misinformation as well as expertise. Facebook Groups may be preferable when discovery, existing relationships, easy sharing, or rapid activity matter more than archival retrieval. Threadloom's three remote images were unavailable, while Gawker's sole local image was decorative, so no image-specific evidence was incorporated.

## What Changed
- Added a community-architecture distinction between distribution through an existing social graph and preservation of specialist knowledge.
- Made identity a two-sided design choice: authenticated profiles can aid social proof, while pseudonymity can protect sensitive participation.
- Added retrieval and thread durability as scaling properties rather than minor interface preferences.
- Added Kinja as a publication-centered example of comments and recommendations becoming a valued community product layer.
