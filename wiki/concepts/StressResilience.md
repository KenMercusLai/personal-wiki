## Definition
[[StressResilience]] is the capacity to appraise threats, tolerate manageable stress, recover, and continue effective action without collapsing into persistent fear or avoidance.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents resilience as an outcome of freely exploring manageable danger, not of indiscriminate exposure to pressure. When a person can choose a challenge, retreat, reassess, learn, and try again, repeated [[Eustress]] may improve both practical skill and the [[ProblemValuationNetwork]] that calibrates future threats.

That claim reverses a common “pressure prepares you” argument. Stress builds resilience only under conditions such as control, tolerable dose, recovery, and meaningful reward; steep or inescapable demands can instead create [[ChronicStress]], disturbed sleep, exaggerated fear, and long-term avoidance.

## Key Claims
- Resilience can grow through repeated, self-directed encounters with manageable challenge.
- Retreat, recovery, and updated threat estimates are parts of resilience rather than signs of failure.
- Practical skill and threat appraisal can improve together during productive exploration.
- Stress exposure without autonomy or recovery may weaken rather than strengthen resilience.
- Childhood conditions are presented as especially important for calibrating later fear and challenge response.

## Evidence
- Exploration loop: [[liang-xing-ya-li-de-ji-zhi]] describes repeated approach, retreat, consolidation, and skill refinement around a dangerous river crossing.
- Control condition: [[liang-xing-ya-li-de-ji-zhi]] argues that unconstrained self-control is central to productive exploration.
- Contrast case: [[liang-xing-ya-li-de-ji-zhi]] says excessive school pressure and loss of decision control can create chronic stress rather than preparedness.
- Developmental mechanism: [[liang-xing-ya-li-de-ji-zhi]] proposes that overpressure can impair the valuation system needed for realistic threat appraisal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not provide longitudinal, clinical, or comparative evidence for its developmental mechanism. Resilience varies with prior trauma, health, resources, social support, and the actual stakes of a challenge, so “manageable” cannot be inferred from an observer's judgment or generalized from one person to another.

## What Changed
- Created the concept with control, recovery, and calibration as necessary qualifications on stress exposure.

## Related Concepts
- [[Eustress]] - manageable rewarded stress is the proposed training input.
- [[ProblemValuationNetwork]] - calibrated threat and reward appraisal supports resilient action.
- [[ChronicStress]] - prolonged uncontrollable stress can erode rather than build resilience.
- [[FreeLearning]] - self-directed exploration supplies control over exposure and retreat.
- [[CoerciveLearning]] - externally imposed pressure can remove the conditions required for resilience-building.
