## Definition
[[OrganizationalScaleTradeoffs]] are the coupled benefits and costs that appear as a company replaces startup generalism and autonomy with specialist capability, resource abundance, strategic constraints, and cross-team coordination.

## Current Synthesis
[[SaumilMehta]]'s Square account rejects a simple competence hierarchy. Scale can fund expert finance support, better workplace infrastructure, and senior leaders who remain directly accessible to individual contributors. The same scale makes product work depend on company strategy, existing customers, scarce hiring, narrower engineering freedom, and a larger set of peer dependencies. Specialization therefore raises local capability while increasing coordination and making marginally useful projects easier to commission; small amounts of unnecessary work can compound across a large system without any individual being lazy or malicious.

## Key Claims
- Scale can improve access to specialist expertise, employee infrastructure, and executive attention without guaranteeing bureaucracy or leadership isolation.
- More people do not imply startup execution speed because strategy, installed-customer obligations, constrained autonomy, hiring, and cross-team dependencies limit the work a large organization can redirect.
- Specialization is both a capability and a coordination cost: it deepens functional skill while reducing the rate at which people flex across roles.
- Resource abundance lowers the forcing function to distinguish essential from merely useful work, so small local choices can aggregate into material organizational waste.

## Evidence
- Capability and access: [[confessions-of-a-startup-mole-in-a-dumb-big-company]] describes specialist finance support, extensive employee amenities, and senior Square leaders interacting directly with contributors across functions and levels.
- Speed constraints: [[confessions-of-a-startup-mole-in-a-dumb-big-company]] attributes slower incumbent response to engineering scarcity, hiring difficulty, company strategy, current customers, reduced degrees of freedom, and product dependencies.
- Specialization mechanism: [[confessions-of-a-startup-mole-in-a-dumb-big-company]] contrasts startup utility players with specialists produced by hierarchical budgeting and career-specific hiring at larger companies.
- Compounded waste: [[confessions-of-a-startup-mole-in-a-dumb-big-company]] uses optional finance work to show how individually small, good-faith requests can accumulate across thousands of employees and millions of hours.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept currently rests on one operator's retrospective inside one competent, well-run technology company. The source supplies no comparative headcount, cycle-time, utilization, employee-experience, or financial data, and it explicitly omits politics, tools, and much of culture. Specialization may be necessary for safety, regulation, reliability, or technical depth, while some startups also accumulate dependencies and waste; the synthesis therefore identifies a mechanism and tradeoff, not a universal size threshold or causal law.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to connect specialization's capability gains with its coordination and resource-allocation costs.
