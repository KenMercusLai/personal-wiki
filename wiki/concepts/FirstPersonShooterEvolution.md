## Definition
[[FirstPersonShooterEvolution]] is the historical development of first-person shooter games as a genre, including changes in viewpoint technology, aiming and movement feel, level design, multiplayer, control schemes, narrative presentation, and persistent online progression.

## Current Synthesis
The GamesRadar+ source treats FPS history as cumulative refinement rather than a sequence of isolated hits. Early experiments established first-person spatial presence; arcade and home platforms commercialized the idea; [[IdSoftware]] made the PC FPS fast, moddable, networked, and culturally durable; console studios such as [[Rare]] and [[Bungie]] adapted the genre to slower pacing, local multiplayer, and then twin-stick controls; later PC studios such as [[Valve]] and [[EpicGames]] expanded the mature genre through cinematic campaigns, tactical mods, and arena competition. By the 2010s, the article sees the FPS moving from discrete campaigns and deathmatch arenas toward blockbuster release calendars and live-service, co-op, MMO-like progression.

## Key Claims
- The FPS genre is anchored by the feel of aiming, moving, shooting, feedback, and immediate tactical adjustment.
- Early first-person games mattered because they introduced perspective, avatar encounters, maps, and spatial combat before the genre had a commercial market.
- PC FPS breakthroughs depended on technical rendering, high-speed input, modding, LAN/dial-up multiplayer, and community-discovered skill depth.
- Console FPS legitimacy required design changes around controller limits, local social play, pacing, stealth, and later twin-stick control.
- The mature FPS split into multiple successful forms: cinematic single-player, arena deathmatch, tactical team play, exploratory adventure, military blockbuster, and persistent co-op service.
- Later FPS success increasingly depended on multiplayer retention systems, progression, community, and recurring online play rather than only campaign novelty.

## Evidence
- Core loop: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] opens by saying successful FPS design is about making aiming, moving, shooting, precision, feedback, and immediate strategy feel good.
- Early form: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] identifies Maze War and Spasim as influential non-commercial first-person experiments, with Maze War showing maze navigation, avatars, shooting, and a level map.
- PC breakthrough: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] says Wolfenstein 3D popularized smooth fast play, while Doom expanded level design, weapons, modding, co-op, and deathmatch.
- Community skill depth: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] presents Quake's deathmatch modes, clans, modding, rocket-jumping, strafe-jumping, and QuakeCon culture as a foundation for early eSports.
- Console adaptation: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] credits GoldenEye 007 with console-optimized level design, local multiplayer, slower pacing, and stealth, then credits Halo with translating FPS control to twin-stick controllers.
- Genre diversification: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] contrasts Half-Life's cinematic single-player design with Unreal Tournament, Quake 3 Arena, and Counter-Strike's online focus.
- Live-service shift: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] frames Destiny as an always-online console FPS built around community co-op and persistent MMO-style progress.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a broad magazine history, not a comprehensive scholarly timeline. It centers prominent Western commercial milestones and gives limited attention to non-Western FPS history beyond Taito's Operation Wolf, to immersive sims outside the cited examples, and to post-2017 developments. Its Destiny 2 discussion is prospective from the source's publication context rather than a retrospective judgment.
