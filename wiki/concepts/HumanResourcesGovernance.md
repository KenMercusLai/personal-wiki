## Definition
[[HumanResourcesGovernance]] is the design of HR leadership, reporting lines, responsibilities, rules, feedback, and onboarding so that people operations remain effective as an organization grows.

## Current Synthesis
[[FredWilson]] treats HR as leadership and culture infrastructure rather than mainly a hiring and departure pipeline. His model hires an HR leader early, revisits the leader's required experience at each major scale transition, and gives the role direct access to the CEO. Written behavior rules, consequences, performance and development feedback for every level including the CEO, and multi-week onboarding then turn that reporting relationship into repeated organizational practice.

An engineering-informed HR model places the function beside the CEO in designing organizational rules and treats employees as users of the company as a product. This broadens governance from structure and programs into an evidence loop: hiring labels and imported practices should become explicit hypotheses, small interventions should be observed, and qualitative experience should be read alongside metrics. Yet people cannot be treated as programmable components; change is slow, trust is difficult to repair, and a rule-maker's broken promises can undermine the system more deeply than a formal process can reveal.

Together, the sources connect hierarchy with credibility: where HR sits signals whether leadership considers people and culture central, while whether HR listens, tests assumptions, keeps commitments, and provides recovery determines whether that status becomes trusted practice. They also connect scale with redesign rather than simple replication, because processes that work around 500 employees may fail around 5,000. The durable contribution is therefore a qualified governance system, not evidence that one reporting structure, professional background, or program guarantees a healthy workplace.

## Key Claims
- HR leadership should be established early and reassessed as organizational scale and complexity change.
- Direct CEO access makes HR a senior governance function and reduces filtering of cultural and leadership information.
- Recruiting and offboarding are necessary HR activities but are too narrow to define the function.
- Behavior rules, consequences, feedback, and development systems make cultural expectations operational.
- Onboarding should teach values, rules, systems, strategy, priorities, leadership, and the wider organization over weeks rather than stop at equipment and introductions.
- HR decisions should make assumptions testable, combine metrics with employee experience, bound experimental harm, and preserve consistency between stated rules and leadership conduct.

## Evidence
- Leadership timing and fit: [[getting-human-resources-right-avc]] recommends hiring an HR leader early and increasing the role's experience level as the company grows.
- Reporting and status: [[getting-human-resources-right-avc]] argues that the HR leader should report directly to the CEO because hierarchy communicates priorities and preserves access to cultural information.
- Functional scope: [[getting-human-resources-right-avc]] rejects an HR organization operating primarily as a recruiting and departure pipeline.
- Culture and feedback: [[getting-human-resources-right-avc]] assigns HR responsibility for culture, leadership, and dignified performance and development feedback, including feedback for the CEO.
- Rules and onboarding: [[getting-human-resources-right-avc]] recommends an early handbook and a multi-week onboarding process covering values, systems, processes, rules, operating plans, priorities, leadership, and the whole organization.
- Rule-design role: [[cheng-xu-yuan-zhuan-xing-hr-wo-fa-xian-de-yi-wai-you-shi]] describes the lead HR role as a partner to the CEO in drafting organizational rules rather than a narrow functional specialist.
- Evidence and employee experience: [[cheng-xu-yuan-zhuan-xing-hr-wo-fa-xian-de-yi-wai-you-shi]] argues for testing recruiting assumptions, treating employees as users, and pairing data with careful listening and nonverbal observation.
- Trust and recovery boundary: [[cheng-xu-yuan-zhuan-xing-hr-wo-fa-xian-de-yi-wai-you-shi]] warns that human change is slow, personnel effects are not readily reversible, and broken promises or inconsistent rule enforcement damage trust.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is grounded in two practitioner essays: a 2017 investor account and a 2026 first-person engineering-to-HR reflection. They supply no comparative organizational data, employee perspectives, implementation results, or evidence that technical-background HR leaders outperform conventionally trained peers. Direct CEO reporting can increase access and status but does not by itself create independence, prevent retaliation, resolve conflicts involving the CEO, or supply board-level oversight. Experiments with hiring, development, or policy affect people under unequal power and cannot be justified only by an MVP label. A handbook, feedback process, measurement loop, and onboarding program can formalize healthy standards or reproduce unhealthy ones; their substance, consent, enforcement, accessibility, procedural fairness, recovery paths, and employee trust remain decisive.

## What Changed
- Expanded HR governance from reporting lines and programs into rule design, testable assumptions, employee experience, and evidence loops.
- Added trust, slow human change, irreversibility, bounded harm, and consistency between promises and conduct as constraints on HR experimentation.
