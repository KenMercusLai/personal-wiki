## Definition
[[HumanResourcesGovernance]] is the design of HR leadership, reporting lines, responsibilities, rules, feedback, and onboarding so that people operations remain effective as an organization grows.

## Current Synthesis
[[FredWilson]] treats HR as leadership and culture infrastructure rather than mainly a hiring and departure pipeline. His model hires an HR leader early, revisits the leader's required experience at each major scale transition, and gives the role direct access to the CEO. Written behavior rules, consequences, performance and development feedback for every level including the CEO, and multi-week onboarding then turn that reporting relationship into repeated organizational practice.

The model connects hierarchy with credibility: where HR sits signals whether leadership considers people and culture central. It also connects scale with redesign rather than simple replication, because processes that work around 500 employees may fail around 5,000. The durable contribution is therefore a governance checklist, not evidence that one reporting structure or program guarantees a healthy workplace.

## Key Claims
- HR leadership should be established early and reassessed as organizational scale and complexity change.
- Direct CEO access makes HR a senior governance function and reduces filtering of cultural and leadership information.
- Recruiting and offboarding are necessary HR activities but are too narrow to define the function.
- Behavior rules, consequences, feedback, and development systems make cultural expectations operational.
- Onboarding should teach values, rules, systems, strategy, priorities, leadership, and the wider organization over weeks rather than stop at equipment and introductions.

## Evidence
- Leadership timing and fit: [[getting-human-resources-right-avc]] recommends hiring an HR leader early and increasing the role's experience level as the company grows.
- Reporting and status: [[getting-human-resources-right-avc]] argues that the HR leader should report directly to the CEO because hierarchy communicates priorities and preserves access to cultural information.
- Functional scope: [[getting-human-resources-right-avc]] rejects an HR organization operating primarily as a recruiting and departure pipeline.
- Culture and feedback: [[getting-human-resources-right-avc]] assigns HR responsibility for culture, leadership, and dignified performance and development feedback, including feedback for the CEO.
- Rules and onboarding: [[getting-human-resources-right-avc]] recommends an early handbook and a multi-week onboarding process covering values, systems, processes, rules, operating plans, priorities, leadership, and the whole organization.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is grounded in one short 2017 investor-authored practitioner essay. It supplies no comparative organizational data, employee perspectives, implementation detail, or measured outcomes. Direct CEO reporting can increase access and status but does not by itself create independence, prevent retaliation, resolve conflicts involving the CEO, or supply board-level oversight. A handbook, feedback process, and onboarding program can formalize healthy standards or reproduce unhealthy ones; their substance, enforcement, accessibility, procedural fairness, and employee trust remain decisive.

## What Changed
- Established HR governance as a scaling system rather than a recruiting-only function.
- Connected HR reporting lines with both information flow and visible organizational priority.
- Added handbook, feedback, and multi-week onboarding as mechanisms for operationalizing culture.
