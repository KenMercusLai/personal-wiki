## Definition
[[AlgorithmicFeastAndFamine]] is the volatile visibility or acquisition pattern created when a publisher, creator, or business depends on ranked social feeds and viral content rather than predictable, diversified, directly controlled channels.

## Current Synthesis
Chen argues that creator-economy startups inherit the instability of social distribution. A viral TikTok or similar feed event can create a sudden acquisition spike, but startups usually need steady compounding growth. Compared with marketplace companies that build both supply and demand through diversified channels, creator-economy startups often lean heavily on creators' social reach. This makes the business exposed to feed volatility, platform rule changes, vertical integration by the underlying platforms, and content ephemerality. More durable growth layers social spikes on top of retention, referrals, SEO, mobile installs, paid acquisition, partnerships, and other channels.

The Chicago Tribune case shows the famine side at post level and demonstrates why averages can conceal it. From December 2016 to March 2017, posts reaching no more than 10,000 people increased from 8 to 242 even while the page gained fans; daily average organic reach remained volatile but superficially familiar because a few stronger posts could offset a rapidly worsening lower tail. The useful diagnostic is therefore not only whether total traffic rose or fell, but how reach is distributed across posts, how the median moves, and whether severe misses are becoming more frequent.

## Key Claims
- Viral creator traffic can create large spikes that do not translate into predictable month-over-month growth.
- Social-media dependency is fragile because feed ranking and content attention can change without a corresponding change in follower count or publishing strategy.
- A stable aggregate average can conceal a worsening median and a rapidly expanding lower tail of poorly distributed posts.
- Subscription and upgrade revenue can stabilize creator businesses by stacking retained revenue over time.
- Underlying platforms can vertically integrate creator monetization and hoard creator relationships.
- Diversified channels make creator-startup growth more durable.

## Evidence
- Viral spike pattern: [[andrew-chen-creator-economy-2-0-what-weve-learned-why-its-hard-and-whats-next]] says a viral TikTok can create a big acquisition spike that then disappears.
- Channel fragility: [[andrew-chen-creator-economy-2-0-what-weve-learned-why-its-hard-and-whats-next]] compares social dependency to prior SEO-dependent content sites hurt by algorithm changes.
- Marketplace contrast: [[andrew-chen-creator-economy-2-0-what-weve-learned-why-its-hard-and-whats-next]] contrasts creator startups with Uber's diversified rider and driver acquisition across paid, SEO, brand, referrals, partnerships, and other channels.
- Subscription stability: [[andrew-chen-creator-economy-2-0-what-weve-learned-why-its-hard-and-whats-next]] says subscription with upgrades has become dominant because it creates more durable revenue from followers.
- Platform integration risk: [[andrew-chen-creator-economy-2-0-what-weve-learned-why-its-hard-and-whats-next]] notes YouTube, Twitch, Twitter, and other platforms paying creators directly.
- Diversification answer: [[andrew-chen-creator-economy-2-0-what-weve-learned-why-its-hard-and-whats-next]] recommends combining social spikes with retention, referrals, SEO, mobile installs, and other channels.
- Lower-tail collapse: [[facebooks-algorithm-isnt-surfacing-one-third-of-our-posts-and-its-getting-worse]] reports that Chicago Tribune posts reaching at most 10,000 people rose from 8 in December 2016 to 242 in March 2017.
- Average-versus-distribution gap: [[facebooks-algorithm-isnt-surfacing-one-third-of-our-posts-and-its-getting-worse]] says daily average reach looked broadly familiar while median reach fell to a record low.
- Follower-count disconnect: [[facebooks-algorithm-isnt-surfacing-one-third-of-our-posts-and-its-getting-worse]] reports diminished organic reach despite more than 130,000 additional page fans.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Chen does not show cohort curves, channel-mix data, or platform-by-platform volatility measures. The Tribune analysis supplies 15 months of first-party observations but covers one publisher, lacks post-level controls, and cannot identify whether frequency, content type, news cycles, competition, Instant Articles, or Facebook ranking caused the change. Some organizations may turn social spikes into durable owned audiences through email, communities, subscriptions, or strong repeat behavior. The concept is a warning about dependency and misleading aggregates, not an argument that algorithmic discovery has no value.

- Extended it from creator-acquisition spikes to publisher reach famine and added median and distribution buckets as diagnostics.
