## Definition
[[AlgorithmicFeastAndFamine]] is the spiky acquisition pattern created when a business depends on social-feed algorithms and viral creator content rather than predictable, diversified growth channels.

## Current Synthesis
Chen argues that creator-economy startups inherit the instability of social distribution. A viral TikTok or similar feed event can create a sudden acquisition spike, but startups usually need steady compounding growth. Compared with marketplace companies that build both supply and demand through diversified channels, creator-economy startups often lean heavily on creators' social reach. This makes the business exposed to feed volatility, platform rule changes, vertical integration by the underlying platforms, and content ephemerality. More durable growth layers social spikes on top of retention, referrals, SEO, mobile installs, paid acquisition, partnerships, and other channels.

## Key Claims
- Viral creator traffic can create large spikes that do not translate into predictable month-over-month growth.
- Dependency on a single acquisition channel is fragile.
- Social-media dependency is especially fragile because feed algorithms and content attention are ephemeral.
- Subscription and upgrade revenue can stabilize creator businesses by stacking retained revenue over time.
- Underlying platforms can vertically integrate creator monetization and hoard creator relationships.
- Diversified channels make creator-startup growth more durable.

## Evidence
- Viral spike pattern: [[andrew-chen-creator-economy-2-0-what-weve-learned-why-its-hard-and-whats-next]] says a viral TikTok can create a big acquisition spike that then disappears.
- Channel fragility: [[andrew-chen-creator-economy-2-0-what-weve-learned-why-its-hard-and-whats-next]] compares social dependency to prior SEO-dependent content sites hurt by algorithm changes.
- Marketplace contrast: [[andrew-chen-creator-economy-2-0-what-weve-learned-why-its-hard-and-whats-next]] contrasts creator startups with Uber's diversified rider and driver acquisition across paid, SEO, brand, referrals, partnerships, and other channels.
- Subscription stability: [[andrew-chen-creator-economy-2-0-what-weve-learned-why-its-hard-and-whats-next]] says subscription with upgrades has become dominant because it creates more durable revenue from followers.
- Platform integration risk: [[andrew-chen-creator-economy-2-0-what-weve-learned-why-its-hard-and-whats-next]] notes YouTube, Twitch, Twitter, and other platforms paying creators directly.
- Diversification answer: [[andrew-chen-creator-economy-2-0-what-weve-learned-why-its-hard-and-whats-next]] recommends combining social spikes with retention, referrals, SEO, mobile installs, and other channels.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The article does not show cohort curves, channel-mix data, or platform-by-platform volatility measures. Some creator categories may turn social spikes into durable owned audiences through email, communities, subscriptions, or strong repeat behavior. The concept is a warning about dependency, not an argument that algorithmic discovery has no value.
