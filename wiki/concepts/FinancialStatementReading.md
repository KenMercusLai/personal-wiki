## Definition
[[FinancialStatementReading]] is the lightweight practice of using a small set of financial metrics to judge whether a company is growing, operating efficiently, and converting revenue and assets into profit.

## Current Synthesis
The article argues that investors do not need to master every accounting metric before making sense of a business. Its simplified checklist asks whether guidance and actual results beat or miss analyst expectations, whether revenue and net income are growing, whether inventory, assets, and receivables turn over efficiently, and whether gross margin, net margin, ROA, and ROE show real profitability. The restaurant analogy grounds those abstractions: revenue, direct costs, gross profit, expenses, net profit, assets, liabilities, and equity become concrete operating levers such as supplier cost, pricing, staffing, rent, dividends, and expansion.

## Key Claims
- Investors can start with a small set of financial-statement questions instead of over-optimizing complex metrics.
- Earnings guidance versus expectations and actual results versus expectations help classify good or bad news.
- Revenue growth and net-profit growth are basic tests of business momentum.
- Turnover metrics indicate operating efficiency and management quality.
- Margins, ROA, and ROE connect profitability to revenue, assets, and shareholder equity.

## Evidence
- Expectations: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] lists guidance versus analyst expectations and actual performance versus analyst expectations as key earnings signals.
- Growth: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] names revenue growth and net-profit growth as central signals.
- Efficiency: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] defines inventory turnover, total asset turnover, and receivables turnover as operating-quality checks.
- Profitability: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] defines gross margin, net margin, total asset return, and net asset return.
- Concrete analogy: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] uses a restaurant with 500,000 revenue, 200,000 direct cost, 120,000 net profit, 600,000 assets, and 400,000 equity to illustrate margin, ROA, and ROE.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source intentionally simplifies financial analysis. It does not cover cash-flow statements, debt maturity, cyclicality, accounting quality, valuation multiples beyond a market-cap screenshot, or sector-specific metrics.
