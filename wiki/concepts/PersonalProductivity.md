## Definition
[[PersonalProductivity]] is the practice of arranging attention, priorities, routines, communication, and task selection so meaningful work gets completed with less waste and avoidable friction.

## Current Synthesis
The wiki's first source treats productivity as a collection of simple, durable habits rather than a comprehensive life-management system. The central pattern is to decide what matters, protect attention while doing it, reduce noisy inputs and bloated outputs, and remove or delegate work that does not require personal effort.

## Key Claims
- Productivity starts with naming the day's most important three to five tasks.
- Single-tasking and reduced distractions matter more than trying to imitate machine-like multitasking.
- Simple routines can support productivity without requiring a complex system.
- Task triage improves output by deleting, delegating, batching, or doing difficult avoided work early.

## Evidence
- Daily priority selection: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] recommends defining three to five most important tasks before the day is consumed by less important work.
- Attention protection: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] favors single-tasking, offline work when needed, and simplifying information sources.
- Lightweight routines: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] argues that efficient work can come from practical habits rather than an elaborate productivity framework.
- Task triage: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] combines batching, deletion, delegation, and doing disliked tasks first as ways to reduce drag.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is an advice article, not a comparative empirical study. It gives broadly applicable heuristics but does not test which habits matter most, how they vary by job type, or when interruptions and collaboration are necessary rather than wasteful.
