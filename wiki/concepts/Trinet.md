## Definition
[[Trinet]] is [[AndreStaltz]]'s speculative name for a future internet-like infrastructure optimized mainly around three dominant platform networks: Google, Facebook, and Amazon.

## Current Synthesis
The Trinet is not presented as an existing protocol or product. It is Staltz's extrapolation from web centralization, net-neutrality risk, mobile app dominance, proprietary cloud strategy, and browser bypass. In the scenario, ISPs and infrastructure providers increasingly optimize for the traffic most users demand, making independent websites less economically attractive and potentially relegating the older open internet to an emulated or premium-access layer.

## Key Claims
- The internet can survive technically while losing its "network of networks" character.
- Platform popularity could give ISPs incentives to sell cheaper access to dominant networks than to the full open internet.
- Independent websites and small e-commerce businesses would be pressured toward platform pages, acquisition, or bankruptcy if broad access became less common.
- The main loss would be freedom: anonymity, equal server reachability, startup independence, and recourse after platform bans.

## Evidence
- Scenario naming: [[andre-staltz-the-web-began-dying-in-2014-heres-how]] describes a possible "network of three networks" and calls it the Trinet.
- ISP pathway: [[andre-staltz-the-web-began-dying-in-2014-heres-how]] argues that weak net neutrality could let access providers privilege Google, Facebook, and Amazon traffic.
- Infrastructure pathway: [[andre-staltz-the-web-began-dying-in-2014-heres-how]] says backbone and routing infrastructure could be optimized for dominant traffic if the old internet became overhead.
- Freedom tradeoff: [[andre-staltz-the-web-began-dying-in-2014-heres-how]] warns that account bans or access restrictions would matter more when alternatives are weak.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is a speculative forecast from a 2017 essay, not an observed final state. It should be treated as a warning model about centralization incentives rather than as evidence that the internet actually became a literal three-network infrastructure.
