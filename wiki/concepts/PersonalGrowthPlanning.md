## Definition
[[PersonalGrowthPlanning]] is a recurring practice for connecting self-knowledge, desired development, measurable goals, energy, and attention-protecting habits across annual, quarterly, weekly, and daily review cycles.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents personal growth as a layered operating rhythm. Motivations identify meaningful directions, values act as longer-lived decision criteria, and strengths and weaknesses establish the current baseline. Quarterly objectives with measurable key results then turn that reflection into a small set of actions, while weekly review keeps them visible and quarter-end grading creates feedback.

The framework also resists treating every weakness as the best development target. Its Strengths Profile interpretation separates competence from energy: a learned behavior may be performed well but drain the person, while an unrealized strength may combine ability with energy. Meditation, notification limits, delayed morning email, and manager discussion are supporting conditions rather than growth goals themselves; they protect focus and improve alignment around the plan.

## Key Claims
- Effective growth planning begins with motivations and values before selecting development goals.
- Strengths and weaknesses provide a baseline, but energy changes which capabilities are sustainable to emphasize.
- A small set of quarterly objectives and measurable key results can turn personal development into observable action and feedback.
- Annual, quarterly, weekly, and daily cadences serve different planning functions and should not be collapsed into one review.
- Managers can support growth more effectively when they understand the individual's motives, values, ambitions, and chosen direction.
- Focus practices and reduced reactive communication can create the attention needed to execute a growth plan.

## Evidence
- Direction and baseline: [[design-your-personal-growth-melissa-mandelbaum-medium]] recommends documenting motivations, values, strengths, and weaknesses before choosing quarterly goals.
- Execution cadence: [[design-your-personal-growth-melissa-mandelbaum-medium]] proposes about three quarterly objectives with one or two measurable results each, weekly visibility beside the to-do list, and quarter-end grading.
- Energy-aware development: [[design-your-personal-growth-melissa-mandelbaum-medium]] distinguishes energizing realized and unrealized strengths from draining learned behaviors and realized weaknesses.
- Attention support: [[design-your-personal-growth-melissa-mandelbaum-medium]] describes daily guided meditation, disabled phone notifications, nighttime silent mode, and delayed morning email as focus practices.
- Social alignment: [[design-your-personal-growth-melissa-mandelbaum-medium]] recommends sharing motivations, values, and the completed plan with a manager.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework is one practitioner's 2017 synthesis of personal experience, employer practices, coaching, and a commercial strengths assessment. It does not establish that its categories are psychometrically valid or that quarterly OKRs, meditation, notification limits, or manager disclosure improve development outcomes across roles and people. Sharing personal motives or values depends on managerial trust and psychological safety. Some jobs require immediate responsiveness, and growth may also require practicing necessary but draining skills rather than only emphasizing energizing strengths. Fixed annual or quarterly cadences should therefore be adapted when circumstances change faster or capacity is constrained.
