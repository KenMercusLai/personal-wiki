## Definition
[[DIKWModel]] is a hierarchy that distinguishes data, information, knowledge, and wisdom by how much meaning, durability, structure, and explanatory power they contain.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses DIKW to explain why systematic learning cannot stop at collecting facts or short-lived information. A single temperature reading is data; a processed current-temperature report is information; a conclusion about whether a city is livable across years is knowledge. Wisdom is the highest level because it answers WHY questions and can sometimes transfer across fields.

This model sharpens the wiki's existing active-learning and knowledge-output material. Output is not only proof that facts were remembered; it is a way to test whether scattered information has become structured knowledge and whether the learner can explain causes, mechanisms, and tradeoffs.

## Key Claims
- Data by itself has little meaning until processed into information.
- Information is meaningful but often short-lived and fragmented.
- Knowledge is more durable because it can form a coherent domain system.
- Wisdom answers WHY questions and signals deeper understanding.
- Some wisdom can transfer across domains, making it more valuable than domain-bound knowledge.
- WHY questions are useful in interviews because they reveal understanding more than memorized answers.

## Evidence
- Data to information: [[bian-cheng-sui-xiang-ru-he-xi-tong-xing-xue-xi]] contrasts a single temperature value with a processed current-temperature report.
- Information to knowledge: [[bian-cheng-sui-xiang-ru-he-xi-tong-xing-xue-xi]] contrasts short-lived weather information with multi-year conclusions about livability.
- Knowledge to wisdom: [[bian-cheng-sui-xiang-ru-he-xi-tong-xing-xue-xi]] says wisdom corresponds to WHY questions and real understanding.
- Transfer value: [[bian-cheng-sui-xiang-ru-he-xi-tong-xing-xue-xi]] values cross-domain wisdom more highly when it can travel beyond one field.
- Interview use: [[bian-cheng-sui-xiang-ru-he-xi-tong-xing-xue-xi]] recommends WHY questions because they are hard to fake and lack simple standard answers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source uses DIKW as a practical explanatory model, not as a formal epistemology. Its boundaries between information, knowledge, and wisdom can be context-dependent: some information becomes knowledge only when organized for a particular purpose, and WHY questions can still be answered poorly if the interviewer lacks domain judgment.
