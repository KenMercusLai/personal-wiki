## Definition
[[LectureNoteTaking]] is the recording, organization, and later development of information presented during a lecture for encoding, review, retrieval, and assessment preparation.

## Current Synthesis
The source provides naturalistic evidence that note completeness can matter in a lecture-dependent university course. Across 38 introductory-psychology students, the total words recorded over ten sampled lectures was more strongly associated with final examination performance than attendance alone, and the association became especially large among diligent attendees. The pattern suggests that being present and capturing the material are distinct: attendance creates access, while note quantity partly represents how much of that access becomes available for encoding and later review.

The result is conditional rather than a universal instruction to transcribe everything. Associations were stronger for a 210-item multiple-choice paper than for a four-question essay paper, consistent with the broader test making omissions more costly. Layout, emphasis, and abbreviation showed little consistent relationship, but the study measured counts rather than the semantic quality of organization. More notes may preserve more examinable information, reflect attention or comprehension, or simply mark motivation and prior ability. The evidence therefore supports completeness as a potentially useful strategy when lectures carry unique, broadly tested content, while leaving causal effects and the best balance between capture and thought unresolved.

## Key Claims
- Lecture attendance and the amount recorded are related but distinct predictors of assessment performance.
- In this course, total note quantity had a strong positive association with exam scores, including among students with similarly high attendance.
- Assessment design moderates the apparent value of note completeness because broad recall tests penalize omitted lecture content more than selective essay exams may.
- Adding material after class may contribute to review and elaboration, but the source provides only a small correlational signal from a few students who did much of it.
- Simple counts of abbreviation, whitespace, indentation, underlining, and highlighting did not show reliable performance relationships.
- Note quantity is not established as a cause; it can also reflect motivation, aptitude, comprehension, speed, or other unmeasured differences.

## Evidence
Quantity and performance:
- [[nye-et-al-student-note-taking-related-to-university-examination-performance]] reports correlations of 0.58, 0.70, and 0.72 between total words and essay, multiple-choice, and combined scores respectively in the 38-student sample.
- [[nye-et-al-student-note-taking-related-to-university-examination-performance]] reports that the top eight students averaged 3,697 total words across target lectures versus 1,551 for the bottom eight.

Attendance boundary:
- [[nye-et-al-student-note-taking-related-to-university-examination-performance]] finds that total-word correlations rose to 0.77 for students attending at least 70% of lectures and 0.87 for those attending at least 80%, despite the narrower attendance range.

Assessment dependence:
- [[nye-et-al-student-note-taking-related-to-university-examination-performance]] attributes stronger multiple-choice relationships to broader sampling of lecture content, greater recall orientation, and likely higher reliability than the four-question essay paper.

Note features:
- [[nye-et-al-student-note-taking-related-to-university-examination-performance]] finds modest positive correlations for material added after class but little consistent association for abbreviation, layout, or emphasis counts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence comes from 38 students who supplied notes after one 1981 introductory psychology course, not from random assignment to note-taking strategies. The reachable local sampling frame, volunteer handover of notes, ten sampled lectures, retrospective attendance inference, and weighted word measures can all introduce selection or measurement error. Motivation, prior achievement, comprehension, handwriting speed, lecture engagement, study time, and note-review behavior remain plausible confounders. The course reportedly had limited lecture-textbook overlap and examinations that rewarded basic information, so other disciplines and assessments may value selection, problem solving, diagrams, or synthesis differently. Very high correlations in groups of 12 or eight are unstable and do not establish a general law. Counts of formatting marks also do not measure whether the notes organized ideas well. The study challenges categorical brevity advice, but it does not prove that verbatim transcription is optimal or that quantity should displace listening and understanding.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from a naturalistic course study linking note quantity, attendance, and examination performance.
- Established assessment coverage and lecture dependence as important moderators of note-taking value.
- Preserved the distinction between a strong association and a causal recommendation to write more.
