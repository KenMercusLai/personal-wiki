Fundraising investor fit means the founder is also qualifying the investor. In Hunter Walk's anecdote, a seed-stage founder met an investor who opened by saying he did not believe her nascent market would exist and asked her to convince him. She ended the meeting rather than treating the exchange as a thesis defense.

The useful distinction is between informed skepticism and low-value resistance. A respected operator, domain expert, or likely partner can stress-test a founder's assumptions and improve the story. An investor with no clear basis for dismissal, or one who turns the meeting into a power test, may be revealing that they are unlikely to invest and may be undesirable on the cap table even if persuaded.

This fit screen protects more than calendar time. Fundraising creates emotional load, and founders can waste attention trying to convert people whose starting posture makes the relationship structurally weak. Saying no to that work is a judgment call about where scarce founder energy should go.

## Signals

- The investor's objection is grounded in market knowledge, customer evidence, or portfolio context.
- The investor is testing the founder's thinking rather than asserting dominance in the meeting.
- A successful outcome would put someone on the cap table the founder would want involved later.
- The conversation can produce useful learning even if the investor ultimately passes.
- The founder is continuing only because rejection feels personally hard to accept.
