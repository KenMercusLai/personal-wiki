## Definition
[[ReverseLearning]] is Wozniak's term here for learning that attaches negative value to a context, cue, or activity, causing future valuation and memory consolidation to be inhibited rather than strengthened.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses reverse learning to explain how bad schooling can damage learning beyond a single disliked lesson. If low-value or coerced study repeatedly occurs in a recognizable context, the learner may associate the context itself with negative value. Later, even neutral cues such as a card color, template, classroom routine, or assigned activity can become inhibitory signals.

In Wozniak's hypothesis, this can still allow short-term recall or local associations, but low valuation prevents durable consolidation and may contribute to learned helplessness when coercion persists.

## Key Claims
- Negative context associations can suppress valuation even when factual associations still form.
- Low valuation can block memory consolidation and transfer to long-term cortical storage.
- Reverse learning may occur when school or software contexts become linked with boredom, punishment, or low-value tasks.
- Repeated coercion can produce competition among neural networks and learned helplessness.
- Changing cues may sometimes recode reward, but persistent coercion can exhaust plasticity.

## Evidence
- Context association: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] gives the example of SuperMemo card colors becoming associated with low valuation in bad school conditions.
- Consolidation claim: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] says low valuation can prevent memory consolidation even when short-term retrieval remains possible.
- Learned helplessness hypothesis: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] links network competition under denied valuation signals to school-induced learned helplessness.
- Plasticity warning: [[zhi-shi-gu-zhi-wang-luo]] compares long-term forced learning to wearing out a memory system's ability to keep responding.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source presents reverse learning as Wozniak's hypothesis about valuation, memory, and schooling. It references OFC damage and reversal-learning findings but does not provide a full empirical review, so the wiki treats the stronger education-damage claims as theoretical and source-scoped.
