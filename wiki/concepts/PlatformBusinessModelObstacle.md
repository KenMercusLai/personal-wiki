## Definition
[[PlatformBusinessModelObstacle]] is a platform failure mode where the technology may improve, but the revenue model, licensing structure, or partner incentive system no longer fits how the market competes.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses [[Microsoft]] and [[WindowsPhone]] as the core example. Microsoft could modernize its mobile operating system, but [[Android]] made a competent smartphone OS free for handset makers, undermining the classic Windows-style licensing business. In this reading, Microsoft's mobile failure was neither pure technical incompetence nor simple human blindness; it was a business-model mismatch against an ecosystem that monetized reach and services differently.

## Key Claims
- Platform quality is not sufficient when the surrounding ecosystem competes on a different revenue model.
- Free licensing can remove the economic space for a paid operating-system licensing business.
- Business-model obstacles can coexist with technical improvement and serious management effort.

## Evidence
- Android contrast: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] says Android aimed to prevent a Microsoft monopoly by creating a more competent and free smartphone OS.
- Licensing failure: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] argues this killed Microsoft's hope for a smartphone licensing business.
- Windows Phone outcome: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] says Microsoft improved the OS but could not get a large licensee and its handset business became effectively nonexistent.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept is source-scoped. Windows Phone also faced app ecosystem, timing, product, carrier, and consumer-demand problems that are not reduced to licensing economics. The page captures Gassee's business-model explanation, not a complete causal model of Microsoft's mobile strategy.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to capture Microsoft's mobile failure as a business-model mismatch.
