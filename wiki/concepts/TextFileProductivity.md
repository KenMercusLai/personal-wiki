## Definition
[[TextFileProductivity]] is a lightweight productivity workflow that uses a plain-text chronological file, usually with a calendar, to turn planned work into daily execution notes and a durable searchable record.

## Current Synthesis
Jeff Huang's source frames the text file as both a task surface and a work archive. The calendar holds future obligations and possible future work; each night, the next day's calendar items are copied into the file as a concrete daily plan. During the day, that plan becomes meeting notes, research notes, completed-work evidence, idea capture, and follow-up reminders. The key design choice is not plain text alone, but the division of responsibility: the calendar prevents future tasks from becoming an anxious undated list, while the text file keeps today's work visible and turns it into searchable memory.

## Key Claims
- A calendar-plus-text-file pair can replace multiple task, note, meeting, and lab-notebook systems for an individual workflow.
- Nightly planning bounds the next day before it starts and makes oversized work easier to defer, split, or remove.
- Daily lists can become useful records when notes are written directly under the tasks and meetings that generated them.
- Plain-text search, consistent phrasing, and simple tags can provide enough retrieval without specialized app structure.
- Email can remain outside the file if lightweight flags identify immediate work, eventual work, and awaited replies.
- Workload control includes deleting future commitments and reducing assigned work, not only finishing more tasks.

## Evidence
- Calendar boundary: [[blog-jeff-huang-my-productivity-app-is-a-never-ending-txt-file]] says the calendar stores future plans, including unscheduled items placed on dates when the author wants to think about them.
- Nightly plan: [[blog-jeff-huang-my-productivity-app-is-a-never-ending-txt-file]] describes appending the next day's calendar items to the text file before bed so the morning starts with a known plan.
- Capacity sizing: [[blog-jeff-huang-my-productivity-app-is-a-never-ending-txt-file]] says unwanted or oversized tasks are moved later or split, and that repeated practice improved daily workload estimation.
- Work record: [[blog-jeff-huang-my-productivity-app-is-a-never-ending-txt-file]] reports using the daily list as notes, meeting minutes, research notebook, and a record of what happened.
- Retrieval: [[blog-jeff-huang-my-productivity-app-is-a-never-ending-txt-file]] uses consistent wording, tags such as `#idea` and `#annual`, and editor search to retrieve old meetings, ideas, and report material.
- Email flags: [[blog-jeff-huang-my-productivity-app-is-a-never-ending-txt-file]] describes red, orange, and yellow flags for immediate action, eventual action, and awaited replies.
- Workload reduction: [[blog-jeff-huang-my-productivity-app-is-a-never-ending-txt-file]] says the author manages overwhelm by unflagging emails, removing lower-excitement calendar items, and assigning less future work.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is an individual account from an academic work setting, not a comparative study of productivity systems. The system assumes comfort with text editing, personal autonomy over future commitments, and a workflow where many meetings, ideas, and notes can live in one private file. It may need modification for team-visible task tracking, compliance recordkeeping, shared project management, mobile-first work, or roles that require rapid inbox response.
