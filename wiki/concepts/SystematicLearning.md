## Definition
[[SystematicLearning]] is learning that builds both breadth and depth in a domain so the learner can truly understand and use what they have learned.

## Current Synthesis
The source frames systematic learning as a structured alternative to fragmented intake. A learner needs a field-wide map as well as enough depth to understand mechanisms, make judgments, and apply the knowledge. For software development, this means learning syntax, standard libraries, third-party libraries, and software engineering as one connected system rather than as isolated tips.

The article's method joins several layers: choose media that can carry systematic depth, select high-quality books across reading types, use the [[FeynmanTechnique]] to expose weak understanding, privilege foundational branches over short-term utility, and aim for the WHY-level explanation captured by [[DIKWModel]] and [[CrossDomainWisdom]].

## Key Claims
- Systematic learning requires breadth and depth together.
- The goal is usable understanding rather than information accumulation.
- Complex domains need a connected body of prerequisites, tools, principles, and applications.
- Media and source selection are part of the learning method because fragmented media can distort the learning surface.
- Foundational branches deserve disproportionate time because they support later and cross-domain understanding.
- Explanation-oriented self-testing reveals whether the learner has truly internalized the system.

## Evidence
- Breadth and depth: [[bian-cheng-sui-xiang-ru-he-xi-tong-xing-xue-xi]] defines systematic learning as requiring both dimensions.
- Software example: [[bian-cheng-sui-xiang-ru-he-xi-tong-xing-xue-xi]] uses programming to show how syntax, libraries, third-party ecosystems, and software engineering form one system.
- Medium fit: [[bian-cheng-sui-xiang-ru-he-xi-tong-xing-xue-xi]] compares media forms and argues that difficult subjects make medium choice more important.
- Foundational priority: [[bian-cheng-sui-xiang-ru-he-xi-tong-xing-xue-xi]] says more foundational branches should receive more attention because they support dependent branches.
- Explanation test: [[bian-cheng-sui-xiang-ru-he-xi-tong-xing-xue-xi]] uses the Feynman Technique and WHY questions as checks on real understanding.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source makes strong qualitative claims about books and fragmented media, but it does not offer empirical comparisons of learning outcomes across media. Its argument is strongest for complex domains requiring durable structure; quick lookup, troubleshooting, or narrow procedural tasks may still benefit from Q&A, short articles, or video.
