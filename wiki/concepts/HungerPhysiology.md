## Definition
[[HungerPhysiology]] is the interacting sensory, neuroendocrine, endocrine, and metabolic regulation of food-seeking and intake, shaped by psychological, behavioral, social, cultural, and economic conditions.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents hunger as a redundant control system rather than a single signal. Homeostatic hunger communicates energy need under scarcity, while hedonic hunger can motivate eating without an immediate caloric deficit. Body-weight stability over time suggests that regulation integrates basal energy use with variable food quantity and quality, macronutrient composition, the thermic effect of food, and physical activity.

This system is also historically situated. A physiology formed under unpredictable food supply could favor surplus intake and fat storage, yet widespread access to affordable, palatable, energy-dense food changes the consequences of that bias and may amplify hedonic eating. Gut microbes may participate in these circuits, but their exact causal role in food-intake control is unresolved. Mechanistic advances may yield treatment targets for obesity or anorexia nervosa, although this archived social-media summary does not establish clinical efficacy.

## Key Claims
- Hunger regulation depends on complex, overlapping pathways, making a single-cause account inadequate.
- Homeostatic hunger responds to energy need, whereas hedonic hunger can operate without an urgent caloric requirement.
- Appetite control integrates both predictable energy expenditure and variable intake, food composition, thermogenesis, and activity.
- Evolutionary pressure may have favored positive energy balance and fat storage under scarcity.
- Modern abundance of palatable, energy-dense food can turn that survival bias into a health liability.
- Microbiome effects and therapeutic applications are plausible but not yet resolved by the supplied evidence.

## Evidence
- Control architecture and weight stability: [[1888076190365401209]] describes redundant neuroendocrine circuits and regulation across basal metabolism, intake, food composition, thermic effect, and activity.
- Hunger modes and environmental change: [[1888076190365401209]] distinguishes homeostatic from hedonic hunger and links the latter's prominence to agriculture and abundant palatable food.
- Evolutionary interpretation: [[1888076190365401209]] frames excess intake and fat accumulation as historically adaptive energy storage with adverse modern consequences.
- Open mechanisms and applications: [[1888076190365401209]] identifies the gut microbiome as an uncertain influence and obesity and anorexia nervosa as possible therapeutic contexts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence inventory contains one short social-media summary of an unidentified NEJM review, not the review itself. It provides no authors, methods, citations, effect sizes, intervention results, or diagnostic guidance. The sharp historical claim that agriculture made hedonic hunger take priority over homeostatic hunger compresses a long, uneven transition and does not isolate agriculture from later industrial food production, income, culture, or individual variation. Relative weight stability does not imply perfect regulation, and the source explicitly leaves the gut microbiome's role unresolved. Proposed treatment targets should not be interpreted as proven treatments or individualized medical advice.

## What Changed
- Created a unified account of homeostatic and hedonic hunger within redundant appetite-control pathways.
- Added evolutionary and food-environment context for why energy-storage bias can become harmful.
- Preserved microbiome causality and therapeutic value as open questions rather than established findings.
