## Definition
[[SmartphonePlatformDisruption]] is the transition in which app-centered iOS and Android ecosystems displaced earlier handset-centered smartphone competitors by changing the required operating-system depth, developer ecosystem, distribution logic, and business model.

## Current Synthesis
The BlackBerry source frames the post-iPhone transition as more than a better-device race. [[IPhone]] and [[Android]] made smartphones into software and ecosystem platforms, which meant older incumbents had to modernize operating systems, attract developers, preserve customer and carrier confidence, and find a viable economic role. [[BlackBerry]], [[Palm]], [[Nokia]], and [[Microsoft]] each failed at a different layer of that transition, making the disruption useful as a diagnostic map rather than a single morality tale about complacency.

## Key Claims
- Platform disruption can combine technical architecture, developer ecosystems, distribution, and business model at once.
- A company can correctly see the threat and still fail if it waits too long to rebuild the platform.
- A modern replacement platform can still arrive too late if app and market momentum have already concentrated elsewhere.
- Transition communication can destroy the incumbent product line before the successor is ready.
- A technically improved platform can fail when the prevailing business model no longer matches ecosystem economics.

## Evidence
- Multi-cause frame: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] asks whether the failures came from human error, technological inadequacy, or business-model collapse and answers with different cases.
- Delayed technology response: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] says BlackBerry saw the iPhone software threat but waited three years before buying [[QNX]].
- Late replacement: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] says [[Palm]] produced [[WebOS]] quickly but too late to gain momentum.
- Communication failure: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] says [[Nokia]] triggered an [[OsborneEffect]] by announcing its platform jump before replacement devices were ready.
- Business-model mismatch: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] says [[Microsoft]] could not build a mobile OS licensing business against free [[Android]].

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is currently based on one retrospective essay from 2016. It emphasizes strategic causation and selected public facts; it does not settle every disputed explanation for BlackBerry, Palm, Nokia, or Windows Phone, and it does not include later smartphone-market, regulatory, or enterprise-mobile history.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish platform-transition failure modes across BlackBerry, Palm, Nokia, and Microsoft.
