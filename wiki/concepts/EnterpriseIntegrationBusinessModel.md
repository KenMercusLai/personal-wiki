## Definition
[[EnterpriseIntegrationBusinessModel]] is a strategy for capturing value by combining fragmented technologies into an accountable business outcome and assuming complexity that enterprise customers do not want to coordinate themselves.

## Current Synthesis
The IBM case says integration becomes valuable when enabling technologies are open or commoditized but immature, varied, and difficult to operate together. A large supplier can differentiate through breadth, trusted relationships, global support, and responsibility for the whole result, then monetize services and proprietary components inside the integrated stack. The advantage is conditional: standardization, stronger customer capability, or a new platform can make custom integration less scarce, while supplier dependence can turn convenience into lock-in.

The 2016 Palantir investigation supplies a younger, software-led version of the same model. Proprietary data-analysis software was paired with forward deployed engineers inside client organizations to produce operational outcomes, from oil-well monitoring to consumer analysis and cybersecurity. That embedded delivery could support unusually large commitments, but it also exposed the model's scaling constraints: prices above $1 million per month, demands for deeper industry expertise, difficult working relationships, heterogeneous client infrastructure, and uncertainty that pilots would produce durable value. Integration responsibility is valuable only when the supplier can repeatedly convert bespoke work into customer outcomes and cash faster than delivery complexity consumes margin and organizational attention.

## Key Claims
- Integration can be the differentiated product even when component technologies are not proprietary.
- Enterprise customers may prefer one accountable supplier over many superior point products when coordination costs and operational risk are high.
- Scale, geographic coverage, customer trust, and middleware can turn integration responsibility into recurring profit.
- The model weakens when standardized services make the same business objective available without extensive customization.
- Past integration success can create lock-in and inward-looking culture that obstruct adaptation to the next platform shift.
- Embedded engineers can bridge product and client context, but the model becomes fragile when each engagement requires expensive customization or scarce relationship work.
- Large commitments and bookings do not establish model quality unless adoption, cash conversion, delivery cost, and retention are also visible.

## Evidence
- Customer value: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] says IBM customers valued global presence and one supplier responsible for the overall solution.
- Market opening: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] describes the early Internet as a fragmented, immature technology set enterprises struggled to integrate.
- Profit mechanism: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] connects managed systems and services with profitable proprietary middleware.
- Erosion: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] argues standardized public cloud became good enough to reduce IBM's monopoly on enterprise problem solving.
- Embedded delivery: [[inside-palantir-silicon-valleys-most-secretive-company]] describes Palantir as a hybrid software and consulting firm that places forward deployed engineers at customer sites.
- Outcome potential: [[inside-palantir-silicon-valleys-most-secretive-company]] reports Palantir systems for BP oil-well management and energy trading plus a ten-year memorandum valued at $1.2 billion.
- Scaling and fit constraints: [[inside-palantir-silicon-valleys-most-secretive-company]] documents lost or hesitant clients citing high prices, limited sustained value, insufficient industry expertise, difficult relationships, infrastructure problems, or weak executive engagement.
- Economic boundary: [[inside-palantir-silicon-valleys-most-secretive-company]] reports $420 million in 2015 cash collections against $1.7 billion in bookings and more than $500 million in spending.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is derived from two historical journalistic or analyst cases rather than a comparative study. Neither source measures integration cost, service quality, customer bargaining power, gross margin, or the distribution of value consistently. Palantir's mixed client record does not show that embedded engineering always fails, and BP's reported success does not show that very large bespoke commitments generalize. Best-of-breed tools, internal platform teams, and standardized managed services can reduce the need for a single integrator, while regulated or unusually complex environments may preserve it.

## What Changed
- Added Palantir's forward-deployed model as a software-led integration case.
- Added repeatability, client fit, delivery cost, and cash conversion as boundaries on integration value.
- Qualified IBM's supplier-scale advantage with Palantir's mixed outcomes across bespoke corporate engagements.
