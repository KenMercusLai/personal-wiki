## Definition
[[IndieGameDevelopment]] is small-team or solo game creation where the developer carries creative direction, technical implementation, distribution risk, monetization choices, and emotional exposure.

## Current Synthesis
The sources portray indie game development as expressive technical work whose market outcome is unstable and platform-shaped. [[AmirRajan]] experiences building [[ADarkRoom]] as flow, identity, and calling, then sees the same game become an app-store phenomenon through a sudden viral spike. [[RickKelly]] leaves a major studio for ownership of his own work, achieves visible mobile-store rankings, and still returns to employment outside games. Together the cases resist a simple lesson: autonomy, craft, and distribution success matter, but rankings, platform economics, timing, luck, revenue durability, and personal financial needs determine whether independent work becomes a sustainable career.

## Key Claims
- Indie game work can be motivated by expressive fit before it has market evidence.
- Mobile platform discovery can create step-function success rather than smooth growth.
- A hit game does not necessarily create a durable full-time studio career.
- Monetization ethics are part of the creative identity, not only a revenue optimization problem.
- Post-launch attention to rankings, reviews, and downloads can become psychologically consuming.
- Porting across platforms may preserve reach without preserving revenue.
- Leaving a studio for creative ownership can improve autonomy without producing a durable independent income.

## Evidence
- Expressive fit: [[a-dark-room-from-sabbatical-year-to-800000]] says Rajan lost track of time building [[ADarkRoom]] and felt nothing existed but him and the creation.
- Weak early signal: [[a-dark-room-from-sabbatical-year-to-800000]] reports 30 first-day downloads and later about 10 daily downloads before virality.
- Viral platform jump: [[a-dark-room-from-sabbatical-year-to-800000]] reports daily sales rising from hundreds to thousands of dollars and then a #1 [[AppStore]] ranking.
- Non-durable hit: [[a-dark-room-from-sabbatical-year-to-800000]] says downloads later fell and later games were not enough to keep Rajan from returning to work.
- Monetization stance: [[a-dark-room-from-sabbatical-year-to-800000]] says Rajan rejected ads, micro-transactions, energy bars, and similar tactics.
- Platform contrast: [[a-dark-room-from-sabbatical-year-to-800000]] says [[Android]] reach did not match iOS revenue for Rajan's games.
- Ownership-driven exit: [[gamasutra-the-great-video-game-exodus]] says [[RickKelly]] objected to broad employer ownership of outside creations and founded an independent company after leaving ZeniMax.
- Visible but insufficient success: [[gamasutra-the-great-video-game-exodus]] reports that Kelly's debut game reached top-100 iOS categories and #34 among new paid Google Play games before he moved into computer security.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Both sources are selected mobile indie-game retrospectives and should not be generalized to every game genre, platform, team size, or monetization model. Rajan's article distinguishes creative success, gross revenue, passive income, and sustainable full-time livelihood; Kelly's account gives store ranks but no title, revenue, costs, or duration. Neither source estimates typical indie outcomes.

## What Changed
- Added Kelly as a second case where autonomy and visible store success did not sustain a full-time game career.
