## Definition
[[CorporateVentureInvesting]] is startup investment conducted by, or through funds affiliated with, an established corporation, potentially across different stages, sectors, strategic purposes, and non-equity programs.

## Current Synthesis
[[Alphabet]] illustrates a multi-vehicle form of corporate venture investing. [[GV]], [[CapitalG]], [[GradientVentures]], and direct [[Google]] investment held distinct broad, growth-stage, AI-focused, and corporate roles, while accelerator grants and impact activity extended the system beyond conventional equity. Low overlap can preserve differentiated mandates and widen option exposure, but deal count and network breadth do not show capital allocation, strategic coherence, or financial performance. Acquisitions and public exits create additional relationships that may matter more than co-investment overlap.

## Key Claims
- A corporation can separate startup investing across stage-, sector-, or purpose-specific vehicles rather than operate one consolidated fund.
- Deal volume measures activity but not capital at risk, ownership, strategic value, or return quality.
- Limited portfolio overlap may indicate mandate differentiation, but it can also reflect incomplete data or decentralized selection.
- Equity investment can coexist with accelerator grants, impact investment, and direct corporate transactions.
- Exit and acquisition networks reveal a different form of overlap from shared initial investments.
- A thematically broad portfolio can create optionality while making the corporate-wide thesis difficult to infer.

## Evidence
- Multi-vehicle structure: [[jason-rowley-a-peek-inside-alphabets-investing-universe]] separates GV, CapitalG, Gradient Ventures, and Google direct investment by role.
- Activity scale: [[jason-rowley-a-peek-inside-alphabets-investing-universe]] reports 103 deals across the four entities in 2017.
- Mandate separation: [[jason-rowley-a-peek-inside-alphabets-investing-universe]] reports only Gusto and Pindrop as shared GV-CapitalG portfolio companies.
- Non-equity extensions: [[jason-rowley-a-peek-inside-alphabets-investing-universe]] identifies Launchpad Accelerator grants and Google.org impact activity.
- Exit relationships: [[jason-rowley-a-peek-inside-alphabets-investing-universe]] reports Alphabet, Cisco, and Yahoo/Oath as recurring acquirers of backed companies and names twelve portfolio companies that had gone public.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
One 2018 article based on Crunchbase cannot establish the current structure or the causal benefits of multiple vehicles. Publicly announced deals may omit confidential transactions and may count participation without weighting check size or ownership. Portfolio breadth can mean deliberate optionality, decentralized experimentation, or weak strategic coherence. Acquiring a previously backed company may reflect information advantage or strategic fit, but it can also raise governance, valuation, and conflict questions not examined here.

## What Changed
- Added a multi-vehicle corporate-investing model grounded in Alphabet's 2017 activity.
- Distinguished co-investment overlap from acquisition and public-exit relationships.
- Added activity-measurement and thematic-coherence limits.
