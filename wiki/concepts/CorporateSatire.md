## Definition
[[CorporateSatire]] is humor that exaggerates workplace rituals, language, incentives, and status performances to reveal what an organization implicitly rewards.

## Current Synthesis
In the Cooper source, corporate satire works by giving intentionally bad advice in the familiar shape of a practical workplace listicle. The joke depends on the gap between the surface goal, appearing smart in a brainstorming meeting, and the implied criticism that corporate meetings can reward strategic-sounding behavior even when it blocks or avoids useful thinking.

## Key Claims
- Satire can expose workplace norms by treating obviously hollow behavior as if it were sound professional advice.
- Corporate vocabulary becomes comic when words such as disruptive, roadmap, framework, platform, and model are detached from concrete decision-making.
- Meeting rituals are an especially strong target because participants can perform thoughtfulness, creativity, or authority in public.
- Visual jokes can strengthen corporate satire by showing the body language, props, and speech bubbles that make empty behavior socially legible.

## Evidence
- Bad-advice frame: [[9-tricks-to-appear-smart-in-brainstorming-meetings-the-cooper-review-medium]] presents tricks for appearing creative while explicitly implying that corporations often resist real ideas.
- Jargon parody: [[9-tricks-to-appear-smart-in-brainstorming-meetings-the-cooper-review-medium]] mocks disruption, 10x thinking, roadmaps, pivots, frameworks, platforms, and models as ways to sound strategic without substance.
- Meeting performance: [[9-tricks-to-appear-smart-in-brainstorming-meetings-the-cooper-review-medium]] turns water runs, sticky-note drawing, analogies, question-questioning, CEO references, and premature "Ship it!" declarations into recognizable meeting theater.
- Visual reinforcement: [[9-tricks-to-appear-smart-in-brainstorming-meetings-the-cooper-review-medium]] includes illustrations of the water/snack offer, sketching, right-question posture, CEO talk, framework talk, and decisive pose.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This concept currently rests on one humor source, so it describes a satirical reading of corporate behavior rather than empirical evidence about all brainstorming meetings. The article intentionally exaggerates; its value is diagnostic and cultural, not procedural.
