## Definition
[[DigitalGifting]] is the use of paid virtual goods as social signals, payments, and attention-seeking messages inside digital platforms.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats digital gifting as the engine of Chinese social livestreaming. A gift is not merely a microtransaction: it publicly signals admiration, buys a chance at recognition, gives the broadcaster immediate financial feedback, and lets the platform share revenue with both app stores and creators. This makes monetization part of the interaction itself, especially in stranger networks where gifts can carry flirtation, status, and request-making without the social awkwardness of direct cash.

## Key Claims
- Digital gifts convert viewer attention and desire for recognition into direct platform revenue.
- Gift revenue aligns platform, broadcaster, and viewer incentives more directly than advertising.
- Expensive gifts and top-fan leaderboards create status competition around broadcaster attention.
- Gifting works as communication, not only payment, in stranger-to-stranger social streams.
- Revenue can be highly concentrated among generous viewers.

## Evidence
- Gift mechanics: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] describes viewers sending questions and virtual gifts to win broadcaster attention and request actions.
- Sports-car screenshot: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] includes an Inke image where a sports-car sticker costs about $45, with the broadcaster likely keeping about $15.
- Incentive split: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] says revenue is split among the app store, broadcaster, and livestreaming platform.
- Broadcaster income: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] says top Chinese broadcasters could make tens of thousands of dollars per month.
- Concentration estimate: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] cites reports claiming 5% of viewers may contribute 70% of virtual-gift revenue.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source reports estimates rather than audited platform-level revenue concentration. It also notes that gifting dynamics may differ when the audience is made of existing friends rather than strangers, and the flirtation/status logic may not generalize across cultures, platforms, or content categories.
