## Definition
[[Eustress]] is brief, manageable stress that is followed by productive progress, reward, or relief and is therefore experienced as beneficial rather than merely aversive.

## Current Synthesis
In [[PiotrWozniak]]'s account, useful stress is not defined by arousal alone. A challenge becomes eustress when a person can voluntarily engage with it, regulate exposure, retreat or recompute when needed, and eventually connect the effort with progress or resolution. Learning and exploration can therefore contain fear or tension without becoming harmful when the [[ProblemValuationNetwork]] keeps expected reward proportionate to effort and danger.

The mechanism is dose- and control-sensitive. An intermediate challenge can sharpen memory, expand skill, and build [[StressResilience]], whereas inescapable or excessive demands can cross into [[ChronicStress]].

## Key Claims
- Eustress combines short-lived acute stress with later reward, progress, or relief.
- Autonomy, reversibility, and challenge calibration help distinguish useful stress from overload.
- Productive struggle can strengthen learning and make important experiences memorable.
- Intermediate difficulty can yield more expected reward than trivial or overwhelming tasks.
- Repeated manageable challenges may build stress resilience when the learner controls exposure.

## Evidence
- Definition and reward: [[liang-xing-ya-li-de-ji-zhi]] describes eustress as acute stress followed by reward or removal of the main stressor.
- Exploration mechanism: [[liang-xing-ya-li-de-ji-zhi]] uses iterative river crossing to show approach, retreat, recomputation, skill acquisition, and possible abandonment.
- Visual model: [[liang-xing-ya-li-de-ji-zhi]] retains a chart in which expected reward peaks at intermediate problem difficulty as reward and success probability move in opposite directions.
- Resilience claim: [[liang-xing-ya-li-de-ji-zhi]] links freely chosen, productive challenge to lasting skills and stronger threat appraisal.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers a theoretical and illustrative model rather than controlled evidence defining safe stress doses or proving that stress itself causes durable learning. Autonomy and successful resolution may be important moderators, and the same nominal task can be manageable for one person but overwhelming for another.

## What Changed
- Created the concept with autonomy, reward, and intermediate difficulty as its defining boundary conditions.

## Related Concepts
- [[ProblemValuationNetwork]] - estimates whether a challenge's expected reward justifies effort and risk.
- [[StressResilience]] - proposed capacity strengthened by repeated manageable challenge.
- [[ChronicStress]] - prolonged or uncontrollable stress is the harmful contrast.
- [[FreeLearning]] - self-direction lets a learner regulate challenge exposure.
- [[CoerciveLearning]] - imposed demands can remove the control that keeps challenge beneficial.
