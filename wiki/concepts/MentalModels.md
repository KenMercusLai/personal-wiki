## Definition
[[MentalModels]] are compact representations of recurring phenomena that help a person interpret situations, anticipate consequences, and transfer a pattern across domains.

## Current Synthesis
Simmons places mental models at the high-density end of a sequence running from social posts through books, book summaries, and field summaries. His case is that a durable model such as the 80/20 rule or opportunity cost compresses recurring relationships and remains useful across business, creativity, relationships, and personal decisions. This makes a model a retrieval and comparison tool rather than a substitute for domain knowledge. Cross-domain reach is valuable only when the analogy preserves the causal structure; otherwise compression becomes oversimplification.

## Key Claims
- A useful model compresses a recurring pattern into a reusable representation.
- Models can retain value longer than topical information and transfer across fields.
- Organizing new observations around models can expose connections between disciplines.
- Applying a model still requires checking its fit to the present domain and evidence.

## Evidence
- Knowledge-density hierarchy: [[while-everyone-is-distracted-by-social-media-successful-people-double-down-on-an-underrated-skill]] ranks models as more condensed than book and field summaries.
- Cross-domain examples: [[while-everyone-is-distracted-by-social-media-successful-people-double-down-on-an-underrated-skill]] uses the 80/20 rule and opportunity cost as patterns applicable to many decisions.
- Reported practice change: [[while-everyone-is-distracted-by-social-media-successful-people-double-down-on-an-underrated-skill]] says Simmons used models to revisit mistakes, connect disciplines, and generate less conventional ideas.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's hierarchy is an opinion informed by the author's experience, not evidence that models consistently outperform books, summaries, or domain-specific instruction. Compact models discard detail, broad transfer can create false analogies, and a memorable name can give weak reasoning an appearance of rigor. Simmons also sells mental-model education, which strengthens the need to separate the concept's usefulness from its promotional ranking.

## What Changed
- Established mental models as compressed, cross-domain representations in the learning corpus.
- Added an explicit warning that transfer requires domain-fit checks and cannot replace detailed knowledge.
