## Definition
[[AssumePositiveIntent]] is the interpersonal judgment practice of beginning a relationship or tense interaction from the working assumption that others are trying to act in good faith unless evidence shows otherwise.

## Current Synthesis
Rick Manelius presents [[AssumePositiveIntent]] as a trust-first stance for professional conflict. In his project-budget story, a client stakeholder named Chris used the phrase to interrupt defensive contract positioning and redirect the group toward a more efficient solution. The concept overlaps with [[HanlonsRazor]] because both resist quick malice attribution, but positive intent is broader: it is less about explaining mistakes and more about choosing a default posture that can create collaboration, speed, and trust. The source's main qualification is that trust-first does not mean boundary-free; repeated violations, dangerous contexts, and bad-faith actors justify skepticism.

## Key Claims
- Assuming positive intent can reduce the time and emotional energy spent second-guessing motives.
- Trust-first collaboration can make tense professional relationships more efficient and solution-focused.
- The stance differs from naivete because it can be withdrawn when a person violates trust on a relevant dimension.
- Low-trust or harmful contexts require skepticism, boundaries, and risk control rather than automatic trust.
- The expected benefit depends on the belief that most people have enough conscience to respond to harm and repair.

## Evidence
- Conflict intervention: [[assume-positive-intent-rick-manelius]] describes Chris interrupting budget and contract defensiveness by asking colleagues to assume positive intent.
- Efficiency argument: [[assume-positive-intent-rick-manelius]] says negative intent assumptions create second-guessing, combativeness, and slower contract-heavy coordination.
- Trust-first path: [[assume-positive-intent-rick-manelius]] contrasts waiting for proof of trustworthiness with extending trust first until violated.
- Boundary condition: [[assume-positive-intent-rick-manelius]] explicitly rejects applying the principle haphazardly in low-trust, dangerous, or previously violated contexts.
- Bad-faith risk: [[assume-positive-intent-rick-manelius]] cites [[MarthaStout]] to acknowledge people who may exploit trust.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The article is a personal essay, not a controlled study, and its central business case is anecdotal. The cited population statistic about conscienceless actors is used as a rhetorical risk frame rather than independently evaluated evidence in this wiki. Positive intent should not override safety boundaries, documented misconduct, power imbalances, repeated deception, legal duties, or situations where a person has already violated trust on the relevant metric.

## What Changed
- Created the concept as a distinct trust-first collaboration heuristic connected to, but broader than, [[HanlonsRazor]].
