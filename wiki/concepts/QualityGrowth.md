## Definition
[[QualityGrowth]] is durable, economically supportable growth generated by real customer value, repeat use, and disciplined product quality rather than primarily by spending, financing, launch attention, or metric presentation.

## Current Synthesis
[[MaiYang]] uses [[KarriSaarinen]] and [[Linear]] to contrast product pull with externally accelerated “virtual fat” growth. The framework does not reject speed, marketing, sales, or capital; it asks whether those inputs amplify a product people already value or temporarily conceal weak retention and quality. Its operational layer joins product scope, defect handling, recurring attention to small experience flaws, and founder understanding of users and quality.

In AI products, the framework gains a selection problem. Easier implementation makes it cheap to add model-driven capabilities and produce a competent first version, so advantage shifts toward deciding what not to build, recognizing subtle experience failures, and sustaining the craft needed for return use. Quality growth therefore connects growth evidence to product judgment: demand, retention, reliability, and economically supportable acquisition must survive after novelty or subsidies fade.

## Key Claims
- Growth quality depends on the customer value and economics beneath the rate, not on speed alone.
- Marketing, sales, financing, and launch attention can amplify demand but cannot by themselves establish durable product pull.
- Repeat use, defect discipline, and coherent workflows are stronger evidence than feature count or polished growth curves.
- AI-cheapened implementation raises the relative value of product judgment, taste, refusal, and experiential polish.
- Quality should be embedded in recurring operating routines rather than reserved for occasional cleanup projects.
- Founder involvement supports quality when it preserves direct understanding without collapsing into universal personal control.

## Evidence
- Growth distinction: [[everyone-can-build-almost-no-one-can-judge]] contrasts capital- and promotion-driven acceleration with growth that emerges from customer value and product recognition.
- Defect operations: [[everyone-can-build-almost-no-one-can-judge]] reports Linear's 48-hour target for high-priority defects and seven-day target for other bugs.
- Habit formation: [[everyone-can-build-almost-no-one-can-judge]] presents “Quality Wednesdays” as repeated attention to small experience defects.
- AI boundary: [[everyone-can-build-almost-no-one-can-judge]] argues that cheap code generation makes judgment over scope, correctness of experience, and final polish more scarce.
- Product coherence: [[everyone-can-build-almost-no-one-can-judge]] rejects feature-checklist competition in favor of designing around users' primary tasks.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept currently rests on one secondary practitioner essay. It supplies no agreed growth-quality metric, Linear cohort data, spend comparison, defect outcomes, causal study, or threshold separating useful acceleration from artificial growth. Quality can conflict with speed, accessibility, experimentation, safety, or survival, and fast growth can reflect genuine demand. Marketing and sales may be essential distribution mechanisms, especially for enterprise or unfamiliar products. A zero-bug inventory can hide closure rather than repair, and aesthetic “taste” can override user evidence or exclude unfamiliar needs. The framework is best used as a diligence question—what durable value and operating evidence produce the curve—rather than as proof that one company practice guarantees sustainable growth.
