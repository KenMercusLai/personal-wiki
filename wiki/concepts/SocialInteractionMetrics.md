## Definition
[[SocialInteractionMetrics]] are measures such as views, likes, shares, comments, and other platform-native responses that publishers use to evaluate distributed content when destination-site visits are no longer the only or best proxy for audience value.

## Current Synthesis
The sources say destination-site uniques become incomplete when content increasingly lives across platforms. Bleacher Report therefore shifted attention toward social interactions and competed for time with all publishers and attention events, not only sports rivals. Evans adds a measurement warning: “views” and time spent are not naturally comparable across autoplay, silent scrolling, skipped clips, Snapchat-style sequences, YouTube, and television. The metric shift follows [[DistributedPublishingStrategy]], but platform-native signals require behavioral definitions and business outcomes before they can be compared.

## Key Claims
- Destination-site audience totals become incomplete when content is consumed directly on external platforms.
- Views, likes, and shares can reflect platform-distributed reach, but their definitions and user behaviors differ by surface.
- Interaction metrics let publishers compare themselves beyond their narrow category.
- Time competition broadens the competitive set from direct topical rivals to all attention surfaces.
- Social metrics should supplement, not erase, owned-audience and revenue measures.

## Evidence
- Metric replacement: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says social interactions are becoming the new uniques.
- Platform distribution context: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says publishers are pushing content directly onto platforms, reducing the centrality of site visits.
- Competitive set: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says Bleacher Report compares itself with all publishers, not only sports publishers.
- Time competition: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says Bleacher Report competes for time not just with ESPN.
- Comparability limit: [[video-is-the-new-html-benedict-evans]] asks how autoplay, silent playback, scrolling, skipping, and format differences change what a reported video view means.
- Reporting control: [[video-is-the-new-html-benedict-evans]] notes that platform owners define and report their own metrics, complicating comparisons of both attention and advertising value.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not show whether interaction metrics predict retention, revenue, brand lift, or owned-audience growth. Platform-native metrics can be volatile, algorithm-dependent, platform-defined, and easier to inflate than business outcomes. Evans's Snapchat, Facebook, YouTube, and television comparisons describe a 2016 measurement problem rather than a current audit of each platform's definitions.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Bleacher Report's shift from site uniques to social interactions.
- Added explicit comparability limits for autoplay, sound, scrolling, skipping, time spent, and platform-controlled definitions.
