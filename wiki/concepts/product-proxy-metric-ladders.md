Source: [7 Ways to Use the Rule of Threes to Build Great Products]({{< relref "/wiki/sources/rule-of-threes-build-great-products.md" >}})

Product proxy metric ladders translate a broad business goal into indicators that can guide product decisions before the official outcome is ready. The source proposes starting with a core business theme such as NPS, conversion, or revenue, finding a product metric that should move before that theme changes, and then tying the pieces together.

The reason for the ladder is measurement latency. A long-term metric such as 30-day conversion can require enough time for a full treatment cohort before the team learns whether work is helping. By then, the product direction may already be locked in. The source therefore recommends an even shorter-term metric, such as a one-day proxy, that can be checked frequently during the development cycle.

Short-window metrics still need interpretation discipline. When the product has international users, time zones can distort very short measurement windows, so the analysis needs a buffer around the period being measured.
