## Definition
[[ViralLoops]] are acquisition mechanisms in which a user's normal action, invitation, share, embed, or public artifact exposes non-users to the product and gives them a reason to join or return.

## Current Synthesis
The source's clearest growth-hacking pattern is the product-mediated loop. [[Hotmail]] appended a signup link to outgoing email, [[Dropbox]] made file access require exposure to the sharing service, [[TripAdvisor]] badges linked hotel visitors back to reviews, [[Spotify]] widgets sent listeners toward accounts, and [[LinkedIn]] public profiles turned search visibility into registration pressure. These loops differ from ordinary word of mouth because the referral surface is built into product use, but they still depend on recipient relevance and low-friction onboarding.

## Key Claims
- Viral loops work when sharing is a natural part of using the product rather than a separate campaign.
- Public artifacts such as profiles, badges, embeds, links, and sent messages can become acquisition surfaces.
- Offline visibility can create similar curiosity loops when product use is socially noticeable.
- Incentives can strengthen a viral loop but are not required when the product action already exposes value.
- A loop needs low-friction conversion after exposure or the attention leaks away.
- Viral-loop examples are vulnerable to hindsight bias when the source does not measure conversion paths directly.

## Evidence
- Built into communication: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] describes [[Hotmail]] adding a signup link to outgoing emails and [[Dropbox]] exposing recipients to the service through file sharing.
- Public web surfaces: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says [[LinkedIn]] public profiles made members discoverable through search and required signup for connection.
- Badge and widget loops: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] presents [[TripAdvisor]] review badges, Skyscanner widgets, and [[Spotify]] widgets as link-generating and account-generating distribution.
- Offline curiosity: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] frames [[Shazam]] users holding phones to speakers and Urban Spoon's shake gesture as visible behaviors that prompt questions from bystanders.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source rarely supplies funnel-level evidence for each loop. Some cases may have grown because of product utility, novelty, market timing, press, search ranking, or advertising support. Viral loops can also decay when platforms restrict sharing surfaces, users perceive invitations as spam, or recipients no longer find the artifact novel.
