## Definition
[[SelfEfficacy]] is a person's belief that they are capable of organizing and carrying out actions that produce intended results.

## Current Synthesis
The available source treats self-efficacy as an outcome of repeated successful action rather than positive assertion alone. Clear goals make relevant action identifiable, habits make that action repeatable, and manageable tasks provide successes from which a person can attempt progressively larger loads. In this model, efficacy and action reinforce one another: successful repetition strengthens perceived capability, which lowers the resistance to showing up again.

## Key Claims
- Self-efficacy develops through successful execution, not merely through holding an aspiration.
- Smaller related tasks can condition a person for more demanding goals.
- Progressively increasing difficulty helps build capability without the frustration of attempting the end goal immediately.
- Habit systems connect goal pursuit to repeated mastery experiences.
- Personality-compatible habits may make sustained action more psychologically workable.

## Evidence
- Progressive load: [[zak-slayback-you-have-to-show-up-every-day]] compares efficacy development to increasing controlled weight in strength training rather than attempting an extreme lift immediately.
- Repeated mastery: [[zak-slayback-you-have-to-show-up-every-day]] says regularly executing habits and achieving increasingly larger goals builds perceived capability.
- Reinforcing loop: [[zak-slayback-you-have-to-show-up-every-day]] argues that repeatedly showing up makes future participation easier.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a short practitioner essay and does not distinguish perceived capability from measured competence or provide empirical evidence for its mechanism. Progressive practice can fail to increase self-efficacy when tasks are poorly chosen, feedback is misleading, structural barriers dominate, or workload ignores recovery, disability, health, and available resources. “Every day” is therefore best understood as continuity of engagement, not a universal requirement for literal daily performance.

## What Changed
- Established a habit-based account of self-efficacy built through repeated, progressively demanding mastery experiences.

## Related Concepts

- [[WorkHabits]] - repeated routines create opportunities for mastery experiences.
- [[PersonalProductivity]] - self-efficacy can reduce the resistance involved in initiating useful work.
- [[SelfDiscipline]] - disciplined repetition can support efficacy, while efficacy can make repetition easier to sustain.
