## Definition
[[ReleaseFocusedSideProjects]] are side-project practices that prioritize one active project, fast usable milestones, public or personal release, and feedback over long-running unpublished scope expansion.

## Current Synthesis
Wang Ziting's retrospective turns side-project productivity into a release-state problem. With limited after-work time, a project that does not reach a usable version quickly loses feedback, motivation, and scope control. [[DeployBeta]] illustrates the failure mode: substantial technical work continued for two years without reaching an external release standard. [[Elecpass]] illustrates the healthier pattern: release early, use the product, then return for focused improvements.

Hack Career adds the job-search version of the same discipline: learning a skill is not enough unless the candidate ships something that can be inspected. For [[JobApplicationSideProjects]], release is only the first threshold; the project also needs relevance, depth, and at least one later improvement cycle so it becomes credible evidence rather than resume filler.

## Key Claims
- Side-project focus should be limited to one project at a time when time and energy are scarce.
- A stage-level usable release creates feedback and motivation before scope expands too far.
- Projects that remain unreleased for too long can accumulate half-finished work and lose momentum.
- Personal use after release can provide enough feedback to support later focused iteration.
- For job applications, shipping must be paired with relevance, depth, and visible iteration.

## Evidence
- Focus rule: [[2018-nian-du-xiao-jie-ji-shu-fang-mian]] names "focus on one project at a time" as an important 2018 lesson.
- Negative example: [[2018-nian-du-xiao-jie-ji-shu-fang-mian]] says [[DeployBeta]] lasted two years but never reached a publishable standard.
- Positive example: [[2018-nian-du-xiao-jie-ji-shu-fang-mian]] says [[Elecpass]] shipped two versions in half a month and spent most of its life in a released state.
- Feedback loop: [[2018-nian-du-xiao-jie-ji-shu-fang-mian]] says delayed release prevents feedback and reduces motivation to improve.
- Job-search evidence: [[3-principles-to-make-your-side-project-stand-out-to-help-you-land-a-job-in-tech-hack-career]] says a side project needs at least two iterations and should be strong enough to represent work quality to employers.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The claim is strongest for time-constrained individual projects. Research-heavy, infrastructure-heavy, or safety-critical projects may need longer unreleased phases, but the sources warn that indefinite unreleased work has real motivational and evidentiary cost. A released project still may not help a job application if it is irrelevant, shallow, or hidden from reviewers.

- Added the job-application qualification that shipping must produce visible, relevant evidence for employers.
