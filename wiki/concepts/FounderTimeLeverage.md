## Definition
[[FounderTimeLeverage]] is the practice of spending money, tools, or delegated effort to return scarce founder time to higher-value work.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats founder time as more scarce than small amounts of cash. Founders should still manage time deliberately, but the article warns against mistaking manual frugality for discipline when inexpensive automation, scheduling tools, or assistance can remove repeated administrative work. The purpose is not status delegation; it is protecting the time needed to improve the business, serve customers, and maintain a sustainable life.

## Key Claims
- Founder time is one of the scarcest startup resources.
- Cheap automation and coordination tools can be rational purchases when they remove repeated low-value work.
- Manual handling of scheduling, reporting, travel, or administrative tasks can be penny-wise and pound-foolish.
- Time leverage supports both better business work and more attainable work/life balance.

## Evidence
- Scarcity frame: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] says nothing in a founder's world is more precious than time.
- Tool examples: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] cites Zapier, Calendly, Fancy Hands, and assistants as examples of buying back time.
- Opportunity cost: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] argues that doing avoidable admin work robs the founder, team, and customers of time for improving the business.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source assumes the expense is small relative to the time returned. Very early or cash-constrained founders still need to judge whether a tool, contractor, or assistant truly removes important friction or merely adds management overhead.
