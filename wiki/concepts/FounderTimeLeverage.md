## Definition
[[FounderTimeLeverage]] is the practice of spending money, tools, or delegated effort to return scarce time to higher-value work, recovery, relationships, or well-being.

## Current Synthesis
The sources now frame time leverage as both an operator discipline and a broader well-being tactic. The startup source treats founder time as more scarce than small amounts of cash: inexpensive automation, scheduling tools, assistants, or outsourcing can remove repeated administrative work and return attention to the business, customers, and a sustainable life. The NPR source broadens the pattern beyond founders by reporting that adults who spent money on time-saving services such as cleaning, lawn care, errands, meal delivery, or grocery delivery reported more positive emotion than when they bought material goods. The synthesis is therefore not simply "delegate more"; it is that buying time can be rational when the avoided task is disliked or low-value, the returned time has meaningful use, and the cost does not create more stress than it removes.

## Key Claims
- Founder time is one of the scarcest startup resources, and ordinary adults also experience free time as a scarce resource.
- Cheap automation, coordination tools, services, and assistance can be rational purchases when they remove repeated low-value or disliked work.
- Manual handling of scheduling, reporting, travel, chores, errands, or household tasks can be penny-wise and pound-foolish when it displaces higher-value work or recovery.
- Time-saving spending can improve reported positive emotion and life satisfaction more than comparable spending on material goods.
- Time leverage supports better business work, more attainable work/life balance, and potentially lower stress.

## Evidence
- Scarcity frame: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] says nothing in a founder's world is more precious than time.
- Scarcity beyond founders: [[buying-time-not-stuff-might-make-you-happier-shots-health-news-npr]] frames free time as a scarce resource for many Americans.
- Tool and service examples: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] cites Zapier, Calendly, Fancy Hands, and assistants as ways to buy back founder time; [[buying-time-not-stuff-might-make-you-happier-shots-health-news-npr]] cites house cleaning, lawn care, grocery delivery, meal delivery, errands, mowing, and bathroom cleaning as time-saving service examples.
- Opportunity cost: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] argues that doing avoidable admin work robs the founder, team, and customers of time for improving the business.
- Happiness evidence: [[buying-time-not-stuff-might-make-you-happier-shots-health-news-npr]] reports that a 60-person weekend experiment found more positive emotion after a $40 time-saving purchase than after a $40 material purchase.
- Life-satisfaction evidence: [[buying-time-not-stuff-might-make-you-happier-shots-health-news-npr]] reports that a 6,000-person survey found people who bought time nearly one rung higher on a 10-point life-satisfaction scale, across a wide income range.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The startup source assumes the expense is small relative to the time returned. Very early or cash-constrained founders still need to judge whether a tool, contractor, or assistant truly removes important friction or merely adds management overhead. The NPR source is stronger than ordinary advice because it reports both experimental and survey evidence, but it still leaves durability uncertain: [[EmanuelMaidenberg]] asks whether the boost in positive emotion is sustained or mainly an immediate response. [[ElizabethDunn]] also notes that people may feel guilty paying others to do tasks they can perform themselves, so social norms and identity can limit adoption even when the purchase is beneficial.

## What Changed
- Broadened the concept from founder-specific operating advice to general time-saving spending.
- Added empirical evidence that buying time can increase positive emotion and life satisfaction compared with buying material goods.
- Added qualifications about guilt, income range, and uncertain durability of the happiness effect.
