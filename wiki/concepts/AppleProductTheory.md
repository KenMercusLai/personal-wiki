## Definition
[[AppleProductTheory]] is [[NeilCybart]]'s framework for interpreting [[Apple]]'s product line as a ladder of increasingly personal computers, where each form factor handles tasks formerly assigned to a larger or less intimate device.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that Apple is not mainly trying to replace old products with new ones. Instead, it uses new form factors, inputs, outputs, custom silicon, miniaturization, and intuitive interaction to create alternatives to more powerful computers. The embedded diagram makes the structure explicit: Mac desktops push the boundaries of a computer; Mac portables take desktop tasks; iPad takes Mac tasks; [[IPhone]] takes iPad and Mac tasks; [[AppleWatch]] takes iPhone tasks; and possible [[SmartGlasses]] would take Apple Watch and iPhone tasks. The 2018 event supplies the live evidence: iPhone XS/XR advances make the phone a stronger AR navigator and computer alternative, while Apple Watch Series 4's heart and fall features show a wrist-worn device doing proactive, body-centered work the iPhone cannot do.

## Key Claims
- Apple's product categories are connected by the mission of making technology more personal.
- The theory is about alternatives to more powerful computers, not one-to-one product replacement.
- Each product category has a role shaped by its form factor, input methods, output methods, and proximity to the body.
- iPhone advances in screens, custom silicon, and cameras make it a stronger alternative to iPad and Mac tasks.
- Apple Watch becomes strategically important when it handles proactive, health, communication, and contextual tasks that reduce reliance on the iPhone.
- Smart glasses fit the theory as a simpler, more intuitive visual layer that could handle tasks now assigned to Apple Watch and iPhone.
- Larger potential user bases are expected for more personal devices if they reduce barriers between people and technology.

## Evidence
- Product ladder: [[above-avalon-connecting-the-apple-dots]] presents Mac desktops, Mac portables, iPad, iPhone, Apple Watch, and glasses as successive categories handling tasks from the category above.
- Alternative logic: [[above-avalon-connecting-the-apple-dots]] says Apple's strategy is based on alternatives to existing products rather than replacements.
- iPhone capability: [[above-avalon-connecting-the-apple-dots]] ties the 2018 iPhones to larger screens, A12 Bionic silicon, and more intelligent cameras.
- Silicon control: [[above-avalon-connecting-the-apple-dots]] argues Apple spent years controlling core technologies and that the A12 Bionic strengthened its competitive advantage.
- Watch role: [[above-avalon-connecting-the-apple-dots]] frames Apple Watch Series 4 as a proactive digital assistant through heart-rhythm and fall-detection capabilities.
- Glasses extension: [[above-avalon-connecting-the-apple-dots]] argues Apple glasses would use glances, voice, custom silicon, miniaturization, and enhanced vision to make technology more personal.
- User-base implication: [[above-avalon-connecting-the-apple-dots]] says more personal devices such as Apple Watch and glasses could eventually exceed the iPhone user base.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a 2018 analyst interpretation of Apple's event and product direction. It does not prove that Apple Watch would fully decouple from iPhone, that Apple would ship mass-market glasses, or that Watch and glasses would surpass the iPhone user base. Its user-base claims are strategic projections grounded in form-factor intuition and Cybart's estimates, not audited future outcomes.
