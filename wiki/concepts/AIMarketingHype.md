## Definition
[[AIMarketingHype]] is the use of "AI" as broad product-positioning language when the underlying user benefit, model behavior, or technical distinction is unclear or only loosely connected to artificial intelligence.

## Current Synthesis
The sources capture a pre-generative-AI version of the same pattern: once a technical term becomes commercially attractive, it spreads across startup positioning and product signage faster than meaningful user value can be explained. The 2017 essay says startups were dressing themselves in AI language, while Sinofsky saw "AI" labels on TVs, washing machines, cameras, gadgets, robot vacuums, and labor-saving products at CES 2019. Samsung's 8K TV display promoted AI upscaling, streaming, content/connectivity, and sound around a Quantum 8K processor.

Neither source denies that machine-learning techniques exist inside products. The 2017 essay predicts that real adoption will eventually make AI use too ordinary to differentiate a startup, while the CES report distinguishes broad AI labeling from more plausible AI-developed or AI-powered products, such as a prosthetic hand that could use EMG receptor training to interpret nerve impulses. The reusable lesson is that AI claims should be judged by the user's visible capability, training or adaptation mechanism, and product outcome rather than by the presence of the label.

## Key Claims
- AI language can become a default marketing badge across unrelated consumer products.
- Product signage may attach AI to ordinary improvements without clarifying what model, adaptation, or user-facing capability is involved.
- Some image-processing or signal-processing uses may be real while still being overmarketed.
- More credible AI claims show a learning or adaptation mechanism connected to a concrete user outcome.
- AI hype can obscure the product-management question of whether the feature is useful, usable, and differentiated.
- As AI techniques normalize, the label may lose signaling value even when the underlying capability is real and widespread.

## Evidence
- Broad labeling: [[ces-2019-a-show-report-learning-by-shipping]] says "AI" was plastered across TVs, washing machines, cameras, gadgets, robot vacuums, and other labor-saving devices.
- TV signage: [[ces-2019-a-show-report-learning-by-shipping]] shows Samsung AI messaging around upscaling, streaming, content/connectivity, sound, and the Quantum 8K processor.
- Weak perceptibility: [[ces-2019-a-show-report-learning-by-shipping]] says the author spent ten minutes at an AI TV demo trying to see the image difference.
- Plausible AI product: [[ces-2019-a-show-report-learning-by-shipping]] cites Brain Robotics' prosthetic hand as a clearer AI-backed example because EMG receptors can be trained from nerve impulses.
- Product-management test: [[ces-2019-a-show-report-learning-by-shipping]] frames CES products as needing usability, utility, and desirability beyond technical labels.
- Startup-label saturation: [[in-a-few-years-no-investors-are-going-to-be-looking-for-ai-startups]] says startups were broadly dressing themselves in AI terminology and predicts that investors would eventually assume relevant AI use.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The critique is about marketing clarity, not a claim that the underlying startups or devices contained no machine-learning or signal-processing work. Both sources predate the later consumer wave of generative AI, so their examples are a historical hype pattern rather than a complete account of contemporary AI claims. The 2017 essay's prediction that investors would stop explicitly seeking AI startups within two years was too aggressive; continued AI signage at CES 2019 is product-marketing evidence rather than direct investor evidence, but it still shows that the label had not rapidly disappeared from commercial positioning.

## What Changed
- Expanded the concept from product signage to startup positioning.
- Added the qualification that normalization may eventually weaken AI's signaling value, while the source's two-year forecast was too aggressive.
