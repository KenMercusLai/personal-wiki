## Definition
[[AIMarketingHype]] is the use of "AI" as broad product-positioning language when the underlying user benefit, model behavior, or technical distinction is unclear or only loosely connected to artificial intelligence.

## Current Synthesis
The CES 2019 report captures a pre-generative-AI version of the same pattern: once a technical term becomes commercially attractive, it spreads across product signage faster than meaningful user value can be explained. Sinofsky saw "AI" labels on TVs, washing machines, cameras, gadgets, robot vacuums, and labor-saving products, with Samsung's 8K TV display promoting AI upscaling, streaming, content/connectivity, and sound around a Quantum 8K processor.

The source does not deny that machine-learning techniques existed inside some products. It distinguishes broad AI labeling from more plausible AI-developed or AI-powered products, such as a prosthetic hand that could use EMG receptor training to interpret nerve impulses. The reusable lesson is that AI claims should be judged by the user's visible capability, training/adaptation mechanism, and product outcome rather than by the presence of the label.

## Key Claims
- AI language can become a default marketing badge across unrelated consumer products.
- Product signage may attach AI to ordinary improvements without clarifying what model, adaptation, or user-facing capability is involved.
- Some image-processing or signal-processing uses may be real while still being overmarketed.
- More credible AI claims show a learning or adaptation mechanism connected to a concrete user outcome.
- AI hype can obscure the product-management question of whether the feature is useful, usable, and differentiated.

## Evidence
- Broad labeling: [[ces-2019-a-show-report-learning-by-shipping]] says "AI" was plastered across TVs, washing machines, cameras, gadgets, robot vacuums, and other labor-saving devices.
- TV signage: [[ces-2019-a-show-report-learning-by-shipping]] shows Samsung AI messaging around upscaling, streaming, content/connectivity, sound, and the Quantum 8K processor.
- Weak perceptibility: [[ces-2019-a-show-report-learning-by-shipping]] says the author spent ten minutes at an AI TV demo trying to see the image difference.
- Plausible AI product: [[ces-2019-a-show-report-learning-by-shipping]] cites Brain Robotics' prosthetic hand as a clearer AI-backed example because EMG receptors can be trained from nerve impulses.
- Product-management test: [[ces-2019-a-show-report-learning-by-shipping]] frames CES products as needing usability, utility, and desirability beyond technical labels.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The critique is about marketing clarity, not a claim that the underlying devices contained no machine-learning or signal-processing work. The report is also from 2019, before the later consumer wave of generative AI, so the examples should be read as a general hype pattern rather than a complete account of contemporary AI product claims.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to separate AI as a product label from AI as a concrete user-facing capability.
