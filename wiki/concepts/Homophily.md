## Definition
[[Homophily]] is the tendency for people to trust or prefer others who seem similar to themselves.

## Current Synthesis
The Airbnb trust source treats homophily as a social bias that can shape marketplace decisions when strangers lack other evidence. Its key contribution is product-relevant: reputation information can counteract similarity bias when enough positive reviews make reliability visible.

## Key Claims
- Similarity can bias trust decisions in stranger-to-stranger marketplaces.
- Reputation information can reduce the need to rely on similarity as a trust shortcut.
- Review systems can therefore support both transaction confidence and bias mitigation.

## Evidence
- Similarity bias: [[building-for-trust-airbnb-engineering-data-science-medium]] says Airbnb found evidence of homophily among travelers in a collaborative Stanford study.
- Reputation counterweight: [[building-for-trust-airbnb-engineering-data-science-medium]] reports that enough positive reviews can counteract homophily.
- Marketplace use: [[building-for-trust-airbnb-engineering-data-science-medium]] connects this effect to Airbnb's reputation system and booking trust.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source says the Stanford publication was forthcoming at the time and does not provide the study design, sample, effect sizes, or later peer-reviewed result. Reputation may mitigate similarity bias in some contexts while leaving other forms of discrimination, platform design bias, or unequal review accumulation unresolved.
