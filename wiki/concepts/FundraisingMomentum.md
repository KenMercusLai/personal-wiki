## Definition
[[FundraisingMomentum]] is the accumulation of timely business evidence, investor attention, process urgency, and responsive diligence that keeps a startup financing process moving toward a term sheet and close despite changing concerns.

## Current Synthesis
[[DavidFrankel]] separates the formal milestones of a raise from the investor's unstable confidence between them. A strong opening impression can decay as references expose weaknesses, industry work changes the apparent market, capital requirements grow, or a newer opportunity captures attention. Momentum is the founder's partial answer to that volatility: new customers, sales progress, tightly spaced investor conversations, credible competition for the deal, and quick diligence responses can give an investor reasons and deadlines to continue. The newer founder checklist adds preparation before the first meeting: pre-qualify enough investors, seek warm introductions, have diligence material ready, verify who can decide and when, and ask for explicit next steps. Its sharper tactical claim is that compressed meetings plus credible FOMO or a later price increase can counter an investor's incentive to wait.

The concept does not imply that speed or excitement makes a company investable. Momentum can preserve attention and update evidence, but it cannot by itself resolve small markets, weak economics, poor references, unsuitable terms, or founder-investor mismatch. A sound process therefore couples urgency with truthful disclosure and substantive operating progress.

## Key Claims
- Investor confidence can rise and fall repeatedly between the visible milestones of a financing round.
- References, industry diligence, and comparison with other opportunities can reduce enthusiasm without producing one decisive rejection event.
- Observable customer, sales, and market progress can update the investment case while fundraising is still underway.
- Concentrating conversations may create simultaneous decisions and reduce the time in which attention or operating evidence can deteriorate.
- Pre-qualifying enough investors, preparing diligence material, and identifying the real decision path can reduce avoidable stalls before and after meetings.
- Seasonal lulls, flat recent revenue, slow responses, and apparent obfuscation can weaken momentum even when the broader company story has not changed.
- Momentum is a decision-process input, not independent proof of business quality or long-run returns.

## Evidence
- Confidence volatility: [[going-up-and-down-the-vc-roller-coaster-by]] says excitement from team, product, and traction can descend into concern as an investor spends more time on a deal.
- Negative updates: [[going-up-and-down-the-vc-roller-coaster-by]] identifies off-list references, a smaller or more contested market, capital intensity, and a newer opportunity as confidence-reducing factors.
- Operating evidence: [[going-up-and-down-the-vc-roller-coaster-by]] recommends timing updates around new customers, sales increases, and visible market progress.
- Process urgency: [[going-up-and-down-the-vc-roller-coaster-by]] recommends a tight process intended to produce simultaneous term sheets and fear of missing out.
- Responsiveness: [[going-up-and-down-the-vc-roller-coaster-by]] says founders should treat product, roadmap, and customer diligence with the responsiveness offered to an important client.
- Process preparation: [[questions-startup-founders-should-ask-angel-investors-and-vcs-but-rarely-do]] recommends enough pre-qualified targets, warm introductions, ready diligence documents, concentrated meetings, and explicit next steps.
- Delay incentives: [[questions-startup-founders-should-ask-angel-investors-and-vcs-but-rarely-do]] argues that waiting often de-risks a deal for investors and identifies credible FOMO or a price increase as the founder's limited sources of time pressure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept rests on two brief practitioner essays, with no deal-level sample, causal comparison, matched counterparty evidence, or return evidence. The founder-side article is unattributed and does not establish how often investors delay, follow, data-mine, or investigate for competitors. Timing news and invoking competing interest can become impression management; genuine seasonality may be unavoidable; a rapid process can reduce deliberation or bargaining quality; off-list references raise fairness and context questions; and fast compliance with requests does not guarantee mutual fit. FOMO should describe real competing interest, not manufacture it. Material concerns should change a decision rather than be overcome merely by urgency.

## What Changed
- Added pre-qualification, warm introductions, ready diligence material, decision-path discovery, and explicit next steps as preparation for a concentrated raise.
- Qualified FOMO as credible process information rather than a substitute for business evidence or truthful disclosure.
