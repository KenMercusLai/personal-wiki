## Definition
[[DistributedPublishingStrategy]] is a media operating model in which a publisher creates, adapts, and monetizes content across owned sites, apps, search, social feeds, and platform-hosted formats instead of treating its destination site as the only meaningful audience surface.

## Current Synthesis
The sources frame distributed publishing as both opportunity and dependency risk. Facebook, Google, Snapchat, and similar platforms concentrate audience attention, so publishers can reach more people by putting content directly where those users already are. Evans adds that each channel is simultaneously an acquisition model and a content model: format, discovery, audience data, advertising, and measurement change together. The tradeoff is loss of control and uncertainty around monetization, which is why [[BleacherReport]] pairs the strategy with channel hedging and advertiser conversations about platform-native branded content.

## Key Claims
- Platform-hosted content can expand audience reach when users spend more time inside social and mobile platforms than on publisher homepages.
- Distributed publishing requires accepting weaker traditional site metrics while building broader brand reach.
- Publishers should hedge across channels rather than depend completely on one platform.
- Owned relationships still matter, but they coexist with search, social, apps, and direct platform distribution.
- Monetization must be designed for content that lives inside external platforms, not only for traffic returned to a publisher site.
- Each distribution surface favors its own format, editorial behavior, analytics, and ad unit rather than acting as a neutral copy of the web.

## Evidence
- Platform adoption: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says publishers such as BuzzFeed and Bleacher Report moved content directly onto Facebook, Google, and Snapchat surfaces.
- Strategic tradeoff: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says Bleacher Report was willing to sacrifice some traditional audience metrics to build a healthier brand and future revenue strategy.
- Hedging logic: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says Bleacher Report avoids going all in on one audience channel and uses search, social, apps, and external platforms.
- Facebook use case: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says Facebook was becoming a video and Instant Articles distribution surface, not mainly a referral source.
- Coupled models: [[video-is-the-new-html-benedict-evans]] says platforms differ in how they acquire users, what content works, what creators can make, what audience data is available, and how money is earned.
- Historical mix: [[video-is-the-new-html-benedict-evans]] retains a BuzzFeed Q3 2015 chart in which Facebook video, direct traffic, and Snapchat supplied 27%, 23%, and 21% of views respectively.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not prove that distributed reach converts to durable profit, audience ownership, or negotiating power. Both capture a 2016 platform moment; the BuzzFeed chart is one publisher's historical snapshot, and later changes to algorithms, video economics, Instant Articles, Discover, and publisher monetization could alter the strategy's payoff.

## What Changed

- Added the claim that distribution channel, content format, analytics, and monetization change as one platform-specific system.
