## Definition
[[DistributedPublishingStrategy]] is a media operating model in which a publisher creates, adapts, and monetizes content across owned sites, apps, search, social feeds, and platform-hosted formats instead of treating its destination site as the only meaningful audience surface.

## Current Synthesis
The sources frame distributed publishing as both an operating system and a dependency risk. Facebook, Google, Snapchat, YouTube, and similar platforms concentrate audience attention, so publishers reach people by creating directly for those surfaces rather than merely syndicating links back to a site. Evans argues that each channel couples acquisition, format, data, advertising, and measurement; BuzzFeed makes that concrete by choosing short video for reach, longer video for watch time, and live programming for election coverage across a network of more than 30 platforms. The tradeoff is loss of control and uncertain monetization, which is why [[BleacherReport]] emphasizes channel hedging and BuzzFeed's later strategy demands better platform payment.

## Key Claims
- Platform-hosted content can expand audience reach when users spend more time inside social and mobile platforms than on publisher homepages.
- Distributed publishing requires accepting weaker traditional site metrics while building broader brand reach.
- Publishers should hedge across channels rather than depend completely on one platform.
- Owned relationships still matter, but they coexist with search, social, apps, and direct platform distribution.
- Monetization must be designed for content that lives inside external platforms, not only for traffic returned to a publisher site.
- Each distribution surface favors its own format, editorial behavior, analytics, and ad unit rather than acting as a neutral copy of the web.
- Format length and programming style should follow the desired platform outcome, such as reach, completion, watch time, or live participation.

## Evidence
- Platform adoption: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says publishers such as BuzzFeed and Bleacher Report moved content directly onto Facebook, Google, and Snapchat surfaces.
- Strategic tradeoff: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says Bleacher Report was willing to sacrifice some traditional audience metrics to build a healthier brand and future revenue strategy.
- Hedging logic: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says Bleacher Report avoids going all in on one audience channel and uses search, social, apps, and external platforms.
- Facebook use case: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says Facebook was becoming a video and Instant Articles distribution surface, not mainly a referral source.
- Coupled models: [[video-is-the-new-html-benedict-evans]] says platforms differ in how they acquire users, what content works, what creators can make, what audience data is available, and how money is earned.
- Historical mix: [[video-is-the-new-html-benedict-evans]] retains a BuzzFeed Q3 2015 chart in which Facebook video, direct traffic, and Snapchat supplied 27%, 23%, and 21% of views respectively.
- Format-to-goal design: [[buzzfeeds-jonah-peretti-news-publishers-only-have-themselves-to-blame-for-losing-out-to-google-and-facebook-the-drum]] says BuzzFeed used roughly 40-second videos for reach and 12-minute videos for watch time.
- Platform-native programming: [[buzzfeeds-jonah-peretti-news-publishers-only-have-themselves-to-blame-for-losing-out-to-google-and-facebook-the-drum]] describes Facebook Live election programs, Snapchat Olympic content, and television development from newsroom reporting.
- Portfolio breadth: [[buzzfeeds-jonah-peretti-news-publishers-only-have-themselves-to-blame-for-losing-out-to-google-and-facebook-the-drum]] says BuzzFeed operated on more than 30 social platforms rather than depending on one distribution surface.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources do not prove that distributed reach converts to durable profit, audience ownership, or negotiating power. They capture a 2015-2017 platform moment; the BuzzFeed figures are company-reported historical snapshots, and later changes to algorithms, video economics, Instant Articles, Discover, live video, and publisher monetization could alter the strategy's payoff. Publishing across many platforms diversifies channel exposure but can also multiply production cost and dependency surfaces.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Bleacher Report's content-everywhere strategy.
- Added the claim that distribution channel, content format, analytics, and monetization change as one platform-specific system.
- Added goal-specific video length, live programming, and multi-platform breadth as concrete distributed-publishing practices.
