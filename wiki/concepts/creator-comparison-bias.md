Creator comparison bias appears when someone wants to make something but evaluates the opportunity only against the visible creators in their feed. The seanwes tv source calls this a pseudo reality: people follow the makers they admire, see the best work repeatedly, and then mistake that stream for a representative sample of the whole world.

The distortion is that non-creators are mostly invisible. A person sees the minority who publish, perform, teach, record, or sell, but not the much larger group that only consumes. The resulting comparison can make a creative field feel impossibly crowded even when most people are not contributing to it.

The source's practical move is to change the reference class. Instead of asking whether one can compete with the most impressive visible examples, the creator should notice that wanting to produce at all already places them in a smaller group. The decision to start should be based on the value they can offer, not on a feed-shaped impression that everyone else is already doing it.

## Signals

- The creator mostly sees people who already publish high-quality work.
- The field feels saturated because passive consumers are absent from view.
- Doubt comes from comparing against admired creators rather than potential audience needs.
- The person treats desire to create as less important than the fact that someone else already creates.
