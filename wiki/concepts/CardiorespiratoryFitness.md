## Definition
[[CardiorespiratoryFitness]] is the body's capacity to support sustained physical work, represented in the Andrew Chen source by age-related [[VO2Max]] curves and practical activity thresholds.

## Current Synthesis
The source frames cardiorespiratory fitness as future functional margin. In the inspected chart, low, average, and high fitness curves all decline from young adulthood into older age, but the curves cross everyday activity thresholds at different points. Chen's interpretation is stark: if someone wants to briskly climb stairs at 75, being merely average may not leave enough capacity, while high fitness preserves a larger buffer. The source therefore treats endurance training as a longevity behavior because it may protect ordinary mobility later, not because it only improves athletic performance now.

## Key Claims
- Cardiorespiratory fitness matters because daily activities require minimum capacity thresholds.
- Higher percentile fitness creates a larger age-related buffer before those thresholds become difficult.
- Average or low fitness in midlife may not be easy to transform into elite-level capacity in old age.
- Personal behavior change can come from seeing future functional tasks mapped against present fitness reserve.

## Evidence
- Functional thresholds: [[andrew-chen-fun-graph-from-peter-attias-book-outlive]] shows brisk stair climbing, jogging, hill walking, and carrying weight upstairs as lines across the VO2 max scale.
- Age buffer: [[andrew-chen-fun-graph-from-peter-attias-book-outlive]] shows the 95th-percentile curve staying above the brisk-stair-climbing threshold at age 75 while average and low curves fall much lower.
- Behavioral inference: [[andrew-chen-fun-graph-from-peter-attias-book-outlive]] says Chen resumed running and added Peloton or outdoor cycling because the chart made the future decline concrete.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not prove that every individual must reach the 95th percentile, nor does it address strength, balance, injury history, disease, disability, or individualized training plans. It uses a single chart to motivate capacity-building, so the claim should remain a practical heuristic rather than a full longevity model.

## What Changed
- Created cardiorespiratory fitness as a longevity-related capacity-reserve concept.
