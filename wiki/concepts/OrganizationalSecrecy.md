## Definition
[[OrganizationalSecrecy]] is the deliberate restriction or compartmentalization of information inside or around an organization; it becomes a governance risk when it prevents important technical claims from being challenged, verified, or corrected.

## Current Synthesis
The Theranos teaser supplies a narrow failure case: secrecy can buy time for leaders who believe a presently unsupported technology will eventually work, but it can also separate external claims from internal expert judgment. The source does not show the controls, incentives, threats, access boundaries, or events through which secrecy operated, so the mechanism remains an editorial proposition rather than a documented process in this capture. Secrecy is not inherently improper—privacy, security, legal privilege, and legitimate trade-secret protection require bounded confidentiality—but those purposes differ from suppressing validation of consequential claims.

## Key Claims
- Secrecy can prolong a gap between an organization's public claims and expert assessment of its actual capabilities.
- Hope of future technical success can motivate continued concealment rather than prompt correction of present claims.
- Leader control over information can weaken technical challenge and organizational error correction.
- Legitimate confidentiality must be distinguished from secrecy that blocks accountable verification.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The available evidence is one publication's standfirst, not the investigative article it summarizes. It supports neither a general causal estimate about secrecy nor a detailed Theranos mechanism. Organizations also need legitimate confidentiality for personal data, security, negotiations, legal obligations, and intellectual property; the governance question is whether scope, access, independent review, and escalation preserve accountability for consequential claims.

## What Changed
- Established a source-scoped failure model linking secrecy, disputed technical claims, and delayed correction.
- Added a boundary between harmful concealment and legitimate bounded confidentiality.
