## Definition
[[ObjectWithdrawal]] is the claim that objects always retain an inaccessible dimension that is not exhausted by use, perception, theory, or any relation.

## Current Synthesis
The article introduces withdrawal through [[MartinHeidegger]]'s hammer: a tool can disappear into smooth practice while it works, then reveal another dimension when it breaks. [[GrahamHarman]] generalizes this into an object-oriented thesis: the real object withdraws not only from human consciousness but from all relations. The result is not pure unknowability; breakdown, art, and metaphor can make hidden aspects felt, but they never capture the object completely.

## Key Claims
- Smooth tool use can make an object functionally transparent.
- Breakdown makes a previously backgrounded object stand out and resist reduction to use.
- Withdrawal applies to both theoretical observation and practical engagement.
- Objects withdraw from other objects as well as from humans.
- Aesthetic experiences can disclose aspects of withdrawn objects without exhausting them.

## Evidence
- Tool transparency: [[cong-hai-de-ge-er-de-chui-zi-dao-yin-yu-de-xi-ju-xing]] describes the hammer as transparent during skilled use.
- Breakdown disclosure: [[cong-hai-de-ge-er-de-chui-zi-dao-yin-yu-de-xi-ju-xing]] uses a broken table leg and Heidegger's damaged hammer to show an object becoming newly salient.
- Generalized withdrawal: [[cong-hai-de-ge-er-de-chui-zi-dao-yin-yu-de-xi-ju-xing]] says objects cannot be exhausted by relations with humans or other objects.
- Aesthetic limit: [[cong-hai-de-ge-er-de-chui-zi-dao-yin-yu-de-xi-ju-xing]] says metaphor can illuminate only a corner of the named object, which continues to withdraw.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source presents withdrawal sympathetically and does not include critiques from rival ontology, phenomenology, or analytic aesthetics. It also relies on illustrative examples rather than empirical or formal argument.
