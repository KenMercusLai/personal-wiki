## Definition
[[RemoteDesignSprint]] is a distributed adaptation of a [[DesignSprint]] that replaces co-located artifacts and conversation with shared digital workspaces, explicit asynchronous context, and deliberate alignment checkpoints.

## Current Synthesis
The Oak case preserves familiar sprint moves—expert interview, “How Might We” notes, goal and questions, mapping, lightning demos, concept sketches, voting, and prototyping—but changes the coordination layer. RealtimeBoard kept notes, votes, and storyboards visible across Berlin and San Francisco; narrated videos and prototype variants conveyed rationale and preferences; extra check-ins reduced the risk that unclear artifacts would compound across time zones. The case supports remote feasibility, not equivalence to a canonical five-day tested sprint: it ran within a four-week design engagement, expanded scope, and reports client alignment without describing representative-user testing.

## Key Claims
- Shared visual workspaces can preserve one inspectable project state across locations and time zones.
- Remote notes need enough context to remain understandable when their author is unavailable.
- Repeated alignment checks are a control against distributed misunderstanding, not mere status meetings.
- Inspiration examples, voting, narrated feedback, and prototype variants can externalize subjective design judgment.
- Remote delivery does not justify uncontrolled scope growth or substituting client approval for user evidence.

## Evidence
- Shared state: [[from-idea-to-app-store-a-design-sprint-case-study-ux-planet]] describes RealtimeBoard holding virtual notes, votes, and sprint material that both client and team could update.
- Explicit context: [[from-idea-to-app-store-a-design-sprint-case-study-ux-planet]] recommends unusually clear “How Might We” notes because remote readers may lack immediate explanation.
- Alignment mechanism: [[from-idea-to-app-store-a-design-sprint-case-study-ux-planet]] reports concept voting, narrated sketch feedback, several animated prototypes, and first-week client buy-in.
- Scope and evidence boundary: [[from-idea-to-app-store-a-design-sprint-case-study-ux-planet]] reports a four-week handoff, warns against its own scope expansion, and does not document user testing.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one successful agency-authored project involving an engaged founder and a team experienced enough to absorb major scope expansion. It does not compare remote and co-located sprints, measure misunderstanding or cycle time, specify asynchronous accessibility, document representative-user research, or show that its tool stack remains current. Launch recognition cannot isolate the effect of the sprint from implementation, the founder's audience, App Store curation, or other product changes.

- Created the concept around the coordination adaptations and evidence boundaries shown by the Oak case.
