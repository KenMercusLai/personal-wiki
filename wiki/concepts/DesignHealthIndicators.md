## Definition
[[DesignHealthIndicators]] are paired observations of design-team morale and current product quality used to infer an organization's present design conditions, likely direction, and appropriate intervention.

## Current Synthesis
[[IreneAu]] distinguishes product quality as accumulated output from morale as an earlier view of whether designers expect future work to improve. Product quality reflects prior strategy, scope, decisions, process, talent, and implementation. Morale reflects whether designers experience clear direction, meaningful influence, sufficient capacity, workable process, pride, and room to iterate. Morale also has a lagging aspect because those feelings respond to accumulated company behavior.

The useful unit is the combination, not either signal alone. Negative morale and poor quality suggest that weak direction, authority, or process may be affecting both current and future work. Positive morale with poor quality can describe a credible turnaround before it appears in shipped output. Negative morale with good quality warns that a mature product may conceal stagnation or an overloaded team. Positive morale with good quality indicates a healthier system whose next challenge is preserving coherence and opportunity as it scales.

Each quadrant implies a hypothesis to investigate, not a verdict. Leadership responses range from clarifying vision and decision paths, through empowering design during a pivot, to focusing scarce designers, funding new bets, or building principles and shared infrastructure. Designers can also use the matrix to look beyond surface polish when evaluating jobs and decide whether the underlying challenge suits them.

## Key Claims
- Product quality and team morale should be assessed together because either one alone can misstate the organization's direction.
- Current product quality is primarily a lagging signal of earlier cross-functional choices rather than a direct measure of present design capability.
- Design-team morale can act as a leading signal when designers have early visibility into unclear direction, weak authority, overload, stalled innovation, or improving support.
- Poor quality with positive morale can be a turnaround state, while good quality with negative morale can be a deterioration state.
- The four states require different responses across vision, empowerment, process, capacity, innovation, and design infrastructure.
- The matrix supports structured inquiry but does not establish causes, predict outcomes by itself, or justify treating a whole company as homogeneous.

## Evidence
- Signal roles: [[ux-is-a-canary-in-a-coal-mine-irene-au]] identifies morale as a leading design-health indicator and product quality as a lagging one.
- Poor quality states: [[ux-is-a-canary-in-a-coal-mine-irene-au]] associates negative morale with unclear vision and weak process, but positive morale with a pivot and increasing design influence.
- Good quality states: [[ux-is-a-canary-in-a-coal-mine-irene-au]] associates negative morale with maturity, stagnation, uncertain growth, or overload, while positive morale indicates direction and development opportunity.
- Intervention fit: [[ux-is-a-canary-in-a-coal-mine-irene-au]] recommends vision and design-executive alignment, sufficient exploration and iteration, aggressive prioritization, innovation capacity, and reusable design infrastructure according to the diagnosed state.
- Decision use: [[ux-is-a-canary-in-a-coal-mine-irene-au]] gives designers a job-evaluation lens and CEOs a question set covering pride, skills, fidelity, influence, hiring, craft, iteration, and decision rights.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework comes from one 2015 practitioner essay and supplies no validated morale scale, product-quality measure, longitudinal test, sample, or comparison group. The proposed causes within each quadrant are plausible alternatives rather than identified mechanisms: poor quality may reflect technical debt, regulation, capital limits, market mismatch, or deliberate tradeoffs, while low morale may reflect compensation, management, interpersonal safety, workload, or recent change. Morale can be both leading and lagging, and product quality can contain immediate as well as historical information. Company-level classification can conceal variation across teams, products, disciplines, tenure groups, and locations, so the matrix should begin investigation rather than close it.

## What Changed
- Created a two-signal design-health model that separates current output from likely direction.
- Preserved all four morale-and-quality states and their distinct intervention hypotheses.
- Bounded the framework as a diagnostic prompt without demonstrated predictive validity or causal identification.
