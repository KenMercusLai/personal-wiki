## Definition
[[ManagementRoleFit]] is the alignment between a person's motivation and capability and the work of enabling a team through mentoring, feedback, staffing, cohesion, conflict handling, resources, and organizational context.

## Current Synthesis
Management is a different job, not merely a reward for excellent individual contribution. The source contrasts enjoyment of producing excellent work with enjoyment of helping other people grow and succeed. A promotion is therefore healthy only when the candidate wants and can perform the manager's actual responsibilities, rather than seeking the role because it is the sole path to greater influence or compensation.

The downside of a mismatch is asymmetric. Moving a strong IC into management can remove valuable execution capacity and install weak management whose decisions and behavior affect several people. A dual-track system reduces that pressure by letting contributors progress economically and organizationally while making management a role selected for fit.

## Key Claims
- Excellence as an individual contributor does not establish aptitude or motivation for people management.
- Interest in management can be contaminated by promotion incentives when no equally credible IC path exists.
- People-management work centers on enabling others through team health, mentoring, reviews, conflict resolution, staffing, resources, and coordination.
- Mis-promoting a strong IC can create two losses: reduced contribution and management harm distributed across a team.
- Management and contribution can overlap, especially for junior managers and senior ICs, without making them the same role.
- Managers should create purpose and enabling conditions while preserving meaningful contributor autonomy over execution.

## Evidence
- Motivation mismatch: [[if-management-is-the-only-way-up-were-all-fd-sparktoro]] opens with a capable writer distressed by a proposed promotion because she wanted to keep writing rather than supervise people.
- Role distinction: [[if-management-is-the-only-way-up-were-all-fd-sparktoro]] contrasts direct creation with responsibility for team happiness, cohesion, empowerment, mentoring, reviews, and support.
- Distributed downside: [[if-management-is-the-only-way-up-were-all-fd-sparktoro]] argues that a mediocre manager spreads problems more widely than an underperforming individual contributor.
- Autonomy boundary: [[if-management-is-the-only-way-up-were-all-fd-sparktoro]] proposes that ICs own much of what, when, where, and how, while managers focus more on who and why.
- Diagrammed responsibilities: [[if-management-is-the-only-way-up-were-all-fd-sparktoro]] visually assigns recruiting, one-on-ones, mentoring, reviews, team emotional health, conflict resolution, collaboration, organization, and budget requests to people wrangling.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The role distinction is normative and based on selected Moz examples rather than comparative evidence. Effective managers sometimes need deep hands-on knowledge, and senior ICs often mentor, give feedback, shape teams, or define strategy. The proposed “who and why” versus “what, when, where, and how” split can oversimplify shared product and organizational decisions. Role fit can also change with experience, life stage, team design, support, and training; it should not become a permanent personality label.

## What Changed
- Created a fit-based distinction between promotion as recognition and management as a separate enabling profession.
- Made the double cost of a poor IC-to-manager promotion explicit.
- Preserved responsibility overlap and the possibility that fit changes over time.
