## Definition
[[JobsToBeDone]] is a research and innovation lens that interprets a product or service choice as an attempt to make progress toward a goal or aspiration within particular circumstances.

## Current Synthesis
The framework shifts attention from who a customer is or which product attributes they mention toward the progress they are trying to make and the forces shaping a specific decision. Its hiring metaphor says that people and organizations pull products or services into their lives when a job arises. A job includes functional requirements alongside social and emotional dimensions, and it can change as circumstances change. Research therefore seeks the story around a choice and recurring circumstances across people rather than treating a direct answer to “why?” as a complete causal explanation.

A layered market interpretation extends this account: competition can overserve the immediate job with features, expense, or saturation while an underlying higher-order purpose remains underserved because a separate resource constraint blocks it. In [[EmergentLayerTheory]], an abstraction that removes that constraint can let an entrant serve the higher-order job directly. Tesla's identity-signaling example makes the social and emotional dimensions explicit, while Uber, WhatsApp, Medium, Google, and the iPhone are broader illustrations. This extension generates hypotheses about disruption but does not establish that jobs fall into a stable hierarchy or that the proposed abstraction caused the companies' growth.

## Key Claims
- Customers use products and services as means of making desired progress in a circumstance.
- A job combines functional, social, and emotional forces.
- The same person or organization may prioritize different jobs when circumstances change.
- Decision stories and recurring circumstances can expose needs that demographics, attributes, or surface explanations miss, including a higher-order purpose hidden beneath an overserved immediate job.
- JTBD can be applied to organizational and social-sector choices as well as consumer purchases.

## Evidence
- Progress and hiring metaphor: [[christensen-institute-jobs-to-be-done-theory]] says people pull products or services into their lives to make progress when jobs arise.
- Multiple dimensions: [[christensen-institute-jobs-to-be-done-theory]] uses a growing family's search for a larger home to distinguish functional space needs, social family and neighborhood concerns, and emotional caretaking or achievement.
- Circumstance change: [[christensen-institute-jobs-to-be-done-theory]] describes a school district's job shifting from state-funding compliance toward being seen as an innovative leader as experience and teacher approval grow.
- Decision-story research: [[christensen-institute-jobs-to-be-done-theory]] contrasts flavor-focused milkshake answers with a circumstance in which an easy-to-hold snack helps relieve stress.
- Wider applications: [[christensen-institute-jobs-to-be-done-theory]] names Nigerian solar adoption, health-care needs, and movement toward microschools as institute use cases.
- Layered jobs and constraints: [[emergent-layers-chapter-3-explosive-growth-the-startup-medium]] argues that customers can be overserved at one job level while a resource constraint prevents direct service of a higher-order job.
- Social and functional illustrations: [[emergent-layers-chapter-3-explosive-growth-the-startup-medium]] interprets Tesla through identity signaling, Uber through transport and earning, and Medium through readership and discovery.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Both sources are advocacy or strategy-oriented explanations rather than comparative evaluations. Their examples do not disclose interview questions, sampling, analysis procedures, alternative explanations, failed cases, or measured innovation outcomes. The framework's resistance to demographic and attribute-first explanations should not be read as evidence that those variables are irrelevant; they may encode material constraints, access, preference, or risk. Danco's lower-order/higher-order language is also interpretive: the source provides no method for establishing a hierarchy, separating constraint removal from distribution or capital, or testing whether a selected job caused growth.

## What Changed
- Added the distinction between an overserved immediate job and an underserved higher-order purpose blocked by a separate constraint.
- Qualified the job hierarchy and company cases as hypotheses rather than causal validation.
