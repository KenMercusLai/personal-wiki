## Definition
[[JobsToBeDone]] is a research and innovation lens that interprets a product or service choice as an attempt to make progress toward a goal or aspiration within particular circumstances.

## Current Synthesis
The framework shifts attention from who a customer is or which product attributes they mention toward the progress they are trying to make and the forces shaping a specific decision. Its hiring metaphor says that people and organizations pull products or services into their lives when a job arises. A job includes functional requirements alongside social and emotional dimensions, and it can change as circumstances change. Research therefore seeks the story around a choice and recurring circumstances across people rather than treating a direct answer to “why?” as a complete causal explanation. The source illustrates this with home selection, school-district technology, milkshakes, solar adoption, health care, and microschools, but it does not present a replicable research protocol or comparative evidence that the lens improves outcomes.

## Key Claims
- Customers use products and services as means of making desired progress in a circumstance.
- A job combines functional, social, and emotional forces.
- The same person or organization may prioritize different jobs when circumstances change.
- Decision stories and recurring circumstances can expose needs that demographics, attributes, or surface explanations miss.
- JTBD can be applied to organizational and social-sector choices as well as consumer purchases.

## Evidence
- Progress and hiring metaphor: [[christensen-institute-jobs-to-be-done-theory]] says people pull products or services into their lives to make progress when jobs arise.
- Multiple dimensions: [[christensen-institute-jobs-to-be-done-theory]] uses a growing family's search for a larger home to distinguish functional space needs, social family and neighborhood concerns, and emotional caretaking or achievement.
- Circumstance change: [[christensen-institute-jobs-to-be-done-theory]] describes a school district's job shifting from state-funding compliance toward being seen as an innovative leader as experience and teacher approval grow.
- Decision-story research: [[christensen-institute-jobs-to-be-done-theory]] contrasts flavor-focused milkshake answers with a circumstance in which an easy-to-hold snack helps relieve stress.
- Wider applications: [[christensen-institute-jobs-to-be-done-theory]] names Nigerian solar adoption, health-care needs, and movement toward microschools as institute use cases.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is an advocacy-oriented overview rather than a comparative evaluation. Its short examples do not disclose interview questions, sampling, analysis procedures, alternative explanations, failed cases, or measured innovation outcomes. The framework's resistance to demographic and attribute-first explanations should not be read as evidence that those variables are irrelevant; they may encode material constraints, access, preference, or risk. The milkshake, school, housing, and application examples illustrate possible interpretations but do not establish causal generality.
