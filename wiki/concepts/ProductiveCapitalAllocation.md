## Definition
[[ProductiveCapitalAllocation]] is the redeployment of cash from businesses with strong returns but limited internal reinvestment capacity into lower-percentage-return opportunities capable of absorbing much more capital and producing greater absolute profit.

## Current Synthesis
[[the-google-capital-company]] separates relative return from absolute productive scale. See's Candies exemplifies a business that earns exceptional returns on little operating capital but cannot sensibly reinvest all its profit in more candy stores. BNSF exemplifies a business that consumes billions in capital yet can produce more annual profit because its useful asset base and market are much larger. [[BerkshireHathaway]] creates value by moving cash between these businesses rather than demanding that each one compound internally.

The source applies the same model to [[Alphabet]]. Google Services is the high-margin cash engine; Google Cloud and AI compute have lower reported margins but faster growth and a potential market extending beyond advertising into economy-wide computation. Services, Gemini, external capacity sales, and TPUs provide multiple paths for productive use. Under genuine compute scarcity, the feedback loop can compound: cash buys capacity, capacity generates more cash, and that cash secures more capacity. The mechanism only creates value if durable demand and returns ultimately justify dilution, depreciation, and construction risk.

## Key Claims
- Percentage return on invested capital and total profit answer different questions about business quality and capital deployment.
- A high-return business with limited reinvestment runway can create more value by funding a larger productive opportunity elsewhere.
- Corporate structure matters because it lets cash cross business boundaries without forcing local reinvestment or immediate shareholder distribution.
- Multiple internal and external uses for one asset base can improve the odds that capital remains productive across uncertain market outcomes.
- When a scarce strategic input is allocated by willingness and ability to pay, cash-generation capacity can become a compounding competitive advantage.
- Productive scale does not excuse weak returns; equity dilution, debt service, depreciation, utilization, and residual value remain decisive.

## Evidence
- Cross-business redeployment: [[the-google-capital-company]] reports that See's sent nearly all profit beyond modest operating-capital needs to Berkshire, which used cash to buy other businesses.
- Relative-versus-absolute return: [[the-google-capital-company]] contrasts See's high pre-tax return on capital with BNSF's far greater annual capital requirement and absolute income.
- Alphabet application: [[the-google-capital-company]] reports Google Cloud growing faster than Google Services while reaching a lower but expanding 33% operating margin in Q1 2026.
- Multi-path utilization: [[the-google-capital-company]] identifies Services, Gemini, frontier-lab capacity sales, and TPUs as alternative returns on Google's compute investment.
- Capital feedback loop: [[the-google-capital-company]] proposes that cash capacity can secure compute capacity, which can then generate more cash for reinvestment.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
This is a source-derived strategy model, not a demonstrated causal law. Businesses differ in accounting, competitive durability, asset life, regulation, cyclicality, and financing conditions; See's, BNSF, cloud infrastructure, and AI accelerators are analogies rather than equivalent assets. High absolute profit can still destroy value when it requires excessive capital, and a large addressable market does not guarantee utilization or pricing power.

The Alphabet application depends on article-supplied segment figures, expected AI demand, claimed TPU cost advantage, and the assumption that capacity can move economically among workloads and customers. Equity issuance may reflect opportunity size or uncertainty, and multiple payoff paths can all disappoint together if the shared compute market is overbuilt. The model therefore requires realized cash returns and opportunity-cost comparison, not only construction, revenue growth, or management signaling.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from the source's See's-to-BNSF and Services-to-Cloud analogy.
- Distinguished high relative returns from large absolute productive profit.
- Added cash capacity as a possible compounding advantage under compute scarcity.
- Preserved return, utilization, depreciation, and dilution as value-creation gates.
