## Definition
[[WorkHabits]] are repeatable patterns of planning, focus, communication, task handling, and daily routine that shape how work gets done.

## Current Synthesis
The sources present habits as a practical substitute for heavyweight productivity systems and as a way to keep long-term choices embodied in daily behavior. Marr's habits cluster around priority definition, attention control, routine design, communication brevity, batching, and deciding what should be deleted or delegated. Banks adds a simpler behavioral rule: habits form through daily repetition, and long-view goals require patience rather than abandoning focus when results are slow. Slayback makes the causal sequence explicit: clear goals identify what to repeat, systems support continued participation, and progressively harder successes can build [[SelfEfficacy]]. He also argues that habits should fit disposition, so repetition supports either accomplishment or creative expression rather than becoming a generic routine imposed on everyone.

## Key Claims
- A habit of setting daily priorities anchors work before lower-value tasks take over.
- Morning routines can preserve conditions that support stronger work performance.
- Concise writing is treated as a work habit because it reduces communication overhead.
- Batching similar tasks protects workflow from repeated interruptions.
- Deleting and delegating are habits that keep personal work focused on what actually needs the worker's attention.
- Daily repetition and patience help important goals survive the period before results arrive.
- Habit systems should connect clear goals to manageable repeated action, fit the person's disposition, and use progressive success to build self-efficacy.

## Evidence
- Priority setting and routines: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] recommends daily MIT selection and personally tailored morning, afternoon, or evening routines.
- Communication discipline: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] advises brief reports and emails that state the point directly.
- Batch processing: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] recommends handling similar messages or tasks at planned times to avoid breaking flow.
- Workload shaping: [[tuimo-10-timeless-work-habits-to-boost-productivity]] frames deletion, tools, and delegation as ways to remove work that does not require direct personal involvement.
- Daily repetition: [[blog-ethan-banks-career-advice-id-give-to-20-30-and-40-something-year-old-me]] says good habits depend on not skipping days.
- Long-view focus: [[blog-ethan-banks-career-advice-id-give-to-20-30-and-40-something-year-old-me]] advises patience when goals are not quickly achievable rather than shifting focus too soon.
- Goal-to-habit system: [[zak-slayback-you-have-to-show-up-every-day]] argues that clear goals need systems that make relevant action repeatable.
- Personality fit and efficacy: [[zak-slayback-you-have-to-show-up-every-day]] recommends habits suited to disposition and links progressively harder successes to self-efficacy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources assume the worker has enough autonomy to choose routines, batch communication, work offline, delegate, or protect daily repetition. Those habits may be harder in roles with externally imposed response times, limited authority, shift work, caregiving demands, disability, or unstable health. Slayback partly qualifies literal daily repetition by allowing psychological continuity without performing the same physical activity every day, but his personality categories and efficacy mechanism remain practitioner claims rather than validated prescriptions.

## What Changed
- Added goal clarity, personality fit, and progressive mastery as conditions connecting habits to self-efficacy.
