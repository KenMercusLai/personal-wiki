## Definition
[[EnvironmentalField]] is the invisible social, material, emotional, and normative environment that shapes a person's behavior before it hardens into habit, character, work style, creativity, or moral judgment.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats "field" as more than physical space. A field includes incentives, rules, peers, family history, intimate routines, cultural inputs, and repeated emotional cues. Its practical claim is double-edged: people are fragile because they are continuously shaped by their surroundings, but they are also freer than they think because they can sometimes leave, redesign, or choose the fields that shape them.

## Key Claims
- A field shapes behavior by changing what feels normal before a person consciously chooses.
- Work, family, intimacy, creativity, and morality are different domains where fields exert similar pressure.
- Choosing where to live, whom to be with, and what work to do is effectively choosing the macro-field of one's life.
- Personal agency depends partly on noticing field effects, not only on applying more willpower inside a bad field.
- A better field can make desirable behavior easier by supplying positive feedback, compatible values, and supportive norms.

## Evidence
- Normalization before choice: [[chang]] argues that rules, incentives, peers, and repeated atmospheres gradually become personality and fate.
- Cross-domain pattern: [[chang]] applies the field idea to work incentives, family-of-origin programming, partner selection, creative receptivity, and moral behavior under scarcity or role pressure.
- Macro-life choices: [[chang]] cites the importance of where one lives, whom one is with, and what one does as large-scale field selection.
- Agency through migration: [[chang]] uses the idea of "moving" from one field to another to argue that leaving unsuitable environments can be an act of self-responsibility.
- Positive feedback: [[chang]] contrasts draining fields with teams, relationships, and creative spaces that make growth or creation more likely.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is essayistic and does not establish a general empirical theory of environmental causation. Its emphasis on choosing fields can understate constraints such as money, family obligation, geography, labor markets, discrimination, or care responsibilities. It also does not deny individual agency; instead, it argues that agency often begins with choosing or redesigning the environment in which agency is exercised.
