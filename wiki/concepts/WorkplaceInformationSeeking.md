## Definition
[[WorkplaceInformationSeeking]] is the deliberate practice of designing, maintaining, and using information flows needed for one's work instead of waiting for relevant context to arrive automatically.

## Current Synthesis
Bosworth treats listening as an operating system rather than a conversational manner. The worker chooses channels, assigns each a review cadence and depth, removes low-value inputs, and gives feedback when a channel could carry more useful signal. The same system looks beyond existing feeds: map dependencies, find people or projects with no reliable update path, request new channels, and record surprising observations for follow-up when they conflict with the current [[MentalModels|mental model]].

Information seeking is also reciprocal. Telling others what one is doing gives them a basis for forwarding relevant context, while explicit callbacks turn a cross-team conversation into a future information contract. Receptive listening completes the loop: an abrasive, incomplete, or weakly evidenced message may still contain a useful signal, so defensiveness should be examined before the content is discarded. The resulting discipline is active but bounded—ownership of information flow includes pruning and prioritization, not indiscriminate consumption.

## Key Claims
- Relevant workplace information rarely arrives completely or automatically, so acquiring it is part of doing the job.
- Channel choice, cadence, and depth should be designed around likely value and attention cost.
- Dependency mapping can expose missing information paths that existing inboxes and groups cannot reveal.
- Surprise is a diagnostic signal that should trigger follow-up when new information conflicts with a working model.
- Outbound context and explicit callbacks make future inbound communication more likely and more relevant.
- Useful listening requires separating a message's substantive signal from the discomfort, rhetoric, or incompleteness of its delivery.

## Evidence
- Ownership and system design: [[boz-listening-is-the-job]] says workers should take responsibility for obtaining necessary information and define which channels they consume, how often, and in how much depth.
- Signal management: [[boz-listening-is-the-job]] recommends removing low-value groups and mailing lists or reviewing them less often when their expected signal does not justify their cost.
- Gap discovery: [[boz-listening-is-the-job]] recommends mapping dependent people and projects, checking for regular information sources, and lobbying for missing channels.
- Model repair: [[boz-listening-is-the-job]] describes recording surprising or inconsistent observations for later investigation rather than letting them pass.
- Reciprocal flow: [[boz-listening-is-the-job]] argues that communicating one's own story helps others recognize relevant information and recommends explicit callback agreements across teams.
- Receptive interpretation: [[boz-listening-is-the-job]] asks listeners to inspect defensive reactions and search imperfect messages for useful feedback.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is a single senior leader's 2017 practitioner essay from Facebook, not a comparison of information systems or measured work outcomes. Reading every message may be feasible because of unusual role authority, channel design, or support and can become surveillance, interruption, unpaid coordination work, or overload in other settings. Channel pruning can also remove weak but important signals, while non-defensive listening must not become an obligation to tolerate abuse, discrimination, coercion, or unsupported accusations. Explicit callbacks work only when partners share definitions, trust, authority, and follow-through; sensitive information may require access controls, confidentiality, escalation, or deliberate non-disclosure.

## What Changed
- Established information-flow ownership as a system combining channel design, gap mapping, surprise-driven follow-up, reciprocal signaling, callbacks, and receptive listening.
