## Definition
[[CEOScalingRole]] is the evolution of a founder or CEO's work from direct doing and individual problem solving toward strategy, culture, senior hiring, coaching, communication, and removing obstacles for the organization.

## Current Synthesis
The sources present CEO work as highly stage-dependent. In the earliest phase, the CEO does everything. As the company scales, direct execution becomes a bottleneck: the CEO must set direction, select senior leaders, manage culture, clear paths for product teams, coach people to solve their own problems, and preserve product sense without trying to personally do all product work. The Thumbtack source sharpens the senior-leadership selection part: CEOs may be evaluating executives whose functional expertise exceeds their own, so they need a process that combines role focus, references, fit assessment, and candid reciprocal feedback.

## Key Claims
- CEOs at scale manage chaos and organize the environment around key product and engineering talent.
- CEO responsibilities concentrate around strategy, culture, senior management selection, and sometimes product.
- Scaling requires moving from solving problems for people to coaching people who can coach others.
- Product sense remains valuable because technology companies create value through products.
- CEOs must eventually stop doing too much hands-on work or they can burn out and damage execution.
- CEOs need humility and process when hiring executives in functions they cannot personally evaluate deeply.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources gather advice from prominent Silicon Valley operators and one founder interview, so the CEO model is product-company and venture-growth weighted. Founder-led service businesses, regulated companies, or scientific/hardware firms may require different balances of technical depth, sales, capital markets, compliance, operations, or board governance.
