## Definition
[[InternetOfThingsData]] is the data and system-redesign opportunity created when cheap networked sensors become ubiquitous through smartphone-scale component supply chains.

## Current Synthesis
The sources argue that IoT should not be treated as one neat category defined by a universal standard. Networked sensors can become common and forgettable, like electric motors, because smartphone-scale supply chains make components cheap and diverse. The Chick-fil-A case shows the operational step beyond collection: combine equipment and transaction signals with a cloud forecast, make a time-sensitive decision locally, and use the result to guide people or automation. Strategic value therefore depends on a complete loop from sensing through interpretation to action, not merely on deploying connected devices.

## Key Claims
- Network connection will become too common to define a coherent product category.
- Smartphone supply chains make sensors and components cheap enough to become ubiquitous.
- IoT standards talk can miss the point because use cases and platforms will fragment.
- Enterprise value comes from new data surfaces that enable system redesign.
- Operational value increases when live local signals modify forecasts and drive timely action near the physical process.

## Evidence
- Category critique: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] compares networked devices to electric motors: common enough to stop feeling like a category.
- Supply-chain driver: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] says smartphone supply chains make sensors and components cheap and ubiquitous.
- Standards qualification: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] argues that "connected to a network" is not one category and will have many standards.
- Enterprise data: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] says sensor explosion creates data explosion, especially in enterprises, enabling fundamental system redesign.
- Local data fusion: [[edge-computing-at-chick-fil-a-chick-fil-a-tech-blog-medium]] combines point-of-sale keystrokes and fryer inventory with cloud-generated demand forecasts.
- Action loop: [[edge-computing-at-chick-fil-a-chick-fil-a-tech-blog-medium]] uses the adjusted forecast to guide food production and anticipates future automation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The broad IoT thesis does not evaluate privacy, security, interoperability cost, data quality, organizational readiness, or failed sensor deployments. The Chick-fil-A evidence is a company-authored 2018 architecture case; it demonstrates one intended workflow but does not independently establish forecast accuracy, business impact, or completion of the projected device rollout.

## What Changed

- Added a restaurant case that closes the loop from sensing and cloud forecasting to local guidance or automation.
