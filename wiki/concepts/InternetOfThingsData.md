## Definition
[[InternetOfThingsData]] is the data and system-redesign opportunity created when cheap networked sensors become ubiquitous through smartphone-scale component supply chains.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that IoT should not be treated as one neat category defined by a universal standard. Instead, networked sensors will become common and forgettable, like electric motors, because the smartphone supply chain makes components cheap and diverse. The strategic significance is especially strong in enterprise settings: more sensors mean more behavioral and operational data, which can support redesigning systems rather than merely adding gadgets.

## Key Claims
- Network connection will become too common to define a coherent product category.
- Smartphone supply chains make sensors and components cheap enough to become ubiquitous.
- IoT standards talk can miss the point because use cases and platforms will fragment.
- Enterprise value comes from new data surfaces that enable system redesign.

## Evidence
- Category critique: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] compares networked devices to electric motors: common enough to stop feeling like a category.
- Supply-chain driver: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] says smartphone supply chains make sensors and components cheap and ubiquitous.
- Standards qualification: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] argues that "connected to a network" is not one category and will have many standards.
- Enterprise data: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] says sensor explosion creates data explosion, especially in enterprises, enabling fundamental system redesign.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source sketches the IoT data thesis but does not evaluate privacy, security, interoperability cost, data quality, organizational readiness, or failed sensor deployments.
