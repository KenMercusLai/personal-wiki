## Definition
[[ResultsEconomy]] is the source's model of work in which compensation and survival depend primarily on produced value and outcomes rather than elapsed time, visible effort, or rule compliance.

## Current Synthesis
The concept, attributed to [[DanSullivan]] and amplified by [[BenjaminHardy]], distinguishes entrepreneurs' direct dependence on revenue-producing customer value from a “time-and-effort economy” that can reward busyness while shielding workers from outcomes. Its durable insight is that effort and value are not identical, so work systems need explicit outcomes and feedback. Its categorical form is too strong: many outcomes are delayed, collective, uncertain, preventive, or hard to measure, and direct performance pay can invite gaming, unsafe risk, short-termism, or punishment for factors outside a worker's control.

## Key Claims
- Time and effort can vary independently from useful results.
- Entrepreneurs face comparatively direct feedback because revenue depends on creating value for customers.
- Salaried employees can still contribute inside a results-dependent business even when their income is less immediately exposed.
- Explicit outcomes can reduce status-seeking, rule-following, and busyness that do not advance the work.
- Performance-based compensation is one possible alignment mechanism, not proof of courage, value, or eventual wealth.

## Evidence
- Effort-result distinction: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] quotes Sullivan observing that long effort can produce no result and little effort can sometimes produce a large one.
- Entrepreneur exposure: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] says entrepreneurs lack guaranteed income and must create customer value for revenue.
- Employee boundary: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] says employees may be salaried while the employing business remains exposed to results.
- Wealth extension: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] cites Stanley for an association between affluence and ownership or incentive pay, without supplying the underlying method.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one motivational essay, not a comparison of compensation systems. Individual outcome pay is difficult where work is collaborative, preventive, exploratory, care-oriented, safety-critical, or affected by unequal territories and resources. Measured targets can displace unmeasured value and create gaming or risk transfer. Salary can support long-horizon judgment, cooperation, and psychological safety, while outcome accountability can be implemented through team goals and review without making income maximally volatile.

## What Changed
- Created a qualified results-versus-effort framework that preserves outcome accountability without treating performance pay as universally superior.
