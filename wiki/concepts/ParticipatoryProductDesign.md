## Definition
[[ParticipatoryProductDesign]] is the practice of involving intended users in forming, reviewing, and revising product ideas and interaction choices before and during implementation rather than treating them only as subjects of a finished-product test.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents participation as a response to demographic distance. When builders do not share the audience's routines, norms, or existing communication habits, school visits, persistent chat groups, mockups, wireframes, and informal conversation can make product decisions less dependent on analogy or founder instinct. [[Houseparty]] used bounded in-person cohorts to test specific room and notification mechanics, while [[MusicalLy]] maintained continuing WeChat and WhatsApp groups and discussed major features before coding.

Participation also interacts with distribution. Early users embedded in dense school relationships can recruit friends and supply behavioral context that a laboratory session misses. Yet learning and growth are not interchangeable: favorable participants can become advocates, but local enthusiasm, App Store rank, or reported minutes do not show which design choice caused adoption. The useful principle is to align participant selection and contact mode with the uncertainty being tested while keeping representativeness, power, consent, and outcome evidence explicit.

## Key Claims
- Direct participation becomes especially valuable when builders differ demographically or culturally from intended users.
- Useful participation can occur through bounded visits, continuing group conversation, mockup review, wireframe review, and observation of adjacent behavior.
- Involving users before coding can expose interaction assumptions while change is still comparatively cheap.
- Participant networks can distribute a social product as well as inform it, creating both leverage and sampling bias.
- Researchers should deliberately examine who is absent rather than treating convenient early adopters as representative.
- A product can start with a narrow participatory cohort and later support uses that cohort did not define.

## Evidence
- Demographic-distance response: [[housepartys-teenage-consultants-help-design-the-app]] says Houseparty's adult founders treated teenage norms as something to learn through trial, visits, and feedback.
- Concrete feature input: [[housepartys-teenage-consultants-help-design-the-app]] reports discussion of room capacity and presence-notification mechanisms with students.
- Pre-implementation review: [[housepartys-teenage-consultants-help-design-the-app]] describes Musical.ly presenting major ideas, mockups, and wireframes to user groups before coding.
- Continuing context: [[housepartys-teenage-consultants-help-design-the-app]] says Musical.ly used daily product and informal conversation to remain immersed in its audience's experience.
- Sampling choice: [[housepartys-teenage-consultants-help-design-the-app]] says Houseparty avoided coastal technology hubs in search of less technology-saturated teenagers.
- Distribution overlap: [[housepartys-teenage-consultants-help-design-the-app]] describes early users recruiting school friends and Elman's view that a successful learning session could also seed awareness.
- Reinterpretation: [[housepartys-teenage-consultants-help-design-the-app]] documents friends, siblings, and a parent adapting Houseparty beyond the initial teenage frame.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one favorable 2017 magazine account of two visible products. It reports no failed participatory cohorts, decision log, rejected feedback, participant attrition, comparison with non-participatory development, or causal effect on retention and revenue. Convenience recruitment through family, alumni, existing users, WeChat, and WhatsApp can amplify socially connected enthusiasts; avoiding coastal technology hubs does not by itself create a representative sample. The article also leaves compensation, informed consent, privacy, safeguarding of minors, moderation, power differences, and ownership of participant contributions unexamined. Participation can inform judgment without turning users into a vote or transferring product accountability to them.

## What Changed
- Created the concept as a pre-implementation and continuing form of target-user involvement.
- Distinguished product learning from the distribution effects of recruiting socially connected participants.
- Added demographic distance, sampling bias, minor safeguarding, and broader reinterpretation as explicit boundaries.
