## Definition
[[MetacognitiveFeedback]] is the loop through which people observe their own thinking, detect gaps or automatic reactions, and use that awareness to revise later information intake, reasoning, or action.

## Current Synthesis
The education source argues that ability growth needs to become visible before it can sustain internal motivation. Research reports, portfolios, reviews, and written analyses are records learners can revisit to compare earlier and current thinking. Wulc's two reading notes add an in-the-moment correction loop: observe a cognitive or emotional reaction, ask what belief, motive, or capability gap may be involved, select a more suitable response, and carry it into action. The first note's inspected information-flow diagram extends metacognition across input filtering, storage of concepts and values, selection of thinking methods, and behavioral output; the second applies the same principle to emotional regulation and deliberate choice. Together the sources present reflection artifacts, momentary self-monitoring, and externalized decision records as complementary feedback channels.

## Key Claims
- Cognitive growth needs an internal feedback loop, not only external project activity.
- Visible artifacts help learners recognize improvements that would otherwise remain hidden.
- Seeing one's earlier reasoning gaps can become a source of intrinsic motivation.
- Intuition, experience, and taste can be explained as trained patterns rather than mysterious gifts.
- Metacognition can interrupt automatic reactions by making the current thought process available for evaluation and replacement.
- Deliberate practice, regular reflection, and attention exercises are proposed as ways to strengthen the correction loop.
- Writing down options, criteria, and assumptions can move a choice from automatic reaction toward slower, inspectable reasoning.

## Evidence
- Internal loop: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] says PBL supplies an external training field, but learners still need to see their own change.
- Visible artifacts: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] names research reports, portfolios, and reviews as records of cognitive growth.
- Growth recognition: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] describes motivation arising when students can clearly identify weaknesses in their own earlier work.
- Trainable judgment: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] reframes intuition as automated experience and taste as trained problem perception.
- Correction sequence: [[blog-wulc-ren-zhi-hong-li-yue-du-bi-ji-1-gai-nian-zhong-su]] models metacognition as moving from an event and automatic response to questioning that response and choosing a more suitable one.
- Input-process-output control: [[blog-wulc-ren-zhi-hong-li-yue-du-bi-ji-1-gai-nian-zhong-su]] and its inspected diagram divide metacognitive intervention into filtering information, selecting concepts or methods, and aligning output with judgment.
- Practice methods: [[blog-wulc-ren-zhi-hong-li-yue-du-bi-ji-1-gai-nian-zhong-su]] recommends deliberate method use, daily review, reflective reading, and meditation as repeated opportunities to notice and redirect attention.
- Emotional intervention: [[blog-wulc-ren-zhi-hong-li-yue-du-bi-ji-2-da-nao-sheng-ji]] proposes awareness, interpretation of the motive or mismatch, and conversion toward an alternative response.
- Deliberate choice: [[blog-wulc-ren-zhi-hong-li-yue-du-bi-ji-2-da-nao-sheng-ji]] recommends activating slower thought, externalizing the comparison, using reminders, and inspecting cognitive bias.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The education source distinguishes real participation and visible change from empty self-congratulation, but it does not define an assessment rubric for telling the two apart. The Wulc sources are secondary reading notes whose examples are motivational rather than psychometric; they do not establish that all thoughts or emotions are voluntarily controllable, that negative emotion has only two roots, or that the proposed exercises reliably strengthen a distinct cognitive mechanism. Negative affect can accurately signal danger, harm, grief, or violated values rather than a belief error or capability deficit. Reflection can also become suppression, rumination, or post-hoc rationalization unless it is bounded and checked against evidence or behavior.

## What Changed
- Extended moment-by-moment correction from cognition to emotional reaction and deliberate choice.
- Added externalized comparison as a way to make assumptions and options inspectable.
- Narrowed the emotional-regulation claim by preserving legitimate negative affect and limits on voluntary control.
