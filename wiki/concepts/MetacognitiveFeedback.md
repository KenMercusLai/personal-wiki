## Definition
[[MetacognitiveFeedback]] is the loop through which people observe their own thinking, detect gaps or automatic reactions, and use that awareness to revise later information intake, reasoning, or action.

## Current Synthesis
The education source argues that ability growth needs to become visible before it can sustain internal motivation. Research reports, portfolios, reviews, and written analyses are records learners can revisit to compare earlier and current thinking. The Wulc note adds an in-the-moment correction loop: observe a reaction, ask why it occurred and whether it fits, select a better response, and carry it into action. Its inspected information-flow diagram extends metacognition across input filtering, storage of concepts and values, selection of thinking methods, and behavioral output. Together the sources present reflection artifacts and momentary self-monitoring as complementary feedback channels.

## Key Claims
- Cognitive growth needs an internal feedback loop, not only external project activity.
- Visible artifacts help learners recognize improvements that would otherwise remain hidden.
- Seeing one's earlier reasoning gaps can become a source of intrinsic motivation.
- Intuition, experience, and taste can be explained as trained patterns rather than mysterious gifts.
- Metacognition can interrupt automatic reactions by making the current thought process available for evaluation and replacement.
- Deliberate practice, regular reflection, and attention exercises are proposed as ways to strengthen the correction loop.

## Evidence
- Internal loop: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] says PBL supplies an external training field, but learners still need to see their own change.
- Visible artifacts: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] names research reports, portfolios, and reviews as records of cognitive growth.
- Growth recognition: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] describes motivation arising when students can clearly identify weaknesses in their own earlier work.
- Trainable judgment: [[jiao-yu-de-xia-yi-bu-qi-er-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] reframes intuition as automated experience and taste as trained problem perception.
- Correction sequence: [[blog-wulc-ren-zhi-hong-li-yue-du-bi-ji-1-gai-nian-zhong-su]] models metacognition as moving from an event and automatic response to questioning that response and choosing a more suitable one.
- Input-process-output control: [[blog-wulc-ren-zhi-hong-li-yue-du-bi-ji-1-gai-nian-zhong-su]] and its inspected diagram divide metacognitive intervention into filtering information, selecting concepts or methods, and aligning output with judgment.
- Practice methods: [[blog-wulc-ren-zhi-hong-li-yue-du-bi-ji-1-gai-nian-zhong-su]] recommends deliberate method use, daily review, reflective reading, and meditation as repeated opportunities to notice and redirect attention.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The education source distinguishes real participation and visible change from empty self-congratulation, but it does not define an assessment rubric for telling the two apart. The Wulc source is a secondary reading note whose examples are motivational rather than psychometric; it does not establish that all thoughts are voluntarily controllable or that the proposed exercises reliably strengthen a distinct cognitive mechanism. Reflection can also become rumination or post-hoc rationalization unless it is bounded and checked against evidence or behavior.

## What Changed
- Broadened the concept from visible learning progress to moment-by-moment observation and correction of thought.
- Added input, processing, and output as three possible intervention points.
- Qualified voluntary-control and training claims as heuristic rather than psychometric evidence.
