## Definition
[[DataMonetization]] is the practice of turning collected, combined, or inferred data into economic value through better internal decisions and operations or through external data, insight, and analytics products.

## Current Synthesis
The sources now support a broader model than selling data or improving advertisements. Internally, data can reduce cost through productivity, prediction, and waste reduction or grow revenue through pricing, personalization, sales, and retention. Externally, offers range from maintained datasets through actionable insights to self-service analytical platforms. Across both paths, raw possession is not the main advantage: value rises when a company harmonizes sources, resolves identities or entities, applies analysis, and embeds the output in a consequential decision or workflow.

Advertising remains the wiki's most detailed mechanism. Traffic and behavior become more valuable through segmentation, targeting, retargeting, recommendation, label trading, and inferred profiles. The Unroll.me case makes a second external path concrete: information extracted while delivering a useful consumer feature can become a separate analytics product sold to another company. The Data Dollar Store then reverses the usual visibility: pricing physical goods in photos, messages, or phone access made the exchange explicit enough to provoke hesitation and selective disclosure. It demonstrates an awareness mechanism, not the market price of data or the fairness of user compensation.

The broader cases add an important constraint: a monetization opportunity should fit business strategy, solve a defined customer problem, and be evaluated against demand, shelf life, alternatives, replicability, governance, security, privacy, permission scope, and users' reasonable understanding. The strongest current judgment is therefore that monetization is a socio-technical operating capability, not an automatic property of owning more data; making exchange visible can improve deliberation without resolving consent, valuation, or power.

## Key Claims
- Data creates internal value through cost reduction and revenue growth as well as external value through saleable data, insight, analytical services, and data-supported attention or product exchanges.
- Monetization begins with a strategy-aligned use case and decision surface; data volume alone does not establish demand or value.
- Combining, harmonizing, and analyzing internal and external sources generally creates more defensible value than distributing raw records.
- Advertising monetization raises traffic value through segmentation, behavioral targeting, auctions, recommendation, retargeting, and look-alike expansion.
- External offers span [[DataAsAService]], insight as a service, and analytics-enabled platforms, while consumer-facing exchanges may conceal or deliberately expose data's role in the transaction.
- Identity resolution, inference, processed profiles, and secondary reuse can create much of the value while making consequential outputs less visible than raw inputs and permission labels.
- Repeatable monetization requires platform, analytics, commercial, governance, cybersecurity, privacy, permission, and partner-management capabilities; an explicit exchange alone does not establish informed consent or fair compensation.

## Evidence
- Internal and external paths: [[demystifying-data-monetization]] separates cost reduction and revenue growth from externally sold data, insight, and platform offers while treating the paths as compatible.
- Use-case and valuation discipline: [[demystifying-data-monetization]] ties opportunity value to customer demand, usage, shelf life, competitors, replicability, and strategic fit.
- Enrichment: [[demystifying-data-monetization]] uses the [[JohnDeere]] and [[AgAnalytics]] case to show operational farm data gaining value through soil, weather, satellite, insurance, and field sources.
- Traffic-plus-data model: [[blog-wulc-ru-he-yong-shu-ju-lai-zhuan-qian]] says segmentation and context raise the value of existing attention and transaction surfaces.
- Demand-shaped targeting: [[blog-wulc-zen-yang-yong-shu-ju-dong-cha-ni-de-yong-hu]] argues that labels should follow advertiser needs and customer decision processes, then be evaluated through Reach/CTR rather than taxonomy neatness.
- Data trading: [[blog-wulc-you-jia-zhi-de-shu-ju-ying-gai-ru-he-jiao-yi]] shows first-party and third-party DMPs turning behavior into label products distributed toward DSP demand through ADX infrastructure.
- Processed output: [[data-factories-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] argues that matching user, content, advertiser, third-party, and off-platform inputs produces valuable profiles that raw-input access does not reveal.
- Feature-to-data-product path: [[heres-the-thing-with-free-apps-and-services]] reports that Unroll.me used email access obtained for inbox management to derive anonymized receipt information sold through Slice Intelligence to Uber.
- Explicit exchange: [[inside-the-store-that-only-accepts-personal-data-as-currency-engadget]] describes merchandise tiers priced in selected photos, recent messages, or staff-selected phone content, making disclosure and loss of control immediately salient.
- Behavioral response: [[inside-the-store-that-only-accepts-personal-data-as-currency-engadget]] reports shoppers checking or preparing their phones and Summers choosing a lower-disclosure purchase with non-identifying photos.
- Operational capability: [[demystifying-data-monetization]] specifies a data platform, self-service analytics, operating model, management oversight, governance, compliance, cybersecurity, and privacy.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The Wulc sources are 2017 advertising overviews, Thompson is a 2018 strategic argument about large aggregators, the Unroll.me article is a 2017 consumer guide based on contemporary reporting, and the MIT Sloan source is a 2018 practitioner framework built from selected cases rather than comparative financial evidence. The Data Dollar Store is a one-week publicity event with no participant count, retention policy, valuation method, outcome study, or ordinary-market comparison. Its merchandise tiers make privacy cost legible but do not price the different downstream value of raw, combined, or inferred data, and rewarding disclosure would not by itself make collection necessary, proportionate, safe, or freely chosen. The claim that external monetization offers the greatest opportunity is therefore source-scoped, not established universally. The three external models can describe increasing transformation, but organizations need not progress through them in order, and a more complex platform is not automatically more profitable or appropriate. Privacy safeguards are described at a high level: anonymization, aggregation, governance, compliance, or a broad authorization prompt do not by themselves establish informed consent, prevent reidentification, make secondary uses expected, or make inferred outputs understandable and contestable.

## What Changed
- Broadened the concept from advertising and profile value to compatible internal and external monetization paths.
- Added a three-model spectrum for externally packaged data, insight, and analytical platforms.
- Reframed repeatable monetization as an operating capability spanning technology, organization, governance, security, and privacy.
- Added secondary use of feature-access data as a distinct monetization path requiring permission and expectation governance.
- Added explicit data-for-goods exchange as an awareness mechanism while rejecting its campaign prices as evidence of market value or fair compensation.
