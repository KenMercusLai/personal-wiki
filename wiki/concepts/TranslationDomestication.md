## Definition
[[TranslationDomestication]] is a localization strategy that adapts names, settings, or cultural cues into the target language and culture to reproduce the audience experience rather than preserve surface foreignness.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues for unusually strong domestication when puns saturate a game's names and dialogue. For [[WomenCommunication]], keeping Japanese names and setting while inserting Chinese homophone jokes would create an incoherent hybrid experience, so the localization instead adapts character names and the implied setting toward Chinese-language plausibility while preserving joke functions. The strategy is justified by player experience: the target audience should notice and react to the localized jokes in roughly the same way as the original audience.

## Key Claims
- Domestication is especially relevant when names themselves carry joke mechanics or semantic payloads.
- Preserving the original setting can be less faithful to experience if target-language puns then feel culturally misplaced.
- Localized names can preserve multiple layers of meaning by replacing form rather than transliterating it.
- Domestication trades surface fidelity for functional and experiential fidelity.
- Aggressive domestication can still introduce awkwardness if franchise history or audience expectations already anchor a work elsewhere.

## Evidence
- Name problem: [[wei-jie-ru-he-yong-bao-li-ji-suan-fan-yi-xie-yin-geng-nv-xing-jiao-liu-fan-yi-bi-ji]] explains that the game has puns embedded throughout character names.
- Setting argument: [[wei-jie-ru-he-yong-bao-li-ji-suan-fan-yi-xie-yin-geng-nv-xing-jiao-liu-fan-yi-bi-ji]] says Japanese-looking names full of Chinese puns would feel neither Japanese nor Chinese.
- Layer preservation: [[wei-jie-ru-he-yong-bao-li-ji-suan-fan-yi-xie-yin-geng-nv-xing-jiao-liu-fan-yi-bi-ji]] shows how a Japanese-Western hybrid name is remade as a Chinese-Western hybrid name while retaining the sensitive-word layer.
- Experience goal: [[wei-jie-ru-he-yong-bao-li-ji-suan-fan-yi-xie-yin-geng-nv-xing-jiao-liu-fan-yi-bi-ji]] reports that Chinese streamers reacted to localized names similarly to Japanese streamers.
- Constraint example: [[wei-jie-ru-he-yong-bao-li-ji-suan-fan-yi-xie-yin-geng-nv-xing-jiao-liu-fan-yi-bi-ji]] contrasts this approach with Ace Attorney Chinese name localizations that insert Chinese puns into Japanese-name contexts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is an argument from one localization project. It does not establish that full domestication is always preferable; games with strong historical setting, brand continuity, or audience attachment to original names may need different tradeoffs.
