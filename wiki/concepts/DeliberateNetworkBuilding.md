## Definition
[[DeliberateNetworkBuilding]] is the practice of choosing relationships, communities, mentors, and recurring information inputs according to the person one wants to become rather than accepting a network formed only by proximity or convention.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats a network as a formative environment, not just a list of contacts. People absorb assumptions, aspirations, habits, and emotional norms from those around them, while reading and viewing extend the same influence beyond face-to-face relationships. Deliberate network building therefore starts with direction: identify the capability, values, or kind of work one wants to develop, then seek people and inputs that embody or support that direction.

This is also a selection problem. Default workplaces, accelerators, investors, and media circles can feel natural while narrowing the range of imaginable choices. The article's startup hive-mind example shows how a network can provide access and legitimacy while simultaneously standardizing ambition and strategy. The practical goal is not to eliminate influence or choose only agreeable people, but to make the influence visible enough to judge its fit.

## Key Claims
- Networks shape perspective, behavior, and identity as well as access to information and opportunity.
- Passive proximity tends to build networks by accident, making inherited assumptions difficult to notice.
- Network selection should follow a stated direction of growth rather than a generic search for impressive contacts.
- Mentors are useful relative to a learner's specific goal; there is no universally good mentor for every direction.
- Shared institutions and media can create reinforcing communities that narrow strategic and personal alternatives.
- Conversations, reading, and viewing are one combined allocation of formative attention.

## Evidence
- Formative influence: [[you-become-your-network-build-it-wisely-savage-thoughts]] says recurring perspectives rub off on people and shape their own outlook.
- Accidental formation: [[you-become-your-network-build-it-wisely-savage-thoughts]] argues that mentors, advisers, friends, and colleagues are often inherited from circumstances rather than consciously chosen.
- Directional mentorship: [[you-become-your-network-build-it-wisely-savage-thoughts]] recommends learning from people who have developed the capability or scale one wants to build.
- Ecosystem convergence: [[you-become-your-network-build-it-wisely-savage-thoughts]] uses founders sharing accelerators, venture capital networks, and blogs as an example of a self-reinforcing hive mind.
- Attention allocation: [[you-become-your-network-build-it-wisely-savage-thoughts]] treats conversations, reading, and viewing as the time inputs from which individual growth emerges.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is reflective advice rather than causal evidence. It cites social-network research only indirectly, does not distinguish selection from peer influence, and does not show that deliberately chosen networks produce better outcomes. Conscious selection can also become status chasing, instrumentalize relationships, create ideological bubbles, or exclude valuable disagreement and serendipity. Access to desired mentors and communities is constrained by geography, money, work, caregiving, discrimination, and reciprocity; people cannot exercise total freedom over their networks.
