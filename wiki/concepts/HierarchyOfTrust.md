## Definition
[[HierarchyOfTrust]] is a five-level user-experience framework that calibrates a website's requests to trust already earned: baseline relevance, preference over alternatives, personal information, sensitive or financial information, and an ongoing relationship.

## Current Synthesis
The framework treats trust as a prerequisite sequence for escalating commitment. A new visitor starts below the pyramid with no site-specific trust; the site first has to show that it is relevant and likely to meet the visitor's need, then give enough evidence and control for the visitor to prefer it. Only after those foundations does the model consider requests for personal data, sensitive or financial data, or a continuing account relationship proportionate.

Its practical value is diagnostic. A forced registration modal, federated-login prompt, or address gate can be mapped to the commitment it demands and compared with what the visitor has been allowed to learn. Clear positioning, representative product content, credible [[SocialProof]], low-risk exploration, and reversible navigation are not ornamental persuasion; they are mechanisms for answering lower-level doubts before a higher-level ask. The model therefore qualifies conversion strategies that optimize early data capture without measuring abandonment, agency, or later trust.

The hierarchy should remain a heuristic rather than a behavioral law. Its Maslow analogy, historical examples, and unlabeled audience provide no causal evidence that every visitor advances through five discrete stages. Prior brand knowledge, referrals, urgency, legal requirements, risk tolerance, and the value offered can change how much commitment a person accepts and in what order.

## Key Claims
- New visitors should be modeled as skeptical until the experience supplies evidence of relevance and trustworthiness.
- Requests for information, money, effort, or relationship continuity should be proportionate to the trust earned so far.
- Baseline relevance and preference over alternatives normally precede requests for personal or sensitive information.
- Forced gates are especially risky when they hide the very content visitors need to evaluate the lower levels.
- Exploration, reversibility, clear positioning, relevant imagery, and credible proof can build confidence by preserving agency and reducing uncertainty.
- Behavioral observation is needed because users may not be able to articulate the trust concern driving abandonment.

## Evidence
- Five-stage structure: [[hierarchy-of-trust-the-5-experiential-levels-of-website-commitment]] defines the progression from baseline relevance through preference, personal data, sensitive or financial data, and an ongoing relationship; the retained pyramid displays the ordered dependency.
- Premature registration: [[hierarchy-of-trust-the-5-experiential-levels-of-website-commitment]] shows a ThredUp modal hiding navigation, positioning, and products while requesting email or linked Facebook and Amazon accounts; its annotated diagram places visitors at level zero while the requests assume levels three and five.
- Premature address collection: [[hierarchy-of-trust-the-5-experiential-levels-of-website-commitment]] shows Nextdoor requesting street address and email from visitors judged to be at level one; the proposed postal-code search would let them establish neighborhood relevance before committing more information.
- Agency and reassurance: [[hierarchy-of-trust-the-5-experiential-levels-of-website-commitment]] recommends taglines, representative imagery, social proof, free browsing, trial, and easy backtracking as ways to address early doubts.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a practitioner explanation, not a validation study. It provides no participant characteristics, experimental comparison, funnel data, effect sizes, or evidence that five levels are exhaustive, discrete, or universally ordered. The screenshots establish historical interface states but not causal abandonment. Existing reputation, word of mouth, urgency, mandatory workflows, risk, incentives, and cultural expectations may compress, reorder, or block the proposed progression. Even after lower levels are satisfied, privacy, accessibility, security, or price concerns can independently make a higher-level request unacceptable.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from the five-level model and its inspected ThredUp and Nextdoor examples.
- Preserved the model as a diagnostic heuristic rather than treating its Maslow analogy as validated behavioral evidence.
