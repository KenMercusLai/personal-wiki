## Definition
[[SmallTeamLeverage]] is the startup pattern where a very small group with limited capital achieves outsized product reach through focus, timing, networks, and fast execution.

## Current Synthesis
The sources present small-team leverage as real but conditional. [[Bump]] reached 2 million users during [[YCombinator]] with five people and $16,000, but the same story also includes luck, a sudden Apple commercial, server failure, and emergency help from the YC network. Meyerson supplies a possible structural explanation: cloud infrastructure and modular distribution, contracting, payment, and collaboration services give small teams capabilities once associated with larger firms. The combined judgment is not that headcount and capital no longer matter, but that compact teams can reach farther when infrastructure, distribution, timing, resilience, and support line up.

## Key Claims
- Small early teams can reach substantial usage before large hiring or funding.
- Low capital can sharpen focus but also leaves little operational slack.
- External distribution shocks can quickly exceed a small team's infrastructure.
- Networks can multiply a small team's resilience when unexpected scale arrives.
- Cloud and modular business services can reduce the fixed capabilities a small product team must build or own itself.

## Evidence
- Early reach: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says [[Bump]] reached 2 million users during YC with five people and $16,000.
- Distribution shock: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says an Apple international TV commercial melted the company's servers.
- Network support: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says the YC network helped rescue Bump during that server crisis.
- Capability access: [[you-are-not-a-commodity-dev-community]] attributes small-team leverage to accessible cloud, distribution, contracting, payment, and collaboration infrastructure.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence consists of one founder's celebratory memory and one entrepreneurial manifesto; neither proves that small teams are always preferable. Bump's story required luck, outside visibility, and emergency help, while Meyerson supplies no cost history, comparison group, survival rate, or outcomes for the companies he cites. Small-team leverage should therefore be read as a possibility under particular conditions rather than a universal operating rule.

## What Changed
- Added modular cloud and business infrastructure as a proposed mechanism for small-team reach while retaining the conditional judgment.
