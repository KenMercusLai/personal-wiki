## Definition
[[IdeaManagement]] is the capture, storage, review, and cultivation of creative ideas so passing inspiration can become usable future work.

## Current Synthesis
The source argues that idea drought is often a system problem, not simply a lack of creativity. Ideas need quick capture because they disappear, a centralized home because scattered notes become unusable, and high-quality input because not every captured thought can become publishable material. The author's Notion database, iOS Shortcut, read-it-later tools, newsletters, podcasts, comment practice, conversations, and relaxed thinking all form a practical idea supply chain.

## Key Claims
- Ideas should be captured immediately because delayed writing often loses the original signal.
- A centralized idea database makes future review and development easier than scattered notes.
- Capture speed matters enough to justify shortcuts or automation when the default tool is too slow.
- Idea supply improves when creators expand high-quality inputs in their chosen domain.
- Published content, comments, conversations, and archived interactions can all become future idea material.
- Relaxed activities such as walking, bathing, breathing practice, meditation, and exercise can help ideas surface.

- Input expansion: [[xuan-ti-zhuan-ye-xing-wan-mei-zhu-yi-shu-ju-he-ling-gan-zhe-xie-chuang-zuo-jiao-lv-gai-ru-he-ying-dui]] follows creators, newsletters, podcasts, Cubox, Readwise Reader, Snipd, and PARA-style organization.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
Capturing more ideas can create a backlog that feels productive without producing finished work. The source's workflow also depends on comfort with Notion, iOS Shortcuts, APIs, and multiple content platforms; simpler capture tools may fit other creators better.
