## Definition
[[NewsroomAnalytics]] is the practice of making audience behavior and content-performance evidence usable inside editorial decisions about presentation, distribution, context, and follow-up.

## Current Synthesis
The Guardian case suggests that newsroom analytics becomes operational when evidence is available to editorial staff at the moment of action, expressed through understandable measures, and connected to possible interventions. Ophan combined article totals, attention time, referral mix, live ranking, threshold alerts, and limited search-query visibility with bookmarklets and mobile access, so staff did not need to become analysts or wait for specialist reports.

The practice is data-informed rather than mechanically data-driven. Staff could test a headline, add links, re-promote a relevant older story, or investigate a referral spike, but the metrics did not decide what was journalistically valuable. The Chris Kyle example supplies an important failure mode: acting on renewed demand without clear temporal framing can turn an audience opportunity into reader confusion.

## Key Claims
- Analytics is more likely to enter routine editorial work when access is broad, fast, and understandable to non-specialists.
- Useful newsroom views connect outcome measures to time, content, referral source, and an available editorial action.
- Real-time feedback lets staff test small editorial judgments, but observed changes do not by themselves prove causal or journalistic improvement.
- Threshold alerts can convert continuous monitoring into exception-based attention.
- Search-query visibility can humanize aggregate acquisition channels by exposing specific audience intent, even when the observable sample is incomplete.
- Traffic responsiveness needs safeguards for context, temporal clarity, and editorial value.

## Evidence
- Adoption and usability: [[constantly-tweaking-how-the-guardian-continues-to-develop-its-in-house-analytics-system-nieman-journalism-lab]] reports more than 900 users, bookmarklets, mobile access, and emphasis on page views and attention time because staff could understand them.
- Actionable context: [[constantly-tweaking-how-the-guardian-continues-to-develop-its-in-house-analytics-system-nieman-journalism-lab]] and its screenshots connect stories with live traffic rate, referral composition, attention time, ranking, and destination queries.
- Feedback and alerts: [[constantly-tweaking-how-the-guardian-continues-to-develop-its-in-house-analytics-system-nieman-journalism-lab]] describes visible results after headline, link, or front-page changes and configurable email rules for exceptional traffic conditions.
- Humanized intent: [[constantly-tweaking-how-the-guardian-continues-to-develop-its-in-house-analytics-system-nieman-journalism-lab]] reports that a live Google-query ticker made search traffic legible as individual people seeking particular information.
- Editorial qualification: [[constantly-tweaking-how-the-guardian-continues-to-develop-its-in-house-analytics-system-nieman-journalism-lab]] notes reader confusion when a 2013 article was re-promoted in 2015 as though it were current.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a favorable 2015 case report rather than a controlled evaluation. It gives no adoption distribution, causal tests, false-alert rate, long-term editorial outcomes, revenue effects, or evidence that page views and attention time capture public value. Google query visibility covered only 10-20 percent of search terms, and real-time feedback may reward short-term traffic, encourage reactive optimization, or mistake correlation after an edit for causation.

## What Changed
- Established newsroom analytics as an accessibility, interpretation, intervention, and governance practice rather than a dashboard alone.
- Added temporal clarity and editorial value as safeguards on traffic-responsive action.
