## Definition
[[BehavioralData]] is data generated by user, customer, patient, or system behavior that can reveal preferences, patterns, risk, demand, or intent.

## Current Synthesis
The source distinguishes behavior data from transaction data because behavior traces can support prediction and automated optimization in ways that static or final transaction records may not. In the source's framework, behavior data is the first gate for [[BigDataIndustryTransformation]]: without it, larger datasets may remain descriptive rather than actionable.

## Key Claims
- Behavior data matters because it captures action patterns before, around, or beyond a completed transaction.
- Transaction data and behavior data require different processing approaches, so digitization alone does not guarantee big-data leverage.
- Behavior data becomes most valuable when paired with full-population processing and automated application.
- Predictive systems such as CTR estimation illustrate why behavior data often creates class-imbalance and sampling problems in practice.

## Evidence
- Behavior versus transaction data: [[da-shu-ju-shi-fou-neng-gou-gai-zao-ni-de-hang-ye]] explicitly separates the two and says their processing methods differ.
- Transformation gate: [[da-shu-ju-shi-fou-neng-gou-gai-zao-ni-de-hang-ye]] lists behavior data as the first feature of industries that big data can transform.
- Practical modeling issue: [[da-shu-ju-shi-fou-neng-gou-gai-zao-ni-de-hang-ye]] uses CTR prediction and negative-sample extraction to show how behavior data creates engineering tradeoffs.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page currently relies on one source and does not yet distinguish behavior data by collection method, consent, privacy risk, sensor quality, or domain-specific validity. More sources are needed before generalizing beyond the article's advertising-centered examples.
