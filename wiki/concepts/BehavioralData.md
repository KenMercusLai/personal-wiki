## Definition
[[BehavioralData]] is data generated by user, customer, patient, learner, or system behavior that can reveal preferences, patterns, risk, demand, context, or intent.

## Current Synthesis
The sources distinguish behavior data from transaction data because behavior traces can support prediction, automated optimization, and monetization in ways that static or final transaction records may not. Behavior data is the first gate for [[BigDataIndustryTransformation]] and a major input to [[DataMonetization]]: ad systems use browsing, search, product views, cart actions, and similar traces to support bidding, retargeting, recommendation, look-alike expansion, and [[BehavioralTargeting]]. The audience-targeting and data-trading sources add two middle layers between raw logs and monetization: actions must be mapped to labels, weighted by intent strength, aged through sliding windows or time decay, and tied to durable enough user identifiers before they become operational audiences or tradable DMP label products. The MOOC analytics example adds an important qualification: granular action traces can still be compressed into shallow effort metrics. In education, clickstreams, video time, problem attempts, and event counts can predict achievement while leaving motivation, prior ability, causal mechanism, and instructional design unresolved.

## Key Claims
- Behavior data matters because it captures action patterns before, around, or beyond a completed transaction.
- Transaction data and behavior data require different processing approaches, so digitization alone does not guarantee big-data leverage.
- Behavior data becomes most valuable when paired with full-population processing and automated application.
- Advertising systems monetize behavior data through auctions, retargeting, recommendation, look-alike expansion, mobile scene inference, and behavior-to-label targeting pipelines.
- Behavior signals vary by intent density: conversion and cart actions are stronger than search or clicks, which are stronger than page views, sharing, and forced ad exposure.
- User identity is a precondition for monetized behavior data because systems must know which user a behavior belongs to before labels can be built or traded.
- Granular behavior logs do not guarantee deep insight if researchers reduce them to obvious summary measures.

## Evidence
- Behavior versus transaction data: [[da-shu-ju-shi-fou-neng-gou-gai-zao-ni-de-hang-ye]] explicitly separates the two and says their processing methods differ.
- Transformation gate: [[da-shu-ju-shi-fou-neng-gou-gai-zao-ni-de-hang-ye]] lists behavior data as the first feature of industries that big data can transform.
- Practical modeling issue: [[da-shu-ju-shi-fou-neng-gou-gai-zao-ni-de-hang-ye]] uses CTR prediction and negative-sample extraction to show how behavior data creates engineering tradeoffs.
- Advertising monetization: [[blog-wulc-ru-he-yong-shu-ju-lai-zhuan-qian]] treats behavior data as the shift from coarse demographic targeting to auction ads, programmatic trading, retargeting, ecommerce recommendation, and look-alike acquisition.
- Scene inference: [[blog-wulc-ru-he-yong-shu-ju-lai-zhuan-qian]] describes mobile behavior, location sampling, and sensor signals being used to infer work state, commuting transitions, or other user scenes.
- Targeting pipeline: [[blog-wulc-zen-yang-yong-shu-ju-dong-cha-ni-de-yong-hu]] shows browsing, search, ad-click, and conversion behaviors being converted into labels, weighted by behavior type, accumulated with sliding windows or time decay, and evaluated through a Reach/CTR curve.
- Intent gradient: [[blog-wulc-you-jia-zhi-de-shu-ju-ying-gai-ru-he-jiao-yi]] ranks decision behavior, active behavior, semi-active behavior, and passive behavior by decreasing value and increasing volume.
- Identity layer: [[blog-wulc-you-jia-zhi-de-shu-ju-ying-gai-ru-he-jiao-yi]] compares cookies, IDFA, Android ID, IMEI, and fingerprinting as ways to attach behavior traces to a user.
- Privacy limit: [[blog-wulc-you-jia-zhi-de-shu-ju-ying-gai-ru-he-jiao-yi]] warns that sparse behavior data can make people reidentifiable even when obvious PII is removed.
- Education analytics caution: [[big-data-mooc-research-breakthrough-learning-activities-lead-to-achievement-edtech-researcher-education-week]] shows learner logs from Khan Academy, Udacity, Google Course Builder, and edX being reduced to minutes, problem counts, activity completion, video time, or event counts.
- Causal limitation: [[big-data-mooc-research-breakthrough-learning-activities-lead-to-achievement-edtech-researcher-education-week]] argues that activity-achievement correlations may reflect motivation, prior ability, course fit, or classroom attention rather than a clean causal effect.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page now spans business automation, advertising monetization, targeting-label construction, data trading, and online learning. The advertising sources are commercially oriented and only partly address consent and privacy through PII limits, opt-out, retention limits, and sparse-data reidentification; they do not resolve whether users understand or accept the inferences being made. The MOOC source shows that prediction is not the same as explanation: behavior traces may identify active learners while saying little about whether a course design caused learning.

## What Changed
- Added behavior intent density, user-identity dependence, and sparse-data privacy risk to the advertising behavior-data synthesis.
