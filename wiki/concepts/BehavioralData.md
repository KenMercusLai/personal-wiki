## Definition
[[BehavioralData]] is data generated by user, customer, patient, learner, or system behavior that can reveal preferences, patterns, risk, demand, context, or intent.

## Current Synthesis
The sources distinguish behavior data from transaction data because behavior traces can support prediction, automated optimization, and monetization in ways that static or final transaction records may not. Behavior data is the first gate for [[BigDataIndustryTransformation]] and a major input to [[DataMonetization]]: ad systems use browsing, search, product views, cart actions, and similar traces to support bidding, retargeting, recommendation, and look-alike expansion. The MOOC analytics example adds an important qualification: granular action traces can still be compressed into shallow effort metrics. In education, clickstreams, video time, problem attempts, and event counts can predict achievement while leaving motivation, prior ability, causal mechanism, and instructional design unresolved.

## Key Claims
- Behavior data matters because it captures action patterns before, around, or beyond a completed transaction.
- Transaction data and behavior data require different processing approaches, so digitization alone does not guarantee big-data leverage.
- Behavior data becomes most valuable when paired with full-population processing and automated application.
- Predictive systems such as CTR estimation illustrate why behavior data often creates class-imbalance and sampling problems in practice.
- Advertising systems monetize behavior data through auctions, retargeting, recommendation, look-alike expansion, and mobile scene inference.
- Granular behavior logs do not guarantee deep insight if researchers reduce them to obvious summary measures.
- Activity traces can be predictive without proving that the activity independently caused the outcome.

## Evidence
- Behavior versus transaction data: [[da-shu-ju-shi-fou-neng-gou-gai-zao-ni-de-hang-ye]] explicitly separates the two and says their processing methods differ.
- Transformation gate: [[da-shu-ju-shi-fou-neng-gou-gai-zao-ni-de-hang-ye]] lists behavior data as the first feature of industries that big data can transform.
- Practical modeling issue: [[da-shu-ju-shi-fou-neng-gou-gai-zao-ni-de-hang-ye]] uses CTR prediction and negative-sample extraction to show how behavior data creates engineering tradeoffs.
- Advertising monetization: [[blog-wulc-ru-he-yong-shu-ju-lai-zhuan-qian]] treats behavior data as the shift from coarse demographic targeting to auction ads, programmatic trading, retargeting, ecommerce recommendation, and look-alike acquisition.
- Scene inference: [[blog-wulc-ru-he-yong-shu-ju-lai-zhuan-qian]] describes mobile behavior, location sampling, and sensor signals being used to infer work state, commuting transitions, or other user scenes.
- Education analytics caution: [[big-data-mooc-research-breakthrough-learning-activities-lead-to-achievement-edtech-researcher-education-week]] shows learner logs from Khan Academy, Udacity, Google Course Builder, and edX being reduced to minutes, problem counts, activity completion, video time, or event counts.
- Causal limitation: [[big-data-mooc-research-breakthrough-learning-activities-lead-to-achievement-edtech-researcher-education-week]] argues that activity-achievement correlations may reflect motivation, prior ability, course fit, or classroom attention rather than a clean causal effect.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The page now spans business automation, advertising monetization, and online learning, but still does not fully distinguish behavior data by collection method, consent, privacy risk, sensor quality, or domain-specific validity. The advertising source is commercially oriented and does not resolve whether users understand or accept the inferences being made. The MOOC source shows that prediction is not the same as explanation: behavior traces may identify active learners while saying little about whether a course design caused learning.

## What Changed
- Added advertising and ecommerce monetization as a major use case for behavioral data.
- Added mobile scene inference as an example where behavior data overlaps with location and sensor-derived user state.
- Added the MOOC analytics qualification that rich behavioral logs can become shallow effort proxies and non-causal correlations.
