## Definition
[[DeliberatePractice]] is structured, feedback-rich practice aimed at improving specific aspects of performance rather than treating elapsed repetition as sufficient for expertise.

## Current Synthesis
The source introduces deliberate practice by criticizing the popular 10,000-hour rule. It argues that an average practice duration does not establish a universal threshold, that practice quality is more informative than time alone, and that people who practiced extensively without becoming elite are usually absent from the motivational version of the story. The concept therefore shifts attention from accumulated hours to task design, feedback, correction, and whether performance is actually changing.

This source does not provide a complete theory of expertise or a protocol for designing practice. Its useful contribution is narrower: time invested is an input measure, not proof of learning, and winner-only examples cannot reveal the range of outcomes among similarly persistent practitioners.

## Key Claims
- Elapsed practice time does not by itself establish the quality or effect of practice.
- A population average should not be converted into a guaranteed threshold for individual mastery.
- Useful practice needs specific targets, informative feedback, and correction rather than undirected repetition.
- Expertise stories need the outcome distribution of comparable practitioners, including people who practiced for similar durations without elite results.
- Practice interacts with prior ability, instruction, opportunity, domain structure, health, motivation, and other conditions.

## Evidence
- Rule qualification: [[dont-let-gurus-sell-you-on-survivorship-bias-sjo-com-sjo-com]] says the popular 10,000-hour claim converts an average with substantial variation into a deterministic promise.
- Quality distinction: [[dont-let-gurus-sell-you-on-survivorship-bias-sjo-com-sjo-com]] argues that detailed, high-quality practice matters more than merely accumulating hours.
- Missing comparison set: [[dont-let-gurus-sell-you-on-survivorship-bias-sjo-com-sjo-com]] notes that people who practiced extensively but remained mediocre are omitted from the familiar success story.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a polemical secondary discussion linking to outside commentary rather than a review of expertise research. It does not define deliberate practice precisely, distinguish it from purposeful practice or ordinary training, quantify the contribution of practice across domains, or show that its named explanations are independent. The critique establishes that hours alone are insufficient; it does not establish that one practice design works universally or that extensive practice is unimportant.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to separate feedback-rich skill improvement from the deterministic 10,000-hour slogan.
