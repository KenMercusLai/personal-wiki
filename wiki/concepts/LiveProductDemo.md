## Definition
[[LiveProductDemo]] is a real-time demonstration in which a product performs its core promise for the audience, turning an abstract claim into directly observable evidence.

## Current Synthesis
In [[JeffLawson]]'s [[Twilio]] seed pitch, the live demo follows a deliberately compressed claim: telephony's complexity had been reduced to five API calls. When [[FredWilson]] expressed disbelief, Lawson did not add a longer market explanation; he named the calls, wrote an application in front of Wilson, asked for Wilson's phone number, and caused the phone to ring within about 30 seconds. The sequence shows why live demonstration can be unusually persuasive when the product's differentiated capability is fast, legible, and reliable enough to perform in the room.

## Key Claims
- A live product demo can convert a surprising value proposition into observable evidence.
- The strongest demo is tightly aligned with the core product claim rather than an unrelated feature tour.
- Using the audience's own input or environment can make the result immediate and personally verifiable.
- Demonstration can shorten a pitch when successful behavior resolves the audience's main doubt.
- A memorable demo proves one capability, not the full market, business, reliability, or investment case.

## Evidence
- Claim-to-proof alignment: [[best-seed-pitch-ever-avc]] moves directly from Lawson's five-API-call telephony claim to a live-coded phone application.
- Audience verification: [[best-seed-pitch-ever-avc]] says Lawson used Wilson's own phone number and made the phone ring within about 30 seconds.
- Pitch compression: [[best-seed-pitch-ever-avc]] records Wilson stopping the pitch after seeing the core capability work.
- Memorability: [[best-seed-pitch-ever-avc]] says Wilson still regarded it as his best seed pitch years later.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is a single retrospective anecdote selected because it was exceptional. The source does not describe failed demos, investor selection effects, the rest of Twilio's diligence, or whether the pattern works for products whose value emerges slowly, depends on many users, cannot safely use live data, or requires reliability and compliance evidence beyond a brief success. A live demo can also fail for reasons unrelated to the underlying product.
