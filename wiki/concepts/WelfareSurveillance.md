## Definition
[[WelfareSurveillance]] is the monitoring, verification, and data collection imposed on people seeking public benefits or poverty relief.

## Current Synthesis
The source frames welfare surveillance as historically continuous rather than newly digital. Colonial overseers of the poor tracked, expelled, or auctioned poor people; modern benefits systems ask highly personal questions, require home visits, and may add drug tests, fingerprinting, biometrics, and database comparisons. The article's legal examples show how courts have treated public assistance as a context where privacy expectations are weakened, turning help for basic survival into a condition of inspection.

## Key Claims
- Public benefits access can require privacy surrender not demanded of higher-income recipients of government transfers.
- Home visits and eligibility checks make the private home part of administrative verification.
- Welfare surveillance is continuous with older systems for policing poor people, not only with modern data systems.
- Courts can normalize intrusive monitoring by treating benefits recipients as having reduced privacy expectations.
- Digital database comparison expands welfare surveillance beyond the visit into persistent data infrastructures.

## Evidence
- Home-visit case: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] recounts Barbara James losing benefits after refusing New York City's welfare home visit policy.
- Legal continuity: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] describes Wyman v. James and Sanchez v. San Diego as cases that upheld welfare home visits.
- Historical lineage: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] describes colonial overseers of the poor tracking, expelling, or auctioning poor people.
- Modern tools: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] lists personal questioning, home visits, drug tests, fingerprinting, biometrics, and cross-database comparison.
- Transfer double standard: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] quotes [[MicheleGilman]] contrasting intrusive welfare checks with mortgage deductions, childcare tax credits, and untaxed employer health benefits.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source criticizes welfare surveillance but does not fully present the administrative arguments for fraud prevention or eligibility verification. Its legal discussion is source-scoped and does not survey every jurisdiction or later case law.
