## Definition
[[ProductLedRetention]] is a retention strategy where customers keep using and paying for a product because the product itself becomes more valuable, more embedded, or more clearly matched to their needs over time.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents product-led retention as the answer to a SaaS market where acquisition channels are visible, cheap, and crowded. A strong product retains users by creating ongoing value rather than relying on old lock-in barriers. The article gives three patterns: broaden the product around a customer's adjacent jobs, expand usage and paid seats inside existing accounts, and align acquisition messaging with the specific feature that retained users actually care about.

## Key Claims
- Product strength is a more durable retention lever than merely acquiring more leads.
- Broadening a product suite can keep customers when their workflow needs expand.
- Onboarding and early usage should encourage the behaviors that predict long-term account expansion.
- Marketing should emphasize the sticky value that best-fit customers continue to use.
- Product-led retention still depends on customer communication, because teams need to know which users and features actually drive repeat use.

## Evidence
- Product strength: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] says companies win or lose in crowded SaaS based on product strength.
- Suite expansion: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] presents [[Intercom]] as a customer-communication product that expanded from chat into live chat, messaging, help desk, and knowledge base tools.
- Account expansion: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] presents [[Front]] as growing through retained users, upsells, higher plan adoption, and net negative churn.
- Message-value fit: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] says [[CrazyEgg]] differentiated against [[GoogleAnalytics]] by emphasizing heatmaps and click visualization in its marketing.
- Customer communication: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] recommends asking who the ideal users are, what they find most useful, and how the product can grow with them.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source's examples are case narratives, not controlled tests. Suite expansion can also create complexity, account expansion depends on team workflows and pricing fit, and feature-led marketing can overfit to one use case if the broader product stops improving.

## What Changed
- Added product-led retention as a pattern joining suite expansion, account expansion, onboarding, and marketing-value alignment.
