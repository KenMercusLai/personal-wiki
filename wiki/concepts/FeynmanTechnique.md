## Definition
[[FeynmanTechnique]] is a learning method that tests understanding by requiring the learner to explain a topic simply, review what cannot yet be explained, and internalize the simplified structure as usable knowledge.

## Current Synthesis
The sources present the Feynman Technique as an explanation-based self-test. One version gives a five-step loop: set a learning target, understand the material through structure and comparison, explain it in simple language, review gaps revealed by that explanation, and internalize the result. 编程随想's version sharpens the diagnostic side: when the learner cannot begin, they may need introductory material again; when they repeatedly stall, they should strengthen blind spots; when an outsider cannot understand the explanation, they should locate the unclear step with that listener.

The technique's practical force comes from treating output as proof of learning. If a learner cannot teach the idea clearly to a novice, the sources argue that understanding is incomplete, and the next study move should be chosen from the failure mode exposed by the explanation attempt.

## Key Claims
- The technique begins with a clear learning target that creates focus.
- Understanding requires classification, structure, comparison, and filtering rather than mere accumulation.
- Simple explanation exposes whether the learner actually understands the subject.
- Review turns failed explanations into a map of missing understanding.
- Internalization is the final goal because the learner forms a usable personal knowledge system.
- Different explanation failures point to different repairs: restart with introductory material, patch blind spots, or improve audience fit.

## Evidence
- Target and understanding: [[feynman-technique-in-practice-indigo-information-acquisition-knowledge-output-methodology]] lists goal-setting and structured understanding as the first two steps of the technique.
- Simple explanation: [[feynman-technique-in-practice-indigo-information-acquisition-knowledge-output-methodology]] says learners should explain the material in the simplest clear language, with a sixth-grade listener as the imagined audience.
- Review of gaps: [[feynman-technique-in-practice-indigo-information-acquisition-knowledge-output-methodology]] describes looking for places where output reveals confusion, then revisiting the knowledge.
- Internalization: [[feynman-technique-in-practice-indigo-information-acquisition-knowledge-output-methodology]] frames simplification and absorption into one's own system as the ultimate purpose of learning.
- Failure diagnosis: [[bian-cheng-sui-xiang-ru-he-xi-tong-xing-xue-xi]] separates not knowing where to start, frequent stalls, and outsider confusion into different feedback signals.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The sources are practitioner explanations rather than controlled studies of the technique's effects. One source treats Dale's Cone as supporting evidence without examining debates around retention percentages, so the strongest source-backed claim is methodological rather than quantitative. The technique also assumes the learner has enough base material and feedback access to attempt an explanation.
