## Definition
[[ProductManagerHiring]] is the role-specific process of defining the product-manager archetype a team needs, sourcing varied candidates, gathering evidence of product judgment and cross-functional influence, and closing the selected person around an honestly matched motivation.

## Current Synthesis
[[ToddJackson]] starts from the work rather than the title: a product manager should articulate what winning looks like, rally a team without relying on command authority, and iterate toward the right result. The hiring rubric therefore combines baseline synthesis, communication, leadership, and organizational effectiveness with stage-dependent technical, analytical, strategic, entrepreneurial, coding, mockup, and hands-on analysis capability. A small company's first product hire may need more bonus execution range than a PM surrounded by specialists.

The proposed funnel combines reciprocal selling and evidence gathering. Candidates move from a mutual-fit screen through interviews with the people they would work beside and, only after an explicit evaluation gate, to a product-analysis presentation and live questions. Prompts test detailed product observation, technical understanding, conflict handling, second-order consequences, willingness to serve the team, and researched motivation. The close begins during the first interaction through direct, frequent communication and culminates in a truthful connection between the role and the candidate's strongest reward, such as impact, autonomy, mission, learning, recognition, or user delight.

## Key Claims
- Role design should state both universal PM responsibilities and the capabilities made important by company, product, and team stage.
- Candidate backgrounds can vary widely, so evaluation should look for evidence of product judgment, building, learning, and influence rather than one resume archetype.
- A staged process should combine reciprocal fit, cross-functional interviews, an explicit continuation gate, and a job-relevant presentation with live questions.
- Interview prompts are most useful when they reveal reasoning detail, customer perspective, technical fluency, conflict behavior, second-order analysis, service orientation, and researched commitment.
- The first or unusually broad PM hire may require hands-on coding, mockup, or analysis skills that larger specialist teams can supply separately.
- Closing is part of candidate experience from first contact and should connect the role honestly to the individual's dominant motivation.

## Evidence
- Role and rubric design: [[find-vet-and-close-the-best-product-managers-first-round-review]] defines three core responsibilities, four baseline qualities, and stage-sensitive additional capabilities.
- Varied candidate evidence: [[find-vet-and-close-the-best-product-managers-first-round-review]] examines conventional PM, early-career technical, consulting, engineering, and business-development profiles while asking what each actually built, learned, or influenced.
- Structured evaluation: [[find-vet-and-close-the-best-product-managers-first-round-review]] proposes a screen, cross-functional interview loop, stop-and-evaluate gate, and product presentation with live questions.
- Job-relevant reasoning: [[find-vet-and-close-the-best-product-managers-first-round-review]] uses concrete prompts to probe product sense, web fundamentals, disagreement, strategic ripple effects, service work, and company motivation.
- Candidate-specific close: [[find-vet-and-close-the-best-product-managers-first-round-review]] recommends frequent direct contact and an honest match between the role and the candidate's strongest reward center.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework is one practitioner's account supported by anonymized examples, not evidence that its attributes, questions, or panel exercise predict performance. It provides no scoring anchors, inter-rater reliability, adverse-impact analysis, accessibility design, candidate-burden analysis, or post-hire validation.

School reputation, prior-company prestige, technical pedigree, enthusiasm, “culture” effectiveness, and presentation polish can become proxies for class, affinity, disability, or access. They should be translated into observable job requirements, offered through proportionate demonstration modes, and checked against actual outcomes. The claim that PMs differ predictably from engineers and designers in motivation is also a broad occupational generalization, so individual inquiry is stronger than discipline-level assumption.

## What Changed
- Established a role-specific hiring model that links PM responsibilities to evaluation rather than starting from title or pedigree.
- Added the explicit stop-and-evaluate gate before a demanding panel exercise.
- Connected candidate communication and motivation discovery to both closing and later retention promises.
