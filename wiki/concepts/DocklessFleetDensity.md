## Definition
[[DocklessFleetDensity]] is the operational advantage created when enough shared vehicles are distributed through a neighborhood that users can reliably find one within a short walk.

## Current Synthesis
The Bird source treats density as a local availability moat. A scooter service is only compelling if riders expect nearby supply when the need arises; otherwise the convenience advantage over walking, ride-hailing, bikes, or transit weakens. Density also compounds with capital and procurement scale because the entrant needs enough vehicles, charging coverage, mechanic capacity, and placement knowledge to match the incumbent neighborhood experience.

## Key Claims
- Dockless services work only when users believe nearby vehicles will be available.
- Early dense deployment can raise the capital needed for late entrants to compete.
- Density interacts with procurement scale because cheaper vehicle acquisition supports more local supply.
- Density requires nightly charging, morning placement, and repairs, not just vehicles on a map.
- Local density can create a service-level advantage even without a classic social network effect.

## Evidence
- Availability threshold: [[all-the-questions-you-wanted-answered-about-bird-scooters-and-their-recent-300-million-funding]] says dockless scooters are compelling only if riders believe they can find one within a short walking distance.
- Entrant cost: [[all-the-questions-you-wanted-answered-about-bird-scooters-and-their-recent-300-million-funding]] argues that thousands of scooters in a neighborhood make new entry difficult without serious capital.
- Operational support: [[all-the-questions-you-wanted-answered-about-bird-scooters-and-their-recent-300-million-funding]] links charger and mechanic networks to keeping scooters charged, placed, and repaired.
- Image evidence: [[all-the-questions-you-wanted-answered-about-bird-scooters-and-their-recent-300-million-funding]] includes an inspected street photo showing many Bird scooters parked together near a beach-path environment, visually supporting dense local availability.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not measure how many scooters per block or per trip demand are required for reliable availability. Density can also create sidewalk clutter, regulatory conflict, vandalism, idle inventory, and safety issues that the article does not analyze in depth.
