## Definition
[[SleepAsPerformanceEnhancer]] is the claim that sleep supports performance by improving cognition, emotional regulation, creativity, decision making, and sustainable energy rather than merely reducing fatigue.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents sleep as a foundation for effective work and daily outlook. Huffington says that regularly getting about eight hours lets her wake naturally, often joyful, and that sleep science has become culturally important because it legitimizes sleep's role in decision making, emotional intelligence, cognitive function, and creativity. The concept therefore qualifies productivity advice that focuses only on effort or output: better performance may require protecting recovery before work begins.

## Key Claims
- Sufficient sleep can make waking natural rather than alarm-driven.
- Sleep is linked in the source to better emotional tone, including waking with joy about the day.
- Sleep science is used to legitimize rest as a performance input.
- Cognitive, emotional, decision-making, and creative benefits are treated as part of work performance.
- Sleep advocacy challenges overwork cultures that treat exhaustion as normal or heroic.

## Evidence
- Natural waking: [[arianna-huffington-on-sleep-mornings-and-work-the-early-hour]] says Huffington gets eight hours 95 percent of the time and usually wakes naturally without an alarm.
- Emotional tone: [[arianna-huffington-on-sleep-mornings-and-work-the-early-hour]] says she wakes joyful when she gets the sleep she needs.
- Scientific legitimacy: [[arianna-huffington-on-sleep-mornings-and-work-the-early-hour]] says sleep science shows roles for sleep and dreams in decision making, emotional intelligence, cognitive function, and creativity.
- Overwork correction: [[arianna-huffington-on-sleep-mornings-and-work-the-early-hour]] connects the sleep campaign to Huffington's exhaustion and ill health from long work hours.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is an advocacy interview, not a clinical review. It makes broad claims about sleep science but does not present study details, population differences, or boundary conditions. The concept should be read as a synthesis of this source's productivity framing, not as medical advice.
