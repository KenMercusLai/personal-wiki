## Definition
[[InMarketJobSearch]] is the strategy of establishing sustained physical presence and credible relocation commitment in a target city before pursuing location-bound employment there.

## Current Synthesis
[[MarkSuster]] presents geographic commitment as part of the job-search signal, not merely a logistical detail after an offer. In his account, strong local roles frequently surface through repeated face-to-face contact with executives, recruiters, founders, investors, and other connectors before those roles enter crowded formal pipelines. Living in the market increases the time and continuity available for that relationship work.

The strategy also addresses employer risk. A candidate who plans to commute for months, sell a house later, wait for a school year to end, or keep several destination cities open may be sincere, but a hiring manager can still infer a higher chance of delayed relocation, family strain, or reversal. Moving first makes the commitment legible, but transfers substantial risk to the candidate; it is therefore a conditional tactic for people who have already chosen the place, not a universal job-search rule.

## Key Claims
- Local presence can improve access to roles that circulate through relationships before public advertising or recruiter searches.
- Sustained networking creates more opportunity than occasional exploratory trips or dormant online contacts.
- Choosing a destination city and choosing a job are coupled decisions; hedging across cities may weaken both searches.
- Relocation uncertainty can make an otherwise qualified candidate less attractive than an equivalent local candidate.
- Moving first shifts financial, housing, family, and career risk from the employer to the candidate.

## Evidence
- Hidden opportunity discovery: [[why-you-cant-search-for-a-job-from-a-remote-location]] says many desirable roles are found through personal introductions before job boards or executive recruiters create crowded candidate pools.
- Sustained presence: [[why-you-cant-search-for-a-job-from-a-remote-location]] contrasts weeks of local meetings with three-day visits every six to eight weeks and occasional LinkedIn messages.
- Commitment signal: [[why-you-cant-search-for-a-job-from-a-remote-location]] describes schooling, house-sale, spousal-study, and long-commute scenarios that employers may interpret as unresolved relocation risk.
- Candidate-side cost: [[why-you-cant-search-for-a-job-from-a-remote-location]] explicitly names the chicken-and-egg fear of moving without confidence that the right role will follow.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a 2016 practitioner essay and supplies no comparative placement, retention, productivity, or family-outcome data. Its claim that in-market presence raises the probability of the ideal job "100x" is rhetorical rather than measured. The advice is specifically about pursuing location-bound roles in a chosen city and should not be generalized to remote-first organizations, roles with standardized remote interviews, or unusually scarce candidates. Moving before securing income may be impossible or irresponsible under housing, visa, disability, caregiving, partner-career, schooling, or financial constraints. Informal networks can also reproduce exclusion and may not identify the best candidate or job.

## What Changed
- Created the relocation-first job-search concept and separated it from remote employment itself.
- Made candidate commitment signaling and candidate-side relocation risk explicit parts of the strategy.
