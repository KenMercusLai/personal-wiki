## Definition
[[PersuasionWork]] is paid human activity centered on selling, branding, advertising, client cultivation, relationship management, and customer experience rather than producing a good or executing a well-defined service task.

## Current Synthesis
The source proposes persuasion work as one destination for labor released by automation. When machines handle cash, retrieve warehouse goods, arrange purchases, or perform standardized legal and medical tasks, people may retain employment by helping customers choose, trust, enjoy, or buy what automated systems produce and deliver. The ATM example makes this a within-job transition: teller work shifts from transaction handling toward the customer-relationship team.

The category has no fixed social value. Information and comparison can improve matching, while endorsement, identity, and hospitality can add real experiential value. Yet rival firms can also spend heavily to transfer customers among nearly equivalent products, and sales targets can create coercive or fraudulent behavior. Employment resilience therefore does not establish productive or socially beneficial reallocation.

## Key Claims
- Partial automation can increase the share of a job devoted to selling, relationships, and customer experience without eliminating the occupation.
- Persuasion remains economically useful when it informs choice, improves matching, or makes an experience more valuable to the customer.
- Competitive marketing can be zero- or negative-sum when it mostly reallocates demand among similar offerings.
- Economies of scale and available monopoly profit may finance more costly competition for customer attention.
- A shift toward persuasion work can help explain why visible automation and continued employment coexist with weak measured productivity growth.
- Sales pressure can degrade into manipulation or fraud when worker incentives reward account creation or switching without customer benefit.

## Evidence
Role redesign:
- [[in-a-robot-economy-all-humans-will-be-marketers-bloomberg]] uses [[JamesBessen]]’s ATM example to show bank tellers moving from cash handling toward marketing and customer relationships.

Cross-sector reach:
- [[in-a-robot-economy-all-humans-will-be-marketers-bloomberg]] identifies client cultivation in legal work, patient experience and acquisition in medicine, demand creation around automated warehouses, and restaurant greeting around automated ordering.

Useful persuasion:
- [[in-a-robot-economy-all-humans-will-be-marketers-bloomberg]] credits marketing with product information, better matching, and endorsement-driven enjoyment.

Waste and misconduct:
- [[in-a-robot-economy-all-humans-will-be-marketers-bloomberg]] contrasts large brand-switching expenditures with small product differences and cites Wells Fargo’s unauthorized accounts as a darker sales-pressure outcome.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one 2017 opinion essay, not a measured occupational-flow, productivity, or welfare analysis. It assumes that machines remain relatively weak at persuasion, branding, and interpersonal welcome, a historical boundary that later AI capabilities may narrow. The ATM example does not establish the same mechanism across sectors, and continued headcount can coexist with wage, hours, surveillance, target-pressure, or job-quality changes. Marketing’s social value also depends on product differences, consumer information, market structure, measurement boundaries, and whose welfare is counted.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to distinguish employment-preserving labor reallocation from socially productive labor reallocation.
- Separated informative and experience-enhancing persuasion from market-share transfer, manipulation, and fraud.
