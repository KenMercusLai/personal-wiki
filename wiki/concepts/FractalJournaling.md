## Definition
[[FractalJournaling]] is a layered review practice in which small timestamped thought fragments are periodically condensed into broader summaries, which are then reviewed again at longer intervals.

## Current Synthesis
In [[StephAngo]]'s workflow, individual thoughts receive date-and-time-prefixed notes, salient fragments are compiled every few days, those reviews are revisited monthly, and monthly reviews feed yearly reflection. The resulting hierarchy preserves a path from broad themes back to their detailed origins. Random note traversal and shallow local graphs complement scheduled aggregation by resurfacing material that did not become salient during regular review.

## Key Claims
- Timestamped fragments make capture fast while preserving when an idea occurred.
- Repeated condensation lets a journal support both close detail and progressively broader themes.
- Keeping links back to earlier notes preserves provenance instead of replacing raw experience with only a summary.
- Random revisits complement scheduled reviews by exposing forgotten ideas and missing relationships.
- Manual review can be valuable as a form of self-understanding, not merely as maintenance overhead.

## Evidence
- Capture layer: [[how-i-use-obsidian]] uses unique notes prefixed with `YYYY-MM-DD HHmm` for thoughts arising during the day.
- Review hierarchy: [[how-i-use-obsidian]] compiles fragments every few days, reviews those reviews monthly, and revisits monthly material yearly.
- Traceability: [[how-i-use-obsidian]] describes zooming among levels of detail and tracing larger themes back to individual thoughts.
- Serendipitous maintenance: [[how-i-use-obsidian]] uses random notes and shallow local graphs to revisit ideas, add links, find inspiration, and update formatting.
- Human role: [[how-i-use-obsidian]] explicitly declines language-model automation because the maintenance process itself reveals personal patterns.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source provides one practitioner's method and enjoyment of the process, not evidence that the cadence improves memory, insight, well-being, or output. Multi-stage review may become burdensome, summaries can omit low-salience material that later matters, and random traversal can consume time without producing useful connections. Different capture volumes and goals may require different intervals or no formal hierarchy.
