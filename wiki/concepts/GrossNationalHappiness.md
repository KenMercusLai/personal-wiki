## Definition
[[GrossNationalHappiness]] is a public-policy frame that treats collective wellbeing as a more important national objective than Gross Domestic Product alone.

## Current Synthesis
The current source associates the concept with [[Bhutan]]'s king in 1972 and uses it to explain the country's international reputation for prioritizing happiness, culture, and conservation. Its strongest contribution is normative: economic output is not a complete measure of a country's success. The source does not, however, explain the metric's dimensions or measurement method, show policy-to-outcome causality, or verify that Bhutan is the world's happiest country.

## Key Claims
- National performance can be evaluated through human wellbeing rather than GDP alone.
- In the source, Bhutan's conservation and cultural-preservation choices are presented as consistent with this broader objective.
- A policy commitment to happiness is distinct from evidence that residents are happier than people elsewhere.
- The concept creates a possible standard for judging development projects by social and cultural effects as well as economic growth.

## Evidence
- Policy priority: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-the-sights-and-sounds-of-bhutan]] reports that Bhutan's king elevated Gross National Happiness over Gross Domestic Product in 1972.
- Observed consistency: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-the-sights-and-sounds-of-bhutan]] connects the frame to conservation, restricted tourism, and cultural preservation.
- Measurement limit: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-the-sights-and-sounds-of-bhutan]] explicitly says the author could not verify Bhutan's reputation as the world's happiest country.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers no index definition, time series, international comparison, resident survey, distributional analysis, or causal evaluation. A government's stated objective may differ from lived wellbeing, and a short guided visit cannot reveal dissent, inequality, political tradeoffs, or the costs of restricted openness. The concept should therefore be treated here as a policy orientation, not as proof of measured national happiness.

## What Changed
- Created a qualified definition that separates the policy objective from claims about measured happiness.
- Connected the concept to the unresolved tradeoff between preservation and opportunity in Bhutan.
