## Definition
[[ArtBlock]] is a source-scoped form of creative inhibition in which pressure, self-criticism, comparison, or fear of failure makes starting or continuing visual art difficult.

## Current Synthesis
The available source treats art block as a family of experiences rather than a single diagnosis. Its detailed mechanism is expectation-driven: an artist compares current work with professional standards, attends mainly to flaws, and interprets the gap as failure despite real improvement. Two interventions lower that pressure from different directions. Retrospective comparison makes progress visible by placing current work beside one's own older work, while permission to make a poor or playful next piece reduces the demand that every attempt prove ability.

## Key Claims
- Art block can emerge when improvement goals harden into expectations that current skill cannot reliably satisfy.
- Flaw-focused self-assessment can hide progress that becomes obvious in a longer time horizon.
- Comparing current work with one's own older work offers a more informative baseline than comparing only with professionals.
- Explicit permission to make an imperfect next piece can lower fear and restore experimentation.
- Continued low-stakes making can reconnect creative activity with enjoyment and generate new feedback.

## Evidence
Progress perspective:
- [[evolving-as-an-artist-and-overcoming-art-block-talk-illustration]] pairs Loish's 2004 and 2016 drawings, where changes in volume, anatomy, gesture, detail, and line confidence make development visible.

Expectation and comparison mechanism:
- [[evolving-as-an-artist-and-overcoming-art-block-talk-illustration]] quotes Loish describing high expectations, stress, and fear of failure as one common form of art block and recommends noticing improvement and reducing professional comparison.

Lower-stakes making:
- [[evolving-as-an-artist-and-overcoming-art-block-talk-illustration]] reports that accepting the possibility of a poor result and drawing for fun reduced pressure for the author.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The article explicitly says there are multiple kinds of art block but examines only one in depth. Its evidence consists of selected before-and-after drawings and two practitioners' reflections, so it does not establish prevalence, causation, treatment effectiveness, or whether the advice generalizes across media and working conditions. Persistent inability to work may also involve burnout, depression, anxiety, material constraints, hostile environments, or health problems that self-compassion and playful practice alone do not resolve.

## What Changed
- Established an initial model of expectation-driven art block and two pressure-reducing responses.
