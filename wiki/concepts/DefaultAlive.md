## Definition
[[DefaultAlive]] is the projected condition in which a startup reaches breakeven before exhausting its cash if expenses remain unchanged and revenue continues growing at its recent rate; otherwise it is default dead.

## Current Synthesis
The test joins growth and [[StartupRunway]] rather than treating either in isolation. A company can be growing and still fail if burn consumes its cash before revenue catches expenses. Naming the state in recurring investor updates creates a forcing function against denial and makes headcount or spending decisions legible in terms of time to breakeven.

The result is a scenario, not a forecast. It inherits the uncertainty of the chosen growth window and assumes stable expenses even though churn, gross margin, hiring, pricing, seasonality, and financing can change the path. Its strongest use is therefore diagnostic: expose the current trajectory early enough to cut burn, improve the business, or raise from a position stronger than imminent cash exhaustion.

## Key Claims
- Revenue growth is not sufficient evidence of survival when the company may run out of cash before reaching breakeven.
- Combining burn, cash, and recent revenue growth makes an otherwise vague runway risk explicit.
- Reporting the state regularly can challenge founder denial before a financing emergency.
- Overhiring moves a company toward default dead by raising fixed expense and reducing its margin for delays.
- The classification is assumption-sensitive and should be accompanied by scenario analysis rather than treated as a certain outcome.

## Evidence
- Survival test: [[jessica-livingstons-pretty-complete-list-on-how-not-to-fail]] defines default alive as reaching breakeven before cash runs out under unchanged expenses and continued revenue growth.
- Operating use: [[jessica-livingstons-pretty-complete-list-on-how-not-to-fail]] says YC asks founders to lead investor updates with whether they are default alive or default dead.
- Expense mechanism: [[jessica-livingstons-pretty-complete-list-on-how-not-to-fail]] connects excessive hiring to higher burn, less time for correction, and emergency fundraising while performance remains weak.
- Financing consequence: [[jessica-livingstons-pretty-complete-list-on-how-not-to-fail]] distinguishes seed investors seeking promise from Series A investors seeking demonstrated performance.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source supplies no formula details, company sample, forecast-error distribution, or evidence that reporting the classification changes outcomes. Constant expense and constant growth are simplifying assumptions, revenue is not gross profit or cash collection, and one recent growth rate may be distorted by base size, churn, seasonality, delayed contracts, or one-off acquisition. A default-dead company may survive through cost changes, financing, or improved economics, while a nominally default-alive company can still fail through retention loss, margin deterioration, operational shocks, or strategic error.

## What Changed
- Created a distinct cash-survival concept that combines growth, burn, runway, and breakeven.
- Framed the alive/dead label as an assumption-sensitive forcing function rather than a prediction.
