## Definition
[[FounderInvestorFit]] is the two-way assessment of whether a founder and prospective investor have enough informed conviction, mutual respect, and compatible working behavior to justify a long-term financing relationship.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats a fundraising meeting as more than a one-way test of whether the founder can persuade. A skeptical question can improve assumptions and storytelling when it comes from relevant knowledge and substantive engagement, but an unsupported assertion can instead reveal a poor working relationship. Because an investor may remain on the cap table long after the pitch, the founder must evaluate the investor's judgment and conduct alongside the probability of receiving capital. In the reported case, ending the meeting protected time and energy after the founder concluded both that investment was unlikely and that the investor was not a desirable partner.

## Key Claims
- Fundraising is a mutual selection process, even though the investor often has more immediate power in the meeting.
- Useful skepticism is grounded in relevant knowledge or reasoning and can help a founder improve assumptions or communication.
- Unsupported demands to “convince me” may test compliance or establish dominance without producing useful learning.
- Cap-table relationships make interpersonal and judgment fit relevant beyond the immediate probability of closing a round.
- A founder can rationally disengage when a meeting is unlikely to produce either investment or decision-useful feedback.

## Evidence
- Substantive-challenge value: [[convince-me-said-the-investor-no-said-the-founder-hunter-walk]] says disagreement can stress-test founder assumptions and improve storytelling.
- Low-information rejection: [[convince-me-said-the-investor-no-said-the-founder-hunter-walk]] describes an investor asserting that a nascent market would not exist without supplying relevant experience or a basis for the claim.
- Power interpretation: [[convince-me-said-the-investor-no-said-the-founder-hunter-walk]] interprets the demand as a possible test or attempt to establish power rather than a thesis-based discussion.
- Relationship test: [[convince-me-said-the-investor-no-said-the-founder-hunter-walk]] argues that the investor was both unlikely to join the cap table and probably not someone the founder wanted there.
- Disengagement decision: [[convince-me-said-the-investor-no-said-the-founder-hunter-walk]] reports that the founder respectfully ended the meeting rather than spending more time and energy persuading him.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept is grounded in a single founder anecdote retold by an investor-author, without the other participant's account. The article does not define an objective boundary between a useful stress test and a power move, and founders can misread terse skepticism, avoid necessary challenge, or prematurely reject investors who need clearer evidence. Fit also does not erase financing constraints: founders with fewer options may be unable to disengage as readily, while oversubscribed rounds give founders more selection power. The strongest conclusion is therefore a decision heuristic, not evidence that refusal reliably improves fundraising outcomes.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from Walk's account of a founder ending an adversarial, low-information investor meeting.
