## Definition
[[PrivacyPovertyDivide]] is the unequal distribution of privacy protection, privacy burden, and privacy harm across class and marginalization.

## Current Synthesis
Privacy is not experienced as a uniform digital-consumer problem. Low-income people often face intrusive collection as a condition of welfare benefits, employment, housing, credit, research participation, prison communication, or access to basic services, while wealthier people receive government transfers and market opportunities with fewer verification burdens. The divide also runs in both directions: marginalized people may be over-surveilled when they need resources and under-documented when records are necessary to prove rights or receive attention. Thompson's claim that many users trade privacy for valuable free services adds a market-choice frame, but this evidence makes the inference from acceptance to indifference unsafe: saving money and lacking viable alternatives can make disclosure a constrained choice.

## Key Claims
- Privacy loss can operate as an additional tax on poverty when survival requires intrusive disclosure.
- Poor and marginalized groups are often early targets for surveillance techniques that later normalize elsewhere.
- Data privacy harms are more concrete for low-income people because records affect benefits, housing, jobs, credit, scams, and predatory offers.
- Privacy self-defense is unequal because time, money, skills, and authoritative advice are unevenly distributed.
- Too little data can also be harmful when people lack records needed to claim rights, resources, or political visibility.
- Continued use of a free data-funded service does not by itself show privacy indifference when money, alternatives, and exit capacity are unequal.

## Evidence
- Survival disclosure: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] describes welfare applicants accepting home visits, personal questions, biometric collection, and database checks to receive benefits.
- Poor-first surveillance: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] cites [[MicheleGilman]]'s warning that surveillance techniques are typically used first on poor people.
- Concrete harms: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] links personal data to benefits denials, housing and job exclusion, predatory financial marketing, and scams.
- Resource inequality: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] uses [[MaryMadden]] and [[ElissaRedmiles]] to show that privacy protection consumes scarce time, skill, money, and advice access.
- Too little data: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] explains that undocumented immigrants, day laborers, homeless people, and people with convictions may be harmed by lack of records or formal visibility.
- Market-choice claim: [[data-factories-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] argues that most users accept privacy costs in exchange for valuable free services and should be empowered through processed-profile disclosure.
- Constrained-choice qualification: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] shows that low-income people can express greater privacy concern while still lacking the resources to refuse collection or repair harms.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The poverty source focuses on U.S. conditions as of 2019 and does not compare later state regimes, benefits changes, or non-U.S. systems. Thompson's 2018 essay offers no representative evidence for the claim that most users do not care about privacy and does not distinguish indifference from price sensitivity, dependency, resignation, or lack of alternatives. Neither source denies that higher-income people face privacy harms; the synthesis is that stakes, exposure, and available defenses are stratified.

## What Changed
- Added a direct qualification to market-choice accounts: use of free services can reflect constrained acceptance rather than low privacy concern.
- Added processed-profile disclosure as potentially useful but insufficient where exit and self-protection capacity are unequal.
