## Definition
[[InterpersonalBoundarySetting]] is the deliberate choice of what conduct, access, timing, setting, and degree of engagement a person will accept in a relationship or interaction.

## Current Synthesis
The source's useful shift is from trying to correct another person's personality to governing one's own participation. Awareness creates a pause between provocation and response; emotional distance keeps attention on observable conduct; and an explicit boundary specifies when, where, or how engagement will continue. This can preserve agency when leaving a workplace, household, or relationship is not immediately possible.

The boundary model is stronger than the article's ten-person taxonomy because it does not require certainty about motive or character. A response can be calibrated to repeated behavior and actual harm without declaring someone permanently "toxic." Boundaries also have limits: they do not by themselves make an unsafe person cooperative, redistribute institutional power, resolve abuse, or replace support, documentation, escalation, or exit planning.

## Key Claims
- Effective boundaries govern one's own access and participation rather than claiming control over another person's inner state.
- Pattern recognition and a pause before responding can reduce reactive escalation.
- Focusing on observable conduct is safer than inferring a fixed personality, motive, or diagnosis.
- Limits should specify the conditions of engagement and be applied consistently to remain credible.
- Selective engagement may be more realistic than total avoidance in shared homes and workplaces.
- Boundaries are one protective tool, not a complete response to coercion, abuse, or severe power imbalance.

## Evidence
- Emotional distance: [[travis-bradberry-10-toxic-people-you-should-avoid-at-all-costs]] recommends observing facts rather than answering emotional chaos in kind.
- Deliberate response: [[travis-bradberry-10-toxic-people-you-should-avoid-at-all-costs]] advises regrouping and buying time before choosing a way forward.
- Engagement conditions: [[travis-bradberry-10-toxic-people-you-should-avoid-at-all-costs]] argues that people can decide when and where to interact even when work or living arrangements prevent total separation.
- Consistency: [[travis-bradberry-10-toxic-people-you-should-avoid-at-all-costs]] says limits must be maintained when challenged.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source offers no measured outcomes showing that its strategy reduces stress, conflict, or harm. Its labels collapse heterogeneous conduct into fixed identities, assume motives that may not be observable, and can encourage contempt or premature cutoff. Emotional distance can also become avoidance, suppression, or disengagement from legitimate criticism. In unsafe or coercive situations, direct boundary enforcement may provoke retaliation; context may require trusted support, formal reporting, professional advice, documentation, safety planning, or exit rather than repeated one-to-one negotiation.

## What Changed
- Created a behavior-based account of boundary setting while rejecting the need for fixed "toxic person" labels.
- Distinguished selective engagement from the article's stronger rhetoric of total avoidance.
- Added power, safety, and evidence limits absent from the source's practical advice.
