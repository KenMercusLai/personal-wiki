## Definition
[[EntrepreneurialQualityAndScale]] is [[VentureHacks]]' source-specific standard that entrepreneurship must both advance each customer more substantially and extend that benefit to more people.

## Current Synthesis
The framework defines quality as depth of customer advancement and scale as breadth of use, then makes their joint pursuit the distinguishing obligation of an entrepreneur. A high-quality product with little reach leaves its value unrealized, while a widely used product that does not improve customer outcomes is merely business as usual and remains vulnerable to better competitors. The useful strategic principle is that teams should resist assuming a fixed tradeoff: when customer advancement and accessibility conflict, innovation should search for a design, process, or business model that improves both. The categorical label is less defensible than the operating principle, because the essay offers a stipulative definition of entrepreneurship rather than evidence that local, craft, lifestyle, or non-growth ventures are not entrepreneurial.

## Key Claims
- Quality should be judged by customer advancement, not by polish or producer effort alone.
- Scale should be judged by the number of people reached, not by organizational size alone.
- Entrepreneurial work seeks depth of benefit and breadth of access together.
- A perceived quality-scale tradeoff is a problem to innovate around rather than a permanent constraint to accept.
- Scale without improved customer outcomes creates competitive exposure.
- Improvement is open-ended whenever a product can become better or more accessible.

## Evidence
- Joint standard: [[venture-hacks-there-is-no-finish-line-for-entrepreneurs]] explicitly defines quality as customer advancement and scale as number of users, then requires both.
- Quality without reach: [[venture-hacks-there-is-no-finish-line-for-entrepreneurs]] uses a great non-scaling Italian restaurant as its example of value that does not meet the essay's entrepreneurial standard.
- Reach without advancement: [[venture-hacks-there-is-no-finish-line-for-entrepreneurs]] uses McDonald's as its example of scale without an attempt to advance product quality.
- Innovation response: [[venture-hacks-there-is-no-finish-line-for-entrepreneurs]] says that if quality and scale cannot both be achieved through the current approach, the entrepreneur must innovate.
- Competitive pressure: [[venture-hacks-there-is-no-finish-line-for-entrepreneurs]] argues that scale without quality leaves a business open to competitors that serve customers better.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework comes from one very short practitioner essay and supplies no empirical evidence that quality and scale never conflict, that innovation can always reconcile them, or that firms pursuing both outperform alternatives. Its definitions are unusually narrow: quality may also include reliability, safety, usability, durability, or fit to purpose, while scale may concern transactions, geography, capacity, or economics rather than user count alone. The restaurant examples assert rather than establish their product-quality and growth intentions. Most importantly, the page records Venture Hacks' normative standard, not a consensus definition of entrepreneurship; local businesses, craftspeople, lifestyle companies, cooperatives, and early-stage ventures can reasonably be described as entrepreneurial under other definitions.

## What Changed
- Created a source-scoped model that treats customer advancement and reach as joint entrepreneurial objectives.
- Distinguished product reach from organizational scale and product polish from customer outcomes.
- Preserved the definition's empirical and taxonomic limits instead of universalizing it.

- [[DoingThingsThatDoNotScale]] - temporary high-touch work can support learning and later reach even though it is not itself scaled.
- [[StartupScaling]] - concerns the organizational systems needed as reach grows rather than defining scale only as user count.
- [[ProductMarketFit]] - customer demand tests whether claimed advancement produces real pull, but does not by itself establish quality.
