## Definition
[[StartupCounterpartyDiligence]] is the independent verification of a prospective client's, supplier's, partner's, or investor's identity, authority, financial capacity, track record, commercial claims, payment behavior, and system access before a startup makes material plans or dependencies around that counterparty.

## Current Synthesis
The Reframed retrospective shows why diligence is not limited to an investor background check. One person reportedly occupied four roles at once: client, advertising supplier, commercial-model informant, and prospective lead investor. Evidence from one role then appeared to validate the others: fast technical delivery suggested competence, claimed advertiser interest suggested revenue, and the promised investment suggested conviction. Because these signals originated from the same counterparty, they were correlated representations rather than independent confirmation.

The operational lesson is to verify each dependency at the point where it can become consequential. Funding plans require identity, decision authority, proof of funds, signed terms, closing conditions, and cash receipt to remain distinct states. Revenue forecasts require contracts, counterparty confirmation, collectability, and bank reconciliation rather than invoices alone. Supplier access requires least privilege, auditability, revocation, and a recovery path. Role concentration raises the diligence bar because one failure can simultaneously remove revenue, infrastructure, forecast credibility, and financing.

## Key Claims
- A promise, term sheet, invoice, valuation, or apparent transaction is not equivalent to cleared cash or independently confirmed revenue.
- Multiple commercial roles held by the same party create correlated risk and should not be mistaken for several independent validation signals.
- Identity, employment history, legal entities, adverse judgments, payment record, funds, authority, customer relationships, and contract status are separate verification questions.
- Technical access should be bounded so that loss of trust in a supplier does not require an emergency rebuild to protect customers or partners.
- Diligence should precede hiring, spending, investor signaling, and forecasts that depend on the counterparty, then continue as new claims or access are added.
- Counterparty deception can be a proximate cause without excusing an unviable business model, weak controls, or founders' responsibility for dependency choices.

## Evidence
- Role concentration: [[from-4-million-to-broke-this-is-our-startup-horror-story]] says the same operator acted as Reframed's client, ad supplier, model adviser, and prospective lead investor.
- Late verification: [[from-4-million-to-broke-this-is-our-startup-horror-story]] reports that a third party's unpaid-invoice warning prompted searches for court judgments and associated companies, followed by a request for proof of funds.
- Funding-state confusion: [[from-4-million-to-broke-this-is-our-startup-horror-story]] describes planning around £250,000 of prospective lead capital and £100,000 from incumbents before the lead commitment was withdrawn.
- Revenue verification: [[from-4-million-to-broke-this-is-our-startup-horror-story]] says the supposed direct advertisements proved to be generally available affiliate links and that advertisers had no known relationship with Reframed.
- Access risk: [[from-4-million-to-broke-this-is-our-startup-horror-story]] says Reframed rebuilt its advertising system before a conference because it could no longer trust the supplier not to inject malicious content.
- Cash integrity: [[from-4-million-to-broke-this-is-our-startup-horror-story]] reports that voiding unpaid client invoices reduced apparent runway to the end of the month.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one founder's retrospective account, not a controlled comparison or independently documented fraud case. It does not establish which specific checks were legally or commercially available, what their cost would have been, whether earlier diligence would have preserved Reframed, or how often similar warning signs predict misconduct. Proof-of-funds screenshots, public-record searches, references, contracts, and credentials can themselves be incomplete or forged; high-confidence decisions may require regulated professionals, primary records, direct institutional confirmation, staged commitments, and controls proportional to the exposure. Excessive diligence can also slow ordinary transactions, disclose sensitive information, or exclude legitimate counterparties, so the goal is risk-calibrated verification rather than suspicion without limit.

## What Changed
- Created a cross-role diligence concept spanning clients, suppliers, partners, and investors.
- Distinguished correlated representations from independent commercial validation.
- Connected financial verification to technical access control and recovery planning.
