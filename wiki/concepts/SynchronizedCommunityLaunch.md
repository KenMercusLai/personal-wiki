## Definition
[[SynchronizedCommunityLaunch]] is a product-distribution strategy that recruits a bounded existing community and coordinates its first exposure or activation so enough connected people can experience the product together.

## Current Synthesis
The TBH case treats fragmentation as a central launch risk for social products. Broad publicity may yield more total attention while placing each user among too few participating friends. A synchronized launch instead chooses a community boundary, assembles interest without immediately exposing the product, and releases access or notification at a socially relevant time. The intended effect is local critical mass: the product becomes usable and discussable within the group as soon as people arrive.

In TBH's reported implementation, school names in public Instagram profiles defined the audience; a mysterious private account generated curiosity; a 24-hour delay accumulated requests; and a switch to public status after school triggered acceptance notifications while exposing the App Store link. The mechanism is therefore not timing alone. It combines segmentation, latent demand, platform notifications, peer density, and a product whose value depends on other community members.

## Key Claims
- Community density can matter more than the raw number of early users for products whose value depends on known peers.
- Delaying individual activation can aggregate latent interest into a coordinated launch moment.
- Existing routines such as school dismissal can supply a shared availability window and offline peer reinforcement.
- Platform state changes and notifications can become launch infrastructure even when the launching company does not control them.
- Repeating the process across bounded communities can support pre-fit product testing without exposing the entire potential audience at once.

## Evidence
- Fragmentation problem: [[internal-facebook-note-here-is-a-psychological-trick-to-target-teens]] reproduces TBH's argument that press-driven launches scatter social-product users and prematurely exhaust attention.
- Coordinated mechanism: [[internal-facebook-note-here-is-a-psychological-trick-to-target-teens]] documents the private invitation account, request-collection interval, after-school URL reveal, public-state change, and resulting notifications.
- Iteration use: [[internal-facebook-note-here-is-a-psychological-trick-to-target-teens]] says the team repeated launches every few weeks while changing products and automating the workflow.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The case supplies no school-level conversion, retention, control group, failed launch, or evidence that simultaneity rather than product appeal, novelty, peer pressure, or selection caused adoption. The method was described as non-scalable, and reliance on another platform creates policy, rate-limit, identity, notification, and breakage risk. Using ambiguous invitations and inferred school affiliation to target minors raises privacy, consent, and manipulation concerns. Coordinated acquisition also cannot establish durable product-market fit: Facebook later closed TBH for low usage.

## What Changed
- Added community density and synchronized activation as distinct launch variables.
- Distinguished accumulating interest from immediately routing each prospect to the product.
- Added ethical and platform-dependence constraints to the critical-mass mechanism.
