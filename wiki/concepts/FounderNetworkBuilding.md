## Definition
[[FounderNetworkBuilding]] is the long-term practice of building relationships with useful, smart, and trusted people by creating value before asking for help.

## Current Synthesis
The source treats founder networking as an operating asset rather than a conference ritual. Relationships matter because startups pass through hard periods where judgment, introductions, partnerships, and outside perspective can change decisions. The recommended method is not transactional favor-trading: founders should identify people worth knowing, help them or trusted people around them, and earn the right to ask for warm introductions or guidance later.

## Key Claims
- Relationships can save a startup during difficult periods by providing guidance and confidence.
- Valuable relationships can create partnerships, introductions, and strategic decisions.
- Networking is not business-card exchange because the card itself has no value.
- The durable method is to help people with something they actually value before expecting anything back.
- Warm introductions are often the practical route to busy or successful people.

## Evidence
- Guidance and resilience: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] says relationships with smart people saved the author many times during difficult startup periods.
- Concrete opportunities: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] cites a product partnership, an interview-series introduction, and a remote-team decision as relationship-driven outcomes for [[Groove]].
- Value-first approach: [[7-no-nonsense-pieces-of-startup-advice-i-wish-i-got-when-i-started]] recommends helping target contacts or people they trust with no expectation of immediate return.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source relies on founder experience and examples rather than a comparative study. Network-building can become distraction if founders confuse broad visibility with useful relationships, or if "helping" is really a disguised sales pitch.
