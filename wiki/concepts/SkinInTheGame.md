## Definition
[[SkinInTheGame]] is alignment between a person's judgment or action and meaningful exposure to the benefits and harms that follow if it succeeds or fails.

## Current Synthesis
The source uses [[NassimNicholasTaleb]] to argue that conviction becomes more credible when the decision-maker bears downside, and that bureaucracy can weaken performance by separating action from consequence. The principle is strongest as an incentive and governance question: who chooses, who benefits, who can observe, and who pays when the choice is wrong? Personal exposure can improve care and honesty, but it does not establish expertise or truth and can become unfair when risks are imposed on people with less power or capacity to absorb them.

## Key Claims
- Bearing downside can align stated belief with consequential action.
- Systems become vulnerable when decision-makers can externalize the harms of being wrong.
- Fear of losing an existing position can promote avoidance and protection of the status quo.
- Risk exposure is evidence of commitment, not proof that the underlying judgment is correct.
- Fair alignment requires attention to who controls the decision and who can actually bear the loss.

## Evidence
- Belief and risk: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] quotes Taleb on willingness to risk something for an opinion.
- Bureaucratic insulation: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] attributes to Taleb a critique of structures that separate people from the consequences of their actions.
- Avoidance and fragility: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] connects feared loss of current position with reduced willingness to take risk.
- Relationship extension: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] argues that shared consequence and mutual investment support relationship freedom.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The supplied evidence is Hardy's selective use of Taleb, not a complete analysis of the original book. Risk-bearing can coexist with incompetence, overconfidence, recklessness, or unethical conduct. Insurance, salary, limited liability, public institutions, and division of labor can pool risk for legitimate reasons. Alignment is also incomplete when leaders choose while workers, customers, families, or the public bear disproportionate downside.

## What Changed
- Created the concept as a decision-rights and consequence-alignment principle rather than a universal demand for personal risk.
