## Definition
[[ProductLeadership]] is the product role of setting and communicating a compelling product vision and strategy strongly enough that teams, executives, and functions can move through risky change together.

## Current Synthesis
The SVPG source distinguishes individual-contributor product management from the product leadership required in larger companies. Startups and small companies may succeed through one strong product team, but large companies often need a leader who can make a future state coherent across finance, legal, marketing, sales, engineering, support, executives, customers, and partners.

[[LeaHickman]]'s [[CreativeCloud]] case is the article's clearest product-leadership example. Adobe's move from Creative Suite licenses to a subscription service threatened a roughly $2B annual license business and touched nearly every function. Hickman's contribution is framed as creating a compelling vision, using prototypes with Kevin Lynch to make the new foundation concrete, and communicating continuously so many interdependent teams could move in parallel.

## Key Claims
- Product leadership becomes more important as product change crosses many functions, teams, and revenue dependencies.
- A compelling product vision helps organizations accept risky transitions before all local concerns are resolved.
- Prototypes can make a strategic future concrete enough for executives and product teams to support.
- Continuous communication is part of product leadership, especially when finance, legal, sales, marketing, engineering, and customers all face different forms of risk.
- Product leadership includes self-disruption when protecting an existing revenue model would leave the company strategically exposed.

## Evidence
- Leadership need in large companies: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says larger companies usually need strong product leadership as well as strong product teams.
- Vision and strategy: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says Hickman needed to articulate a compelling whole greater than the sum of its parts.
- Prototype persuasion: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says Hickman worked with Kevin Lynch on prototypes showing the power of the new foundation.
- Continuous communication: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] describes a sustained campaign of communication with leaders and stakeholders across Adobe.
- Self-disruption: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] frames Creative Cloud as Adobe disrupting its own Creative Suite revenue model before external disruption forced the issue.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source presents product leadership through a successful retrospective, so it may understate how much Adobe's transition also depended on market timing, executive sponsorship, customer alternatives, pricing details, and implementation execution. It also reflects SVPG's product-leadership theory rather than an independent organizational study.

## What Changed
- Created Product Leadership as the large-company, vision-and-strategy form of product work.
