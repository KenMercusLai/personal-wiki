## Definition
[[DataGeneratingProcess]] is the assumed real or simulated system of factors, interactions, and noise that produces observed data.

## Current Synthesis
RORIRI uses body weight as an intuitive example: diet, activity, metabolism, mood, workplace snacks, age, sex, muscle mass, and many smaller factors jointly generate the observed outcome. Thinking in DGP terms keeps statistics from becoming detached formula work. Before decomposing variance, interpreting correlations, or trusting regression coefficients, the analyst should ask what system could have produced the observed variation, which variables may share sources of movement, and which relevant causes were left outside the model as [[StatisticalError]].

## Key Claims
- Observed data are outputs of a layered system rather than isolated numbers.
- Modeling starts by asking how variables may have been produced and connected.
- DGP thinking helps explain why clean textbook decompositions often fail in real samples.
- Omitted pieces of the generating process become part of the model's error term.
- Regression bias depends on whether omitted causes are related to included predictors.
- Simulated code examples can make the dynamism of a data-generating process visible.

## Evidence
- Outcome generation: [[li-jie-fang-cha-de-ke-jia-xing-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] decomposes weight into diet, activity, metabolism, and further nested causes.
- Simulation practice: [[li-jie-fang-cha-de-ke-jia-xing-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] asks readers to rerun code examples to feel how generated samples vary.
- Shared movement: [[li-jie-fang-cha-de-ke-jia-xing-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] uses covariance to show that total variation includes interactions among variables, not only isolated factors.
- Error term: [[li-jie-wu-cha-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] describes the true model as an unreachable full formula whose omitted factors become the model's error.
- Omitted-variable case: [[li-jie-wu-cha-luo-li-li-de-shu-ju-zhong-xin]] shows that leaving out exercise can bias calorie-intake coefficients when exercise also moves with calorie intake.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The articles use DGP primarily as an explanatory frame, not as a full causal-inference method. Knowing that data were generated by a system does not identify the system's true causal structure without design, measurement, and assumptions. The full generating process remains partly inaccessible, so analysts use simplified models and residual diagnostics under explicit limitations.

## What Changed
- Added error terms and omitted variables as regression consequences of incomplete DGP knowledge.
