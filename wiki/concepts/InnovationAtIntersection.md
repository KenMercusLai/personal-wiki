## Definition
[[InnovationAtIntersection]] is the pattern of creating new ideas by recombining older ideas, observations, techniques, or datasets from different fields, roles, or knowledge domains.

## Current Synthesis
The source presents innovation as recombination rather than pure invention from nothing. Its strongest examples move across levels: [[MickPearce]] uses termite-mound temperature regulation to inform [[EastgateCentre]]'s building design, while SaaS teams combine customer-success pain points, sales objections, marketing assets, finance constraints, and data sources to generate better business hypotheses.

The concept is also organizational. Because modern knowledge has become too large for one person to master, [[BenjaminJones]]'s burden-of-knowledge argument turns innovation into a team-design problem: progress depends on assembling complementary expertise and making the interfaces between those experts productive.

## Key Claims
- New ideas often come from recombining existing ideas rather than inventing from a blank slate.
- Cross-field combinations can produce practical design insights, as in biomimetic architecture.
- Modern specialization makes complementary teams more important than lone generalists.
- Business innovation can happen in ordinary meetings when teams combine partial observations.
- Data integration is one mechanism for making different organizational knowledge bases meet.

## Evidence
- Recombination frame: [[all-new-ideas-are-combinations-of-old-ideas]] opens with [[FransJohansson]]'s claim that new ideas are combinations of old ones.
- Architecture example: [[all-new-ideas-are-combinations-of-old-ideas]] describes [[MickPearce]] designing [[EastgateCentre]] with techniques inspired by termite nests and passive temperature regulation.
- Knowledge burden: [[all-new-ideas-are-combinations-of-old-ideas]] cites [[BenjaminJones]] on the decline of the Renaissance-person model under expanding modern knowledge.
- SaaS team examples: [[all-new-ideas-are-combinations-of-old-ideas]] contrasts what customer success, sales, marketing, and finance each know about pain points, objections, case studies, promotions, and cash impact.
- Data intersection: [[all-new-ideas-are-combinations-of-old-ideas]] cites [[TwilioSegment]] Sources and [[Looker]] Blocks as a way to analyze funnels across many customer lifecycle stages.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source is a short strategy essay, not an empirical theory of innovation. It does not show how to evaluate which combinations are valuable, how to avoid superficial analogy, or how to manage coordination costs when teams with different expertise collaborate. Its termite-building example is illustrative; the source does not independently analyze the engineering details behind Eastgate's performance.

## What Changed
- Created the concept to connect cross-field creativity, specialization, and business-team collaboration.
