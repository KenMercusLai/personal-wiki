## Definition
[[PricingPsychology]] is the study and practical use of perceptual, cognitive, linguistic, and contextual mechanisms that affect how people notice, interpret, compare, and choose prices.

## Current Synthesis
The current evidence portrays a displayed price as one input to a constructed judgment, not a self-sufficient signal. People compare it with remembered, expected, competing, advertised, and merely adjacent numbers; its apparent magnitude can also inherit cues from words, position, typography, color, spacing, and pronunciation. Fluent numerical relationships can make offers feel coherent, while anchors and assortment structure change the baseline against which value is judged. These mechanisms are conditional: a cue that implies cheapness in one setting can imply poor quality, manipulation, distance, or irrelevance in another. Pricing tactics therefore need experiments tied to business outcomes and trust, not only short-run choice.

## Key Claims
- Price judgments depend on reference points, including prior prices, competitors, expectations, adjacent numbers, and assortment order.
- Semantic and visual magnitude cues can transfer to a nearby price and alter perceived expensiveness.
- Digit structure and phonetic fluency can affect processing, recall, attention, and fit with the product or bundle.
- Left-digit anchoring makes some just-below prices feel materially lower despite a minimal economic difference.
- Promotion frames change attention: percent versus amount, coupon versus integrated reduction, and round versus precise discounts imply different benefits or urgency.
- Decoys and anchors can redirect comparison toward a target option without improving that option's underlying economics.
- Effects are heterogeneous and can reverse with product type, timing, numeracy, audience, medium, discount size, and visual execution.

## Evidence
- Reference construction and anchoring: [[nick-kolenda-pricing-a-list-of-tactics]] assembles evidence on prior and competitor comparisons, descending price order, incidental anchors, large nearby numbers, and decoy options.
- Perceptual magnitude: [[nick-kolenda-pricing-a-list-of-tactics]] connects smallness words, leftward location, font scale, currency-symbol treatment, and spatial separation with judgments of size or savings.
- Numerical and verbal fluency: [[nick-kolenda-pricing-a-list-of-tactics]] covers multiples, divisible bundle prices, alliteration, spoken length, price endings, repeated digits, and product-digit congruence.
- Promotion framing: [[nick-kolenda-pricing-a-list-of-tactics]] contrasts amount and percentage discounts, coupons and visible reductions, and round and precise percentages.
- Context boundaries: [[nick-kolenda-pricing-a-list-of-tactics]] explicitly reports caveats, failed replications, pilot findings that did not reach significance, and speculative applications needing validation.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence inventory currently rests on one practitioner synthesis rather than direct review of every cited study. The source mixes peer-reviewed findings, field experiments, secondary examples, author-run A/B tests, pilots, and conjecture, so not every tactic has equal evidential weight. Several mechanisms offer competing prescriptions that only become coherent after specifying the customer, product, medium, time horizon, and desired inference. Short-run purchase intention or option choice also does not establish retained revenue, satisfaction, fairness, or trust. Finally, the saved capture contains only 22 of 102 advertised tactics and cannot represent the full guide.

## What Changed
- Established pricing psychology as a conditional system of reference points, perceptual cues, number fluency, and assortment structure.
- Distinguished cited effects from the author's pilots and speculative extensions.
- Made trust and longer-run business outcomes explicit countermetrics to immediate conversion.
