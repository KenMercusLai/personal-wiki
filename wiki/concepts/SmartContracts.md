## Definition
[[SmartContracts]] are code-mediated contractual mechanisms that can automatically execute or enforce parts of an agreement, reducing some reliance on manual trust, negotiation, or enforcement.

## Current Synthesis
In Dellanna's essay, smart contracts appear as one possible [[TrustMinimizationTechnology]] rather than as a full blockchain topic. Their strategic relevance is that they could reduce the portion of aggregator value tied to making unfamiliar parties safe enough to transact. If contractual performance, payment, or enforcement can move partly into code, some transactions may need less platform-provided trust, though demand ownership and user experience still matter.

## Key Claims
- Smart contracts can be interpreted as trust-minimization infrastructure.
- Their aggregator relevance is strongest where platform value comes from reducing vetting, contracts, insurance, or enforcement costs.
- They are more likely to erode a portion of aggregator profit than to automatically replace demand-owning platforms.
- Code-mediated enforcement still leaves risks around adoption, governance, errors, security, and real-world interfaces.

## Evidence
- Technology example: [[aggregators-and-trust-luca-dellanna]] names smart contracts as a possible future influence on current aggregators.
- Cost replacement: [[aggregators-and-trust-luca-dellanna]] argues that part of aggregator profit comes from replacing costs such as contracts, insurance, vetting, and lawyers.
- Demand qualification: [[aggregators-and-trust-luca-dellanna]] says incumbent demand ownership makes direct aggregator displacement unlikely.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The source does not explain smart-contract architecture, blockchain consensus, legal enforceability, security incidents, oracle problems, or current adoption. This page therefore treats smart contracts only as a trust-minimization example within aggregation theory.
