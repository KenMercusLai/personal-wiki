## Definition
[[CrossMediaSearchResponse]] is the immediate search activity that follows exposure to another medium, such as people using phones to look up a brand, product, advertisement, or event while watching television.

## Current Synthesis
Google's 2016 Super Bowl analysis presents search as a second-screen response to mass television advertising. The reported response was overwhelmingly mobile: smartphones contributed 82% of ad-driven searches, compared with 11% on desktop or laptop and 7% on tablets. More than 7.5 million searches were classified as incremental, and relative activity was highest in the first two quarters. This makes search volume a useful behavioral signal between exposure and deeper outcomes, but not a direct measure of persuasion or economic return. Its interpretation depends on an explicit counterfactual, time window, query rules, device and person deduplication, and downstream outcome evidence that the source does not provide.

## Key Claims
- Television advertising can trigger near-synchronous search behavior on a separate device.
- Smartphone availability makes mobile search the dominant response channel during a live broadcast in this 2016 case.
- Event structure and audience attention can shift response timing; the first half led in 2016, while a lopsided 2014 game reportedly produced more second-half ad searching.
- Brand-search volume can compare which advertisements stimulated curiosity, but rankings without absolute values conceal effect size.
- Incremental search is an intermediate outcome between media exposure and sales, not evidence that the ad changed durable preference or created profitable customers.
- Cross-media measurement needs a credible baseline and transparent attribution rules because coincident search activity may reflect the broadcast, the game, social discussion, prior campaigns, or other publicity.

## Evidence
- Device response: [[google-82-of-super-bowl-ad-searches-happened-on-mobile-up-from-70-search-engine-land]] reports 82% smartphone, 11% desktop or laptop, and 7% tablet shares for ad-driven search.
- Aggregate lift: [[google-82-of-super-bowl-ad-searches-happened-on-mobile-up-from-70-search-engine-land]] reports more than 7.5 million incremental searches and 40% more lift than the preceding year's Super Bowl advertisers.
- Timing: the retained chart in [[google-82-of-super-bowl-ad-searches-happened-on-mobile-up-from-70-search-engine-land]] shows the second quarter narrowly leading the first, followed by the third and fourth quarters, with halftime lowest.
- Advertiser variation: the retained ranking in [[google-82-of-super-bowl-ad-searches-happened-on-mobile-up-from-70-search-engine-land]] places Audi, Acura, Universal Pictures, Honda, and Kia in the top five and shows automotive brands taking four of those positions.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The only source is a brief 2016 report of Google-supplied aggregate data. It gives no raw series, axis units, absolute brand volumes, control group, attribution window, query taxonomy, unique-person count, statistical uncertainty, or independent replication. Search can express confusion, entertainment, comparison, criticism, or pre-existing interest as well as favorable ad response. Device share is also conditional on the event, period, audience, platform coverage, and contemporary device habits. The evidence supports a historically specific behavior pattern, not a universal mobile share or causal return estimate.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from the reported mobile device mix, incremental-search lift, within-game timing, and advertiser ranking.

## Related Concepts
- [[MarketingAttribution]] - assigns credit to the prior media exposure and therefore requires a defensible counterfactual and measurement window.
- [[BrandAdvertising]] - mass-reach advertising can produce immediate branded search without proving longer-term brand or sales effects.
- [[MobileInternet]] - always-available smartphones provide the response surface during another media experience.
- [[CommunicationMultitasking]] - cross-media search is a specific, externally triggered form of simultaneous media and device use.
- [[AttentionManagement]] - game flow, advertisement timing, and competing stimuli shape when viewers redirect attention into search.
