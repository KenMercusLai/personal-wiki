## Definition
[[PodcastDiscovery]] is the process by which listeners encounter shows or episodes they did not already know to seek, through platforms, recommendations, communities, charts, search, or other distribution surfaces.

## Current Synthesis
The bounded source presents discovery as a two-sided matching problem: creators need listeners, while some listeners want more shows but lack an effective path to them. [[DavidKadavy]]'s podcast reportedly gained downloads when episodes appeared on [[ProductHunt]], lost them when an episode was omitted, recovered after delayed submission, and then fell 20–30% when Product Hunt's podcast section shut down. This makes discovery infrastructure economically meaningful to creators, but it also shows how easily an audience can depend on a surface controlled by a platform whose core strategy lies elsewhere.

The evidence is strongest as a channel-dependence case and weakest as a market-size estimate. Downloads do not necessarily represent people or completed listening, bot requests can inflate them, and one show's before-and-after history cannot establish demand for a standalone discovery product. Useful discovery evidence should therefore connect exposure not only to downloads but also to unique human listeners, listening depth, repeat behavior, subscriptions, retention, and direct return.

## Key Claims
- Podcast discovery can create material audience reach for an individual show.
- Platform-owned discovery surfaces can disappear when podcasts no longer fit the owner's strategy.
- A creator's download response can reveal channel dependence without measuring the total discovery market.
- Download counts are an imperfect proxy because automated requests and shallow human traffic may not represent listening.
- Durable discovery should be evaluated through downstream listener behavior, not exposure or downloads alone.

## Evidence
- Channel response: [[just-how-big-is-the-podcast-discovery-gap-startup-grind-medium]] reports lower downloads after an episode was not submitted to Product Hunt and a rebound after delayed submission.
- Channel removal: [[just-how-big-is-the-podcast-discovery-gap-startup-grind-medium]] reports an immediate 20–30% download decline when Product Hunt Podcasts stopped updating and accepting submissions.
- Metric integrity: [[just-how-big-is-the-podcast-discovery-gap-startup-grind-medium]] distinguishes Product Hunt traffic from bot-inflated direct-MP3 requests while acknowledging that some bot traffic may still exist.
- Product inference: [[just-how-big-is-the-podcast-discovery-gap-startup-grind-medium]] proposes a discovery app because the author interprets the download response as unmet listener demand.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The concept currently rests on one creator-authored 2017 case. The source provides annotated screenshots and a percentage estimate rather than exportable event data, does not compare episodes for topic or release timing, and does not measure unique listeners, completion, subscriptions, retention, or sponsor outcomes. Product Hunt said page views did not automatically load MP3 files, but that does not validate every download as human listening. The source also moves from channel response for one show to a broad product opportunity without market sizing, user research, competitive analysis, or evidence that listeners would repeatedly use a dedicated discovery app.

## What Changed
- Created the concept from a historical case linking Product Hunt exposure and withdrawal to one podcast's reported downloads.
- Separated evidence of channel dependence from evidence of total listener demand or product viability.
- Added human listening and retention as necessary qualifications on raw download counts.
