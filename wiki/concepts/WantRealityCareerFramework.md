## Definition
[[WantRealityCareerFramework]] is [[TimUrban]]'s career-decision model in which the best candidate paths come from the overlap between what someone authentically wants and what is realistically reachable through their starting point, target, improvement pace, and persistence.

## Current Synthesis
The framework has two major audits. The Want Box asks what the person actually wants after separating authentic motives from inherited or outdated motives. Urban's Yearning Octopus diagram makes this plural: personal, social, lifestyle, moral, and practical yearnings can all make claims on a career choice, and the yearning hierarchy shows that actions reveal which motives and fears are actually prioritized. The Reality Box asks what career paths are possible after replacing stale conventional wisdom with a current reading of the career landscape, specific game boards, starting point, success threshold, pace of improvement, and persistence. The overlap becomes the option pool, from which the person chooses a next "dot" to test.

The framework's distinctive move is that it does not stop at selecting a path. The diagrams also show a strategy loop where action produces results, feedback, and new information; later loops update the Want side as the person changes and the Reality side as the world changes. Career planning therefore becomes repeated hypothesis testing rather than a one-time identity choice.

## Key Claims
- Career options should be generated from the overlap of authentic wants and realistic reachability.
- Wants need interrogation because motives can come from parents, peers, culture, heroes, past selves, or unexamined conventional wisdom.
- Reality assessment should model a career as a changing game board rather than a fixed ladder or lottery.
- Success likelihood depends on pace and persistence across the whole career game, not only current skill, talent, or credentials.
- Choosing a career dot is a hypothesis test that should feed back into future strategy, self-knowledge, and world knowledge.

- Priority hierarchy: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-how-to-pick-a-career]] uses the inspected shelf/bowl/trash-can diagram to distinguish non-negotiables, top priorities, avoid-failure motives, optional wants, and resisted impulses.
- Reality audit: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-how-to-pick-a-career]] asks readers to assess the current landscape, specific career game boards, starting point, success point, pace, and persistence.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The framework is a reflective decision aid, not a validated predictive model. Its language of wants and reality can make constraints more visible, but it may still understate structural barriers, discrimination, health limits, immigration limits, caregiving obligations, or labor-market shocks. It also depends on honest introspection; a person can misclassify inherited wants as authentic or underestimate the cost of a long pursuit.
