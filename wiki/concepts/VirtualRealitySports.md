## Definition
[[VirtualRealitySports]] is the production and distribution of immersive sports experiences intended to give remote audiences a stronger sense of presence, perspective, or interaction than conventional two-dimensional viewing.

## Current Synthesis
The NBA case suggests that sports VR is best understood as a production system and learning program, not merely a headset format. A recurring partner and schedule make camera placement, direction, packaging, distribution, and audience response iteratively improvable; live games provide the core value, while highlights, documentaries, talk formats, training, esports links, and branded objects widen the portfolio.

Its access proposition is strong where arena attendance is scarce and global fandom is broad, but access is not the same as adoption. Headset availability, the difficulty of demonstrating immersion through ordinary media, camera quality, comfort, production cost, and uncertain incremental value constrain the medium. Early long-session or enthusiastic-user signals cannot substitute for audience, retention, satisfaction, and economic comparisons.

## Key Claims
- Repeated live production can turn isolated demonstrations into an operational learning loop.
- Immersive perspective is additive to television, mobile, and in-person attendance rather than automatically substitutive.
- Global remote access creates a plausible use case because only a small fraction of fans can attend an arena.
- Sports VR can span live viewing, highlights, storytelling, training, promotion, social presence, and prospective interaction.
- Engagement quality may matter early, but modest reach and absent economics keep the adoption thesis unresolved.
- Hardware access and demonstration difficulty create a marketing bottleneck specific to experiential media.

## Evidence
- Production learning and complementarity: [[inside-the-nbas-virtual-reality-strategy]] describes the NBA's 25-game NextVR schedule and Marsilio's view that each broadcast should improve while VR remains a third option.
- Access and global distribution: [[inside-the-nbas-virtual-reality-strategy]] contrasts the league's reported 155 million core fans with less than one percent attending arenas and notes worldwide rollout outside China.
- Portfolio breadth: [[inside-the-nbas-virtual-reality-strategy]] covers Finals highlights, Oculus experiences, House of Legends, referee training, spatial movement, esports exploration, and team or player promotion.
- Adoption constraints: [[inside-the-nbas-virtual-reality-strategy]] reports modest contemporary numbers, headset-marketing difficulty, skeptical basketball figures, and disputed live-camera readiness.

## Counterevidence & Qualifications
The evidence is one 2017 trade article centered on the NBA and its executive, not a comparative study of sports, production methods, or viewers. Long engagement may reflect novelty or self-selection, social enthusiasm is unquantified, and none of the reported experiments establishes retention, willingness to pay, comfort, accessibility, cost, or profitability. Forecasts that every game would be produced in VR and that household headset access would trigger mass adoption remain untested within the bounded source.

## What Changed
- Established sports VR as a recurring production-and-learning system rather than a collection of demonstrations.
- Separated the remote-access proposition from evidence of mass adoption.
- Added portfolio breadth and experiential-marketing constraints to the concept.
