## Summary
[[CBInsights]] compiles 323 startup shutdown writeups from founders, investors, journalists, and company notices, using failure narratives as evidence for recurring startup risk patterns. The collection reports that 70% of upstart tech companies fail, often around 20 months after first financing, and that consumer hardware outcomes are harsher, with 97% of seed or crowdfunded companies eventually dying or becoming zombies. Across examples, [[StartupFailurePatterns]] cluster around weak market demand, exhausted funding, premature scaling, bad timing, platform dependence, hardware and operations complexity, regulatory or legal shocks, fraud or governance failure, and teams losing focus before a sustainable business emerges.

## Key Claims
- Startup failure is usually multi-causal: weak [[ProductMarketFit]], poor unit economics, fundraising shortfalls, competition, and operational complexity often reinforce one another.
- [[StartupRunway]] matters because cash buys iteration time only when teams use it to test the right assumptions; many examples ran out of money before reaching a fundable or self-sustaining model.
- [[MarketTiming]] can break technically plausible companies when markets, customers, infrastructure, regulation, or enterprise buying cycles mature more slowly than a venture-backed cost structure allows.
- Consumer hardware, robotics, AR/VR, healthcare, food delivery, and marketplace businesses are especially exposed to capital intensity, trust, logistics, adoption friction, and regulatory risk.
- Platform dependence can turn growth into fragility when a startup relies on SEO, Facebook, app stores, YouTube/Facebook distribution, Apple/Google/Amazon ebook economics, or another gatekeeper's policy.
- Founder-authored post-mortems are useful because they expose decision logic, but they also rationalize outcomes after the fact and may understate internal conflict, misconduct, or avoidable execution errors.

## Key Quotes
> "hardware is hard" - Reach Robotics founder reflection on consumer-hardware difficulty.

> "we failed to create a scalable business" - Tutorspree post-mortem on single-channel SEO dependence.

> "money was not the issue" - Nouncer post-mortem on missing partners and developers.

## Connections
- [[CBInsights]] - publication and research firm that compiled the post-mortems.
- [[StartupFailurePatterns]] - concept created from the recurring shutdown mechanisms across the collection.
- [[ProductMarketFit]] - many cases describe user love, technology, or early traction without a market pull strong enough to sustain growth.
- [[StartupRunway]] - the collection repeatedly frames funding shortfalls, burn, failed financing, and cash exhaustion as the hard deadline on learning.
- [[MarketTiming]] - examples such as VR, drones, lidar, batteries, and diagnostics show markets developing more slowly than the companies' cost structures.
- [[StartupFocus]] - several failures involve premature scaling, pivot churn, split strategies, or overinvestment before a coherent path emerged.
- [[CustomerLedProductDevelopment]] - the cases warn that imagined demand, ignored customers, and insufficient validation can leave teams solving weak or non-scalable problems.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradictions found. The source reinforces existing wiki themes around [[ProductMarketFit]], [[StartupRunway]], [[StartupFocus]], and [[TroughOfSorrow]], but broadens them from single-founder retrospectives into a multi-case failure corpus.
