## Summary
[[MarkSuster]] argues that a request requiring one response should name one owner rather than address several people ambiguously. Connecting email practice to [[ResponsibilityDiffusion]], he treats the `To` field as an action assignment, `cc` as information only, and sequential escalation as more reliable than asking a group to decide who will act. The advice is practical and plausible but rests on practitioner observation rather than measured response outcomes.

## Key Claims
- A request should identify one person as the action owner; copied recipients should be included only when they need the information.
- Sending nonessential copies creates reading work for every recipient even when no action is expected.
- When several people receive the same undifferentiated request, each can assume someone else will respond, leaving the task unowned.
- A direct personal appeal makes the social obligation and cost of nonresponse more salient than a request to an unspecified group.
- In Suster's venture-capital example, one recipient can triage a deal and forward it to a better-matched colleague while recording the new owner in a tracking system.
- Large-group input can use blind copies when participants do not need one another's responses, reducing reply-all noise and unnecessary address disclosure.
- If the first named owner does not respond, the sender can approach a second person rather than assigning the same task to several people at once.

## Key Quotes
> "Ask one person to do one person's job." - Suster's rule for making an action request accountable.

> "nobody knows who owns responsibility for the response." - the failure mode of addressing several possible owners.

## Connections

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction was found. The source extends the wiki's email-state material from tracking obligations after receipt to assigning one accountable recipient before sending.
- The article is a short practitioner essay based on Suster's experience rather than a controlled comparison. Some work legitimately needs parallel review, shared decisions, redundancy, or multiple accountable roles; the useful boundary is one clear owner per requested action, not one recipient for every message.
