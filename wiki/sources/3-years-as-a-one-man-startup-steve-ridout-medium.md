## Summary
[[SteveRidout]] reflects on three years building [[Readlang]] as a one-person language-learning web app. The article is unusually direct about early bootstrapped economics: signups and revenue grew sharply, users reported real value, and expenses stayed low, yet the founder was still earning less than minimum wage after three years.

## Key Claims
- Solo bootstrapped product work can show strong usage and revenue growth while still producing weak hourly earnings for an experienced developer.
- Revenue transparency is useful for other bootstrappers but socially risky because low revenue can reduce perceived legitimacy and high revenue can invite competition or jealousy.
- User impact and direct testimonials can justify continuing a low-income product when the product appears to help people meaningfully.
- Ramen profitability changes the decision calculus: it does not prove a company is financially attractive yet, but it can make continued iteration plausible.
- Growth projections are psychologically powerful but uncertain; the author explicitly treats continued work as a bet rather than a guaranteed trend.

## Key Quotes
> "Usage didn't explode, but grew in fits and starts." - on the gap between hope and actual growth.

> "still making less than minimum wage" - on the financial reality after three years.
