## Summary
[[CourtneySeiter]] reflects on four years at [[Buffer]] to argue that [[RemoteWork]] can be broadly workable when teams deliberately replace office cues with trust, rituals, communication norms, tooling, and periodic physical presence. The piece mixes personal productivity observations with team-level practices: remote work increases focus for some people, but it also requires boundaries, over-communication, intentional serendipity, time-zone tooling, and care around Slack urgency. The inspected images are mostly decorative home-office, separator, quote-card, and author assets; the quote cards reinforce points already present in the prose about shutdown rituals, guilt, body-language loss, and time zones.

## Key Claims
- Remote or remote-flexible work is possible for more companies than the author once assumed, but people should not become second-class team members because they are out of the office.
- Trust is a prerequisite: managers should judge results rather than visibility and should trust people they have chosen to hire.
- Remote workers need deliberate personal boundaries, including start/end rituals, laptop shutdown, preserved mornings, physical movement, and work-only space where possible.
- Distributed teams need intentional social and communication design: periodic retreats, impromptu video hangs, video chat, over-communication, positive intent, and emojis or GIFs as partial substitutes for body language.
- Slack and other tools enable remote work but can also create false urgency, so teams should slow decisions down on purpose.
- Time zones and WiFi are material operational constraints that remote teams must actively manage rather than treat as background details.

## Key Quotes
> "Visibility does not equal productivity." - on remote trust

> "Slow it down on purpose." - on Slack urgency

> "Assume the best intentions" - on communication without body language
