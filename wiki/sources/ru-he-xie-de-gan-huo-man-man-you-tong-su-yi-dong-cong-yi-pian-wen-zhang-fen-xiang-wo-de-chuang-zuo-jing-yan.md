## Summary
This article is a process retrospective on writing a clear, substantive tutorial article about game-trailer story structure. The author moves from passive course notes into [[ActiveLearning]] by choosing a tractable topic, designing an explanation-first structure, collecting trailer examples, testing the draft with readers, and using [[PersonalKnowledgeManagement]] notes to speed publication. It adds a concrete account of [[ExplanatoryWriting]] as audience-centered writing that combines clear framing, concrete examples, feedback, and iterative revision.

## Key Claims
- [[ActiveLearning]] improves when accumulated notes are turned into public practice rather than kept as reassuring but unused study material.
- Topic selection should consider freshness, available knowledge reserves, and reader usefulness, then limit work in progress to preserve [[AttentionManagement]].
- [[ExplanatoryWriting]] needs a clear reader promise, target audience, background motivation, concrete-before-abstract explanation, and example-rich analysis.
- [[PersonalKnowledgeManagement]] supports writing when notes, databases, checklists, and platform-specific publishing knowledge are reusable at the moment of production.
- Reader testing can reveal unclear assumptions, excessive length, missing context, and format problems before publication.
- Purpose-first tool choice matters: the author chooses Notion, Bilibili video notes, Obsidian placeholders, and draft-sharing methods based on the job each must perform.

## Key Quotes
> "就大多数事情而言，最好的学习方式是自己动手去做、去练习。" - on learning by practicing.

> "文章是我更熟悉的创作形式，可以更快的启动、预期的产出速度也更快" - on choosing writing as the fastest feedback path.

> "选择工具方法前，先明确目的" - on choosing tools from concrete workflow needs.
