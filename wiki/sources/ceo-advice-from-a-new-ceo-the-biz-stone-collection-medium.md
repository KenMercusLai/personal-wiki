## Summary
[[BizStone]] reflects on becoming CEO of [[Jelly]] after years of working alongside other CEOs, distilling five early leadership lessons around communication, advice-seeking, firing, praise, and decisiveness. The source frames CEO work less as omniscience than as responsibility for making context visible, absorbing outside perspective, protecting culture, recognizing good work, and choosing a direction under uncertainty. The inspected images are a Biz Stone portrait, a tiny duplicate thumbnail, an illustrative plant photo near the firing section, and a Star Trek still supporting the Picard leadership anecdote; they add presentation context but no separate factual claims beyond the prose.

## Key Claims
- CEOs must communicate often because missing context lets fear fill the gap, even in small companies.
- Operational facts can change strategic urgency: at [[Twitter]], learning that the service could get roughly one million signups in a day when it stayed up reframed reliability as a growth constraint.
- CEOs should ask widely for advice because more smart people exist outside the company than inside, but they still need to choose which advice to act on.
- Firing is a core CEO responsibility when misconduct, persistent poor performance, or culture-damaging behavior threatens the company.
- Praise and recognition matter beyond compensation because they make effort and good work visible.
- Leadership does not require having every answer; it requires making a decision and moving with confidence while continuing to figure things out.

## Key Quotes
> "In the absence of knowledge, fear fills the void." - on why CEO communication has to be frequent.

> "Ask, listen, and take action." - on outside advice before Jelly's first launch.

> "The thing is, not firing people is a very good way to ruin your entire company." - on the cost of avoiding hard personnel decisions.
