## Summary
This 36氪 translated business article argues that [[DifferentiationStrategy]] is necessary when categories become crowded, features become copyable, and competitors converge through benchmarking. It qualifies that claim with [[ByronSharp]]'s distinction between differentiation and [[BrandDistinctiveness]], then surveys ways companies can escape sameness through positioning, customer experience, [[BlueOceanStrategy]], [[CategoryCreation]], brand meaning, and [[PersonalBranding]].

## Key Claims
- Feature-level advantages rarely last because successful functions are copied and eventually become category defaults.
- Competitors often become more similar over time because they benchmark each other instead of thinking from customer goals.
- Differentiation must be meaningful enough for non-expert customers to notice, not just visible to category specialists.
- Large market share and mental availability can make familiarity more powerful than subtle differentiation.
- Useful differentiation strategies include being first, owning an attribute, becoming a preferred supplier, claiming heritage, showing leadership, focusing on a target market, using a distinctive method, or being visibly hot.
- [[BlueOceanStrategy]] and [[CategoryCreation]] shift strategy away from direct rivalry by reframing demand, value, or the problem itself.
- Brand meaning, customer experience, and personal presence can be harder to copy than product features.

## Key Quotes
> "差异化必须来自功能以外的地方。功能别人总能赶上的。" - on why feature advantages are temporary

> "市场份额比与众不同更重要。" - on the qualification that familiarity and popularity often drive consideration

> "你的品牌就抵御商品化之盾。" - on brand as a long-term defense against sameness

## Connections
- [[DifferentiationStrategy]] - central strategic frame for giving customers a reason to choose one company over competitors.
- [[BrandDistinctiveness]] - qualification from [[ByronSharp]] that recognizability and mental availability can matter more than perceived difference in some B2C contexts.
- [[CategoryCreation]] - approach used by companies such as [[Drift]] to own a new problem frame rather than compete on an existing feature checklist.
- [[BlueOceanStrategy]] - related strategy for creating new demand where differentiation and low cost can reinforce each other.
- [[PersonalBranding]] - treated as a differentiation lever when founders, executives, or visible operators become part of the product's public meaning.
- [[SaaSMarketing]] - the article's examples such as [[ConvertKit]], [[Mailchimp]], and [[Drift]] show positioning and category focus as marketing assets for software companies.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction. The source qualifies the wiki's creator and SaaS marketing material by arguing that audience, market share, and brand recognition can outperform fine-grained product superiority when buyers cannot or will not compare deeply.
