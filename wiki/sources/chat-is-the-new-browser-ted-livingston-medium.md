## Summary
[[TedLivingston]] argues that chatbots were approaching a web-like platform transition in late 2016: primitive at first, but advantaged by lower friction than native mobile apps and likely to improve as developer tools, payments, discovery mechanics, and viral sharing matured. The article compares chatbot evolution to early websites, uses [[FacebookMessenger]], [[Kik]], [[Hipmunk]], and [[WeChat]] as examples, and treats chat as a candidate replacement for both the mobile web and app-install flows. The inspected images support the historical analogy: they show Kik chat imagery, tiny early White House/Yahoo/Google web screenshots, and a chart claiming [[WeChat]] via Tenpay grew to roughly 40% of China's mobile payment transactions after being near zero three years earlier.

## Key Claims
- [[MessagingAsPlatform]] can follow the web's path: early experiences may look crude, but a low-friction platform can improve until it competes with native apps.
- [[ProductFlowFriction]] is the article's core mechanism: chatbots avoid app-store search, downloads, account creation, and learning curves by letting users start through a scan, username, or link.
- [[FacebookMessenger]] and [[Kik]] had opened bot platforms in 2016, and early bot examples such as [[Hipmunk]] suggested chat could outperform the mobile web for some transactions.
- Suggested replies, Kik Codes, web bubbles, invites, and mentions are presented as interaction and distribution advances that could make bots easier to use and share.
- Payments are framed as the missing economic layer for bot ecosystems, with Facebook testing Messenger chatbot payments and [[WeChat]]/Tenpay serving as the strongest proof of chat-based commerce.
- The article is an optimistic 2016 snapshot and should be read alongside later critiques of the first bot-platform wave.

## Key Quotes
> "Chatbots have a fundamental friction advantage" - on why chat might improve faster than its early quality suggests.

> "The evolution of the next great platform has begun." - on the author's 2017 expectation for chatbot platforms.
