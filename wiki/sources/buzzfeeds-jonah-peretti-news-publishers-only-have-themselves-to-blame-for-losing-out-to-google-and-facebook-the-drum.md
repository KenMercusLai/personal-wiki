## Summary
In a 2017 interview, [[JonahPeretti]] presents [[Buzzfeed]] as a long-horizon digital media company built for real-time, personalized, shareable, and global distribution rather than as a youth publication. He argues that legacy publishers weakened themselves by protecting short-term earnings instead of investing in digital, while describing [[Facebook]] and [[Google]] as valuable distribution partners and treating platform-native video, free global news, data-informed creative, and a combined news-and-entertainment portfolio as strategic advantages. ## Key Claims
- Legacy publishers had years of strong cash flow and ample warning about the internet, but many prioritized quarterly earnings over digital investment and later blamed the platforms that built stronger information-delivery models.
- BuzzFeed's intended audience is digitally native rather than permanently young, and its strategy spans entertainment, lifestyle brands, and a global news network.
- Free, scaled news can gain reach when premium competitors adopt paywalls, although Peretti acknowledges that funding journalism remains an ecosystem-wide problem.
- News and entertainment are complementary: major news events create repeat visits and institutional purpose, while entertainment and service brands supply broad reach and varied formats.
- Platform-native distribution requires format-specific design; BuzzFeed reports using roughly 40-second videos for reach and 12-minute videos for watch time rather than defaulting to an industry-standard middle length.
- Partnerships with Facebook, YouTube, NBCUniversal, Oxygen, Snapchat, and more than 30 social platforms turn distribution into product development, audience learning, and cross-format commissioning.
- BuzzFeed's Audience Engine uses performance data from prior publishing to inform branded-content pitches, connecting editorial experimentation to advertising sales.

## Key Quotes
> "Our goal wasn't to be a youth brand, our goal was to be a modern media company" - Peretti on BuzzFeed's intended identity.

> "We should be making 40-second videos for reach and 12-minute videos for watch time" - Peretti on matching video length to the desired audience outcome.

> "We tend to be like we are your friend...as opposed to being the authority who tells you what to think." - Peretti on BuzzFeed's editorial voice.

## Contradictions
- The interview's defense of Facebook and Google conflicts in emphasis with [[9-boxes]], where Peretti later argues that large platforms capture most digital advertising value and underpay professional content creators. The positions can coexist as a change in bargaining emphasis: publishers may have caused part of their own weakness while platforms may still distribute value unfairly.
- The article reports company scale, revenue projections, engagement, valuation, video completion, and audience figures without independent methodology or later outcome data; they establish BuzzFeed's 2017 self-presentation rather than durable business performance.
- Peretti's expectation that Facebook, YouTube, and other platforms would invest more in news is a forecast, not evidence that platform funding would become sufficient or stable.
