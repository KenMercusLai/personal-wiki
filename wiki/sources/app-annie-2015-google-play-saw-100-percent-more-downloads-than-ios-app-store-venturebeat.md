## Summary
VentureBeat reports [[AppAnnie]]'s 2015 app-store retrospective: [[GooglePlay]] generated twice as many downloads as Apple's [[AppStore]], while Apple's store generated 75% more revenue than Google Play. The article frames mobile platform economics as a split between Android's broad download scale, driven by emerging markets, and iOS's stronger monetization, driven mainly by China, the U.S., and Japan. It also notes that games remained the dominant revenue category on both stores, especially on Google Play.

## Key Claims
- [[GooglePlay]] widened its download lead over Apple's [[AppStore]] in 2015, moving from 60% more downloads in 2014 to 100% more downloads in 2015.
- Apple's App Store widened its revenue lead over Google Play, rising from more than 70% higher revenue in 2014 to 75% higher revenue in 2015.
- [[MobileAppStoreEconomics]] separated reach from monetization: Android-scale downloads did not translate into higher store revenue than iOS.
- Google Play download growth was fueled by emerging markets, especially Brazil, India, Indonesia, Turkey, and Mexico.
- Apple's App Store revenue growth was driven primarily by China, the U.S., and Japan.
- Games remained the main revenue engine, contributing 90% of Google Play revenue and 75% of iOS App Store revenue.

## Key Quotes
> "Google Play saw 100 percent more downloads than Apple's App Store." - headline metric.

> "Apple's App Store made over 70 percent more in revenue than Google Play." - 2014 revenue comparison, followed by the reported 75% 2015 figure.
