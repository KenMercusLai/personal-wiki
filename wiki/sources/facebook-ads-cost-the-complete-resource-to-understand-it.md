## Summary
This [[AdEspresso]] article uses aggregated campaign data to present historical [[FacebookAdvertisingCosts]] benchmarks for 2017, 2018, and Q1 2019. It explains cost as an auction outcome shaped by timing, bidding strategy, placement, relevance metrics, audience competition, campaign objective, and available traffic volume rather than as a stable platform price. Its most durable guidance is to optimize for the business outcome, test audiences and creative, and treat time-based averages as diagnostic context rather than a scheduling rule. All repeated local `.jpg` and `.png` embeds resolve to the same unrelated HTML page rather than valid images, so the charts could not be independently inspected and no image was retained.

## Key Claims
- Facebook advertising is auction-priced, so bid strategy, audience competition, placement, timing, and Facebook's relevance rankings jointly affect delivery and cost.
- AdEspresso says its benchmark dataset came from more than $636 million in managed ad spend, with currencies converted to U.S. dollars; the 2018 and Q1 2019 CPC analysis is limited mainly to website-click and website-conversion objectives.
- The source reports conversion-objective CPC falling for four consecutive quarters, from $2.55 in Q1 2018 to $0.55 in Q1 2019, while December 2018 overall CPC reached $0.44 amid holiday competition.
- Weekend clicks were only slightly cheaper and overnight clicks had much lower volume, so small cost differences by day or hour should not override campaign reach and outcome requirements.
- Page-like costs generally rose through 2018 into Q1 2019, while app-install costs were described as lower early in the year and higher in Q3 and Q4.
- Advertisers should optimize for the desired conversion rather than select a nominally cheaper objective whose clicks or impressions do not produce the intended result.
- The benchmarks are historical aggregate reference points, not predictions for a specific campaign or substitutes for testing creative, audience, relevance, and downstream economics.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction identified. The source qualifies any fixed-cost interpretation of paid social by showing that objective, season, hour, audience, competition, and delivery volume alter observed prices.
- The article is a first-party benchmark from an advertising vendor and does not disclose campaign counts, advertiser mix, weighting, dispersion, statistical uncertainty, or controls for changes in geography and objective mix.
- The embedded chart files are invalid and unrelated HTML, so the prose's chart readings and exact plotted comparisons could not be visually verified from the supplied source package.
- Reader comments contain individual campaign experiences and later discussion, but they are self-selected anecdotes rather than part of the benchmark dataset.
