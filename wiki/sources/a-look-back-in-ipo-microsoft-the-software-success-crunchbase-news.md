## Summary
Crunchbase News looks back at [[Microsoft]]'s 1986 IPO as an unusually profitable, cash-positive software-company debut rather than a capital-hungry venture exit. The article argues that [[BillGates]] and Microsoft could wait to go public because the company had recurring product momentum, annual profits, cash in the bank, and only a small outside investment taken mainly for advice. It frames the IPO as evidence that software economics already looked powerful before Microsoft's later platform, antitrust, cloud, and product eras.

## Key Claims
- [[Microsoft]] went public in 1986 at $21 per share after more than a decade of profitable growth.
- The company had released or sold major early products before the IPO, including a BASIC interpreter, MS-DOS for IBM's PC, a mouse, Word, Excel, and Windows.
- [[SelfFundedSoftwareGrowth]] let Microsoft avoid the usual pressure from cash depletion or impatient venture investors.
- [[BillGates]] reportedly preferred a lower IPO pricing range because he was uncomfortable with pushing Microsoft's market value above half a billion dollars.
- Microsoft's single $1 million investment from Technology Venture Investors is presented as board/advice capital rather than operational necessity.
- Microsoft's pre-IPO revenue and net income grew quickly, with 1985 revenue above $100 million and net income of $24 million.
- The article contrasts Microsoft's modest IPO-era valuation multiples with later tech-market expectations.

## Key Quotes
> "We were cash positive" - Gates explaining Microsoft's operating history.

> "That money sat in the bank" - Gates on the venture investment's purpose.
