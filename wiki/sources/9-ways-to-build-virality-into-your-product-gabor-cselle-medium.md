## Summary
[[GaborCselle]] argues that consumer startups need viral distribution designed into the product from the beginning because low average revenue per user makes paid acquisition hard to sustain. The article gives nine product-integrated viral mechanisms: two-sided rewards, vanity metrics, collaboration, embeds, social artifacts, messaging artifacts, promotional signatures, first-message onboarding through existing channels, and highly visible hardware. Its examples show [[ViralLoops]] as a design problem that joins incentive, artifact, channel, context, and product quality rather than a marketing feature that can be glued on later.

## Key Claims
- Consumer products should build a viral channel into core mechanics early, because retrofitting virality later is difficult and paid acquisition may not fit consumer economics.
- Two-sided rewards work when both inviter and invitee have a reason to act, as shown by [[Dropbox]] bonus storage and [[GoodEggs]] grocery credits.
- Vanity metrics can motivate users to invite or broadcast to others when public counts, followers, or connections become status goals, as with [[Twitter]] and [[LinkedIn]].
- Collaboration products such as [[Slack]], [[GoogleDocs]], and [[Airtable]] become viral because the product's full value appears when users invite coworkers.
- User-generated artifacts can spread through embeds, social networks, messaging links, watermarks, and canonical URLs when the artifact teaches recipients what the product is for.
- Messaging products can spread through outgoing signatures or first messages on existing channels, as with [[Hotmail]], [[Mailchimp]], and [[GroupMe]], but only when the in-product experience is better than the fallback channel.
- Highly visible hardware such as [[Square]] terminals and [[NestCam]] can create offline awareness, though hardware loops are harder to measure and iterate than software loops.

## Key Quotes
> "Consumer product startups have to bake a viral channel into their product from the get-go." - summary claim about early viral-channel design.

> "Rewarding both sides is crucial" - explanation of why referral incentives need value for both sender and recipient.

> "conversion and loyalty all have to happen based on the quality of the product" - qualification that viral exposure does not replace product value.
