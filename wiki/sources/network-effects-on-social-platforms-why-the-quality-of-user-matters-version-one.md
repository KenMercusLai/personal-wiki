## Summary
This 2015 Version One essay argues that social-platform network effects depend not only on user count but on who joins, how that person relates to existing users, and what the person contributes. It distinguishes communities, private social networks, public follow networks, and messaging by the kind of participation that creates value and therefore by the critical mass each product needs.

## Key Claims
- A new social-platform user does not add uniform value: relationship relevance, influence, activity, and content contribution can matter more than raw membership.
- Communities such as [[Reddit]] rely relatively more on participant volume because strangers can create aggregate discussion value even without prior relationships.
- Private networks such as [[Facebook]] become useful through dense groups of meaningful existing relationships, then gain additional value as school, work, and family graphs overlap.
- Public follow networks such as [[Twitter]] can gain disproportionate value from influential or highly productive accounts because a minority may create much of what the wider audience consumes.
- Messaging products such as [[WhatsApp]] can become valuable at a very small scale when even two people have a strong, frequent communication relationship.
- Social-platform launch strategy should target the specific users and relationship density required by the product rather than optimize undifferentiated signup totals.

## Key Quotes
> "the value of a network shouldn't be measured just by size" - the essay's central qualification to quantity-only network-effect definitions

> "you can arguably reach critical mass with just two people" - on messaging value emerging from one high-frequency relationship

## Contradictions
- The essay qualifies quantity-only accounts of network effects, but it offers conceptual examples rather than measurements of marginal user value, platform thresholds, retention, or causal growth effects.
- Its platform taxonomy is simplified: communities can depend on experts and relationships, private networks can derive value from creators and weak ties, public networks need consumers as well as influencers, and messaging products often require broader contact coverage beyond one active pair.
