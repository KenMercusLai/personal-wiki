## Summary
This Grasshopper article argues that [[BillboardAdvertising]] can build awareness, but is usually a blunt and expensive channel for small businesses seeking directly attributable sales. It frames billboards as most plausible for local, mass-market, high-ticket, or creatively brand-led businesses, while pushing many small firms toward more targeted, durable, and measurable marketing such as [[SaaSMarketing]], SEO, PPC, lead generation, blogging, and email. The inspected lead image was a decorative green brand mark; the referenced billboard and vehicle images were unavailable because the local asset folder was missing.

## Key Claims
- Billboard costs vary heavily by city, with the article citing four-week bulletin ranges of $11,000-$23,000 in Boston, $8,400-$15,000 in San Diego, and $3,000-$4,000 in Milwaukee.
- A billboard can make sense when a business can tolerate roughly $1,000 to acquire a customer, especially for high-ticket or mass-market products.
- Billboards are weak for personalization and direct-response targeting because every nearby driver sees the same message.
- The author says a Grasshopper billboard increased sales calls from advertisers rather than producing a clear increase in customer sales.
- Billboard location quality must be vetted in person because weak traffic or a mismatched road can make the placement memorable for the wrong reason.
- Branded vehicles can be a cheaper, more mobile alternative to fixed billboards for some small businesses.
- [[Mailchimp]] is presented as a positive billboard case because the campaign aimed at customer delight and brand love rather than direct sales.

## Key Quotes
> "Although billboards are great for building brand awareness, they're not always a direct link to cold hard cash." - thesis of the article

> "Every advertiser comes out of the woodwork to see if they can sell ads to you" - Mike Morris on a side effect of visible ad spending

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found. The source qualifies acquisition-heavy marketing material by showing that offline awareness can be useful, but only when cost tolerance, audience breadth, location quality, and measurement expectations fit the business.
