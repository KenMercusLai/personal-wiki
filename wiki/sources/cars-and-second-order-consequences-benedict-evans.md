## Summary
[[BenedictEvans]] argues that electric vehicles and autonomous driving should be read as system-wide infrastructure shifts, not just product substitutions inside the car industry. The essay uses [[AutomobilitySecondOrderEffects]] to trace how EVs and autonomy could reshape fuel retail, maintenance, electricity demand, tax bases, crashes, insurance, vehicle design, congestion, parking, land use, public transport, labor, logistics, urban form, and surveillance.

## Key Claims
- Electric vehicles reduce mechanical complexity, which can shrink internal-combustion maintenance work, change supplier demand, and potentially lengthen vehicle life once technology cycles stabilize.
- EV adoption can undermine gas stations, convenience-store impulse sales, tobacco distribution, gasoline tax revenue, and fuel-subsidy politics while increasing and reshaping electricity demand.
- Autonomous driving's first-order safety effects extend into insurance, emergency services, legal costs, vehicle design, cycling safety, and economic activity around accidents.
- Autonomy can increase road capacity through smoother computer driving, platooning, fewer collisions, and less signal-like behavior, but induced demand may absorb those gains.
- Parking is a major second-order lever because autonomy and on-demand fleets could relocate or reduce parking demand, freeing road capacity and urban land while creating new empty-vehicle trips.
- Cheap autonomous on-demand rides can blur the boundary between taxis, ride-hailing, shuttles, buses, and public transport, with ambiguous effects on congestion and transit access.
- Autonomous vehicles also create surveillance and policing consequences because mobile sensor platforms could record public life beyond what is needed for driving.

## Key Quotes
> "it was easy to predict mass car ownership but hard to predict Wal-mart" - Evans on why downstream consequences are harder than the technology shift itself.

> "An autonomous car is a moving panopticon." - Evans on non-driving uses of AV sensor data.
