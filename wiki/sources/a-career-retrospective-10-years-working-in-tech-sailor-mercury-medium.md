## Summary
[[AmyWibowo]] reflects on an unconventional decade in technology that moves through MIT, robotics research in Japan, creative interface research, startup and [[Airbnb]] engineering, art-tech side projects, and self-published computer-science zines. The essay argues that art and engineering can become one career thread, but it also documents how harassment, sexism, and underestimation made that thread costly to sustain. The embedded images reinforce the synthesis: author illustrations establish the art/computing identity, screenshots show emotion-aware robotics and garment-pattern systems, and project photos show Legoizer murals, knitting-machine hacking, and illustrated CS zines as concrete creative-technical artifacts.

## Key Claims
- [[CreativeTechnicalCareer]] can emerge through repeated experiments rather than a conventional ladder, especially when a person combines technical skill with art, education, and craft.
- [[ReinforcementLearning]] becomes both a robotics method and a career metaphor: repeated decisions under uncertainty can improve future choices without complete guidance.
- Creative computing research can make interfaces for emotional robots and garment design by treating human expression, body-scale interaction, sewing geometry, and physical making as legitimate computer-science problems.
- [[WorkplaceSexismInTech]] can operate through harassment, ignored contributions, credibility denial, blame shifting, and advice to leave engineering for feminized work.
- [[Airbnb]] gave the author a larger engineering environment where front-end growth work, localization, and internationalization connected product engineering to cross-cultural use.
- [[ComputerScienceZines]] made CS education more accessible by combining self-publishing, illustration, low perfection pressure, and topics such as encryption, operating systems, image processing, and programming languages.

## Key Quotes
> "This is why I'm still here, fighting." - on using art-computing projects to recover the magic of computers after sexism and burnout.
