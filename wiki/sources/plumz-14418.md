## Summary
[[Plum]] argues that invitation-only game preview events are primarily controlled publicity rather than broad product testing: developers select media and creators, subsidize access, and obtain favorable attention while ordinary players cannot independently test performance, controls, genre fit, or development readiness. Using [[PhantomBladeZero]] and [[WuchangFallenFeathers]] as cautionary cases, the essay claims that repeated previews can inflate expectations and conceal uncertainty that a public demo would expose, but its game-quality, media-behavior, and commercial-outcome claims are first-person interpretations without contracts, comparative data, or independent verification.

## Key Claims
- Invitation-only previews can turn scarce early access, travel, hospitality, gifts, embargoes, and future invitations into soft pressure on media and creators without an explicit payment-for-praise agreement.
- Repeated preview coverage can make marketing activity look like evidence of development completeness, product quality, or schedule confidence even when those conclusions do not follow.
- A public demo provides players with direct evidence about hardware performance, controls, difficulty, and genre fit; a curated offline session reaches fewer people and keeps the developer in greater control of access and framing.
- The author interprets [[WuchangFallenFeathers]]' launch backlash as an expectation gap amplified by repeated previews and identity-based hype rather than proof that the underlying game was uniformly poor.
- [[PhantomBladeZero]]'s delay after years of previews led the author to distrust its development readiness and warn of a similar backlash, but the prediction remains speculative.
- Refund rights do not fully neutralize misleading promotion because some purchasers do not play promptly, some storefronts and platforms limit refunds, and bundles can separate a game key from a refundable game purchase.
- Marketing-first incentives may damage trust in domestic single-player games and divert scarce development resources, although the essay supplies no budgets, staffing records, sales analysis, or industry-wide comparison.

## Key Quotes
> “所谓的‘线下试玩会’并非是真的为了玩家能提前玩游戏测试并收集反馈用的，而是一场类似‘手机发布会’一样的属于媒体和 KOL 的付费宣传 Show，是一个大型的广告。” — the article's central interpretation of invitation-only previews.

> “少一些‘线下试玩会’，多一些脚踏实地，在流量为王的时代，凭借质量来获得口碑。” — the author's recommendation to favor product quality and public evidence over controlled hype.

## Connections
- [[Plum]] - author offering a first-person critique of domestic game marketing and media incentives.
- [[PhantomBladeZero]] - delayed action game used to argue that years of preview exposure do not prove release readiness.
- [[WuchangFallenFeathers]] - launch case used to connect curated previews, inflated expectations, performance complaints, genre mismatch, and backlash.
- [[ControlledGamePreviewMarketing]] - the article's proposed distinction between a publicity event and an openly testable demo.
- [[AccessJournalism]] - future access, hospitality, gifts, and embargo privileges can create influence without an explicit editorial bargain.

## Contradictions
- The essay rejects a deterministic “bad game” reading of [[WuchangFallenFeathers]], calling it a solid, comparatively hard Soulslike while arguing that promotion raised expectations beyond what the product could satisfy.
- Its conclusions about media silence, developer control, studio collapse, refunds, and the likely trajectory of [[PhantomBladeZero]] are asserted from observation rather than supported with contracts, participant testimony, sales records, technical tests, or a systematic sample.
- Public demos can improve independent evaluation, but the source does not measure their packaging cost, security risk, representativeness, conversion effect, or ability to predict final quality.
- The retained image is explicitly AI-generated editorial illustration. It visualizes the author's alleged incentive structure but is not evidence that a developer paid a particular reviewer.
