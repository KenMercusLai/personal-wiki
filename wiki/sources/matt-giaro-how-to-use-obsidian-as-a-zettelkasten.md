## Summary
[[MattGiaro]] presents a deliberately minimal [[ZettelkastenMethod]] for creators: capture quickly in [[Bear]], let ideas sit for two or three days, move only durable material into [[Obsidian]], rewrite it in one's own words, and add at least one meaningful link. The workflow favors a flat Markdown vault, one idea per note, little metadata, and reuse in published work over plugins, elaborate templates, classification, or accumulation for its own sake. It is a self-reported business and note-taking practice rather than comparative evidence that the stack improves understanding, creativity, or revenue.

## Key Claims
- [[NoteToolFit]] can justify a two-app workflow when a fast mobile capture tool and a slower linked thinking environment serve different stages better than one application.
- A two-to-three-day capture delay can act as a filter: ideas that still seem valuable are processed into permanent notes, while weak fragments can be discarded before they clutter the durable archive.
- Permanent notes should restate one idea in the writer's own words, add personal interpretation, and link to at least one related note rather than preserve copied source text.
- A flat vault with descriptive note titles, minimal metadata, and few or no folders can reduce classification work and allow structure to emerge through links.
- Notes become useful when they are combined into articles or other output; collecting information without processing or reuse can become hoarding rather than knowledge work.
- Obsidian's plain Markdown files, offline use, and cross-platform availability are presented as longevity and portability advantages, while the core workflow requires no community plugins.

## Key Quotes
> "Bear (for capture) + Obsidian (for thinking)" — on assigning different tools to different stages

> "Don't just save notes. Create first drafts." — on rewriting notes for later output

> "You're an idea-maker, not an idea-manager." — on keeping the system subordinate to creative work

## Contradictions
- The two-to-three-day deletion filter conflicts conditionally with [[HerbertLui]]'s advice to put everything into a low-pressure card practice and let clear thinking emerge; the sources describe different tolerances for archive noise and premature judgment rather than establishing one superior gate.
- The one-link minimum can reduce isolated notes, but [[OrphanNotes]] warns that links added to improve graph appearance can be semantically weak. The rule is useful only when a genuine relationship exists.
- The flat-vault and minimal-metadata prescription differs from domain-specific, academic, archival, and big-note workflows that benefit from folders, properties, templates, or specialized tools. Giaro presents a personal creator workflow, not a universal information architecture.
- The reported 3,000-plus notes, three years of use, six-figure writing business, and publishing results are self-reported without comparison, attribution analysis, or independent verification; they do not show that the workflow caused the business outcome.
- The source makes time-sensitive product claims about pricing, sync alternatives, platforms, and offline behavior without a product comparison or current technical verification.
