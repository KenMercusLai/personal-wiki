## Summary
[[PiotrWozniak]] presents the [[KnowledgeValuationNetwork]] as the brain's fast, personalized system for judging whether knowledge is coherent, applicable, and valuable for one's goals. The article connects that motivational theory to neural valuation circuitry, especially the orbitofrontal cortex and [[CommonCurrencyModel]], and argues that [[LearnDrive]], [[FreeLearning]], and healthy childhood conditions build stronger valuation networks than coerced schooling. Its SuperMemo origin story makes the claim personal: Wozniak says school nearly suppressed the goal-directed learning path that led to [[SuperMemo]].

## Key Claims
- [[KnowledgeValuationNetwork]] values knowledge mainly by applicability to personal goals rather than by probability or novelty alone.
- Emotional and rational valuation can run through partly separate pathways, with personality and education shaping whether rational valuation can override immediate emotional valuation.
- Well-organized semantic knowledge can make rapid expert decisions feel automatic, because value propagates through a network of goals, concepts, and associations.
- [[LearnDrive]] depends on matching available information to current semantic knowledge and valuation signals; one-size-fits-all schooling often ignores this condition.
- [[FreeLearning]], play, low chronic stress, and broad behavioral space are presented as the healthiest conditions for developing strong valuation networks.
- Neural valuation may integrate knowledge-based, reward-expectation, and bodily valuation signals through the OFC-centered [[CommonCurrencyModel]].
- [[CoerciveLearning]] and [[ReverseLearning]] can attach negative value to learning contexts, suppress consolidation, and contribute to learned helplessness.

## Key Quotes
> "知识估值主要依赖知识对于实现个人目标的适用性。" - on goal applicability as the core valuation criterion.

> "为了高效学习，估值必须很高。" - on valuation as a condition for efficient learning.

> "无休止的虚假奖励和轻微处分可能会使知识估值过程关闭" - on coercive schooling damaging valuation.
