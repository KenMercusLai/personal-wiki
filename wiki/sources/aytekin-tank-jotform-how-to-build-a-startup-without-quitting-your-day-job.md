## Summary
[[AytekinTank]] argues that side projects can grow inside protected pockets of time without immediately replacing salaried work. Drawing on [[Jotform]], [[Slack]], Google 20 percent time, and other examples, the article frames side projects as low-pressure experiments that should be fun, imperfect, customer-value-oriented, and open to becoming the founder's core business later.

## Key Claims
- [[SideProjectIncubation]] depends on small, protected, frequent work sessions rather than waiting for long uninterrupted blocks.
- Side projects can energize core work because they operate in a lower-pressure space where failure is less existential.
- Strong side projects often start by giving customers or communities a valuable extra rather than by attacking the core business directly.
- Enjoyment and permission to be imperfect keep side projects alive long enough for learning and unexpected direction changes.
- [[BootstrappedSaaS]] can emerge from long side-project compounding, as [[AytekinTank]] says [[Jotform]] grew for five years before he quit his job and without outside funding.
- [[Slack]] illustrates a related pivot path: an internal chat tool built during the failed game Glitch became the core product.

## Key Quotes
> "Something brewing behind the scenes can turn into your life's work." - on the upside of side-project incubation.

> "It's a pressure-free playing field." - on why side projects can support creativity.

> "The best way to approach a side project is as an experiment" - on avoiding premature outcome fixation.
