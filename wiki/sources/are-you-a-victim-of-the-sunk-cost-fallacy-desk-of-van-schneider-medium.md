## Summary
Tobias van Schneider explains [[SunkCostFallacy]] through writing, waiting for a bus, overeating paid-for food, and his decision to keep working on [[DotMailApp]] after the project no longer seemed promising. The article argues that time, money, emotion, pride, and public commitment can make people defend an old investment by adding new investment, even when the original cost cannot be recovered. It also links the bias to [[ProductStickiness]], warning that designers can use accumulated user investment to keep people attached to products after enjoyment or value has faded.

## Key Claims
- A sunk cost is already paid and unrecoverable, so it should not control future decisions.
- Time, financial, and emotional investment make abandonment psychologically harder.
- [[SunkCostFallacy]] can keep people writing weak articles, waiting for unlikely buses, eating past hunger, staying in jobs, or continuing projects for pride rather than value.
- [[DotMailApp]] became a personal example of sunk-cost behavior because emotional investment and fear of public abandonment extended the project.
- Designers can exploit sunk-cost dynamics as [[ProductStickiness]], especially in games where prior progress or time investment makes leaving feel like a loss.
- Awareness and a direct "why am I still doing this?" question are presented as the practical defense.

## Key Quotes
> "our decisions are manipulated by our investments" - the article's general explanation of time, financial, and emotional investment.

> "Essentially it means defending an investment you've made by investing even more." - summary of the circular trap.
