## Summary
[[Leetao]] reflects on a workflow in which a new idea is discussed with AI, reduced to a minimum experiment, and judged through observable intermediate steps and results. That loop speeds both validation and abandonment, but repeated AI-supported rejection left him thinking without practicing and weakened the pleasure of creation. He responded by restarting [[Memox|memox]], deleting about 80% of its code to preserve one function, and releasing [[VoiceFloat]] around one core task, using simplicity and hands-on building to restore creative momentum.

## Key Claims
- AI can shorten the path from an idea to a minimal experiment and make both continuation and abandonment easier.
- A useful validation loop has observable, testable intermediate states rather than only an ambiguous final conclusion.
- Evidence is valuable for rejecting weak assumptions, but repeated desk-based research and negative results can create an idea-filtering loop that substitutes analysis for craft.
- Existing software may still leave room for more individualized products even when market research says an idea is unoriginal or crowded.
- Radical scope reduction can revive a paused project: the author reports cutting roughly 80% of memox's code and retaining one function.
- Creative motivation returned after the author resumed building memox and shipped the single-purpose VoiceFloat.

## Key Quotes
> “常在思考但是手上却没有半点功夫，人就容易迷茫。” — on analysis becoming detached from practice.

> “Keep It Simple, Stupid.” — the rule used to narrow memox and VoiceFloat.

## Contradictions
- The essay qualifies a strong interpretation of [[ActionBiasInAI]]: action and experimentation can produce useful disconfirming evidence, but treating every negative AI research report as a stop signal can itself become a form of inaction.
- The author's confidence that unmet personal expectations justify more individualized software is experiential evidence, not proof of demand, differentiation, retention, or a viable business model.
