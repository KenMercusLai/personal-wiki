## Summary
This CodeAhoy article argues that frequent web search is compatible with programming expertise because developers work across too many languages, frameworks, APIs, and implementation details to memorize everything. It presents [[SearchAssistedProgramming]] as an expert practice of finding candidate solutions and validating reasoning while retaining responsibility for judging sources rather than copying them blindly. A short [[Netty]] case reports 23 searches while producing a 255-line Java client and server, but supplies no comparison group or quality measure.

## Key Claims
- Experienced programmers may search as much as or more than beginners because expertise includes knowing what to look up and how to assess the result.
- External lookup is useful for volatile or low-value-to-memorize details when developers work across multiple languages and frameworks.
- Search can support both option discovery and validation when a programmer suspects that an approach or assumption may be wrong.
- [[SearchAssistedProgramming]] requires evaluating candidate answers; copying a result without understanding or verification is not presented as expertise.
- In the author's [[Netty]] example, 23 Google queries accompanied 255 lines of working Java client-and-server code, or roughly one query per ten lines.

## Key Quotes
> "they don't blindly follow or copy-paste any solution" - on evaluation as the boundary between search and uncritical reuse.

> "1 query every 10 lines of code" - the author's calculation from one unfamiliar-framework example.

## Connections
- [[SearchAssistedProgramming]] - central practice of using search for recall, research, and validation while preserving human judgment.
- [[Google]] - search engine the author treats as part of a developer's working toolkit.
- [[StackOverflow]] - one of the main destinations reached during the Netty example.
- [[Netty]] - unfamiliar Java networking framework used in the article's worked example.
- [[SystematicLearning]] - complementary practice for building durable domain structure rather than treating lookup as a complete learning method.
- [[DeveloperDocumentation]] - primary technical reference material represented in the example by the Netty website and JavaDocs.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction with existing wiki content. The article complements [[SystematicLearning]] by distinguishing quick lookup and problem research from the durable understanding built through systematic study.
- The one-query-per-ten-lines figure is one self-reported unfamiliar-framework episode, not evidence of a normal or desirable search rate, and lines of code are not a measure of task difficulty or software quality.
- The article attributes "Never memorize something that you can look up" to Albert Einstein without supplying a source; the attribution should not be treated as verified evidence.
