## Summary
RORIRI argues that AI-era education should move beyond tool-use training toward an expanded [[ComputationalThinking]] made of [[StatisticalModelThinking]], abstraction and programming, and [[AcademicWriting]]. The essay treats AI as a powerful executor whose value depends on human ability to define problems, model uncertainty, structure tasks, inspect evidence, and care enough about a real question to pursue it.

## Key Claims
- AI weakens the value of merely executing structured knowledge tasks, but increases the value of defining tasks, boundaries, and evidence chains.
- [[ComputationalThinking]] remains central, yet needs extension because real-world data are noisy and real problems are not cleanly pre-defined.
- [[StatisticalModelThinking]] teaches learners to see every description as a lossy model plus error, making bias, uncertainty, and [[PHacking]] easier to inspect.
- [[AcademicWriting]] is framed as problem-solving training because writing forces questions, evidence, assumptions, and reasoning gaps into visible form.
- [[ProjectBasedLearning]] can join statistics, programming, and writing inside real inquiry projects without rebuilding the entire curriculum.
- [[MetacognitiveFeedback]] matters because learners gain motivation when reports, portfolios, and reflection make their own cognitive growth visible.
- [[TasteAsProblemSense]] is the human ability to notice and care about a real question before AI or any other tool begins execution.

## Key Quotes
> "AI 是一个强大的执行工具" — the essay distinguishes execution from human task definition.

> "观测量 = 模型 + 误差" — the source's compact model-thinking formula.

> "提出一个真实的问题" — the essay's answer to what remains distinctly human.
