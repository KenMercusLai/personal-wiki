## Summary
Greylock Perspectives argues that employees and early adopters become unusually fluent in a product, so their preferences stop representing the needs of prospective and newly arriving customers. The essay qualifies [[CustomerLedProductDevelopment]]: teams should keep listening to users, but segment feedback by expertise and growth relevance, test comprehension with less-fluent people, and avoid adding power-user features that increase [[CognitiveOverheadInProductDesign]] without improving acquisition or activation.

## Key Claims
- Startups can begin from founder insight or from customer listening, but durable customer-led growth requires skills that may initially be harder to scale.
- Early adopters tolerate flaws, invent workarounds, and become power users whose feedback is useful but increasingly optimized for their own expert workflows.
- Employees share the product vision and accumulate even more domain knowledge, so employee enthusiasm can falsely validate features that do not help growth.
- Product familiarity creates a [[BuilderUserFluencyGap]] even when employees demographically resemble the original target customer.
- Teams should test product direction with new or prospective users because expert feedback can bias experiments toward niche features.
- Additional power-user functionality has limited engagement upside when those users are already highly engaged, while its complexity can make the product harder for newcomers to understand.
- The more important growth task is to delight people who are not yet users or who were not delighted by the existing product.

## Key Quotes
> "Your early users are also no longer the customer." - on expertise changing whose needs a user represents.

> "find new people to delight" - the essay's growth priority.

- [[ProductUserSegmentation]] - the essay separates employees, early adopters, power users, new users, and prospective users by product knowledge and growth role.

## Contradictions
- The article qualifies, rather than rejects, the wiki's customer-led-development material. Existing customers provide grounded problems and operating evidence, but their requests are not automatically representative of acquisition, activation, or broader-market needs.
- The recommendation to focus on new and prospective users is source-scoped practitioner advice; the essay supplies no experiment, market category, or quantitative comparison showing when newcomer needs should outweigh retention, revenue, accessibility, or expert-workflow requirements.
