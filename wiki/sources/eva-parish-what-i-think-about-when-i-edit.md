## Summary
[[EvaParish]] presents a genre-spanning editing practice organized around intended meaning, audience knowledge, linguistic precision, and scan-friendly structure. The article connects [[EditingForClarity]] to planning as well as sentence-level revision: establish the point and reader first, then clarify references, remove excess words, name actors and mechanisms, replace vague language, supply context, stabilize tone, and use visual structure. Its rules are ultimately heuristics rather than absolutes, because the stated goal is to make deliberate choices that serve the message.

## Key Claims
- A private preamble stating the main point, audience, narrator, tone, or mood gives drafting and editing an explicit success criterion.
- Strategic repetition can orient readers: state the purpose, deliver the material, close the loop, and replace ambiguous demonstrative pronouns with precise nouns.
- Concision improves usability when it removes hedges, indirect instruction, stacked clauses, and other words that do not add meaning.
- Active voice can expose missing understanding by forcing a writer to name the person, service, or component responsible for an action.
- Specific verbs and concrete descriptions usually communicate more than adverbs, jargon, cliches, or unexplained acronyms.
- Writers should model the reader's prior knowledge by defining terms, expanding acronyms on first use, adding brief context, and linking optional detail.
- Headings, short paragraphs, lists, tables, bold emphasis, and whitespace support readers who scan rather than read linearly.

## Key Quotes
> "Say exactly what you mean" - on the first tenet of the author's editing philosophy.

> "Take out all unnecessary words" - on the second tenet.

> "The point of editing is to think about how you're using language" - on judgment taking priority over mechanical rule following.
