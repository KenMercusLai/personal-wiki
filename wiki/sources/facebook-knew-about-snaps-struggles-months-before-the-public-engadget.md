## Summary
[[JonFingas]] reports that [[Facebook]] used aggregated app-frequency and duration data from [[Onavo]] Protect to detect slowing [[Snapchat]] use soon after Instagram Stories launched. The same telemetry reportedly informed Facebook's [[WhatsApp]] acquisition and live-video response to Meerkat and Periscope, illustrating [[CompetitiveIntelligence]] built from consumer product data. The article says collection was disclosed, but it questions meaningful notice, competitive use of customer data, and possible conflict with Apple's App Store rules.

## Key Claims
- Onavo Protect gave Facebook aggregated visibility into how frequently and how long people used rival apps.
- Facebook reportedly observed Snapchat usage slowing after Instagram Stories launched and could reinforce its anti-Snap strategy before the trend became public.
- Onavo data reportedly influenced Facebook's decision to acquire WhatsApp after revealing its strength in markets including Spain.
- Facebook also used rival-app observations when deciding to add live video in response to Meerkat and Periscope.
- Formal disclosure does not settle whether users understood that a security-branded VPN supplied competitive intelligence to its owner.
- The resulting information advantage could let an incumbent copy, acquire, or counter emerging competitors earlier than public market signals would permit.

## Key Quotes
> "turning customers' own data against them" - Askhan Soltani's criticism of the competitive use.

> "apps have incorporated market research services like this 'for years'" - Facebook's defense.
