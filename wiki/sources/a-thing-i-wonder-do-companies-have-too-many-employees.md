## Summary
[[CoachTony]] questions the assumption that company success naturally requires large headcount. Using an anecdote about 4,000 engineers where only 400 seem productive, plus the example of [[WhatsApp]] selling for $19 billion with 55 employees, the essay argues that many workers may be underutilized because organizations fail to create real opportunities for them. Its core claim is that exceptional output depends less on innate talent alone than on situations that give people meaningful responsibility.

## Key Claims
- Companies may overvalue headcount growth and undervalue whether new hires have real work, responsibility, and opportunity.
- Apparent low productivity inside large engineering organizations does not necessarily mean most employees are fraudulent or incapable; they may be waiting for useful work.
- Venture investors can treat rapid hiring and hundreds of employees as proof of startup success without questioning the value of those hires.
- [[WhatsApp]] is used as a counterexample to headcount-as-success because it reportedly sold for $19 billion with only 55 employees.
- "10x people" are framed as a product of "10x situations": talent produces outsized results only when the organization gives it room.
- Fewer employees might be happier and more fulfilled if companies designed work around real contribution instead of treating employees like children.

## Key Quotes
> "The general consensus is that of our 4,000 engineers, only 400 do anything. The problem is that nobody knows which 400." - quoted friend describing an engineering organization.

> "10x people come from 10x situations." - Coach Tony on talent and opportunity.
