## Summary
[[MitchellHarper]] expands [[FredWilson]]'s three-part CEO model: set and repeatedly communicate company vision and strategy, recruit and retain exceptional people, and preserve enough cash to survive adverse conditions. His advice links those responsibilities rather than treating them as separate functions: a credible vision helps close scarce talent, disciplined hiring and communication turn strategy into coordinated action, and conservative runway plus measured channel economics keeps the organization alive long enough to execute.

## Key Claims
- CEOs should concentrate on vision and strategy, talent, and cash because most high-leverage executive work maps to one of those responsibilities.
- Vision must be repeated beyond the leadership team, explained in terms of stakeholder purpose, and connected to each employee's role through a regular all-hands cadence.
- Strategy should adapt to material market change, but frequent wholesale reversals impose increasing cost, complexity, and confusion as the company grows.
- CEOs should stay personally involved in critical recruiting, plan hiring at least two quarters ahead, sell impact and upside, and evaluate motivation, humility, values, and behavior rather than resumes alone.
- Candidates should be chosen for the company's foreseeable needs—Harper relays an approximately 18-month horizon—because startup roles change rapidly as the organization grows.
- A financing round should fund at least 18 months under an adverse scenario, while growth spending should be constrained by survivability and tested channel returns rather than optimistic forecasts.
- Small channel tests should precede aggressive sales and marketing spend; the article suggests scaling when returns are strong, ideally around three times spend, while preserving the source-specific nature of that threshold.

## Key Quotes
> "Sets the overall vision and strategy of the company and communicates it to all stakeholders" - the first responsibility in the three-part CEO model Harper attributes to Wilson.

> "Makes sure there is always enough cash in the bank" - the model's survival constraint.

> "Skills can be taught, personalities cannot be changed" - Harper's reason for looking beyond a candidate's resume.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction was identified. The article compresses rather than exhaustively defines CEO work; governance, customers, compliance, operations, culture, and crisis response may enter through its three categories but are not independently examined.
- The annual limit on complete strategy changes, two-quarter recruiting lead time, 18-month adverse-case runway, 18-month candidate horizon, and three-times channel return are practitioner heuristics without comparative evidence or definitions of how they should vary by company type, financing environment, or measurement window.
- The claim that a CEO understands the market and competition better than anyone else may describe Harper's preferred founder-led model, but it conflicts with the broader need for distributed expertise, dissent, customer evidence, and board oversight when executive judgment is incomplete.
