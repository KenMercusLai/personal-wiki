## Summary
This Li Jiaoao investment essay uses the book Because Unique and Wang Ning's public stories to answer three doubts about [[PopMart]]: whether revenue depends on irrational blind-box gambling, whether [[Labubu]] can sustain high revenue, and whether the company can keep creating hit IP. The author argues that Pop Mart's model rests more on product love, series-based [[BlindBoxRetail]], continuing [[DesignerToyIPOperations]], and [[WangNing]]'s founder-led organizational evolution than on artificial scarcity alone.

## Key Claims
- [[BlindBoxRetail]] at Pop Mart works by adding randomness within a chosen series, not by maintaining a single lottery-like rare prize pool across an entire IP.
- Pop Mart's revenue is presented as supported by real demand for appealing designer toys rather than mainly by non-rational scarcity speculation.
- [[Labubu]] carries concentration risk because it grew from 368 million yuan in 2023 to 14.1 billion yuan in 2025, about 38% of company revenue in the author's account.
- [[DesignerToyIPOperations]] can extend an IP's life and revenue by repeatedly refreshing poses, styles, materials, uses, collaborations, licensing, daily goods, and theme-park or spatial experiences.
- [[PopMart]] is portrayed as a continuously evolving organization that moved from small retail to toy retail, then to self-operated IP and broader IP operations.
- [[WangNing]]'s student-era star-wand story is used as evidence of early retail instinct, quick recovery from supply shocks, and a "good product first" operating belief.

## Key Quotes
> "盲盒只是一种销售方式" - on treating blind boxes as a sales format rather than the core source of demand

> "好产品才是第一位的" - on the lesson the author draws from Wang Ning's early retail experience
