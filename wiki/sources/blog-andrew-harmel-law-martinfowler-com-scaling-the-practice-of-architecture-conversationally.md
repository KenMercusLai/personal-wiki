## Summary
[[AndrewHarmelLaw]] proposes [[DecentralizedArchitectureGovernance]] built around the [[AdviceProcess]]: anyone may make an architectural decision after consulting affected people and relevant experts, without requiring consensus. [[ArchitectureDecisionRecords]], an [[ArchitectureAdvisoryForum]], team-sourced architectural principles, and an internal [[TechnologyRadar]] make the decisions, dissent, direction, and local technical landscape visible while leaving ownership with the decision-taker. The model aims to spread architectural judgment and learning across autonomous teams, but depends on inclusion, psychological safety, and architects resisting shadow control.

## Key Claims
- Architectural decision authority can be distributed if decision-takers must seek, listen to, and record advice from affected people and relevant experts.
- The size of the consultation set reveals a decision's impact and can encourage smaller, more reversible decisions.
- ADRs work as both durable decision records and thinking checklists when they capture context, options, consequences, advice, and status.
- An open advisory forum can spread organizational and architectural knowledge without becoming an approval board when originators retain decision ownership.
- Team-sourced, testable principles and a locally maintained technology radar give autonomous teams shared direction and situational awareness without centralized prescription.
- The elements work as a system: ADRs carry advice, principle fit, and radar implications into recurring conversations where decisions and shared guidance can be revisited.
- The model fails when participation narrows to usual suspects, off-grid decisions are punished instead of examined, or senior architects quietly reclaim control.

## Key Quotes
> "anyone can make an architectural decision" - the Advice Process rule

> "we are not looking for consensus here, but we are looking for a broad range of inputs and voices" - distinction between consultation and collective approval

> "I'm failing to get the right conversations happening, with the right people, at the right time" - the architect's reframed responsibility

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found. The article complements [[ArchitectureAlignmentForces]] by specifying a participatory decision process, but its reported gains in decision speed, quality, ownership, and learning are practitioner observations rather than comparative evidence.
- The model explicitly treats technical strategy and cross-functional requirements as surrounding context rather than explaining how to create or validate them, so it is not a complete architecture-governance system by itself.
