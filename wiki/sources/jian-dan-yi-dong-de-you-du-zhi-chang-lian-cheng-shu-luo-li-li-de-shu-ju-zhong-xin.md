## Summary
[[RORIRI]] argues that hostility toward [[ObjectivesAndKeyResults]] and [[AgileSoftwareDevelopment]] often reflects management misuse rather than the tools' stated purposes. The essay contrasts OKRs with KPIs, adaptive learning with waterfall plans divided into sprints, and user-driven change with arbitrary product-direction changes, then connects these substitutions to [[WorkplaceIncentiveDesign]] and [[PsychologicalSafety]] in authoritarian workplaces.

## Key Claims
- [[ObjectivesAndKeyResults]] should communicate a new direction through ambitious objectives and measurable progress, while KPIs monitor the health and operating floor of established work.
- Attaching compensation or performance ratings to deliberately ambitious OKR completion encourages employees to lower targets, protect themselves, and optimize the score instead of exploring uncertain opportunities.
- Applying an OKR-style tolerance for partial completion to hard operating KPIs is the inverse category error; service availability and other minimum standards cannot simply inherit a 70-percent aspiration rule.
- [[AgileSoftwareDevelopment]] addresses uncertainty through short increments, real user feedback, and revision of later work, whereas dividing a fixed top-down plan into sprints changes reporting cadence without creating adaptive learning.
- Product-manager preference changes are not equivalent to market feedback; in Scrum-like work, a stable sprint boundary can protect current commitments while new evidence changes the backlog for a later iteration.
- Refactoring, testing, and code-quality standards are part of sustainable iterative delivery because postponing them makes later change progressively harder through [[TechnicalDebt]].
- The essay interprets these tool distortions as symptoms of authority and incentive systems that preserve managerial control while borrowing human-centered vocabulary.

## Key Quotes
> “把 OKR 当 KPI 用，再把 Waterfall 切成 Sprint 假装敏捷开发。” - on relabeling control systems without changing their operating logic.

> “没人知道标准到底是什么，所有人都在猜老板的心思。” - on the trust cost of mixing aspiration and evaluation rules.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found. The source strengthens existing criticism of ceremony-led Agile adoption and narrow metric incentives by describing how labels can remain while feedback, technical quality, and safe experimentation disappear.
- The essay is a practitioner argument without organizational cases, outcome measurements, or citations. Its 70-percent OKR heuristic, fixed two-to-four-week sprint range, universal “every sprint” refactoring prescription, and claims about what management textbooks say should be treated as illustrative rather than universal standards.
