## Summary
[[HenryWard]] presents the full-day course used to introduce new and existing [[EShares]] employees to the company's values and execution model. The course combines pervasive MVP language, a professional-sports-team analogy, synchronized rituals, software-mediated leverage, recurring one-on-ones, deliberate capital deployment, market-based compensation, and the ambition to develop alumni who later build important companies. It is a founder-authored 2015 culture manual rather than evidence that these practices produced the intended performance, retention, fairness, or durability outcomes.

## Key Claims
- Culture becomes operational when a company teaches a shared vocabulary, repeats rituals, and makes expectations concrete enough to guide daily decisions.
- eShares treated every employee as a "software person" whose role was to translate real-world problems into software, automate work, and begin a new learn-execute cycle.
- [[OrganizationalLeverage]] differs from efficiency: the stated goal is to maximize impact for a fixed effort rather than minimize effort for a fixed output.
- The professional-sports-team model supported synchronized 8:30 a.m. starts, company-wide show-and-tells, visible performance expectations, and role-based market compensation.
- Manager one-on-ones were recurring listening walks intended to strengthen relationships across reporting layers, not only review immediate work.
- Financial discipline meant targeting a $600,000 monthly burn, funding additional projects from revenue, and deploying roughly $20 million in cash gradually through a "siege" rather than rapid-spend strategy.
- [[MarketBasedCompensation]] was tied to external skill value and individual marketability, reviewed after nine months and annually thereafter, with a stated 75th-percentile target and learning environment positioned as part of the employee bargain.
- Ward framed the company's deepest legacy as the people it developed, drawing on PayPal's alumni network and the idea that a startup should be difficult enough to teach resilience without making success seem impossible.

## Key Quotes
> "For a fixed amount of effort, how can you maximize your impact?" - Ward's leverage test.

## Contradictions
- The source rejects efficiency in favor of leverage, while much of the distinction is rhetorical: automation can raise leverage precisely by improving process efficiency, and fixed-output work can still be valuable.
- Mandatory synchronized office attendance and aggressive performance metaphors may conflict with remote work, schedule flexibility, accessibility, caregiving, inclusion, and psychological-safety practices elsewhere in the wiki.
- Market pricing and periodic downward salary adjustment are presented as objective, but the source supplies no measurement method, bias controls, pay-equity analysis, or evidence that employees could verify the assessment.
- The 2015 profitability path and cultural durability claims are plans, not reported outcomes.
