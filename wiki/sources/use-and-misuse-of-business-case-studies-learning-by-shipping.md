## Summary
[[StevenSinofsky]] argues that business and product development are context-heavy social practices, so cases are most useful as material for disciplined questioning rather than recipes to copy. He distinguishes the formal [[BusinessCaseMethod]] - a researched, multi-perspective account explored through guided classroom discussion - from the common misuse of successful-company stories as catalogs of tools, roles, spending choices, or validation for decisions already preferred. His "food synthesizer" analogy captures the central warning: imitating an organization's visible form without its interdependent people, culture, processes, constraints, and history reproduces the appearance of a system rather than its substance.

## Key Claims
- Formal case teaching uses questions and discussion to make learners analyze a complex situation from a manager's position; the case is not designed to deliver one definitive answer.
- Strong cases emerge from investigating an organizational situation across functions and participants rather than beginning with a theory or relying only on executive and public-relations accounts.
- Business and product teams are interdependent systems whose visible artifacts cannot be detached safely from culture, informal process, incentives, resources, personalities, and history.
- Success cases invite [[SurvivorshipBias]]: favorable outcomes make every visible team attribute look causally correct while luck, serendipity, and failed alternatives disappear.
- Failure cases create the inverse distortion by making every feature of the organization look obviously wrong, even though failed systems can contain practices worth preserving.
- Copying tools, job roles, org structures, campaigns, spending decisions, or acquisitions is especially risky when leaders choose what is easy to implement rather than what fits their context.
- Cases can become confirmation devices when readers emphasize similarities to a desired decision and overlook the differences where the decisive substance may lie.

## Key Quotes
> "The goal is not to find the answer or prove a point, but to explore or even attempt to live through the experience of managers going through a difficult time." - on the purpose of formal case discussion.

> "The theme for all of these cautions is to avoid the 'form' of a case study unless you are really willing to understand and put to work the substance of a case study." - on transfer across organizations.
