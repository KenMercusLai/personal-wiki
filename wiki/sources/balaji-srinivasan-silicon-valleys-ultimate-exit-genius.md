## Summary
[[BalajiSrinivasan]] argues that [[SiliconValley]] should treat [[ExitAsGovernance]] as a peaceful complement to political voice: build alternatives, lower switching costs, and make bad policies less able to trap people. The talk contrasts Silicon Valley with the [[PaperBelt]], claiming software is disrupting media, education, finance, and regulation, while technologies such as [[Bitcoin]], telepresence, 3D printing, quantified self, drones, and smart property could make exit easier. ## Key Claims
- [[ExitAsGovernance]] complements voice by making complaints, votes, reforms, or patches more credible when people can leave for alternatives.
- [[SiliconValley]] is framed as culturally and institutionally shaped by exit through startups, forks, enforceable non-competes being absent in California, venture funding for disruption, and the browser back button.
- The [[PaperBelt]] names incumbent U.S. institutions centered on paper-era industries in Boston, New York, Los Angeles, and Washington, D.C.
- Silicon Valley's disruptive power is described as threatening the Paper Belt's control over entertainment, advertising, publishing, education, finance, transportation, lodging, and regulation.
- The proposed "ultimate exit" is an [[OptInSociety]]: voluntary, technology-run, and ultimately outside the United States rather than a direct political takeover.
- Technologies including [[Bitcoin]], 3D printing, telepresence, quantified self, drones, and smart property are presented as ways to turn regulation, capital controls, immigration, medicine, warfare, and property into software-mediated domains.
