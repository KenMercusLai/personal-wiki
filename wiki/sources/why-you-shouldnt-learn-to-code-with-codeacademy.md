## Summary
This historical critique argues that [[Codecademy]] can introduce language syntax and motivate beginners but cannot, by itself, teach the reasoning, persistence, tool use, and applied practice needed for software development. It recommends supplementing bite-sized lessons with projects, deliberate review, problem solving, research, and feedback. The extensive comment thread both corroborates the gap between completing exercises and building independently and qualifies the headline by describing Codecademy as a useful entry point, refresher, or syntax primer when paired with other learning environments.

## Key Claims
- Programming requires decomposition, whole-program reasoning, debugging research, communication, and persistence rather than syntax recall alone.
- One-pass, bite-sized exercises can feel rewarding without producing durable retention unless learners revisit and apply the material.
- A browser lesson environment can hide practical skills such as compiling or running code, using editors and terminals, integrating files, package management, and writing readable code.
- Learners should move into projects, programming puzzles, note-taking, flashcards, and community feedback to turn isolated language features into usable skill.
- The comment thread repeatedly reports a transfer gap between guided exercises and independent building, but it also presents Codecademy as valuable for beginners, experienced programmers learning a new syntax, and learners who already have a place to apply the material.
- The criticism describes an older version of the product; commenters note that later paid paths and projects changed parts of the offering, although their assessments remain mixed.

## Key Quotes
> "Syntax is not programming." — the article's distinction between language mechanics and applied skill

> "Codecademy does not teach you to think like a coder." — the central criticism of guided syntax drills

## Connections

## Contradictions
- The headline's broad warning is qualified by many commenters who found [[Codecademy]] effective as an accessible first exposure, a refresher, or a syntax-learning supplement.
- Commenters disagree about whether later Pro and intensive courses corrected the article's complaints; their reports include both successful practical learning and continued frustration with missing explanation, abrupt project difficulty, and weak support.
