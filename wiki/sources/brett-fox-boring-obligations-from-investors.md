## Summary
[[BrettFox]] argues that a founder's relationship with investors creates recurring work after financing closes, including quarterly financial reporting, cap-table updates, insurance and valuation documentation, references for a venture firm's limited partners, technical diligence on prospective investments, and attendance at portfolio events. These requests may look administrative or optional, but the article presents them as part of maintaining [[FounderInvestorRelations]] because investors use company information in portfolio reviews and need portfolio-company cooperation when raising funds or evaluating deals. The anecdotes also extend [[FounderInvestorFit]] beyond selection: different investor styles can impose materially different costs after they join the cap table.

## Key Claims
- Investors commonly request recurring financial and operating information such as revenue, gross margin, expenses, net income or loss, cash, debt, capital raised, headcount, burn, valuation, D&O insurance, and next-round timing.
- Founders should treat reporting requests seriously because investors use the information in portfolio reviews; delegating preparation to a controller does not remove founder responsibility for review.
- A venture firm's own limited-partner fundraising can create reference requests for portfolio-company founders.
- Investors may ask founders and their teams to evaluate prospective portfolio companies, turning operating expertise into part of the investor relationship.
- Portfolio dinners and retreats consume founder time and sometimes travel money, yet attendance may be socially or strategically difficult to decline.
- Investor style affects the burden and character of the post-close relationship, from a restrained annual dinner to a costly multi-day retreat.

## Key Quotes
> "You never get a boring request from your investors." — Fox's reframing of apparently routine investor obligations as consequential relationship work.

> "You need to be supportive of your investors requests for help with their investors." — on portfolio founders serving as references during a venture firm's fundraising.

## Contradictions
- The article qualifies [[FounderTimeLeverage]] and the selective-disengagement case in [[FounderInvestorFit]]: before investment, a founder may end a low-value meeting, but after an investor joins the cap table, reporting, references, diligence help, and event attendance may be difficult to refuse.
- No direct contradiction was found with [[VentureCapitalFundStructure]]. The source adds an anecdotal operating link between LP-backed fund economics and requests made of portfolio-company founders.
- The account is one founder's retrospective and does not supply investor perspectives, contractual terms, representative frequency or cost data, or evidence that every request is mandatory or beneficial.
