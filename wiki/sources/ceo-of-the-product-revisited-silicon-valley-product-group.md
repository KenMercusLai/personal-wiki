## Summary
[[MartyCagan]] revisits the controversial [[ProductManagerAsCEO]] metaphor for [[SiliconValleyProductGroup]], arguing that it is useful only when it means broad business responsibility, decision ownership, and integration across company functions, not being anyone's boss. The article says the metaphor has become more important because many people with Product Manager or Product Owner titles lack deep training and underperform the actual [[ProductManagement]] role. It qualifies the metaphor with humility, earned trust, and the need to preserve team respect.

## Key Claims
- The "CEO of the product" metaphor is dangerous when product managers read it as permission to act like formal bosses.
- The metaphor is valuable when it teaches product managers to understand the whole business and take responsibility for hard decisions.
- Many product managers have little real training beyond shallow Product Owner courses, creating a gap between title and needed role.
- Strong product managers work across legal, privacy, pricing, sales, marketing, finance, contracts, launch, technology, customers, analytics, customer success, and security.
- Early startups often have founders or actual CEOs doing this product-integration work, but that does not scale across many products.
- Product managers need humility, earned respect, and team trust even as they own broad business accountability.
- Done well, product management can become a proving ground for future startup CEOs.

## Key Quotes
> "If this sounds easy to you, you should probably read the list again more slowly." - on the breadth of PM work.

> "this does not mean they are the boss of anyone" - on the metaphor's boundary.
