## Summary
[[HappyXiao]] recounts how [[AustinRief]] and [[MorningBrew]] changed a college ambassador program across three semesters: a highly selective cohort produced one exceptional result but suffered heavy attrition, automated mass recruitment expanded campus reach but weakened support and outcomes, and a tiered hybrid combined broad recruiting with intensive help for the most committed students. The case frames [[CampusAmbassadorPrograms]] as a portfolio-and-support problem in which a small active minority creates much of the value, while intrinsic interest, career signaling, and enablement may matter more than cash.

## Key Claims
- Selecting 12 apparently proactive students did not prevent attrition; half reportedly quit within weeks, while one remaining ambassador generated 1,000 registrations in two weeks.
- Automating recruitment produced more than 100 ambassadors and broader campus coverage, but reduced one-to-one coaching and yielded few exceptional performers.
- The hybrid program recruited about 50 students automatically, then promoted active participants into an advanced private group with peer learning and closer support.
- Scripts, flyers, practical guidance, and visible advancement gave committed ambassadors tools and status rather than relying only on recruitment volume.
- The hybrid reportedly reached more than 100 campuses and produced more than ten ambassadors with over 500 subscribers each.
- Rief's summary says the strongest ambassadors generated most of the value, program development took substantial time, and monetary incentives often backfired relative to affection for the product and résumé value.

## Key Quotes
> "找到培养你最好的大使的方案，他们将带来80% 的价值" - on concentrating enablement around the strongest ambassadors.

> "金钱往往会起到反作用" - on the reported limits of cash incentives.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found. The case qualifies a simple quality-versus-quantity choice by showing that neither selective recruiting nor automated scale alone reliably identified and enabled productive ambassadors.
- The article is a short secondary retelling with self-reported, historical numbers. It supplies no retention, conversion denominator, campus-level distribution, acquisition cost, control group, or evidence that the ambassador program alone caused Morning Brew's later growth; "80%" is presented as a heuristic rather than a measured allocation in this cohort.
