## Summary
[[FirstRoundReview]] interviews [[PayalKadakia]] about how [[ClassPass]] moved from the unused Classtivity class-search product to Passport and then to a subscription marketplace. The article argues that marketplace product-market fit comes from solving the real behavior problem, not from press, capital raised, polished technology, or the first product metaphor. The inspected local image is a portrait photograph of Kadakia and adds attribution context but no separate factual evidence beyond the prose.

## Key Claims
- Press, accelerator attention, funding, and social attention can be [[VanityMetrics]] when the core marketplace transaction is not happening.
- Early founders should return to the problem they are trying to solve and test whether the offer actually changes customer behavior.
- Manually handling parts of the product can reveal which backend work matters and which technology is premature.
- A compelling offer can beat polished UX before fit: Passport generated 20,000 reservations in six months despite a bare-bones product.
- User workarounds can reveal demand: repeated Passport signups showed that customers valued variety more than the team's intended one-time sampling path.
- [[ClassPass]] treated reservations per person as the clearest fit metric because it tied engagement, churn, revenue, studio value, and mission impact together.
- Kadakia applies the [[FoggBehaviorModel]] to marketplace behavior: motivation, ability, and triggers all matter, and Classtivity initially overfocused on ability while underbuilding motivation.

## Key Quotes
> "if you can't get the transaction to happen, the technology isn't going to solve it." - Kadakia on validating the core marketplace behavior before overbuilding.

> "I’d rather have 150 people using it weekly than 1,500 attending the occasional class." - Kadakia on reservations per person as the true-north metric.
