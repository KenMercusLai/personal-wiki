## Summary
Fortune profiles [[Coinbase]] as the first billion-dollar U.S. cryptocurrency startup after the 2017 boom, tracing how [[BrianArmstrong]] tried to turn a simple crypto on-ramp into a regulated, institution-facing blockchain finance company. The article frames Coinbase's advantage as a mix of ease of use, compliance posture, first-mover scale, and investor backing, while emphasizing fragility from the post-boom price collapse, outages, customer-service failures, fraud costs, the Ethereum flash crash, and Bitcoin Cash controversy. The inspected images include portraits of Armstrong, Tina Bhatnagar, and Balaji Srinivasan, a Coinbase office photo, childhood photo material, and a chart showing customer accounts rising toward 25 million while Bitcoin's price fell to about $6,410 by September 13, 2018.

## Key Claims
- [[Coinbase]] grew from a 2012 user-friendly Bitcoin exchange into a crypto finance company with 25 million customer accounts, roughly $1 billion in annual revenue, and a reported profitable private-company position.
- [[BrianArmstrong]] framed Bitcoin and blockchain as ways to make money movement faster, cheaper, and less dependent on banks or governments, but Coinbase's mainstream path required regulatory cooperation rather than anti-government posturing.
- Coinbase's 2017 growth exposed operational weaknesses: outages, delayed customer support, fraud costs, an Ethereum flash crash without a circuit breaker, and controversy around Bitcoin Cash trading.
- The post-2017 crypto bust threatened Coinbase's trading-fee model because asset prices, trading volumes, and commission margins all fell while Robinhood, Square, and eToro pushed lower-fee competition.
- Coinbase responded by hiring experienced operators, improving support under Tina Bhatnagar, pursuing broker-dealer and banking approvals, offering institutional custody, and acquiring companies under Emilie Choi.
- [[SecurityTokens]] are presented as Coinbase's most promising expansion path: tokenized ownership of private companies, real estate, art, or other assets could create more liquid alternative investment markets.

## Key Quotes
