## Summary
In a short Thrive Global questionnaire, [[JeffBezos]] says he prioritizes eight hours of sleep because it helps him feel energized and make a small number of important decisions well. He also prefers "work-life harmony" to a strict balance metaphor, arguing that satisfaction and energy can reinforce performance across work and home rather than forming a fixed tradeoff.

## Key Claims
- Bezos treats roughly eight hours of sleep as a personal operating requirement for energy and enthusiasm, not as time taken away from productive leadership.
- [[DecisionQuality]] matters more than maximizing daily decision count when a leader's small number of consequential judgments affects [[Amazon]] shareholders.
- Extra waking hours gained by shortchanging sleep may create only the appearance of productivity if judgment and interaction quality deteriorate.
- [[WorkLifeBalance]] can be framed as reciprocal harmony: positive experience at work can improve family participation, while positive experience at home can improve energy at work.
- The interview supplies one executive's self-report and reasoning, not comparative evidence that eight hours is optimal for every person or that sleep caused better company outcomes.
- All three local images were inspected and omitted as non-evidentiary portraits from the same Jeff Bezos photo session: a lead crop, a tiny thumbnail, and a larger seated portrait.

## Key Quotes
> "Making a small number of key decisions well is more important than making a large number of decisions." - Bezos on why sleep can matter to shareholders.

> "Quality is usually more important than quantity." - Bezos on decisions and interactions.

## Connections
- [[JeffBezos]] - interview subject describing his personal sleep requirement, decision priorities, and work-life framing.
- [[Amazon]] - company whose shareholders Bezos uses to explain why executive decision quality matters more than additional decision volume.
- [[SleepAsPerformanceEnhancer]] - sufficient sleep is presented as an input to energy, enthusiasm, judgment, and interaction quality.
- [[DecisionQuality]] - the interview distinguishes the value of a few important decisions from the raw number of decisions or working hours.
- [[WorkLifeBalance]] - Bezos replaces a strict tradeoff metaphor with reciprocal positive spillover between work and home.
- [[PersonalProductivity]] - the source questions whether extra waking hours count as real productivity when their quality falls.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found. The work-life-harmony framing qualifies models that treat work and home only as competing allocations, but it does not remove time, care, health, power, or resource constraints documented elsewhere in the wiki.
