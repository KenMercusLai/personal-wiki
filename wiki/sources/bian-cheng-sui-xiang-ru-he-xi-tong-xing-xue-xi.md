## Summary
[[BianChengSuiXiang]] argues that [[SystematicLearning]] requires both breadth and depth, with the practical goal of understanding a field well enough to use it. The article treats [[LearningMediaChoice]] as part of method design, favoring books for difficult domains, then connects reading selection, the [[FeynmanTechnique]], foundational time allocation, the [[DIKWModel]], and [[CrossDomainWisdom]] into one learning framework.

## Key Claims
- [[SystematicLearning]] means building both a broad map and deep grasp, not merely collecting scattered material.
- [[LearningMediaChoice]] matters more as a domain becomes complex; the article favors books over fragmented social and Q&A streams for systematic study.
- Reading should move across popular, introductory, and specialized works, with quality, plurality of schools, and author style all affecting comprehension.
- The [[FeynmanTechnique]] tests learning through teaching-oriented explanation and exposes whether the learner should revisit introductory material, patch blind spots, or improve audience fit.
- Learners should allocate more time to foundational branches than immediately useful branches because foundations carry across domains.
- The [[DIKWModel]] distinguishes short-lived information from durable knowledge and reserves wisdom for WHY-level understanding.
- [[CrossDomainWisdom]] is especially valuable because some explanatory principles transfer across military strategy, management, politics, technology, investing, and careers.

## Key Quotes
> "系统性学习需要同时具备【广度】与【深度】。" - on the definition of systematic learning.

> "书籍：系统化学习的最好媒介形态，没有之一" - on books as the preferred medium.

> "Wisdom 对应\"WHY 型问题\"。" - on wisdom as explanatory understanding.
