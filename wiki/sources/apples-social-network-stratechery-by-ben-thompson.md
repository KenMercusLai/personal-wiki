## Summary
[[BenThompson]] argues in [[Stratechery]] that [[Apple]]'s decision to stop reporting iPhone, iPad, and Mac unit sales exposed a measurement gap in its mature growth story: Apple wanted investors to focus on Services, engagement, installed base, and customer value, but it would not provide active-customer or revenue-per-user metrics. The article frames [[TodayAtApple]] as a clue to Apple's broader strategy: Apple is trying to build a controlled, real-world social network that deepens user engagement and makes Apple products feel like an ongoing relationship rather than one-off device purchases. The inspected chart materially supports the unit-sales argument by showing iPhone unit growth flattening near zero from 2017 into 2018 while revenue growth rose toward roughly 30%, implying price and mix were carrying growth more than unit expansion.

## Key Claims
- Apple's decision to stop reporting iPhone, iPad, and Mac unit sales made strategic sense if unit growth was unlikely to improve, but it also removed a useful input for understanding ASP-driven revenue growth.
- iPhone revenue and unit growth moved together early in the product line, diverged around the iPhone 6 and iPhone X pricing cycles, and by 2018 showed revenue growth rising while unit growth was nearly flat.
- [[TodayAtApple]] reveals Apple's attempt to turn retail stores into free, curriculum-driven gathering places that deepen the customer relationship after device purchase.
- [[AppleRealWorldSocialNetwork]] captures Thompson's frame: Apple cannot easily build a traditional algorithmic social network, but it can facilitate real-world social interaction through owned stores, devices, Apple IDs, sessions, and local creative programming.
- Services revenue is important, but Thompson argues Apple's new reporting still misses the equivalent of social-network unit metrics: active customers and revenue per user across devices and services.
- [[TimCook]]'s preference for active devices over active users is easier for Apple to measure but less useful for investors trying to evaluate installed-base monetization and customer growth.
- [[LucaMaestri]]'s revenue and gross-margin focus makes financial sense, but the article argues that Apple should show the customer-level metrics underlying its new story.

## Key Quotes
> "real social way" - Ahrendts' phrase for Today at Apple sessions.

> "Apple is in effect trying to build a social network in the real world" - Thompson's core analogy.

> "we will no longer be providing unit sales data" - Maestri's reporting-change announcement.
