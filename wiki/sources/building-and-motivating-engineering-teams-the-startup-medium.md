## Summary
This [[TheStartup]] article argues that small startups can no longer motivate engineers by relying on older stereotypes about developers or by substituting perks for professional respect. The source frames [[EngineeringTeamMotivation]] around three axes: market-rate money, product and business purpose, and adult respect expressed through accountability, goals, and cross-functional partnership. It treats the expanded New York startup and engineering talent market as the context that makes positioning, compensation realism, and meaningful product involvement necessary.

## Key Claims
- Engineers need competitive compensation as a baseline; below-market pay weakens resilience when inevitable startup problems appear.
- Most startups cannot rely on uniquely hard technical problems as their main hiring pitch, so they must make mission, product learning, and business context compelling.
- Engineering motivation improves when engineers participate in non-technical product and business decisions rather than receiving work over the wall.
- Cross-functional product teams help engineers feel connected to customers, operations, marketing, and product decisions.
- Respect is different from pampering: professional engineers should receive clear goals, accountability, partnership, and chances to grow beyond coding alone.
- Startups that cannot pay at the very top of market must balance compensation with undeveloped talent, voice, learning, and business partnership.

## Key Quotes
> "Money does not solve all problems for most people, but lack of money exacerbates all irritations." - on compensation as a resilience baseline.

> "Respect is not pampering" - on treating engineers as accountable professionals.
