## Summary
Liz Gannes reports [[EvanWilliams]]'s candid account of how [[Odeo]] violated his own narrow-and-small startup advice by pursuing an expansive podcasting vision, building for creators he did not resemble, reacting too slowly to Apple, raising heavily before proving direction, and letting organizational commitments drown out his judgment. The company responded by shrinking from 14 employees, targeting web podcast listeners Williams understood firsthand, and isolating experimental work such as [[Twitter]] in a two-person team, although it still lacked a settled business model.

## Key Claims
- Early publicity and investor interest can amplify scope before a startup has established a focused product and audience.
- Founders who are not users of the behavior they support need stronger customer-learning mechanisms; Odeo's web recording tools were too simple for podcasters.
- A strategically plausible long-term answer can still arrive too late for a startup that needs a foothold against a fast-moving platform competitor.
- Raising substantially more capital than an initial experiment requires can turn hiring, board expectations, and press attention into inertia before product direction is clear.
- Founder intuition is easiest to use when it remains close to observed behavior; money and organizational commitments can mute it without proving the intuition correct.
- Odeo's downsizing, listener-focused repositioning, and separation of Twitter into a small dedicated team were attempts to restore focus rather than evidence that the business had already recovered.

## Key Quotes
> "Trying to build too much" - Williams's first diagnosis of Odeo's failure.

> "your gut can get drowned out" - on how capital, hiring, governance, and publicity weakened his judgment.

## Contradictions
- The source qualifies [[FounderInstinct]] rather than endorsing unaided intuition: Williams's gut warned him, but his admitted distance from podcast creation also helped produce a weak creator tool.
- The account does not establish that raising less money, moving faster, or following Williams's intuition would have produced product-market fit. It is a short conference report based largely on the CEO's retrospective, with no customer, employee, investor, or Apple perspective and no operating metrics beyond headcount and funding amounts.
