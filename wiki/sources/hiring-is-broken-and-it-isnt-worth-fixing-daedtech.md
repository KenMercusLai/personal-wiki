## Summary
[[ErikDietrich]] argues that mass developer hiring creates an impedance mismatch: candidates want evidence of individual contribution to matter, while high-volume employers need cheap, standardized filters. Rather than refine cold interviews indefinitely, he proposes constraining organizations to grow through people whose work is already known, accepting slower growth in exchange for relationship-based evidence. The comment thread materially qualifies that proposal by raising job relevance, unequal candidate bargaining power, geographic and network access, affinity bias, referral quality, and the risk of excluding capable outsiders.

## Key Claims
- Resume screens and algorithm or trivia interviews can ignore public work that demonstrates shipping, teaching, and community value, but reviewing every candidate's portfolio imposes costs that large employers may be unable or unwilling to bear.
- The resulting mismatch is structural: candidates seek individualized evaluation while high-volume organizations optimize for repeatable processing of many applicants.
- Candidates with enough leverage can refuse trivia-oriented processes, state deal breakers in advance, prefer code review or public work samples, and exit an unsuitable interview diplomatically.
- Organizations should test a stronger constraint than incremental interview repair: stop treating unknown applicants as cold commodities and build longer-lived pathways through prior collaboration, internships, communities, and trusted work relationships.
- Slower headcount growth may be a feature when the alternative is expanding an organization whose employee proposition, work allocation, or hiring machinery is already weak.
- Commenters counter that algorithmic questions can sometimes be job-relevant and that conventional preparation is portable evidence of learning effort even when it poorly represents daily work.
- Relationship-led hiring can reproduce network privilege, affinity bias, geographic exclusion, and weak referrals; many candidates also lack the economic freedom to boycott common interview formats.

## Key Quotes
> "Interviewees want to be evaluated and match-made as individual humans" - on the candidate side of the mismatch.

> "Let's stop trying to fix the hiring process and start trying to figure out how to replace it." - on institutional redesign.

## Connections

## Contradictions
- The proposal conflicts with process-improvement approaches in [[HiringSystemDesign]]: it treats cold selection as the wrong institution, while the broader synthesis argues that relevant evidence, transparency, calibration, candidate choice, and feedback can improve it.
- The article supplies argument, analogy, and anecdotes rather than comparative outcome evidence. It does not show that relationship-led hiring predicts performance, scales across roles, or reduces bias.
- The comment thread identifies a central trade-off: referrals and prior collaboration provide richer evidence but may narrow access for newcomers, remote workers, immigrants, anxious candidates, and people outside established technical networks.
- Claims about Google interview volume, motives, daily algorithm relevance, and future developer movement away from megacorporations are illustrative or speculative rather than independently established here.
