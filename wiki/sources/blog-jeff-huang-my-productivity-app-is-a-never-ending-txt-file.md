## Summary
[[JeffHuang]] describes a 14-year productivity system built from one calendar and one ongoing plain-text file. The calendar holds future commitments, while the nightly text-file entry turns the next day's calendar items into a bounded daily plan that becomes a searchable work record, meeting log, research notebook, and capacity-feedback system.

## Key Claims
- [[TextFileProductivity]] works by pairing a calendar for future commitments with a daily plain-text entry for today's execution and notes.
- [[PersonalProductivity]] improves when a worker sizes tomorrow's workload the night before, moves oversized work back into the calendar, and avoids an endlessly growing undated todo list.
- [[PersonalKnowledgeManagement]] can be extremely lightweight when daily task lists become a searchable chronological archive of meetings, decisions, ideas, and accomplishments.
- A consistent plain-text style, simple tags, and editor search can provide enough retrieval and aggregate statistics without a specialized productivity app.
- Email can be managed with a small flagging system that separates immediate work, eventual work, and awaited replies instead of treating inbox zero as the main goal.
- Workload control sometimes means unflagging emails, removing calendar items that no longer feel worth doing, and intentionally assigning less future work.

## Key Quotes
> "I gave up and started just tracking in a single text file" - on replacing multiple productivity systems with one text file.

> "that daily todo list is where I also take notes" - on the list turning into a record of work done.

> "There's no running \"todo\" list with items that keep pushed back day after day" - on avoiding an unbounded task backlog.
