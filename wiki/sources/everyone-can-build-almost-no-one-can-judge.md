## Summary
[[MaiYang]] interprets [[KarriSaarinen]]'s 20VC interview and [[Linear]]'s public practices as an argument for [[QualityGrowth]]: durable growth should come from customer value and disciplined product quality rather than capital, promotion, or feature-count theater. As AI lowers implementation cost, the article says [[TasteAsProblemSense|taste]], product judgment, selective scope, defect discipline, and close founder involvement become scarcer advantages. The account is a secondary synthesis that cites public interviews and Linear policies but supplies no independent retention, defect, growth, or causal comparison.

## Key Claims
- [[QualityGrowth]] is sustainable growth rooted in real customer value and product pull rather than spending, financing, marketing pressure, or selectively presented numbers.
- [[Linear]] operationalizes quality through a [[ZeroBugsPolicy]] that reportedly handles high-priority defects within 48 hours and other defects within seven days instead of leaving them in an indefinite backlog.
- Linear's “Quality Wednesdays” are presented as a recurring way to notice and repair small experience defects before they accumulate, making quality a habit rather than a periodic project.
- AI lowers the cost of producing code and serviceable products, which raises the relative importance of deciding what not to build, detecting experiences that are usable but wrong, and polishing the details that sustain use.
- Feature checklists and platform breadth can disguise weak product judgment; scope should follow the user's main workflow rather than the availability of new model capabilities.
- Founder involvement is framed as informed presence in product, user, and quality decisions, not personal control of every task.
- Return behavior, defect handling, and the team's actual allocation of attention provide stronger evidence of durable quality than launch heat or a polished growth curve alone.

## Key Quotes
> “AI 把代码门槛拉低了，但把‘判断力门槛’抬高了。” — the article's central claim about implementation and judgment.

> “Quality Growth 不是‘慢一点增长’，而是‘用对质量的极致要求，换来更扎实、更可持续的增长’。” — the author's synthesis of Linear's operating stance.

## Contradictions
- The article is [[MaiYang]]'s synthesis of interviews, Linear posts, and her own builder experience rather than an independent study of Linear. It provides no cohorts, revenue or spend data, defect distributions, customer comparisons, or before-and-after results showing that the named practices caused sustainable growth.
- The 48-hour and seven-day targets are reported from Linear's public policy, but the supplied article does not define priority classification, measurement start and stop, closure rules, exceptions, reopen rates, or whether every product defect is covered.
- Zero-backlog language does not mean defect-free software; immediate repair can also be unsafe or uneconomic when reproduction, dependencies, coordinated releases, or formal risk acceptance require bounded delay.
- “Founder mode” and product taste are underspecified and can rationalize micromanagement, aesthetic gatekeeping, weak delegation, or decisions unsupported by user evidence. Close involvement is useful only when paired with feedback, expertise, accountability, and organizational capacity.
- The source's sole local image was opened and classified as a promotional interview thumbnail. Its “How to Fundraise Like a Pro” text does not add evidence for the article's claims, so it was omitted and no asset manifest was required.
