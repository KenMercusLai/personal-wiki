## Summary
[[JasonCalacanis]] explains why [[InsideCom|Inside.com]] stopped investing in its dedicated news app and concentrated on email after app usage failed to grow while email alerts showed stronger engagement. He attributes dedicated news apps' weakness to social networks absorbing news discovery, and frames the pivot as preserving the app's editorial experience and a 180,000-address audience while moving to a simpler, lower-cost delivery model. The reported 50% open rate and accelerating list additions are promising but early, segmented, self-reported signals that do not establish retention, revenue, profitability, or durable product-market fit.

## Key Claims
- Calacanis says strong reviews, press, Apple featuring, and favorable user feedback did not translate into breakout growth for Inside's app, while several peer news apps also shut down or failed to break out.
- He argues that social networks displaced dedicated news apps because people preferred news selected and shared by friends inside an existing habit.
- He estimates that a cross-platform app company at reasonable scale required roughly 12 people and $2–3 million per year in San Francisco, and recommends proving strong growth on one platform before expanding to others.
- Inside identified email alerts as its strongest engagement signal, segmented its 180,000-address list, and moved the app's summarized-news experience into an email product called the Inside Daily Brief.
- The author reports that the most interested 10% of the segmented audience opened 50% of twice-daily emails; the list added 800 addresses in January and 3,900 by nearly halfway through February.
- The email operation reportedly required three roles rather than 12: a writer, a chief executive, and a growth or marketing practitioner.
- His pivot rule is to act when the company is not growing and peers are dying, then concentrate resources on the simplest promising asset or behavior already visible in the failed model.

## Key Quotes
> "News apps failed because social networks succeeded."

> "If you’re not growing, you’re dying."

> "If you’re going to pivot, look at everything you’ve learned and put everything into the most promising, simple piece of your failed business."

## Contradictions
- Qualifies praise, press, platform featuring, and positive feedback as evidence of product quality but not sufficient evidence of growth or product-market fit.
- Qualifies the categorical claim that news apps failed: the essay discusses selected peers and two large-company products, but supplies no market-wide usage data or later outcomes.
- The reported 50% open rate applies to the most interested 10% of a segmented list, not the full audience, and the source gives no click, retention, unsubscribe, revenue, acquisition-cost, or profitability data.
- The staffing and cost comparison is the author's rough 2016 estimate; it does not isolate equivalent scope or account for editorial, infrastructure, compliance, deliverability, or later scaling costs.
