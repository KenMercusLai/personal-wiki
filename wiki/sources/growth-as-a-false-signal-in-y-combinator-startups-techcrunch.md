## Summary
[[DougRenert]] argues that a short burst of early revenue growth is a weak standalone predictor of durable startup success, especially when percentages are calculated from tiny bases or reflect one-time publicity. Using anonymized metrics from 22 [[YCombinator]] companies, he contrasts impressive Demo Day growth claims with implausible constant-rate extrapolations and recommends examining engagement, retention, user love, and [[UnitEconomics]] before treating growth as evidence of [[ProductMarketFit]].

## Key Claims
- [[PaulGraham]]'s cited YC heuristic treats 5–7% weekly growth as good and revenue as the preferred measure, but the essay argues that founders and investors can overextend this guidance into a single-factor success test.
- The 22 reporting companies claimed 6–200% month-over-month revenue growth, with a 60% average, 41% median, and six companies at least doubling monthly.
- Mechanically extending those rates for one year produces about $21 billion in combined monthly revenue, illustrating why a short-window rate should not be read as a durable trajectory.
- Growth is more credible when it persists and is not mainly caused by a launch article, newly opened waitlist, or one-time influencer campaign.
- Most accelerator companies are still exploring their businesses at Demo Day, so engagement, retention, customer enthusiasm, and early [[UnitEconomics]] may be more informative than a headline growth percentage alone.
- The calculation is illustrative rather than predictive: company names are withheld, two revenue estimates apply an assumed 15% take rate to reported GMV, and constant compounding is knowingly unrealistic.

## Key Quotes
> "This period of exploration should be expected and embraced." - on why Demo Day companies may not yet be ready to optimize for growth.

> "We should examine its early unit economics closely." - on evaluating the business beneath the growth rate.

## Contradictions
- Qualifies [[PaulGraham]]'s cited growth heuristic: revenue growth can be useful, but the essay rejects treating a brief percentage increase as a sufficient predictor of long-run success.
- Qualifies [[YCombinator]]'s Demo Day signal by arguing that selection, publicity, and a short reporting window do not establish durable growth or product-market fit.
- The article's own extrapolation should not be mistaken for a forecast. It intentionally compounds fixed monthly rates to expose their implausibility, while withholding the company-level data needed to audit base effects or compare later outcomes.
- Three evidence-bearing charts were referenced, but all three original TechCrunch image URLs returned HTTP 404 during ingest. Their prose-repeated aggregate figures are included above; any additional company-level visual detail could not be inspected or retained.
