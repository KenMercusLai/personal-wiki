## Summary
[[FarnamStreet]] builds the [[CircleOfCompetence]] out of [[WarrenBuffett]]'s 1996 shareholder-letter rule that an investor only needs to be able to evaluate the companies inside the area they actually understand, and that the size of that area matters less than knowing where its boundary sits. The essay grounds the idea in a restaurant whose basic economics most readers could learn well enough to invest in, contrasted with microchip and biotech companies that most readers could not judge at the same level. It then takes the model outside investing: Buffett's furniture-store manager Mrs. B refused to own stocks because she did not understand them, and [[CharlieMunger]] turns the same boundary into a life question of finding where you have an edge and playing only there. Farnam Street's instruction is to define the perimeter, operate inside it, expand it slowly, and never be afraid to say "I don't know".

## Key Claims
- A circle of competence is the knowledge a person has built through experience or study, and some domains require far more specialty than others to evaluate.
- Buffett's rule from the 1996 shareholder letter is that an investor does not have to be an expert on every company, or even many, only on the companies inside the circle - and that knowing its boundaries is vital while its size is not.
- The circle can be widened, but only slowly and over time, and mistakes are most often made when a person strays outside it.
- Competence is domain-specific rather than a general trait: Mrs. B understood cash, furniture, and real estate well enough to buy thousands of end tables and twenty odd-lot carpets, but had nothing to do with stocks.
- A narrow circle can be an advantage because devotion to it produces focus, which the essay credits for Mrs. B overcoming her handicaps to build the largest furniture store in Nebraska.
- Munger generalizes the model to life: figure out your own aptitudes, avoid games where other people hold the aptitude, and play where you have an edge, using the attainable goal of becoming the best plumbing contractor in a small town against the near-hopeless goal of being the best tennis player in the world.
- The essay's practical takeaway is honesty as much as knowledge: define the perimeter, operate inside, work to expand it, never fool yourself about where it stands today, and never be afraid to say "I don't know".

## Key Quotes
> "You don't have to be an expert on every company, or even many. You only have to be able to evaluate companies within your circle of competence. The size of that circle is not very important; knowing its boundaries, however, is vital." - Buffett's 1996 Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter, the essay's core statement of the idea.

> "I'm no genius. I'm smart in spots - but I stay around those spots." - Tom Watson Sr., quoted as a plain-language version of the same discipline.

> "She understands cash. She understands furniture. She understands real estate. She doesn't understand stocks, so she doesn't have anything to do with them." - Buffett on Mrs. B, showing that abstaining outside the circle can coexist with extreme success inside it.

> "You have to figure out where you've got an edge. And you've got to play within your own circle of competence." - Charlie Munger extending the model from investing to life.

> "Never fool yourself about where it stands today, and never be afraid to say 'I don't know'." - the essay's closing instruction.
