## Summary
Crunchbase News explains [[StartupFinancingMechanics]] through a fictitious startup, Internet of Wings, moving from incorporation to an unpriced seed round and then a priced Series A. The case shows how [[UnpricedSeedFinancing]] instruments such as SAFEs can convert through discounts or valuation caps, causing different share counts, investor returns, post-money valuation, and [[CapTableDilution]] outcomes than a simple pre-money-plus-cash formula would suggest. It is a finance-literacy source for founders, employees, and builders who need to reason about cap tables rather than only memorize fundraising vocabulary.

## Key Claims
- A startup cap table begins with issued common shares, founder allocations, and an employee option pool before outside financing.
- [[UnpricedSeedFinancing]] delays explicit valuation: investors provide cash now in exchange for future shares when a priced round happens.
- Convertible notes and SAFEs both can convert into equity later, but SAFEs are presented as founder-friendlier because they are not debt and do not carry maturity dates or interest.
- Seed investors often use discounts and valuation caps to compensate for early risk and protect against being squeezed by a high later valuation.
- Series A pricing depends on pre-money valuation and shares outstanding, but seed conversion rights can create additional shares at lower effective prices.
- [[CapTableDilution]] reduces founders' and the employee pool's percentage ownership, while the value of their stake can still rise if the company's valuation grows.
- Dilution matters for control because voting power often follows share count or share class.
