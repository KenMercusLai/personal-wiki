## Summary
This CS183C synthesis collects scaling lessons from [[ReidHoffman]], [[EricSchmidt]], [[MarissaMayer]], [[BrianChesky]], [[DianeGreene]], [[JeffWeiner]], and other operators. It frames [[Blitzscaling]] as a stage-sensitive choice to trade efficiency and safety for speed when competition and market timing make delay dangerous. The article's recurring lesson is that scaling changes the company itself: hiring, culture, communication, founder behavior, and CEO priorities all have to be rebuilt as the organization crosses new orders of magnitude.

## Key Claims
- [[Blitzscaling]] means deliberately accepting high burn, fragility, and possible collapse in order to move faster than ordinary operational support would allow.
- Startup advice is stage-specific: the source's OS scale table moves from OS1 "family" to OS5 "nation," and [[MarissaMayer]] cites the lesson that processes break at each order of magnitude.
- Before [[ProductMarketFit]], startups should hire slowly; after the model starts working, recruiting becomes a central scaling system.
- [[DoingThingsThatDoNotScale]] can be the route to scale because a small base of intensely loyal users gives teams better learning than a large base of indifferent users.
- [[StartupCulture]] scales through hiring bars, repeated leadership messages, explicit values, and visible executive behavior rather than slogans alone.
- [[ScalingCommunication]] becomes necessary once employees no longer share the same context; all-hands, written updates, orientation, and repeated priorities replace informal alignment.
- [[CEOScalingRole]] shifts from doing or solving everything toward strategy, culture, senior hiring, path clearing, coaching, and product-sense stewardship.

## Key Quotes
> "Keep it small, then once you hit the point where it's working, you grow as fast as possible." - Eric Schmidt on timing growth.

> "Culture at scale is all about repetition" - Brian Chesky on preserving shared beliefs as Airbnb grew.
