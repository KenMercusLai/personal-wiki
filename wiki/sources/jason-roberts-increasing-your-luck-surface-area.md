## Summary
[[JasonRoberts]] argues that people can increase their exposure to serendipitous opportunities by combining substantive work they care about with effective communication of that work to other people. He compresses the idea into `L = D * T`, where luck is modeled as the interaction of doing and telling: doing develops expertise and visible enthusiasm, while telling lets other people recognize and act on that value in ways the creator may not predict. The equation is a conceptual heuristic rather than an empirical model, and the saved source provides an archive date but no original publication date.

## Key Claims
- [[LuckAndEffortInSuccess]] includes opportunity exposure as well as preparation: useful work creates something others can respond to, while communication increases the number of possible responses.
- Working deeply on a genuine interest can develop expertise whose value may exceed the creator's initial use for it.
- Awareness can magnify the value of expertise because observers may offer employment, partnership, investment, or other unanticipated opportunities.
- Passion can have social value independent of expertise because visible enthusiasm may attract attention and participation.
- Roberts formalizes the interaction as `L = D * T`, with doing and telling acting as complements rather than substitutes.
- The source does not measure any variable, establish proportionality, or show that broader communication always produces relevant or beneficial opportunities.
- The supplied Markdown contains no image references.

## Key Quotes
> "In other words, you make your own luck." - Roberts's agency claim about increasing exposure to serendipity.

> "L = D * T" - the article's compact representation of luck as doing multiplied by telling.

## Contradictions
- The source complements [[LuckAndEffortInSuccess]] by adding communication to preparation and action, but its multiplicative equation is not a measured causal relationship and cannot distinguish audience size from relevance, trust, timing, access, or communication quality.
- “Making your own luck” can overstate control. Structural advantage, platform distribution, geography, language, safety, discrimination, caregiving, and discretionary time affect both the capacity to do visible work and who is able to notice it.
- Passion and public enthusiasm are not reliable proxies for expertise, product value, or sustainable work; communication can also create unwanted exposure, distraction, imitation pressure, harassment, or reputational risk.
