## Summary
[[DavidLee]] recommends a short set of external startup checklists rather than inventing a new framework: [[PeterThiel]]'s seven startup questions, [[SequoiaCapital]]'s business-plan and pitch-deck checklist, [[AndreessenHorowitz]]'s startup metrics lists, and [[BillGurley]]'s marketplace-evaluation factors. The article frames [[StartupEvaluationChecklists]] as useful memory aids for entrepreneurs, while warning that writing down checklist items is much easier than executing them.

## Key Claims
- Startup checklist work can be borrowed from strong existing frameworks rather than rebuilt from scratch.
- [[PeterThiel]]'s seven questions test whether a startup has breakthrough technology, good timing, a small-market monopoly wedge, the right team, distribution, long-term defensibility, and a non-obvious secret.
- [[SequoiaCapital]]'s business-plan and pitch-deck checklist is recommended as a fundraising preparation aid.
- [[AndreessenHorowitz]]'s metrics lists are recommended for understanding how startups make money and measure progress.
- [[BillGurley]]'s digital-marketplace factors are recommended for founders building marketplace businesses.
- Checklists and simple rules are easy to write down but hard to execute.

## Key Quotes
> "writing them down is easy but executing on them is hard." - on the gap between checklist adoption and operating discipline.
