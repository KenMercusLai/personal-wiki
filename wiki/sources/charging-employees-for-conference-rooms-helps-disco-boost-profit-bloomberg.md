## Summary
Bloomberg Businessweek reports that [[Disco]] uses a company-wide internal market called Personal Will, where meeting rooms, desks, computers, team services, work assignments, overtime, and improvement ideas are priced in a virtual currency called Will. The system, designed by [[ToshioNaito]] and backed by CEO [[KazumaSekiya]], extends [[AmoebaManagement]] from team-level units to individual workers, combining [[InternalMarketManagement]], [[WorkplaceIncentiveDesign]], and [[Gamification]]. The article credits the approach with higher operating margins, lower overtime, and unusually high worker pay, while warning that pressure, short-termism, research distraction, and cultural fit limit its transferability.

The inspected lead and inline images show the same portrait of Naito in an office setting; they identify the program designer but add no independent factual claim beyond the prose. The tiny phone image and repeated related-story thumbnails are article chrome or duplicates and were not used as evidence.

## Key Claims
- [[Disco]] prices internal resources and services in Will so employees feel the opportunity cost of meetings, desks, PCs, umbrellas, support work, and colleagues' time.
- The Personal Will system makes employees bid for tasks, move between managers, bill teams for services, and receive quarterly yen payouts based on virtual-currency balances.
- Bloomberg reports improved operating margin, share-price appreciation, high employee pay, and government recognition after the system's 2011 implementation.
- The system reduces some waste, including unnecessary meetings and overtime, by turning inefficient behavior into explicit internal cost.
- Internal competitions in the Colosseum and internal crowdfunding use game-like scoring, wagers, and peer funding to surface and test profit-improvement ideas.
- The system has meaningful costs: some engineers quit, bonuses are uncertain, pressure is continuous, quarterly profit focus can encourage short-termism, and adoption outside Japan has been difficult.
