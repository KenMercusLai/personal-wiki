## Summary
[[NerdWallet]] describes a rewrite of its engineering job architecture after the original rubric, built for about 25 engineers, no longer worked cleanly for a roughly 100-person engineering organization. The article argues that career architecture should express values, expectations, rationale, and examples rather than invite engineers to treat promotion as a checklist. It also shows how the company handled subjective evaluation, product/platform fairness, manager beta testing, and rollout as a living-document refactor.

## Key Claims
- [[EngineeringCareerArchitecture]] works better as a craft framework than as a checklist of discrete promotion tasks.
- Leveling remains a necessary but blunt instrument for compensation, growth signaling, and external legibility in a nontrivially sized engineering organization.
- Role levels become clearer when the organization combines high-level role descriptions with observed examples of engineers already embodying each level.
- Fair evaluation requires career expectations to account for different impact paths, such as product engineers influencing product managers, designers, and researchers rather than only the engineering organization.
- Refactoring a job architecture should be validated by comparing old and new expectations, beta testing with managers, accepting contextual feedback, and explaining both the change and its rationale.
- The inspected images include a promotion-diff screenshot that reinforces the leveling theme and a decorative architecture photograph used as a captioned joke rather than independent evidence.

## Key Quotes
> "Code as craft is not a new concept" - on treating engineering growth as mastery rather than certification.

> "Leveling is a blunt instrument" - on the tradeoff between nuance and organizational consistency.
