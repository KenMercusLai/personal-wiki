## Summary
Ben Schippers argues that software builders are naturally drawn toward technical possibility, flexibility, and visible feature breadth, while usable products require a different discipline: frame the human problem, make consequential choices, and remove capabilities that do not serve it. The essay connects [[BuilderUserFluencyGap]] and [[CognitiveOverheadInProductDesign]] to engineering incentives, then frames [[UserCenteredDesign]] and [[UtilityOrientedUX]] as acts of constraint rather than merely visual polish. Its two remote image references resolve to the same decorative photograph of a green Uglydoll, which was inspected and omitted because it adds no product evidence.

## Key Claims
- Engineers can begin with a solution or technical capability before the human problem has been framed correctly.
- Greenfield exploration rewards discovering what technology can do, whereas product design must decide what it should do for a particular user purpose.
- Feature controls make a system's possibility space visible to builders and peers but can transfer flexibility, choice, and interpretation costs to users.
- Elegant products require subtraction: teams must discard technically interesting options that do not help the intended user.
- Making choices for users can reduce clutter rather than diminish them, provided those choices are grounded in real user needs.
- Technology is necessary infrastructure for a software product, not the benefit users ultimately care about.

## Key Quotes
> "You are not insulting people by making choices for them, you are cleaning junk out of their lives." - on constraint as a user benefit.

> "Technology is just the plumbing" - on separating implementation capability from product purpose.

## Connections
- [[BuilderUserFluencyGap]] - builders' intimate knowledge and enthusiasm make exposed flexibility feel more valuable and less burdensome than it may feel to users.
- [[CognitiveOverheadInProductDesign]] - knobs, buttons, and broad capability impose choices and interpretation work on users.
- [[UserCenteredDesign]] - correct problem framing begins with the life improvement sought by the intended user rather than the available technology.
- [[UtilityOrientedUX]] - the product's value lies in what it enables for people, not in the machinery itself.
- [[ProductDesignPrinciples]] - explicit principles can help teams rule out technically possible but purpose-incoherent features.
- [[SoftwareEngineering]] - technical exploration is one part of product creation, not a substitute for product judgment.

## Contradictions
- The article's claims about what engineers “always” prefer are broad practitioner generalizations without comparative research, observed teams, user studies, or outcome measures.
- Removing choice is beneficial only when the team understands user goals and preserves necessary agency, accessibility, safety, expert depth, and recovery; subtraction alone does not establish good design.
