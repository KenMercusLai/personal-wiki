## Summary
POLITICO describes how [[BernieSanders]]' 2016 presidential campaign benefited from a loose, largely unpaid network of volunteer coders who built websites, apps, maps, phone-bank tools, event systems, and clearinghouses around the official campaign. The article presents [[VolunteerCampaignTechnology]] as both an operational advantage and a governance problem: speed, enthusiasm, and open collaboration produced useful tools, but overlapping apps, weak vetting, and unclear accountability created risks for a presidential campaign.

## Key Claims
- [[JonHughes]] built [[VoteForBernie]] outside the campaign, and its primary/caucus lookup function drew more than 2 million unique visitors before the official campaign launched a related voter tool.
- The broader [[BernieSanders]] volunteer tech ecosystem included more than 1,000 coders producing roughly one new app per week across Reddit, Slack, GitHub, Hustle, and email.
- [[FeelTheBern]] showed how volunteer-built policy explainers could become canvassing material, not just online persuasion content.
- The campaign's staff, including [[KennethPennington]] and [[ZackExley]], used the volunteer network as a distributed labor pool while also hiring some contributors into official technical roles.
- The article contrasts the Sanders network with [[HillaryClinton]]'s more hierarchical campaign technology operation, arguing that the volunteer model moved faster but with less centralized control.
- [[CampaignTechnologyAccountability]] becomes the main qualification: tools such as housing, rides, and canvassing apps can create safety, targeting, or reputational risks when no one clearly owns vetting and consequences.

## Key Quotes
