## Summary
[[AndrewBosworth]] reflects on running disliked product reviews in 2012 and turning them around through "The P Rules," a set of meeting norms meant to make reviews useful without disrespecting presenting teams. The article argues that senior leaders should avoid piling on, pedantry, pontificating, prescription, permission capture, pessimism, and interruptions so teams keep ownership while still receiving useful guidance. It also frames [[ProductReviewMeetings]] as power-sensitive leadership settings where the leader's body language, curiosity, reference to people and ideas, conflict framing, emotional naming, and humor affect organizational trust.

## Key Claims
- Product reviews can become feared when leaders over-contribute and under-appreciate the presenting team.
- Review participants should avoid repetition, nitpicking, tangents, solution prescription, responsibility transfer, reflexive pessimism, and interruptions.
- Leaders should identify problems and acceptable constraints without taking solution ownership away from the team.
- Curiosity is more useful than shutting down weak ideas because bad ideas can expose either team misunderstandings or leader misunderstandings.
- Presenters need room to finish and answer questions; slide limits, numbering, and pre-reads can keep discussions informed without derailing them.
- Senior leaders carry a power gradient in reviews, so even mild interjections can feel much larger to presenters.
- Meeting leadership includes making people feel seen and heard through eye contact, open questions, explicit references to people and ideas, shared-goal reminders, naming concern, and light humor.

## Key Quotes
> "Once a point has been made, move on." - on avoiding piling on.

> "Let Presenters Present." - rule for preserving presenter agency.
