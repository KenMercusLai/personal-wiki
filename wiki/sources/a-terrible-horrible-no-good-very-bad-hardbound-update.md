## Summary
[[NathanBashaw]] reflects on [[Hardbound]], a mobile-first illustrated nonfiction-summary product that had strong qualitative love, notable usage, and some paying customers but could not close a new funding round before cash ran low. The post is both a startup update and a founder failure reflection: Hardbound had about 7,000 weekly readers, 1,200 paying customers, nearly 100,000 app downloads, about 250,000 web readers, and over 20 million tapped content pages, yet its roughly $2,500 monthly revenue could not support about $13,000 monthly spend. Bashaw frames the next step as a pause for learning and possible model change, while preserving the larger mission of using technology and storytelling to widen perspective, deepen empathy, and sharpen knowledge.

## Key Claims
- Strong product love and usage do not necessarily create fundable [[ProductMarketFit]] when growth is not fast enough and unit economics remain unresolved.
- [[StartupRunway]] becomes painfully concrete when a team is spending far more than it earns and fundraising does not close.
- Investor rejection centered on two perceived risks: moderate user growth and the time-and-money cost of producing high-quality Hardbound stories.
- [[FailureOwnership]] can separate responsibility from self-hatred by shifting from "why couldn't I?" to "why didn't I?" and treating failure as an object for learning.
- The source's inspected images reinforce its narrative frame: Madison Square Park grounds the initial resignation conversation, while desert-road car imagery visualizes acceleration, running out of gas, and continuing toward an unreachable horizon.

## Key Quotes
> "I talked to 72 investors, but I wasn't able to close the round." - context for the fundraising failure.

> "Instead of 'Why couldn't I do it?' I asked myself 'Why didn't I do it?'" - context for the failure-learning frame.
