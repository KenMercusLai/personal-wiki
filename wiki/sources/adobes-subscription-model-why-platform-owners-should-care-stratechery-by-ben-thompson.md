## Summary
[[BenThompson]] argues in [[Stratechery]] that [[Adobe]]'s move to subscription-only [[CreativeCloud]] is more than a product packaging change: it is a business-model shift that better fits high-value, high-learning-curve [[ProductivityAppSubscriptions]]. The article uses [[Photoshop]] to show how packaged software misprices different users, while subscriptions can lower entry friction, capture value over time, reduce churn incentives, and make productivity apps more viable on app-store platforms. Its two inspected diagrams contrast packaged software's flat price and large late-stage consumer surplus with a subscription staircase that lets producer revenue rise as consumer value rises.

## Key Claims
- [[Adobe]]'s Creative Cloud shift matters because new business models are rarer than new product versions.
- Packaged productivity software mismatches value: casual users face too much upfront cost, while professional users can capture enormous consumer surplus after one purchase.
- [[SubscriptionSurplusAlignment]] improves the fit between price paid and value received because users can start cheaply and producers earn recurring revenue as use deepens.
- Subscriptions give software makers stronger incentives to maintain the product and reduce churn because revenue depends on continued user satisfaction.
- [[ProductivityAppSubscriptions]] are especially important for apps with high learning curves, high upfront investment, ongoing maintenance, and users whose value grows over time.
- App-store economics can under-support serious productivity apps when platforms offer only low upfront prices, high upfront prices, or limited trials.
- Platform owners should support subscriptions for productivity apps because indispensable apps can increase [[PlatformStickiness]] and make users less likely to switch ecosystems.

## Key Quotes
> "very rarely do they come out with new business models" - on Adobe's move.

> "Platform owners should add subscription support" - core recommendation.
