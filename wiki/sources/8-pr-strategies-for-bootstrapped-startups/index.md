## Summary

This article treats public relations as strategic communication that shapes how customers, prospects, investors, government officials, media, and other publics view a company. For bootstrapped startups, the article argues that PR cannot simply be outsourced to a large agency; founders need a deliberate process for deciding which story to tell, whom to tell it to, and what business outcome the coverage should support.

The first step is goal selection. A startup should decide whether PR is meant to support a product launch, hiring, fundraising, geographic expansion, customer acquisition, customer retention, or some other concrete objective. That goal determines the audience, the story angle, and the publications or journalists worth pursuing.

The article then focuses on pitch quality. Before contacting media, a founder should be able to summarize the startup in one sentence: the problem, the target market or user group, the solution, and why the solution matters. Full pitches should be short, personalized, addressed to one journalist at a time, and tied to that journalist's publication and audience. Generic mass emails, excessive background detail, timezone-blind outreach, and repeated unsolicited follow-ups are treated as ways to damage the relationship.

Media targeting is framed as research work. Rather than chasing the largest technology publications by default, founders should identify the publications, blogs, journalists, and editors their actual customers read. The article recommends building a contact list in a spreadsheet or CRM and using PR tools only when they help match a startup's niche to relevant media contacts.

Finally, the article argues that startup PR should continue after launch. New funding, product features, services, market changes, customer data, and guest posts can all become credible reasons to seek coverage. Reverse pitching services such as journalist-source request platforms give founders another path: respond to journalists who are already looking for expert sources instead of only cold-pitching stories.

## Key Points

- PR goals should be explicit enough to determine the audience, angle, and target publication.
- A startup's story needs a concise one-sentence pitch before longer outreach begins.
- Good journalist outreach is personalized, brief, audience-aware, and respectful of exclusivity and follow-up boundaries.
- Strategic targeting matters more than chasing famous publications whose readers may not match the market.
- PR can support hiring, fundraising, visibility, customer acquisition, and retention, but those outcomes should not be confused with press for its own sake.
- Launch announcements are only one PR occasion; data, feature changes, industry shifts, guest posts, and reverse pitching can sustain coverage over time.
