## Summary
[[MattHaughey]] argues that small, time-bounded side projects can broaden skills, improve happiness, and sometimes redirect a career or become a business. [[DawnSharifan]] adds a workplace distinction: outside interests may support curiosity, teamwork, and recovery, but asking about them in interviews can distract from job ability and introduce affinity bias, so [[Slack]] encourages sharing them after hiring rather than using them as a selection signal.

## Key Claims
- [[SideProjectIncubation]] can produce learning, creative range, career change, or a new venture without needing commercial success as its only valid outcome.
- A practical start is to replace one low-cost use of time, choose one desired skill or creation, work on it for a few weeks, and then review the result.
- Side projects and hobbies may contribute to [[BurnoutPrevention]] by creating recovery, enjoyment, and identities outside paid work.
- Team or sporting hobbies may build experience with cooperation, mutual support, and collective success, although the article provides no outcome evidence.
- [[InclusiveHiring]] should keep evaluation focused on job ability because impressive outside projects can distract interviewers and shared interests can create affinity bias.
- [[Slack]] is described as encouraging employees to share outside interests in new-hire introductions and social channels after hiring.

## Key Quotes
> "Side projects help people avoid burnout" - Sharifan on the potential wellbeing benefit of outside interests.

> "focus on ability to do the job" - Sharifan on keeping hobbies out of candidate evaluation.

## Connections

## Contradictions
- The article qualifies career advice that treats side projects as necessary hiring evidence: it values them after hiring while warning that interview questions about them can introduce irrelevant preference and similarity bias.
- The historical success stories and the author's own career are selected examples, not evidence that side projects usually become careers or businesses; the article also does not address unequal access to discretionary time, money, health, or domestic support.
- Both referenced images were opened and omitted as decorative: a Slack-branded text banner and a stylized office illustration added no evidence beyond the prose.
