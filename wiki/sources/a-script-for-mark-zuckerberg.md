## Summary
[[BenThompson]] writes a fictional earnings-call speech for [[MarkZuckerberg]] that recasts [[MetaPlatforms]] as an entertainment-and-advertising company rather than an aspiring general-purpose platform. The proposed strategy treats AI investment as both defensive and revenue-expanding: machine learning improves recommendation and ad matching, generative systems expand creative supply, and AI-mediated experiences could make more inventory monetizable. To discipline a large compute buildout, Thompson proposes renting spare capacity on a reclaimable spot basis and requiring Meta's internal uses to beat the outside rental value represented by a [[ComputeHurdleRate]].

## Key Claims
- Facebook's feed, mobile transition, Instagram evolution, and short-form video growth are presented as evidence that revealed user preference and technological change should outweigh fidelity to an earlier product form.
- Thompson's fictional Zuckerberg identifies platform ownership as a repeated strategic distraction: it allegedly delayed native mobile investment, encouraged excessive virtual-reality spending, and obscured the core advertising business.
- The essay argues that entertainment is unusually compatible with advertising because voluntary attention creates both inventory and demand for commercial discovery.
- AI is framed as an existential hedge for a digital company and as a direct improvement to recommendation, targeting, creative generation, and prediction of desired content and ads.
- The proposed growth mechanism is inventory expansion: AI-generated or AI-selected experiences could make more of Meta's surfaces monetizable without charging users directly.
- Meta's human-connection, entertainment, shopping, and consumer focus is presented as a strategic contrast with enterprise productivity, subscriptions, and human-replacement use cases.
- Renting spare compute with a contractual ability to reclaim it would help finance capacity while forcing internal projects to clear an observable market hurdle rate.

## Key Quotes
> "We will build, because we must, but we will let the market decide who gets to use it" - the fictional speech's compute-allocation rule.

> "Our goal is to celebrate humans, to connect them, to entertain them, and to enable commerce among them." - Thompson's proposed statement of Meta's differentiated purpose.

## Contradictions
- This is Thompson's fictional recommendation, not an actual Zuckerberg transcript, Meta commitment, earnings disclosure, or independent account of executive motives. Its admissions and promised compute-rental policy must not be attributed to Zuckerberg or Meta as facts.
- The essay's affirmative case for targeted advertising does not resolve privacy, consent, measurement, market-power, content-quality, or well-being concerns documented elsewhere in the wiki.
- The claim that AI can make every pixel monetizable is a strategic forecast. The source supplies no utilization data, unit economics, user-response evidence, rental contract, or proof that internal ad returns will exceed external compute prices.
- Reclaimable short-term rentals can improve utilization and price discipline, but the source does not address workload interruption, customer concentration, capacity fragmentation, networking and power constraints, or whether outside demand persists when Meta needs the capacity back.
- The article says Reality Labs absorbed "twelve figures," but it does not define the time window or calculation, and the fictional voice makes this an authorial characterization rather than a company-reported total.
- The source contains no effective image references. The closing YouTube embed is retained in the raw article as a link and was not treated as a local visual asset.
