## Summary
[[DavidSkok]] interviews [[BillMacaitis]] on using [[MarketingAttribution]] to turn startup marketing from intuition-led spending into a measurable growth engine. Drawing on [[Salesforce]], [[Zendesk]], and [[Slack]], the article argues that once a company has [[ProductMarketFit]] and begins testing multiple channels, it should track touchpoints across the full buyer journey rather than relying on first-click or last-click credit. The article also shows how attribution can extend beyond acquisition into content, brand, offline channels, team-level SaaS metrics, lifecycle marketing, and customer satisfaction.

## Key Claims
- [[MarketingAttribution]] asks whether marketing money is being spent wisely by assigning credit across the touchpoints that contribute to customer outcomes.
- First-click, last-click, and simple linear models can misvalue long B2B buyer journeys because prospects may need many touches before becoming leads or customers.
- Algorithmic attribution is presented as the best-practice direction because it uses larger touchpoint datasets and downstream outcomes to estimate channel contribution.
- Startups should not scale marketing before [[ProductMarketFit]], but should set up attribution early once they begin using several channels.
- The first marketing hire should often be a data-oriented [[MarketingOperations]] person who can partner with engineering and the data warehouse.
- Useful attribution connects campaigns to [[DeepFunnelMetrics]] such as leads, opportunities, signed deals, expansion ARR, ACV, retention, and satisfaction rather than stopping at trial signup.
- [[Slack]] shows that attribution can include offline, brand, word-of-mouth, survey, team-level, and [[NetPromoterScore]] signals, not only clickable acquisition channels.
