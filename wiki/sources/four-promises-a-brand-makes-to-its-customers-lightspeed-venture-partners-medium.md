## Summary
[[AdamTaussig]] defines a brand as four promises about product, price, channel, and marketing, then defines [[BrandEquity]] as the accumulated customer belief created by fulfilling those promises consistently. The essay treats recommendation and [[NetPromoterScore]] as evidence of a word-of-mouth loop, but its market-leadership claim is an unsupported association rather than proof that high NPS or brand equity causes growth. Its practical conclusion for [[StartupBrandStrategy]] is that company behavior creates the brand, while visual branding can only reinforce what the operating system already delivers.

## Key Claims
- A brand promises a safe and effective product or service, a fair price, a convenient channel, and salient marketing that explains effective use.
- Brand equity accumulates when a company delivers those four promises consistently and can be impaired when it fails on any dimension.
- Customer belief can spread through recommendations, creating a word-of-mouth loop around fulfilled brand promises.
- The source proposes [[NetPromoterScore]] as a proxy for customers' willingness to recommend and therefore for brand equity.
- Entrepreneurs should treat brand as an effect of product, pricing, distribution, and communication choices rather than as visual identity alone.
- Visual branding may strengthen an already credible brand but cannot independently substitute for consistent customer experience.

## Key Quotes
> "Brand is an effect, not a cause." - the essay's distinction between earned customer belief and surface branding.

> "a brand is a set of four promises you make to your customer" - the product, price, channel, and marketing framework.

## Contradictions
- The essay complements the wiki's behavioral model of [[StartupBrandStrategy]], but its assertion that high-NPS companies tend to remain market-share leaders supplies no cited dataset, timeframe, category controls, or causal design.
- [[NetPromoterScore]] can measure stated recommendation likelihood, not brand equity in full; sampling, survey design, customer tenure, and the gap between stated intent and actual referral limit the proposed proxy.
