## Summary
[[AlexTurnbull]] presents seven founder lessons from building [[Groove]]: a business must create value and profit, relationships compound into advice and opportunities, products tend toward commoditization, customer conversations are non-optional, founders should buy back time, success should be self-defined, and early teams must learn to sell a vision. The article strengthens the wiki's startup material by shifting from validation mechanics toward founder operating judgment: avoid tactic guilt, build value-giving networks, defend brand and culture, protect founder time, and work backward from a personally chosen definition of success.

## Key Claims
- A business only truly needs to deliver something valuable and make a profit; other "must-do" tactics are negotiable and can become fatal distractions.
- [[FounderNetworkBuilding]] matters because relationships with smart people can produce guidance, partnerships, introductions, and strategic confidence when the startup hits painful periods.
- Product features are increasingly easy to copy, so [[ProductCommoditization]] pushes founders to build defensible brand and culture rather than relying only on the product artifact.
- [[CustomerLedProductDevelopment]] requires frequent customer conversations; the suggested operating goal is five 15-minute conversations each week.
- [[FounderTimeLeverage]] means spending money on automation, scheduling tools, assistants, or outsourcing when those purchases return scarce founder time to higher-value work.
- [[FounderSuccessDefinition]] should be personal and concrete enough that the business can be built backward from the desired life, not from inherited hustle mythology.
- [[StartupVisionSelling]] is a learnable leadership skill for recruiting cofounders, investors, team members, beta customers, partners, and suppliers before the company has much proof.

## Key Quotes
> "Make something valuable. And sell it at a profit." - the article's compressed test for whether advice names a true business necessity.

> "They join you because of who you'll be tomorrow." - on why early-stage founders must sell vision.
