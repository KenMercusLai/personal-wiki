## Summary
[[DerekSivers]] argues that titles such as entrepreneur, athlete, leader, risk-taker, success, or good friend should describe present conduct rather than past achievement. The essay frames [[ActionGroundedIdentity]] as a discipline of using the past tense for completed accomplishments and reserving present-tense identity claims for work still being done. Its motivational claim is that unearned labels can supply premature satisfaction and weaken the action needed to make them true.

## Key Claims
- A role title should be continually supported by current behavior rather than retained indefinitely after the relevant activity ends.
- Holding an outdated title can create satisfaction without action and make future success feel guaranteed by identity alone.
- Retiring an inaccurate title is an act of self-honesty that clarifies what a person is actually doing now.
- Discomfort at losing a valued identity can be converted into a concrete choice: resume the work required to earn it.
- Describing completed achievements in the past tense separates legitimate accomplishment from unsupported present status.

## Key Quotes
> "You have to keep earning it." — the essay's rule for keeping a role title in the present tense.

> "But success comes from doing, not declaring." — the distinction between action and identity assertion.
