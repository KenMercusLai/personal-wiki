## Summary
[[RescueTime]] uses anonymized activity data from nearly 50,000 users and cited interruption research to argue that keeping email and instant messaging available during other productive work creates pervasive [[CommunicationMultitasking]]. It reports that communication tools overlap with 40.1% of productive time and leave the average knowledge worker only 1 hour 12 minutes per day without email or IM multitasking. The article connects those observations to task-switching delays, stress, self-interruption, and the value of batching communication, but it does not expose enough methodology to establish causal effects or reconcile its “average” language with a reader's claim that the graphs showed medians.

## Key Claims
- [[CommunicationMultitasking]] is task switching rather than true simultaneous work, even when email or IM is merely kept open beside a primary task.
- The source reports that communication tools overlap with 40.1% of productive time and that knowledge workers receive only 1 hour 12 minutes of communication-free productive time per day.
- Email checks can create both immediate resumption costs and longer detours when messages trigger additional work; the article cites estimates ranging from 64 seconds to more than 9 minutes depending on the interruption.
- [[AttentionManagement]] is undermined by both external alerts and self-initiated checking, with cited research reporting 23 minutes 15 seconds before return to an interrupted task after intervening activities.
- Task-switching cost rises with task complexity, so communication interruptions can be especially costly during cognitively demanding work.
- Batching communication and suppressing alerts can protect longer focus blocks, although the article recognizes that email and IM remain necessary in many jobs.

## Key Quotes
> "the average knowledge worker spends 40.1% of their productive time a day multitasking with communication tools" - on the reported overlap between productive work and email or IM use.

> "the average knowledge worker only has 1 hour and 12 minutes a day for completely focused work" - on communication-free productive time in the dataset.

> "Constantly multitasking with communication tools tricks us into thinking they’re the most important part of our day." - on urgency displacing deliberate priority.

## Connections
- [[RescueTime]] - company whose anonymized user data supplies the article's central measurements.
- [[CommunicationMultitasking]] - the pattern of switching between productive work and email or instant messaging.
- [[AttentionManagement]] - communication checks fragment focus and consume task-resumption capacity.
- [[NotificationDesign]] - alerts determine when external communication can interrupt ongoing work.
- [[PersonalProductivity]] - batching communication is proposed as a way to preserve longer productive blocks.
- [[WorkplaceCollaboration]] - response-time expectations and genuine urgency constrain how fully workers can isolate themselves from communication.

## Contradictions
- A reader comment says the article's graphs displayed medians while the prose repeatedly described an “average” worker; the captured lazy-load placeholders contain no chart data with which to verify or resolve that discrepancy.
- The source reports observational RescueTime aggregates and cites separate interruption studies, but provides no sampling frame, operational definition of productive time, measurement protocol, distribution, uncertainty, or causal comparison. The numerical results should therefore remain source-scoped rather than treated as universal knowledge-worker benchmarks.
- Necessary responsiveness, emergency work, support, operations, care, and tightly coupled collaboration may make some interruption beneficial or unavoidable, so communication-free time is not by itself a complete measure of productivity.
