## Summary
[[FirstRoundReview]] interviews [[DanPupius]] about hiring systems he helped develop at [[Medium]] and [[Range]]. Pupius treats the process and the resulting team as two products: define the intended team, translate it into observable criteria, give candidates fair ways to demonstrate those criteria, assign decision authority, measure candidate and employee outcomes, and revise the documented system in focused sprints. The sole embedded image is a decorative office photograph with an indistinct planning board and adds no evidence beyond the article, so it was not retained.

## Key Claims
- A hiring system should be managed like a product with a vision, principles, testable tactics, meaningful measures, feedback loops, explicit decision rights, candidate focus, documentation, and eventual repeatability.
- Team vision should specify the complementary traits, values, and skills the whole organization needs rather than pursue isolated impressive individuals or a generic "world-class" ideal.
- Interview judgments should be tied to observable behavior against a predeclared rubric; pedigree and other weak predictors should be explicitly excluded.
- Candidates should receive the rubric in advance and be offered multiple relevant ways to demonstrate ability, while new interviewers should be calibrated through shadowing and reverse shadowing.
- A hiring manager should make the final evidence-based decision, with the rest of the panel expected to disagree and commit rather than require consensus.
- Funnel sources, speed, drop-off, close rate, demographic patterns, candidate feedback, offer acceptance, employee sentiment, and retention can support different feedback horizons.
- Process changes should be batched into themed retrospectives or sprints so teams can learn from attributable changes before automating the system.

## Key Quotes
> "If you can't back up a judgment with something the candidate did or said, then it shouldn't count." - on evidence-based evaluation.

> "The 'end user' for the hiring process you're building is the candidate." - on candidate-centered measurement.

## Contradictions
- The source's hiring-manager decision authority differs from [[bar-raisers-at-coinbase-if-youre-not-a-hell-yes-youre-a-no]], where an independent trained interviewer can veto the hiring manager; both avoid panel consensus but place the final control differently.
- The article offers practitioner experience rather than comparative outcome data. It does not establish that its rubric reduced bias or caused retention, and its suggested gender ratio is binary and does not address broader identities, selection-rate auditing, accessibility, compensation, advancement, or legal and ethical limits on informal backchannels.
