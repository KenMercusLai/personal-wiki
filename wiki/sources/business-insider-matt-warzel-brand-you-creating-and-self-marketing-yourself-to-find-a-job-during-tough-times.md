## Summary
[[MattWarzel]] treats a career brand as a consistent, employer-facing account of a candidate's expertise, distinctive value, and business contribution. He combines self-assessment, a personal mission statement, career journaling, market research, the marketing mix, SWOT analysis, and a practiced elevator speech into a [[CareerPlanning]] and [[PersonalBranding]] process for targeting roles and communicating transferable value during a difficult labor market.

## Key Claims
- Self-marketing should begin by deciding what the candidate offers, which employers need it, and why those employers should care.
- A personal mission statement can align goals, values, past successes, and intended contribution with employers that hold compatible priorities.
- Assessments and career journaling can surface recurring strengths, preferences, transferable skills, setbacks, and career alternatives, but candidates should reject results that do not fit their lived experience.
- A useful career brand is consistent across resumes, cover letters, interviews, portfolios, business cards, and online profiles while remaining adaptable to different target industries.
- Differentiation requires translating skills and accomplishments into employer benefit, especially evidence of revenue gained, costs saved, or problems solved, rather than merely listing capabilities.
- Career planning can borrow market research, the four Ps, and SWOT analysis to connect a candidate's offer, promotion channels, access points, compensation requirements, internal capabilities, and external labor-market conditions.
- A rehearsed elevator speech should concisely connect background, qualifications, desired work, company interest, distinctive value, and a specific request for next action.

## Key Quotes
> "A career brand is an image that portrays you as an expert in your field, attracts your ideal employer, and reveals how you can help their business." - on defining an employer-facing career brand.

> "Marketing these skills, not just job history and accomplishments, puts you in higher demand" - on emphasizing transferable value.
