## Summary
[[DariusForoux]] argues that business and career success often come less from being marginally better than from being meaningfully different and therefore first in a recognizable category. Drawing on [[AlRies]], [[JackTrout]], [[BlueOceanStrategy]], and [[ScottAdams]], the essay connects market differentiation to personal [[SkillStacking]]: combining ordinary competence in writing, speaking, selling, or domain expertise can create a distinctive position that direct comparison misses.

## Key Claims
- In business and careers, direct competition to be "better" is weaker than becoming different enough to define or occupy a new category.
- [[BlueOceanStrategy]] and [[CategoryCreation]] both challenge red-ocean competition by creating less contested demand or comparison frames.
- Being first in a category can matter more than inventing the underlying idea; public association is the strategic prize.
- [[SkillStacking]] makes differentiation practical because people can combine merely good skills into a rare profile rather than trying to become world-class at one skill.
- Writing, public speaking, and selling are broadly useful skills that can make a specialist's work more distinctive and valuable.
- The source's inspected local image is a decorative [[DariusForoux]]-branded character illustration and adds no independent evidence beyond author branding.

## Key Quotes
> "Everyone is interested in what's new. Few people are interested in what's better." - Foroux quoting Ries and Trout on novelty over marginal improvement.

> "You see, it's not about being better, it's about being different." - on difference as the route to a new category.

> "Ask, 'How am I different?'" - on replacing direct superiority questions with positioning questions.
