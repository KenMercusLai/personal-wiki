## Summary
[[StephAngo]] describes a deliberately low-friction [[Obsidian]] practice built around controllable Markdown files, few folders, reusable properties and templates, and dense internal links. The workflow lets structure emerge from categories, backlinks, unresolved links, periodic compilation, and random revisits rather than requiring every note to be classified in advance. Ango also treats manual review as part of understanding, while using a separate Jekyll publishing vault as an explicit exception to his one-vault preference.

## Key Claims
- [[FileOverApp]] favors user-controlled files in readable, retrievable formats so digital artifacts can outlast any one application.
- A low-friction [[PersonalKnowledgeManagement]] system can keep most authored notes in one vault root, reserve a few folders for references, clippings, attachments, daily link targets, and templates, and use categories for overlapping views.
- Dense internal links, including unresolved links, preserve possible future connections and make the history of an idea traceable as the archive grows.
- [[FractalJournaling]] compiles timestamped thought fragments into progressively broader periodic reviews, preserving both detailed provenance and higher-level themes.
- Random note revisits and shallow local graphs can resurface old ideas, reveal missing links, and support maintenance, but Ango keeps this process manual because doing it helps him understand his own patterns.
- Reusable, composable templates and properties reduce repeated decisions while allowing categories to share fields such as dates, people, themes, locations, and ratings.
- Consistent conventions—plural categories and tags, ISO-style dates, a seven-point rating scale, and a written personal style guide—trade some flexibility for lower ongoing decision cost.
- Obsidian can support a file-based publishing pipeline through Git, Jekyll, and Netlify, although Ango separates the site into its own vault and notes that simpler hosted publishing options exist.

## Key Quotes
> "It embraces chaos and laziness to create emergent structure." — on the intended character of the workflow

> "Unresolved links are important because they are breadcrumbs for future connections between things." — on linking before a destination note exists

> "Don't delegate understanding" — on retaining manual review and maintenance

## Contradictions
- The source complements output-oriented PKM accounts by showing notes feeding essays and publication, but Ango values manual revisiting even when it is not the fastest route to visible output.
- The preference for one vault and very few folders is explicitly personal and is partly broken for website publishing; it should not override workflows needing collaboration, access boundaries, compliance, or separate failure domains.
- The article is a practitioner description, not comparative evidence that this organization model, seven-point scale, manual review, or chosen plugins improve recall, insight, or writing quality for other users.
