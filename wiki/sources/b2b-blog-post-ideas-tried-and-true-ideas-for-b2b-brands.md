## Summary
This practitioner article presents eight reusable [[B2BContentFormats]]: search-optimized explainers, educational case studies, original-data analysis, new-concept thought leadership, exposés, list posts, instructional guides, and company updates. It argues that useful, searchable, evidence-rich content can support [[ContentLedAcquisition]] by earning discovery, links, shares, authority, and goodwill, while company news is better suited to nurturing an existing audience than creating durable external value. The source is a historical collection of selected success examples rather than a comparative test of the formats.

## Key Claims
- Search-optimized explainers can put a B2B brand in front of people already asking relevant questions, as illustrated by Trello's guide to scrum and agile project management.
- Case studies are more persuasive when they teach a transferable method with examples, recommendations, or templates instead of merely announcing a client result.
- Original research can earn backlinks, press citations, conference references, bookmarks, and internal circulation when the analysis helps practitioners make decisions.
- Introducing a new concept can establish thought leadership, but it requires experimentation, research, and a willingness to publish ideas before they feel fully settled.
- Exposé-style posts attract discussion by challenging accepted industry beliefs, while lists make information easy to scan and instructional posts answer concrete problem-solving queries.
- Company updates help nurture existing relationships and signal progress, but usually have less long-term search, sharing, or citation value than audience-centered formats.
- A B2B editorial plan should combine formats according to audience value and purpose rather than publishing only company news.
