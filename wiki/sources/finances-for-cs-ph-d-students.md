## Summary
[[DavidAndersen]] gives U.S. computer-science Ph.D. students a 2016 personal-finance plan aimed at building flexibility rather than maximizing consumption: live below stipend income, save internship earnings, use tax-advantaged post-tax accounts when eligible, and invest simply. The article links [[GraduateStudentFinancialPlanning]] to career optionality, a graduation cash cushion, debt triage, health-plan risk, housing transaction costs, and low-cost [[IndexFundStrategy]]. Its numerical tax, contribution-limit, fund, and eligibility details are historical and require current, individualized verification.

## Key Claims
- Early saving can create enough financial slack for graduates to choose lower-paid public-interest work, change fields, retire early, or absorb unexpected transitions.
- Stipends, summer internships, and carefully bounded side work create different saving opportunities, but side work should not delay the degree or create conflicts of interest.
- For the article's 2016 U.S. audience, low graduate-school tax rates made eligible Roth contributions attractive, while taxable accounts preserved liquidity for moving and early-career expenses.
- Housing and high-deductible health plans require scenario analysis because closing costs, short holding periods, deductibles, and cash reserves can outweigh apparent savings.
- Broad low-cost index or target-date funds are presented as the default; day trading, market timing, concentrated stock picking, brokers' hot tips, and high-fee active funds are rejected.
- High-interest debt should generally be addressed promptly, while subsidized debt and other choices depend on interest, liquidity, risk tolerance, and current rules.

## Key Quotes
> "Spend less than you earn on your stipend." - the plan's starting rule.

> "Don't day-trade" - the article's investing guardrail.

## Contradictions
- The article's broad U.S.-index recommendation is narrower than [[IndexFundStrategy]]'s later synthesis, which adds market-context, valuation, concentration, and implementation-channel qualifications.
- Its 2016 tax brackets, exemptions, contribution and income limits, Social Security treatment, HSA and Roth rules, fund tickers, fees, and minimums are not current guidance and may not apply to fellowship funding, international students, or a particular university.
