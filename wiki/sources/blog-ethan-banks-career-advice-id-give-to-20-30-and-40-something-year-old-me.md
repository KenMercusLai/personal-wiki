## Summary
[[EthanBanks]] writes age-staged career advice from the perspective of what he would tell his younger self in his 20s, 30s, and 40s. The article argues that career growth is not just technical skill or effort: it also depends on risk timing, humility, goal clarity, emotional intelligence, networks, trustworthy employers, boundaries, habits, restraint, and mental health. No image references were present in the source Markdown.

## Key Claims
- Early career is a good time to take asymmetric risks, including starting a business, because obligations and downside may be lower.
- Technical competence does not automatically entitle someone to promotion, compensation, or authority; responsibility, hierarchy, interpersonal judgment, and compensation incentives must be understood separately.
- Dishonest business owners are a warning sign for employees because dishonesty toward partners or customers can extend to staff.
- Mid-career workers need explicit goals, realistic self-assessment, emotional intelligence, relationships, risk tolerance, and recovery to avoid endless effort without arrival.
- Later-career judgment includes saying no, building daily habits, holding a long view, choosing restraint over consumption, and refusing to spend remaining time on other people's priorities.

## Key Quotes
> "Being good at your job doesn't mean you deserve a promotion." - on separating skill from advancement.

> "Define your goals so you know when you've reached them." - on avoiding endless effort.

> "Relaxation is not laziness but a necessity." - on recovery.
