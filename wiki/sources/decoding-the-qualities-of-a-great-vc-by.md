## Summary
[[SteveSchlafman]] proposes a trait model for strong venture investors, then asks whether firms or new programs could identify and develop those qualities systematically. He argues that selection cannot be separated from apprenticeship, mentorship, networks, and experience across company and market cycles, while hoping that new models widen access beyond venture capital's existing capital, geography, experience, and relationship barriers. The essay is an exploratory practitioner framework, not a validated predictor of investment performance.

## Key Claims
- Great venture investors combine curiosity, domain expertise, passion, focus, authentic relationship building, persuasion, emotional intelligence, and conviction; Schlafman also records community suggestions including rapid learning, first-principles thinking, calm, humility, and vision without endorsing every addition.
- Journalistic practices—finding knowledgeable people, asking strong questions, checking answers, and communicating succinctly—offer one useful model for investor research and communication.
- [[VentureInvestorDevelopment]] is partly an apprenticeship problem because repeated exposure to portfolio-company problems, mentorship, failures, and changing market cycles shapes investment taste and judgment over years.
- A scalable investor-development system might combine software, education, institutional training, brand, and network mechanisms, but soft skills, relationship quality, and experiential judgment are difficult to score reliably.
- Programs modeled loosely on [[YCombinator]], [[500Startups]], fellowships, syndicates, or scout networks could broaden the investor pipeline, yet their value would depend on whether they expand access rather than reproduce incumbent patterns.

## Key Quotes
> "Given experiences play such an important role in shaping an investor, I’m honestly not sure if it’s even possible to develop a system that delivers alpha by finding and training future investors unless it’s a longer term endeavor." — on the limit of short-form selection and training

> "I also hope these efforts will expand the pool of candidates (think: gender, race, geography)." — on the intended access benefit of investor-development innovation

## Contradictions
- The essay treats a broad trait list as a promising selection input while also arguing that much investor judgment is produced through years of apprenticeship and market-cycle experience; it does not show how a scoring system could distinguish latent ability from accumulated access.
- Schlafman hopes to diversify venture capital, but evaluating candidates through established networks, conventional experience, or validation by incumbent “giants” could reproduce the very capital, geography, and relationship barriers he identifies.
- The fourteen attributes are a mixture of the author's initial list and social-media suggestions that he explicitly does not fully endorse. No outcome data, comparison group, operational definitions, or prospective test establishes that the traits predict investment returns or founder outcomes.
