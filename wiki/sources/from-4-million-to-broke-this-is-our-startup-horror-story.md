## Summary
An unnamed [[Reframed]] cofounder recounts how the company treated one nearby operator as a client, advertising supplier, and prospective investor, then discovered alleged unpaid invoices, adverse court judgments, unverifiable funds, invented advertising deals, and false credentials after building a £350,000 round and financial forecast around his representations. When the proposed investment disappeared and the founders removed doubtful receivables from their cash calculation, the reported runway contracted to the end of the month, the team lost its jobs, and the founders covered remaining debts personally. The retrospective connects [[StartupCounterpartyDiligence]], [[StartupRunway]], and [[StartupFailurePatterns]] while acknowledging that Reframed itself had not yet built a commercially viable business.

## Key Claims
- A financing offer, negotiated valuation, apparent legal activity, or signed paperwork should not be treated as available cash before identity, authority, funds, and closing conditions are independently verified.
- Letting one counterparty act as client, supplier, revenue-model expert, and lead investor concentrated commercial, technical, and financing risk in the same unverified relationship.
- Reframed's founders report that they began meaningful diligence only after a third party warned that the prospective investor had not paid about £25,000 in invoices; a search then found court judgments and numerous dissolved or indebted companies associated with the names he used.
- The founders say the supplied advertisements were ordinary affiliate links rather than direct advertiser relationships, invalidating both claimed deals and the forecast used to support the raise.
- Rebuilding the advertising system became necessary because the outside supplier retained the ability to inject advertisements during a major conference deployment.
- Apparent runway was overstated by unpaid client invoices; removing those receivables reportedly left the company only until month-end and forced it to tell employees to find new jobs.
- The author assigns responsibility on both sides: the counterparty's alleged deception accelerated the collapse, but Reframed's lack of an independently viable commercial model made the company vulnerable to it.

## Key Quotes
> "We need to do due diligence earlier." - the founders' central procedural lesson.

> "The financial model was broken." - after the founders determined that the advertising relationships and forecasts were not reliable.

> "It's not Simon's fault that we didn't have a commercial business." - the author's boundary on causal responsibility.

## Connections
- [[Reframed]] - startup whose proposed round, advertising model, team, and solvency are the subject of the account.
- [[StartupCounterpartyDiligence]] - independent verification of identity, funds, authority, payment history, claims, and system access before operational dependence.
- [[StartupRunway]] - conditional investment and doubtful receivables created a materially misleading picture of available operating time.
- [[StartupFailurePatterns]] - weak commercial viability, counterparty concentration, inadequate diligence, technical control risk, and cash exhaustion compounded rather than acting alone.
- [[FundraisingMomentum]] - a large apparent commitment created social proof and accelerated planning, but momentum did not establish that funds existed or would close.
- [[StartupFinancingMechanics]] - the account distinguishes a proposed valuation and investment commitment from cash actually received.

## Contradictions
- The source qualifies optimistic fundraising narratives: £350,000 offered at a £4 million valuation did not create liquidity, and the apparent lead commitment weakened rather than strengthened the company once operating plans and incumbent participation depended on it.
- The story is a 2016 first-person retrospective containing serious allegations about named people. The supplied document provides no court records, contracts, bank evidence, invoices, advertiser statements, employee accounts, or response from the accused parties, so those allegations remain attributed claims rather than independently established facts.
