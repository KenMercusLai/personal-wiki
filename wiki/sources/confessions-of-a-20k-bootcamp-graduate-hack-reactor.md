## Summary
Writing under the name Nori Maki Arare, a [[HackReactor]] graduate argues that the program's US$19,780 price was not justified by its compressed instruction, recent-graduate support, JavaScript-only curriculum, and employment results. The account describes roughly six weeks of lectures and paired two-day sprints followed by six weeks dominated by team projects and job-search preparation. It is useful as a detailed historical student perspective on [[CodingBootcampEducation]], but it is one pseudonymous 2016 retrospective rather than an independently verified or current program audit.

## Key Claims
- The author describes cohorts of about 45 students and estimates roughly 180 students in the program at a time, using those figures to question the relationship between tuition revenue and instructional resources.
- During the first six weeks, students reportedly received roughly hour-long lectures, completed topics through paired two-day sprints, watched solution videos, and queued for help from recent graduates employed as temporary instructional staff.
- The second six weeks reportedly shifted toward assigned-team thesis work, legacy and greenfield projects, resume preparation, hiring lectures, and a career-search sprint rather than continued classroom technical instruction.
- The published curriculum covered JavaScript-centered browser, server, database, authentication, deployment, React, Angular, algorithm, and data-structure work, but the author believed learning only one language reduced graduates' job-market range.
- The article challenges Hack Reactor's change from a claimed 99% placement within three months to 98% within six months and argues that admitting already-qualified students makes those figures hard to interpret.
- The author reports finding work after a few months while knowing other graduates who were still searching, and recommends self-study or a cheaper program instead of paying the reported US$19,780 tuition.
- Alumni reunions and continuing resume help are portrayed as possible social incentives against public criticism, although the source provides no comparative review or alumni-survey evidence.

## Key Quotes
> "The second 6 weeks you're basically learning on your own." - the author's summary of the instructional shift.

> "You should know what you're getting into." - the article's advice to prospective students.

## Contradictions
- The account challenges Hack Reactor's marketing and outcome interpretation, but it provides no audited denominator, exclusion rules, salary data, job-quality definition, or direct copy of the cited outcome report.
- The author describes the program as both capable of admitting already-qualified people and inadequate value for graduates; that supports a selection-bias concern but does not establish what outcomes those students would have achieved without the program.
- The article is a single pseudonymous graduate's perspective published in 2016. Its cohort sizes, revenue estimate, hardware, tuition, curriculum, staffing, alumni practices, and placement claims are historical and unverified here, not claims about the current program.
- Both distinct local image files were opened. They show the same generic classroom photograph, with one stored as a thumbnail; neither adds source-specific evidence, so both were omitted and no asset manifest was created.
