## Summary
王翼之 presents [[SerenityInvestor|Serenity]] as an anonymous small-cap technology investor using [[ChokepointInvesting]] to identify indispensable, underfollowed components in AI supply chains before they become market consensus. The essay then interprets the workflow through [[BayesianUpdating]]: structural research forms a prior, a position is opened under uncertainty, continuing diligence updates conviction, and capital rotates toward the currently strongest thesis. The analogy is useful as a disciplined evidence loop, but the article does not provide the probabilities, valuation inputs, portfolio accounting, risk controls, or corroborating records needed to validate either the Bayesian characterization or the reported returns.

## Key Claims
- The article claims that Serenity earned a 4,502% return in 2026 and had previously recommended indium-phosphide substrate company AXTI before a rise from US$12 to US$70; neither claim is documented in the supplied source.
- [[ChokepointInvesting]] begins by mapping an industry's physical dependencies through papers and bills of materials, then looks for hard-to-substitute materials, equipment, or technology.
- The method favors underfollowed “hidden champions” over conspicuous platform companies and seeks to act before institutional research and market consensus catch up.
- Its operating sequence is thesis formation, rapid execution, post-trade diligence, and dynamic rotation toward the bottleneck judged most active.
- The author maps that sequence onto [[BayesianUpdating]]: structural research supplies a prior, new diligence evidence changes the posterior, and a broken thesis should trigger reduction or exit rather than price-based rationalization.
- Positive evidence may include patents, long-term customer contracts, or superior yields; negative evidence may include substitution risk, customer insourcing, or dishonest management.
- The essay concludes that decisions should rest on probabilistic expected outcomes anchored in structure rather than certainty or historical extrapolation, although it does not actually calculate expected value.

## Key Quotes
> “我们需要验证的不是‘股价涨没涨’，而是‘当初让我下注的那个逻辑是否还在被事实强化’。” — distinguishing thesis evidence from price movement.

> “概率的锚点不是历史，而是结构；决策的依据不是确定性，而是期望值。” — the essay's summary of its probabilistic investment philosophy.

## Contradictions
- The move from US$12 to US$70 is approximately a 483% price gain, or a final value about 5.83 times the starting value; it does not by itself support the article's stated 1,000% floating gain.
- The 4,502% annual return, Reddit ban, AXTI recommendation, “invention” of the method, and trade history are asserted without links, dates, screenshots, account statements, position sizes, cash flows, realized-versus-unrealized separation, or independent corroboration.
- Research-derived conviction is not a numerical Bayesian prior unless hypotheses, base rates, likelihoods, and updating rules are made explicit. The essay uses Bayesian language as an analogy rather than presenting a calibrated probabilistic model.
- Choosing the opportunity with the highest posterior probability does not necessarily maximize expected value: payoff size, downside, valuation, correlation, liquidity, transaction costs, taxes, position sizing, and model error also matter.
- Trading before diligence is complete may capture an information advantage, but it also increases exposure to false premises, manipulation, illiquidity, and confirmation bias. The article supplies no entry gate or maximum-loss rule.
- Dynamic rotation may improve capital use, but it can also create turnover costs, short-horizon chasing, and inconsistent comparisons between different hypotheses; no benchmark, drawdown, or out-of-sample record is supplied.
