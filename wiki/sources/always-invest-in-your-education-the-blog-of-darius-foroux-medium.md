## Summary
[[DariusForoux]] argues that education should be treated as a continuous [[SelfEducationInvestment]] rather than a degree or one-time accumulation. After describing a period when he stopped reading, attending conferences, and deliberately learning, he frames daily learning as a survival strategy that improves decisions, opens opportunities, supports earning power, and remains valuable even if other assets are lost.

## Key Claims
- Knowledge is perishable: people forget and lose capability when they stop practicing and improving what they know.
- Education should be a constant supply, closer to food or exercise than to a credential collected once.
- Better learning supports better decisions by encouraging facts, research, and focus on what matters.
- Learning expands opportunities by exposing people to new ideas and making closed-mindedness less likely.
- Skill and knowledge can improve earning power when they make a worker or entrepreneur more useful and less interchangeable.
- Education is a lifelong safety net because money, reputation, jobs, and clients can disappear, while practiced knowledge and skills remain reusable.
- The article's image repeats the central claim visually: "Always invest in this thing" points to a brain, signed by Foroux.

## Key Quotes
> "Education is a survival strategy." - Foroux's practical justification for self-education.

> "It's education that matters; not degrees." - distinguishing learning from credentials.
