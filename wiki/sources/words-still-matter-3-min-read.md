## Summary
[[EvWilliams]] restates [[Medium]]'s founding mission and argues that the dominant online-media system rewards captured attention regardless of quality, context, truth, or conscious choice. He presents reader subscriptions, an ad-free experience, an open paywall, and partner compensation based on member value as an attempt to align the platform with depth and understanding rather than clicks. The source's large editorial collage is decorative, and its 60-pixel copy is a duplicate thumbnail, so both were omitted.

## Key Claims
- Information consumption shapes thought and behavior, so the quality of the media environment matters rather than merely the quantity of available content.
- The dominant media ecosystem makes it difficult to find signal, facts, nuanced ideas, and high-level discourse amid abundant content.
- Online publishing's prevailing reward rule is summarized as "attention equals money," which pays for captured attention regardless of quality, context, or deliberate user choice.
- Personalized systems can mistake compulsion for preference, then supply more of whatever users cannot stop watching or consuming.
- Declining advertising revenue per reader, worsening experience, and eroding privacy leave less room for research, fact-checking, polish, nuance, and complexity while rewarding speed and volume.
- Direct reader subscriptions can remove the conflict between serving readers and serving advertisers when the product is ad-free and must earn payment by improving content and experience.
- Medium's proposed open paywall combines work from independent contributors and publishers, with partner earnings tied to depth and value for members rather than fleeting advertiser attention.

## Key Quotes
> "The current reward system that drives content online looks like this: Attention = Money." - Williams's diagnosis of online-media incentives.

> "Let's take back control of what we pay attention to and what gets rewarded." - the article's closing call for deliberate attention and reader-funded incentives.

## Contradictions

- Claims about declining ad revenue per reader, worsening privacy, and a nadir of truth and trust are asserted without comparative evidence in the source.
