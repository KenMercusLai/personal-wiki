## Summary
[[AnnDerrickGaillot]] asks six music professionals how they discover new music after streaming platforms and personalized recommendations displaced many older discovery routes. Their answers make [[MusicDiscovery]] a mixed practice spanning [[Spotify]] and [[SoundCloud]], [[Bandcamp]] tags and buyer trails, trusted writers, professional submissions, friends, record stores, liner notes, and live shows. The interviews suggest that human context can create deeper exploration than a single recommended track, but they are selected personal accounts rather than comparative evidence about discovery quality.

## Key Claims
- Effective discovery uses several channels rather than treating an algorithm, publication, platform, or social circle as sufficient on its own.
- Human networks provide context and trust: friends, artists, managers, journalists, clerks, labels, publishers, and local scenes all act as filters.
- Bandcamp supports intentional exploration through genre tags, editorial work, purchase trails, and other users' collections.
- SoundCloud is presented as especially useful for emerging hip-hop, rap, and trap that may not yet appear on Spotify.
- Spotify's related artists, friends' playlists, Discover features, and Release Radar can lower the barrier for newcomers, although [[DelaneyMotter]] reports that its recommendations often produce isolated song listening rather than durable attachment to an artist.
- Live shows, record-store browsing, liner notes, and tracing musical histories create branching paths through scenes, collaborators, genres, and production credits.
- Abundant self-publishing makes more music available while increasing the problem of choosing which path deserves attention.

## Key Quotes
> "I rely a lot on my network in Atlanta." - [[JenMalone]] on professional and local discovery

> "I was always trying to listen my way backwards from the artists and scenes I liked." - [[HuaHsu]] on historical and lineage-based exploration

> "There's nothing like that old school discovery of just going through the stacks" - [[MarcusMoore]] on record-store browsing and human recommendation

## Connections
- [[MusicDiscovery]] - central practice synthesized from six contrasting professional routines.
- [[AnnDerrickGaillot]] - author who assembles and frames the six interviews.
- [[JenMalone]] - combines SoundCloud, Spotify, Instagram, licensing contacts, artists, and an Atlanta network.
- [[LaurenRearick]] - searches Bandcamp tags and buyer activity while following blogs, musicians, Twitter, and Spotify releases.
- [[HuaHsu]] - follows friends, magazines, record stores, Bandcamp buyers, and musical lineages.
- [[DrDemento]] - relies on direct submissions, a specialist clearinghouse, and established media while acknowledging a preference for familiarity.
- [[DelaneyMotter]] - emphasizes friends and live shows and treats Spotify as more useful for newcomers than for her own deeper discovery.
- [[MarcusMoore]] - combines shows, Bandcamp editorial work, email, SoundCloud, record stores, liner notes, and cross-genre comparison.
- [[Bandcamp]] - supports tag search, editorial crate digging, and social trails through purchases and collections.
- [[SoundCloud]] - presented as an early channel for emerging rap, hip-hop, and trap.
- [[Spotify]] - offers related artists, playlists, Discover, and Release Radar but receives mixed judgments about depth.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction with existing wiki pages. The source expands Spotify's profile from product, growth, management, acquisition, and design cases into music discovery.
- The six accounts are role-, genre-, place-, and time-specific anecdotes from 2018; they do not compare recommendation diversity, artist outcomes, listening depth, or discovery success under controlled conditions.
- Several speakers have professional access to submissions, labels, publishers, editors, and early releases that ordinary listeners may not share.
