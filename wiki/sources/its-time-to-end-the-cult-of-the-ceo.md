## Summary
Ab Banerjee argues that companies, investors, and media over-identify organizations with a single chief executive, obscuring the management team's contribution and concentrating expectations, stress, and perceived market risk around one person. The proposed remedy is primarily communicative: make the wider executive team visible in reports, investor updates, interviews, and public engagements, then extend the same team-centered recognition deeper into the organization.

## Key Claims
- [[CEOCentricLeadership]] is reinforced jointly by media attention and companies that present the CEO as a solitary, all-powerful leader.
- Concentrating public identity and accountability on one CEO can obscure the executives and teams that make consequential operating decisions.
- The source attributes CEO stress, excessive workload, insomnia, and blame for uncontrollable market conditions partly to expectations that one leader must be all-seeing and always visible.
- The source argues that markets become more vulnerable to leadership transitions when company value is perceived as inseparable from one executive.
- Companies can reduce this concentration by featuring executive teams in annual reports and distributing interviews, public appearances, and investor communication across functional leaders.
- Team-centered recognition should extend below the executive level because company performance usually reflects collective work rather than one star performer.

## Key Quotes
> "CEOs are not alone at the top. We just like to pretend that they are." - on the gap between actual management work and its public representation.

> "Companies need to increase the visibility of their management teams." - the article's proposed starting point for changing that representation.

## Contradictions
- The article qualifies CEO-centered accounts of strategy and performance elsewhere in the wiki: a CEO can have distinctive responsibilities and influence without being the sole cause of organizational outcomes.
- The reported valuation, stress, insomnia, resignation, and death effects are attributed to named organizations but the supplied document includes no study titles, methods, samples, dates, or links; they remain source-reported figures rather than independently verified evidence.
- Greater executive visibility may distribute recognition and succession confidence, but the source provides no outcome comparison showing that the proposed communication changes reduce CEO stress, improve management performance, or stabilize market value.
