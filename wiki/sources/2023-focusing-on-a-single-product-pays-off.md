## Summary
[[MaxRozen]] reflects on 2023 as a year when [[ROZENMD]] and [[OnlineOrNot]] grew faster than expected because he doubled down on one product instead of running many disconnected small bets. The article argues that business learning, long-lived product presence, and old user relationships compound over time, while a full-time role at [[Cloudflare]] makes patient after-hours SaaS growth possible. It also notes that frequent diary writing and a ThinkPad hardware article created reflection loops and accidental product awareness.

## Key Claims
- Focusing on one working product can beat a portfolio of unrelated small bets when the hardest work is moving from no traction to early recurring revenue.
- [[OnlineOrNot]] benefited from long-term compounding: old pre-launch prospects returned, early free users converted, and old bugs only became visible after enough time and usage.
- [[BootstrappedSaaS]] can approach salary-replacement potential while the founder still prefers keeping a full-time job because constrained morning work improves shipping discipline.
- Frequent diary posts can support founder learning even when they do not attract large audiences.
- [[Cloudflare]] employment enabled slow, patient work on [[OnlineOrNot]], while the author's 2023 work connected him to [[CloudflareD1]].
- A non-core hardware article about replacing a MacBook Air with a ThinkPad T480 accidentally drove discovery interest toward [[OnlineOrNot]].

## Key Quotes
> "what I learn about running the business compounds" - on why OnlineOrNot growth improved with time.

> "I work better knowing I have two hours to ship something each morning." - on keeping full-time employment.
