## Summary
[[AmirRajan]] describes leaving well-paid corporate software work, using savings and a liquidated 401k to take a sabbatical, and porting [[ADarkRoom]] to iOS after connecting deeply with the original web game. The article is both a success story and an anti-success-story: [[ADarkRoom]] reached #1 on the [[AppStore]] and grossed over $800,000, but Rajan emphasizes luck, privilege, anxiety, dwindling revenue, and the eventual return to contract or corporate work.

## Key Claims
- [[SabbaticalCareerExperiment]] can reveal meaningful work, but it often depends on years of savings, low expenses, family constraints, health luck, and tolerance for career risk.
- [[IndieGameDevelopment]] may provide intense creative fulfillment even before commercial validation; Rajan describes losing track of time while building [[ADarkRoom]] and finding a calling in digital evocative experiences.
- App-store success can be wildly discontinuous: [[ADarkRoom]] moved from tiny daily downloads to viral sales days, #1 ranking, 250,000 downloads, and then a decline to about 100 downloads per day.
- Luck and privilege are central in the source's interpretation of success; hard work and craft do not guarantee a sustainable indie-game career.
- Post-success anxiety can be severe: Rajan describes sleeping poorly, refreshing rankings for 18 days, purging public writing after returning to work, and later rebuilding a more peaceful relationship to employment.
- Ethical or expressive product choices can limit monetization options; Rajan rejected ads, micro-transactions, energy bars, and other tactics even while trying to keep building games.

## Key Quotes
> "There is such a heavy dose of luck in success." - on the article's central qualification.

> "I was one of the lucky ones." - on privilege and chance behind the sabbatical.

> "this will come to an end." - on anxiety during the #1 App Store run.
