## Summary
[[JeanLouisGassee]] argues that [[BlackBerry]], [[Palm]], [[Nokia]], and [[Microsoft]] failed in the smartphone transition for different reasons rather than one generic incumbent problem. [[BlackBerry]] saw the iPhone's operating-system threat but waited years before buying [[QNX]], Palm moved faster with [[WebOS]] but arrived too late, Nokia understood the ecosystem war but damaged its existing business through the [[OsborneEffect]], and Microsoft faced a [[PlatformBusinessModelObstacle]] once free Android made mobile OS licensing untenable.

## Key Claims
- [[SmartphonePlatformDisruption]] did not punish every incumbent the same way; failures mixed technology gaps, timing errors, ecosystem strategy, and business-model collapse.
- [[BlackBerry]] had a primitive software platform relative to the [[IPhone]] and waited until 2010 to acquire [[QNX]], three years after the iPhone launch.
- [[Palm]] recognized its old platform problem and built [[WebOS]], but the Palm Pre ecosystem arrived too late and failed to gain momentum.
- [[Nokia]] understood the "war of ecosystems" under Stephen Elop, but announcing the Windows Phone transition before replacement devices were ready created an [[OsborneEffect]].
- [[Microsoft]] improved its mobile software but could not sustain a handset licensing business once [[Android]] made a capable smartphone OS free to licensees.
- The inspected images reinforce the source's argument: BlackBerry's physical-keyboard handset illustrates its legacy design center, the sales chart shows a steep post-2010 unit collapse, and the Android screenshot frames the late Android move as a "best of both worlds" pitch.
