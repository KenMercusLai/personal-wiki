## Summary
[[AndreyAzimov]] describes leaving Railsware for a one-year, full-time experiment in independent product building: keep personal spending near $500 per month, launch repeatedly, publish revenue, and reach $1,000 in monthly revenue by March 2019. A May 2019 update says he launched seven apps, achieved the goal, and became Product Hunt's Maker of the Year, but the article does not provide an app-by-app revenue history or evidence sufficient to attribute the result to any single tactic.

## Key Claims
- A low personal cost base can turn a modest recurring-revenue target into a practical definition of profitability for an independent creator.
- Writing down fears and their worst plausible outcomes can make a risky career decision feel bounded rather than unknowable.
- Public goals, revenue reporting, and a fixed deadline can serve as commitment devices when the creator expects inconsistency, procrastination, or unfinished work.
- Pre-fit product work should prioritize finding a painful problem for which customers will pay rather than polishing an idea in isolation.
- Launching multiple products can be used as a search strategy when the creator lacks confidence that the first idea will find demand.
- Limited coding experience does not prevent product experiments, but it constrains scope and makes learning during delivery part of the plan.
- The article's later update reports a successful outcome—seven apps and the revenue goal reached—without enough operating detail to estimate reproducibility, failure rate, workload, or which product created profitability.

## Key Quotes
> "I want to ship products and earn $1,000/month" - on the concrete income target.

> "I think I will learn on the way" - on treating limited technical experience as a constraint rather than a stop condition.

## Contradictions
- The plan's many-product search strategy qualifies [[StartupFocus]] and the later [[SmallProductPortfolio]] evidence favoring compounding around one proven product; the sources describe different stages rather than a universal contradiction.
- Unlike [[SideProjectIncubation]], this account removes the employment bridge and accepts a fixed personal runway, increasing both focus and downside exposure.
- The reported success is a brief retrospective update, so it does not establish that public accountability, quitting employment, or high launch volume caused the outcome.
