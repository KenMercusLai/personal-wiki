## Summary
[[StevenSinofsky]] argues that writing is not the opposite of agility but a practical way to make plans, strategy, and execution legible inside companies. The source contrasts written strategic memos with PowerPoint-style decks, warning that terse slides invite divergent interpretation while written rationale gives teams shared context for decentralized tradeoffs. It also qualifies writing culture by noting that memos take time, can be gamed, and can become goals unto themselves, especially when successful-company rituals such as [[Amazon]] six-page memos are copied without care.

## Key Claims
- [[StrategicWriting]] forces decision makers to explain why a plan should work, which assumptions shaped it, and how stakeholders will react.
- Written plans help execution scale because people making micro-decisions can refer back to the framework, rationale, and logic behind the original choice.
- Decks are often inadequate for strategy because they emphasize selling takeaways and let readers invent their own ground truth from bullets, titles, or lone graphics.
- Writing builds short- and medium-term corporate history, reducing the chance that organizations repeat mistakes because they no longer remember why earlier decisions were made.
- Writing culture has failure modes: successful rituals can become status targets, memo constraints can be gamed, and months-long memo production may confuse the document with the work.
- Executives and long-tenured employees should keep writing themselves because delegating the substance of writing also delegates the thinking.

## Key Quotes
> "Agility comes from planning." - on why writing supports rather than blocks adaptive work.

> "A deck is not a plan" - on the risk of treating persuasive slides as shared strategic context.

> "Writing is risky." - on why sharing ideas in written form should count as a valued cultural act.
