## Summary
[[LenShneyder]] reports that [[SendGrid]] sent 1.6 billion emails on Black Friday 2016, using holiday-weekend volume, opens, device mix, open delay, subject-line length, and subject-line vocabulary to argue that email remained a large-scale retail revenue channel. The article frames Black Friday as a larger email-volume peak than Cyber Monday, with retail demand sustaining high weekend volume and mobile opens approaching half of SendGrid's observed opens. It also argues that shorter subject lines produced higher engagement than the more common nine-word subject lines in an early-November sample.

## Key Claims
- [[EmailMarketingAtScale]] changes the problem from simple delivery to infrastructure and analytics capable of handling billion-message daily volume and rare retail peaks.
- Black Friday generated SendGrid's largest holiday email peak in 2016, reaching 1.6 billion messages and exceeding Cyber Monday volume.
- [[MobileEmailEngagement]] had become a default design constraint: mobile accounted for just under half of SendGrid opens, while external [[Litmus]] tracking put Apple and Android opens around 54%.
- Mobile users opened email slightly faster than desktop users, making consumer email behavior closer to near-real-time communication.
- [[SubjectLineOptimization]] in the early-November sample favored brevity: nine-word subject lines were most common, but two-to-four-word subject lines had the highest engagement.

## Key Quotes
> "Sending 1.6 billion emails is hard" - framing SendGrid's Black Friday scale problem.

> "Mobile came in just under 50%" - device-mix observation from SendGrid's holiday analysis.

> "2-4 words had the highest engagement rates" - subject-line-length finding from SendGrid's early-November analysis.
