## Summary
[[MichaelAbehsera]] argues that public writing is a high-leverage complementary skill for designers because it can attract traffic, clients, speaking invitations, professional relationships, teaching revenue, and jobs. Using short interviews and examples from five designers, he recommends writing for the audience one wants, helping with a specific problem before mentioning paid work, reading admired writers, choosing subjects strong enough to overcome reluctance, and beginning before the idea or draft feels perfect. ## Key Claims
- Writing can turn design knowledge and opinions into discoverable professional signals that produce opportunities beyond design delivery itself.
- Eyal Zuri attributes Muzli traffic growth partly to frequent, lighthearted content, while Paul Jarvis connects audience-specific educational writing to client and course demand.
- Jarvis recommends writing for the audience one wants rather than defaulting to other designers, helping with a concrete struggle before making a restrained paid offer.
- Nick Babich keeps articles focused by targeting six five-to-seven-minute pieces per month and reports that writing led to a conference speaking invitation.
- Matt West recommends reading admired writers closely, choosing topics where interest outweighs reluctance, taking a position, and staying centered on one idea.
- Andrew Graunke describes a public Crunchbase redesign proposal as a way to start dialogue with the company's CEO, while Abehsera says a Toptal founder discovered his Medium writing and recruited him as lead editor.
- A sustainable writing practice starts with a point to make, separates drafting from editing, and improves through repetition rather than waiting for a perfect idea.

## Key Quotes
> "Just write for the audience you want to have." - Paul Jarvis on aligning public writing with the people and opportunities the writer hopes to reach.

> "The most difficult part is starting, so don't wait until you find the perfect idea." - Michael Abehsera on reducing the entry barrier to a writing practice.
