## Summary
This article presents [[ReadingNoteWorkflow]] as a practical entry point for [[ZettelkastenMethod]] practice, using mystery-fiction reading notes in [[Obsidian]] as the worked example. It argues that card notes need a suitable "sweet spot" rather than more meta-discussion about note-taking, and that book notes supply that context because readers can create entry notes, outline notes, and opinion notes from ordinary reading. The workflow connects books, authors, genres, awards, tricks, and personal judgments through backlinks, local graphs, spoiler controls, and graph coloring.

## Key Claims
- [[ReadingNoteWorkflow]] is a concrete way to begin card-note practice because book reviews and summaries already require restating material in one's own words.
- Card notes work better when applied to a domain with real substance, such as mystery-fiction reading, rather than being used only to study note-taking itself.
- Entry notes can store book metadata, plots, publication background, genre labels, influences, tricks, and other relationship-rich attributes.
- Outline notes can turn reading histories, author chronologies, genre canons, and award lists into linked reading maps.
- Opinion notes extend traditional reviews by letting readers write compact, flexible reflections from multiple perspectives.
- [[Obsidian]] features such as backlinks, local graph views, spoiler-folding HTML, task plugins, and graph groups make the reading-note system easier to inspect and navigate.
- Card-based reading notes trade exhaustive long-form reviews for a sustainable, lightweight, linkable record of ongoing reading.
