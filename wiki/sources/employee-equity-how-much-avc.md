## Summary
[[FredWilson]] proposes a repeatable [[EmployeeEquityGrantSizing]] method for startups that have moved beyond their first exceptional hires. The method converts role and salary into a target dollar value, then converts that value into fully diluted shares using a current company "best value" rather than a 409A valuation. The framework aims to make grants more consistent and understandable, but its 2010 role multipliers are explicitly obsolete and its growth examples are expectations rather than risk-adjusted employee outcomes.

## Key Claims
- The first roughly three to ten key hires may require negotiated percentage-point grants because joining before the company is established is an unusually uncertain commitment.
- Once a core team exists, a startup can size employee grants from a role bracket, a salary multiplier, a current company value, and the fully diluted share count.
- The calculation is `grant shares = base salary × role multiplier ÷ company best value × fully diluted shares`, equivalently dividing the target grant value by the implied current share price.
- CEO and COO grants belong to the board rather than the ordinary employee formula, while retention grants can reuse the method on a shorter two-year refresh cadence.
- Grants should be communicated in dollar terms as well as shares so employees can understand the intended compensation value and its dependence on company growth.
- The published 2010 multipliers—0.5× for senior management, 0.25× for directors, 0.1× for key functions, and 0.05× for other roles—are historical examples that the source warns are below the later market.

## Key Quotes
> "Getting someone to join your dream before it is much of anything is an art not a science." - why the formula does not govern the earliest key hires.

> "Using a methodology, whether it is this one or some other one, is a good practice to promote fairness and rigor" - the article's reason for systematizing later employee grants.

## Contradictions
- The source's illustrative 2010 role multipliers are explicitly stale; the embedded warning says using them later would put grants below market, especially for senior hires.
- The expected three-to-fivefold four-year company-value increase and possible tenfold breakout describe venture expectations, not observed employee outcomes or a risk-adjusted valuation.
- Calling dollar value tangible qualifies but does not displace [[EmployeeEquityRisk]]: the method omits strike price, tax, vesting loss, dilution, preferences, debt, and liquidity probability.
