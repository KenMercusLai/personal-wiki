## Summary
[[BenedictEvans]] argues that computing ecosystems first gain overwhelming scale by reaching a new and much larger customer base, then use the resulting investment and component economics to displace the incumbent ecosystem. The PC followed that path against mainframes and workstations, and the ARM, [[IOS]], and [[Android]] [[MobileEcosystem]] was following it against PCs; however, a mobile market reaching nearly everyone leaves no obvious larger human market for a successor. Evans therefore separates device replacement from shifts in industry attention: just as the web made Windows less strategically important without immediately reducing Windows sales, AI could make mobile less central without replacing smartphones.

## Key Claims
- The PC ecosystem did not initially win by selling to mainframe or workstation buyers; it reached a much larger new population, gained superior economies of scale, and only later displaced older computing categories.
- Mobile was repeating that sequence, with projected annual ARM/iOS/Android unit sales around ten times PC sales and an installed base several times larger, concentrating component investment in mobile-oriented sensors, cameras, and radios.
- A new ecosystem's first step is reaching an untapped market, while direct replacement of the old ecosystem comes later and can take decades even after the industry's center of gravity moves.
- Because mobile can reach almost every person, the next computing ecosystem has no obvious larger human customer pool from which to build the same kind of scale advantage.
- The [[InternetOfThingsData|Internet of Things]] adds many objects but may remain dependent on smartphone components and control surfaces rather than form one coherent successor platform; VR likewise draws on PC, phone, and console ecosystems.
- Augmented reality could be a new interaction step after the command line, mouse-based GUI, and multitouch, but its use of smartphone CPUs makes its status as a separate ecosystem uncertain.
- A strategic transition need not replace the incumbent device base: the web reduced Windows' control over languages, formats, and protocols while Windows sales continued growing, and AI could similarly redirect attention away from mobile.

## Key Quotes
> "Ecosystem scale won." - conclusion from the PC industry's displacement of workstations and other older systems.

> "The centre of gravity moves." - qualification that strategic importance can shift long before an installed base declines.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found. The source sharpens [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] by explaining the historical mechanism behind mobile's scale advantage and by separating ecosystem displacement from an attention shift.
- The mobile, PC, and installed-base figures are forward-looking estimates from 2016, not current measurements, and the essay does not test whether its projected scale ratios were reached.
- The treatment of IoT, VR, AR, and AI is exploratory. It identifies candidate transition patterns but does not establish which became an independent platform or whether AI ultimately commoditized mobile in the way the web commoditized Windows.
