## Summary
[[HoraceDediu]] argues that [[AppleWatch]] had already become a serious business by 2017, overtaking [[Rolex]] revenue on estimated sales only two and a half years after launch. The article frames the Watch as an [[IPhone]] companion that is following the iPhone's own path from dependent activation to eventual independence, with LTE, W2 silicon, faster Wi-Fi, faster processing, and better power efficiency enabling more phone-like jobs. Its larger claim is that [[AppleWatch]] is not simply a miniature iPhone: it can inherit the iPhone's iPod, internet, and phone "tentpole" roles while opening a separate [[WearableComputing]] direction around fitness and health.

## Key Claims
- [[AppleWatch]] was estimated at roughly 15 million units and about $4.9 billion in annual revenue, enough to exceed [[Rolex]]'s 2016 watch revenue.
- The embedded chart estimates about 33 million cumulative Apple Watch units and about $12.3 billion cumulative Watch revenue since launch.
- Apple Watch remained an [[IPhone]] companion in 2017 because activation and coverage still depended on the phone, but Dediu argues that eventual independence is likely by analogy with the iPhone's former dependence on a PC or Mac.
- Series 3's LTE, W2 processor, faster Wi-Fi, faster processor, and better power efficiency make independent voice entry, Siri, maps, music streaming, Find My Friends, and phone calls more plausible on the wrist.
- The Watch is stealing usage from the iPhone, first through alerts, timekeeping, and basic messaging, then through calls, music, and maps.
- The Watch repeats the original iPhone's launch frame as a small-screen iPod, internet communicator, and phone, while its most original market-creation path points toward fitness and health.

## Key Quotes
> "Today the new Watch is a small-screen iPod, an Internet Communicator and a Phone." - Dediu's analogy to the iPhone launch.

> "But it's not just a miniature iPhone." - transition from iPhone analogy to fitness and health as the Watch's separate direction.
