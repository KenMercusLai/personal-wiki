## Summary
[[HoldenKarnofsky]] argues that deep learning works better when reading is organized around writing a provisional view rather than around open-ended information gathering. His [[LearningByWriting]] loop starts with an important-if-true claim, reads a small amount on both sides, states a premature hypothesis, attacks its weaknesses, researches the most decision-relevant subquestion, revises or flips sides, and repeats until outside feedback becomes the next weakness-finding mechanism.

## Key Claims
- [[LearningByWriting]] makes reading more focused by keeping it in service of a bottom-line view instead of treating intake as the main goal.
- Early, explicit hypotheses are useful even when they are premature because they reveal which subquestions are most likely to change the current view.
- The core loop alternates between defending a claim, listing its weaknesses, targeted reading or discussion, and revising or switching sides.
- Productive investigation requires choosing which subquestions not to pursue, because many debates can expand into endless dimensions.
- Feedback from knowledgeable readers, experts, and the public extends the same weakness-finding loop after the author can no longer easily tear apart the draft alone.
- The method is mentally taxing because writing exposes confusion, makes project duration hard to predict, and repeatedly forces judgment about whether more research is worth it.

## Key Quotes
> "always have a hypothesis" - on keeping investigation oriented around a provisional bottom line.

> "single biggest difference-maker" - on why writing-centered investigation improves retention and view formation.
