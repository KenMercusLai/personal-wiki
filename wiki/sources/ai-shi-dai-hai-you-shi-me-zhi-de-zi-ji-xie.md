## Summary
[[YeungYeah]] argues that cheap AI answers and automated summaries weaken the case for accumulating generic technical explanations or excerpt-style notes, but not for writing itself. The essay retains three human-owned forms—personal outlines and indexes, process narratives, and genuine opinions or feelings—and uses its own voice-transcription and AI-polishing history to frame [[AIAssistedWriting]] as acceptable when the author's core ideas and judgments survive, while acknowledging that repeated processing may dilute personal voice.

## Key Claims
- Note-taking should distinguish subjective synthesis from excerpt collection: writing in one's own words can test understanding, while copied or lightly organized material may become an archive that is rarely revisited.
- AI makes structured explanations and automatic document summaries cheap, but faster capture does not establish that the resulting notes will be read, remembered, or useful.
- Personal outlines, keyword indexes, and conceptual frameworks remain valuable because a person still needs enough orientation to know what to ask and how to retrieve details.
- Generic technical troubleshooting posts face a changed value proposition when an AI assistant can answer shallow questions directly, while deep technical writing remains costly and quickly evolving.
- Process records have narrative and autobiographical value even when they are not reusable technical references.
- Personal judgments, lived experience, emotion, and an identifiable human voice remain forms of writing that generated text cannot represent on the author's behalf.
- AI transcription and polishing can serve expression when the originating ideas and judgments remain human-owned, but repeated rewriting may still flatten the voice that made the material personal.

## Key Quotes
> "AI 能给你答案，但你需要知道该问什么问题。" - on why personal outlines and indexes still matter.

> "只要核心的想法和判断仍然是自己的" - on the author's boundary for acceptable AI processing.

## Contradictions
- The essay qualifies output-first note-taking claims by distinguishing personally reconstructed understanding from copied or automatically summarized material; producing more text is not itself evidence of learning.
- The claim that shallow technical posts have little remaining value is a first-person judgment. It does not measure search behavior, model reliability, discoverability, source diversity, public correction, or the value of verified practitioner records.
- The author supplies no before-and-after drafts or reader evaluation, so the amount of voice lost during transcription and polishing remains unknown.
