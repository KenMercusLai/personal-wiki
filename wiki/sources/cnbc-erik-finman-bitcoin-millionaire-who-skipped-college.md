## Summary
[[CNBC]] profiles [[ErikFinman]], who turned an early [[Bitcoin]] investment into capital for the online tutoring company [[Botangle]], later accepted bitcoin when selling its technology, and held 403 bitcoin when the article valued the position above $1 million. Finman uses that outcome to argue for experience-driven, internet-enabled learning over college, while the article explicitly notes Bitcoin's volatility and presents sharply divergent price forecasts. The source Markdown contains no effective image references.

## Key Claims
- Finman invested a $1,000 gift in Bitcoin at age 12 in 2011, sold the first investment in late 2013 for about $100,000, and used the proceeds to start Botangle.
- Botangle connected frustrated students with teachers over video chat; in 2015 Finman sold its technology for 300 bitcoin instead of the offered $100,000 cash.
- At publication, CNBC reported that Finman held 403 bitcoin, then worth about $1.09 million, plus smaller positions in Litecoin and Ethereum.
- Finman framed Bitcoin and blockchain networks as peer-to-peer infrastructure that could remove intermediaries, reduce overhead, and support services without a central hub.
- He attributed much of his education to running a business and using free internet resources rather than completing high school, a GED, or college.
- The article pairs Finman's extreme upside forecast with an explicit volatility warning and a much more conservative analyst forecast.

## Key Quotes
> "I can proudly say I made it, and I'm not going to college." - Finman on winning his bet with his parents.
