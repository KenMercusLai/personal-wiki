## Summary
[[BenedictEvans]] argues that a smartphone camera should be understood as a programmable [[ImageSensorAsInput|image sensor and input method]], not only as a device for making and sharing photographs. Once capture, processing, display, and software occupy one device, products such as [[Snapchat]] Lenses and Stories can reduce interface abstraction, create [[AuthenticallyMobile|mobile-native]] forms, and point toward [[AugmentedReality]] and computer vision systems that interpret what the user shows them. The essay is a 2016 strategy argument whose device-scale estimates, product examples, and AR forecasts are historical rather than current evidence.

## Key Claims
- Near-universal camera-equipped smartphone adoption greatly expands the number of people capturing images and the volume of images produced, though the article's five-billion-user and 50-to-100-times-film figures are contemporary estimates.
- Calling the sensor a camera and its output a photo can hide its wider software potential: images need not be saved, capture need not be limited to a shutter press, video need not be linear, and text or graphics can be native layers rather than later edits.
- [[ImageSensorAsInput]] reframes the camera as an input method analogous to multitouch, allowing software to interpret any capturable signal rather than only reproduce older photographic forms.
- [[Snapchat]] Lenses and Stories join sensor and screen into one interaction mechanism: the user looks through the device while software transforms or organizes what the sensor sees.
- [[AuthenticallyMobile]] products are not merely easier on phones; their central interaction depends on mobile hardware and context and may make little sense on a desktop.
- Mature head-worn [[AugmentedReality]] could invert the smartphone pattern: phones absorbed physical objects into apps, while AR may place virtual objects and applications back into perceived physical space.
- Computer vision reduces explicit user input by letting software recognize objects, people, or scenes that a user shows it, extending the rule that software should not request information it can detect or infer.

## Key Quotes
> "the device becomes the sensor and the sensor becomes the app" - on Snapchat Lenses and Pokémon Go reducing the abstraction between input, display, and software.

> "it's more like an eye, that can see" - on computer vision changing the camera from a capture device into a perceptual input channel.

## Contradictions
- The essay complements the wiki's existing Snapchat and AR sources by generalizing their camera examples into an input model; it does not establish that Snapchat originated the model or would control the resulting platform.
- The five-billion-phone forecast, annual image-volume estimate, and examples involving Apple Live Photos, HoloLens, Magic Leap, Pokémon Go, Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest, and Tinder are bounded to 2016 and are not current market evidence.
- The source predicts that mature head-worn AR will make virtual objects appear convincingly present, but provides no adoption, accessibility, safety, privacy, performance, or business evidence for that outcome.
- Computer vision can reduce manual input only when its recognition is sufficiently accurate and appropriately authorized; the essay does not examine errors, bias, consent, surveillance, or data governance.
