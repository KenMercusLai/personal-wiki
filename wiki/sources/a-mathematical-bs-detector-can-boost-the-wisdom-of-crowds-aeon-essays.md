## Summary
[[GeorgeMusser]] summarizes research led by [[DrazenPrelec]] arguing that [[WisdomOfCrowds]] often fails when many people independently reuse the same common information. The essay presents [[Metaknowledge]] - people's knowledge of what others know or believe - as a way to detect overconfident guessing, recover hidden information, and weight crowd judgments more intelligently.

## Key Claims
- [[WisdomOfCrowds]] works poorly when shared information is overcounted and minority information is drowned out.
- Confidence is an unreliable weighting signal because knowledgeable people can be diffident while inaccurate people can be highly certain.
- [[Metaknowledge]] can be measured by asking respondents both for their own answer and for a prediction of other respondents' answers.
- [[FalseConsensusEffect]] can reveal hidden beliefs because people's predictions about others are partly anchored in their own views.
- Metaknowledge can improve surveys, incentivize truthful reporting, and screen knowledgeable respondents from guessers or reflexive contrarians.
- Strong consensus remains a useful proxy for truth; the metaknowledge correction mainly matters when the majority is weaker.

## Key Quotes
> "There's more information there." - Prelec on extracting richer signals from crowds

> "Good metaknowledge is precious." - Musser's closing judgment
