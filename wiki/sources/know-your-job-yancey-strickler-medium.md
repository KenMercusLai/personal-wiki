## Summary
[[YanceyStrickler]] recounts advising a successful founder who interpreted his team's stronger independent performance as evidence that he was no longer useful. Strickler reframes [[CEOScalingRole]] around organizational success: choose a direction, build the team capable of reaching it, create distance from daily execution, and keep vision and team judgment in continuous conversation. The essay treats the founder's discomfort as a role transition rather than proof that he should quit.

## Key Claims
- A CEO should evaluate personal effectiveness partly through the success of the organization and leaders they assembled, not only through the continued superiority of the CEO's own ideas.
- The CEO's work shifts toward knowing where the organization is going and hiring people who can get it there.
- Strategic perspective requires deliberately creating time, space, and separation from office detail instead of remaining continuously in operational work.
- Peer conversations, customer contact, reading, solitude, and other perspective-changing experiences can help a leader examine the business through its intended future.
- Direction is not a complete operating plan: the team contributes detail and execution through an ongoing conversation with the CEO about the vision.
- A founder who feels less directly useful may need to grow into a changed leadership role rather than leave the company immediately.

## Key Quotes
> "The CEO's job is to build an organization that succeeds." - Strickler's central reframe.

> "Now's the time to grow into your new job." - his conclusion about role transition.
