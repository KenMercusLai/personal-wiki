## Summary
In this early-2023 Forbes interview, [[SamAltman]] argues that [[ChatGPT]]'s breakthrough came less from a fundamentally new base model than from fine-tuning, a useful interaction design, and a willingness to ship an existing capability. He expects [[ArtificialGeneralIntelligence]] to arrive as a disputed, gradual transition rather than a single unmistakable event, while warning that full AGI could strain capitalism and require new arrangements for profit sharing, access, and governance. The interview also presents [[OpenAI]]'s public releases, APIs, selective open source, Microsoft deal protections, and downstream-provider duties as a layered [[ResponsibleAIRelease]] strategy rather than a single open-versus-closed choice.

## Key Claims
- ChatGPT's launch moment came from fine-tuning and the right interaction paradigm around a base model that had already been available through the API, not from an entirely new underlying technology.
- Altman pushed to ship ChatGPT despite internal doubt, treating real public use as a way to test demand and help society experience both the benefits and downsides of advanced AI.
- The interview predicts continuing exponential improvement but explicitly allows for an unexpected technical stumbling block.
- [[ArtificialGeneralIntelligence]] is framed as a slow takeoff whose boundary will remain contested, with multiple AGIs preferable to one company owning the field.
- Altman says full AGI could disrupt capitalism enough to require new thinking about how profits, access, and governance are distributed.
- [[ResponsibleAIRelease]] is presented as a mixed strategy: public products, progressively stronger APIs as safety permits, selective open source, contractual safeguards, and accountability for companies closest to end users.
- ChatGPT itself is not presented as a replacement for Google Search; the larger opportunity is to invent experiences beyond the search-query model.

## Key Quotes
> "It's not actually fundamentally new technology that made this have a moment." - Altman on ChatGPT's fine-tuning and interaction design.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction with existing wiki content. The interview qualifies simplistic accounts of ChatGPT as a wholly new model by attributing its breakout to fine-tuning, interaction design, and product release around an existing API-accessible base.
- Calling ChatGPT an "open tool" refers to public availability, not open-source code or weights; the same interview describes selective rather than universal open sourcing.
- Altman's claims about exponential progress, slow AGI takeoff, capitalism, and the effectiveness of corporate safeguards are forecasts and interested-party judgments from February 2023, not demonstrated outcomes.
