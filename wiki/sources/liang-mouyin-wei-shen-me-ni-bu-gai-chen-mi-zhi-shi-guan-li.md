## Summary
[[LiangMouyin]] argues that personal knowledge management can become a self-referential hobby where people collect methods, tools, and workflows instead of producing useful work. The article recommends simplifying around a personally fitting method and small tool stack, then judging the system by actual output rather than by how sophisticated the knowledge base appears.

## Key Claims
- The explosion of note apps and methods can create [[AttentionManagement]] problems when the learner has more knowledge-management content than actual knowledge to manage.
- [[PersonalKnowledgeManagement]] becomes hollow when it mainly stores knowledge-management advice rather than supporting writing, learning, or decisions.
- Other people's PKM workflows should not be copied wholesale because their needs, habits, and contexts differ.
- [[NoteToolFit]] should be evaluated by personal use and friction reduction, not by fashionable tool complexity.
- [[KnowledgeOutput]] is a practical test of whether a knowledge-management system is helping; the author's WeChat public account is treated as the real output library.
- Simplifying to INKP, [[Obsidian]], flomo, and WeChat reduced the author's anxiety around note methods and software.

## Key Quotes
> "他们用笔记软件，管理的是一堆「知识管理」知识。" - on meta-knowledge replacing substantive work.

> "当把输出作为检验知识管理的标准" - on using output as the test of a knowledge-management system.

> "唯有放弃折腾，才能找到属于自己的方法。" - on abandoning tool-chasing to find a personally fitting workflow.
