## Summary
[[AlbertWenger]] argues that a cryptocurrency bubble can finance useful experimentation and the infrastructure of a decentralized internet while still exposing participants to distorted judgment, misleading metrics, leverage, volatility, and fraud. Drawing lessons from the dot-com bubble, he recommends preserving outside perspective, measuring real end-user adoption, avoiding borrowed exposure, maintaining segregated liquidity, taking some gains during the ascent, and staying involved for the long term if the underlying technology matters. The essay is a May 2017 investor perspective rather than an empirical comparison of bubbles or a current assessment of cryptocurrency markets.

## Key Claims
- Financial bubbles often accompany technology waves and can accelerate real innovation and infrastructure buildout even when prices and projects become excessive.
- Bubble participants can become trapped in an internally coherent narrative that evaluates projects only against other projects in the same speculative environment.
- Pageviews and cumulative registrations are weak evidence of progress when compared with actual adoption and use by end users.
- Borrowing, short exposure, and contract-embedded leverage make participants fragile because violent price moves can force losses before a long-term thesis is resolved.
- Holding dry powder, segregating funds, and intentionally taking some money off the table can preserve the ability to participate after a crash.
- Experimentation and fraud expand together during a boom, so enthusiasm for decentralized systems does not remove the need for buyer skepticism.
- Calling a market a bubble does not reveal when it will end; believers in the underlying technology may be better served by resilient long-term participation than by complete avoidance.

## Key Quotes
> "Retain your critical faculties." - Wenger's first lesson for resisting a bubble's self-reinforcing internal logic.

> "Do not, under any circumstance, borrow to invest in a bubble." - the essay's strongest financial guardrail.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction was found. The essay complements [[TechnologyEnablerStack]] by arguing that speculative capital can help build enabling infrastructure, while that page explains why later products can succeed after an earlier boom fails.
- The claim that bubbles routinely finance valuable innovation is historical interpretation, not demonstrated here through comparative investment, infrastructure, or welfare data.
- "Actual adoption" is not operationally defined, and adoption alone does not establish retention, utility, decentralization, safety, or economic sustainability.
- Taking money off the table and holding dry powder can reduce ruin risk but do not identify valuation, timing, allocation, custody, or counterparty risk.
- The decentralized-internet thesis, scam prevalence, and cryptocurrency-market conditions are historically situated in May 2017 and should not be treated as current measurements or outcomes.
