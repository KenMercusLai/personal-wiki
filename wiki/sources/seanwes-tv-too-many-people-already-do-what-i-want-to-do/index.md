## Summary

This source addresses the discouragement that comes from wanting to create in a field where many visible people already seem to be doing the same thing. It argues that feeds distort perception because they surface active creators and high-quality work while hiding the much larger population that only consumes.

The article reframes competition as market validation. A crowded space can show that demand already exists, that audiences have been educated by other creators, and that a new entrant can improve one part of an existing solution rather than needing a wholly original category.

It also argues that audience size should not be treated as the moment when work starts to matter. A creator with a small number of followers or subscribers can still influence people directly, build one-on-one relationships, and turn early supporters into ambassadors for later growth.

The source rejects originality as the main test of whether someone should publish. Instead, it says people can reuse strong ideas through their own experience, style, perspective, and voice, allowing a familiar message to reach a different audience or resonate differently with the same one.

Finally, the article treats the expanding Internet audience and the imbalance between consumption and production as reasons to start now. Its practical conclusion is that there is still room for new creators because most people do not consistently put their work into the world.

## Key Points

- A creator's feed overrepresents the best visible producers and underrepresents the larger non-producing population.
- Existing competitors can be evidence that a market is real and already primed.
- The smallest current audience may matter because early relationships are deeper and more personal.
- Distinct voice, experience, and perspective can make a familiar idea newly useful.
- Starting is still worthwhile because most people consume far more than they create.
