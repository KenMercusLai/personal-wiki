## Summary
In this first-person [[StartupGrind]] essay, a marketing director recounts relocating for an anonymized Silicon Valley startup after its founders promised a large budget, salary, bonus, equity, and severance, only to encounter role confusion, repeated unpaid wages, alleged forged transfer confirmations, and dismissal after encouraging wage claims. The account extends [[StartupJobDiligence]] beyond pitch quality and runway questions: candidates and employees should verify financial and payroll claims, treat boundary-testing and founder self-promotion as evidence, stop increasing exposure when wages fail, and preserve records for official remedies.

## Key Claims
- The author reports that a rushed interview and relocation process, unusually generous terms, work requests during a pre-employment vacation, and a “hire fast and fire fast” posture were early warnings that speed and promises were outrunning operational discipline.
- Founders emphasized their biographies and connections more than the product, could not agree on core selling points, lacked differentiation or traction, and pursued investors despite having represented the company as already well funded.
- Company spending and hiring reportedly expanded before product-market evidence, while payroll moved from a provider to cashier's checks, pay stubs and bonuses went missing, and founders began borrowing substantial sums from employees.
- The author says employees then received repeated explanations for missing payroll and individualized transfer confirmations that matched an online template in dates, account digits, and timestamps; no money arrived.
- H-1B-sponsored employees were especially exposed because unpaid wages were coupled with dependence on the startup for immigration sponsorship and continued trust in a cofounder.
- After filing a California wage claim, the author preserved contracts, emails, payment promises, work records, task-board screenshots, and transfer documents, then alleged she was fired in retaliation for encouraging coworkers to file claims.
- The practical lesson is to distinguish ordinary startup uncertainty from deception: verify claims, document commitments, protect personal runway, and use official wage or retaliation channels when an employer stops paying.

## Key Quotes
> “hire fast and fire fast” - the CTO's recruiting posture, presented by the author as an early warning about employment stability.

> “There’s a huge moral and legal difference between buying time with lies versus calculated fraud.” - the author's distinction between startup cash stress and deliberate deception.
