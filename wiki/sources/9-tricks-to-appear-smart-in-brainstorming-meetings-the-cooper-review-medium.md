## Summary
Sarah Cooper's satirical article presents brainstorming meetings as corporate theater where a person can appear creative, strategic, and decisive without contributing useful ideas. The piece mocks workplace status signals such as pseudo-depth, jargon, executive name-dropping, meta-framing, performative quirkiness, and premature decisiveness. Its simple line illustrations reinforce the satire by showing meeting participants using gestures, speech bubbles, and faux-authoritative poses to dramatize empty participation.

## Key Claims
- Brainstorming meetings can reward visible performance of creativity more than actual idea quality.
- Helpful gestures, sketching, simple analogies, and meta-questions can be used as low-effort status signals in group settings.
- Corporate jargon about disruption, roadmaps, frameworks, platforms, and models can make a speaker sound strategic while avoiding concrete substance.
- Referencing the CEO or declaring "Ship it!" lets a powerless participant borrow executive or decision-making authority.
- The article's illustrations are evidence-bearing examples: they show the water-and-snack escape, meaningless drawing, right-question posture, CEO-adjacent talk, framework talk, and "Ship it!" pose as recurring meeting-performance tropes.
