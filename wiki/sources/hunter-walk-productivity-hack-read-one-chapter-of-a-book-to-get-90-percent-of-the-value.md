## Summary
[[HunterWalk]] jokingly proposes that readers of formulaic self-help, management, and broad social-analysis books can often capture most of the distinctive idea by locating the thesis chapter—usually chapter two—through the table of contents. The essay treats forewords and credential-setting openings as skippable and later examples as optional validation, extending [[FocusedReading]] and [[SpeedReadingMethod]] with an aggressively selective chapter-level heuristic rather than evidence for a literal 90% rule.

## Key Claims
- Some idea-driven nonfiction follows a predictable structure: endorsement, author credentials, a central thesis, then repeated examples or anecdotes.
- In that structure, the chapter that states the central idea may contain most of the book's distinctive value.
- The high-value chapter is often chapter two, but it may be chapter three or another chapter identifiable from the table of contents.
- Readers can save time by locating and reading the thesis chapter before deciding whether the supporting examples deserve attention.
- The “90%” figure is presented as a playful productivity claim, not as a measured result.

## Key Quotes
> “only read the second chapter” — Walk's intentionally blunt rule for formulaic idea-driven nonfiction.

## Connections
- [[HunterWalk]] - author of the selective-reading heuristic.
- [[FocusedReading]] - the table of contents is used to target the chapter most likely to contain the central idea.
- [[SpeedReadingMethod]] - chapter selection becomes a fast evaluation pass before any deeper reading.
- [[PersonalProductivity]] - the essay frames selective reading as conserving scarce time and attention.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction was found. The essay sharpens existing selective-reading advice but is much more aggressive than workflows that first map a whole book and deep-read several high-value sections.
- The proposed structure applies only to a stereotyped subset of idea-driven nonfiction, not fiction, technical reference, history, cumulative argument, or books whose value lies in evidence, method, style, or sustained development.
- The article supplies no measurements, book sample, comprehension test, retention evidence, or method for validating the claimed 90% share.
