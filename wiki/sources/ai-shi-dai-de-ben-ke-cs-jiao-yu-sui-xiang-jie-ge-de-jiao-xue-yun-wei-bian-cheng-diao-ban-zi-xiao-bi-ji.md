## Summary
This practitioner reflection argues that capable coding agents break the old equivalence between completed programming assignments and student understanding. It proposes that [[AIEraCSEducation]] should preserve fundamentals while shifting its center of gravity toward requirement analysis, architecture, verification, research taste, oral explanation, and accountable AI use. The article also notes that AI broadens [[ProgrammingLiteracy]] for non-CS students, but warns that fast output can coexist with ignorance of how a system works.

## Key Claims
- Traditional CS education builds knowledge through repeated engineering practice, but students can now submit passing implementations without writing or understanding the code.
- AI should be neither prohibited wholesale nor treated as a substitute for learning; curricula need a deliberate division of labor between foundational practice and AI-assisted work.
- Human leverage is moving from following a given plan toward turning ambiguous requirements into architecture, supervising implementation, verifying reliability and safety, choosing research directions, and protecting academic ethics.
- Homework completed outside supervision is becoming a weak discriminator of individual understanding, increasing the value of oral defense, live presentation, dialogue, and assessment of reasoning.
- AI-enabled exams raise equity problems because model quality, ecosystem compatibility, network access, local hardware, and institutional inference capacity can all affect performance.
- Possible assessment responses include a common AI service, complete AI-use records, tasks that explicitly evaluate AI collaboration, and more human interaction, but each adds cost, compatibility, or scalability constraints.
- AI can expand non-CS students' practical reach by letting them build software for their own fields, while CS students who delegate blindly may become bounded by the model's competence and lack transferable foundations.

## Key Quotes
> "作业已经不再能区分同学，不能代表同学对知识的掌握情况" - on why unsupervised assignments no longer measure individual mastery reliably.

> "人类会更多地变成一个鉴别器" - on the growing importance of judging plans, outputs, correctness, and quality.

> "不能完全禁止 AI，也不能完全依赖 AI" - the article's governing curriculum constraint.

## Contradictions
- The article reports similar teaching-assistant experiments across several courses but gives no course names, protocols, sample sizes, assignment types, model versions, or measured learning outcomes.
- Its claim that homework has "completely" become an AI giveaway overstates variation across supervised work, physical labs, personalized tasks, staged submissions, and courses with different constraints.
- Oral defenses and presentations may reveal understanding but require substantial staff time and can measure language confidence, anxiety, or presentation skill alongside technical mastery.
- A common institutional AI service could reduce tool inequality without eliminating differences in prior experience, hardware, accessibility, or familiarity with the selected ecosystem.
- The proposed architect/AI-implementer division is a forward-looking role model, not evidence that novices can acquire architectural judgment without substantial implementation practice.
