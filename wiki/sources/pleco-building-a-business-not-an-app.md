## Summary
[[BenThompson]] contrasts the weak economics reported for the paid RSS reader Unread with [[MikeLove]]'s long-running [[Pleco]] business. He argues that Pleco became the leading Chinese dictionary app by combining a specific customer need with licensed content, handwriting recognition, free distribution, paid add-ons, deliberate pricing, and cross-platform availability rather than relying on code quality or App Store luck alone. The account is a favorable single-company case without audited revenue, customer, retention, or cost data, so it demonstrates a plausible operating model rather than the typical prospects of independent developers.

## Key Claims
- Love launched Pleco for Palm in 2001 around Chinese-language learning, differentiating it through licensed Motorola handwriting recognition and an exclusive Pocket Oxford Chinese dictionary rather than code or interface design alone.
- Pleco entered iOS in December 2009 after competing Chinese dictionary apps were already present, but adopted Apple's newly available free-with-in-app-purchase model from launch.
- The free product included additional licensed dictionary content intended to outperform free CC-CEDICT alternatives, while paid dictionaries, stroke-order diagrams, fonts, document reading, and OCR provided several upgrade paths.
- Pleco retained relatively high Palm-era price points on iOS: the article reports launch bundles at $50, $100, and $150, followed by a $40 feature-only basic bundle whose lower price reportedly increased net revenue.
- Android generated about one-third of iOS revenue while requiring more than one-third of development time, but Love treated it as brand expansion that kept Pleco recommendable regardless of a learner's phone platform.
- Thompson argues that a durable independent app business depends on customer understanding, licensing and supplier relationships, pricing, business-model design, continuing investment, and niche ownership as well as development.
- Thompson recommends App Store trials and paid upgrades so quality can be evaluated before purchase and established developers can finance continued product investment.

## Key Quotes
> "To be an indie is to first and foremost be a businessperson." - Thompson's summary of the Pleco case.

> "Love's development skills are a means, not an end." - on treating software as one capability within a business.

## Contradictions
- The article rejects a universal claim that polished App Store apps are a lottery, but one favorable retrospective cannot establish base rates or eliminate the role of timing, platform policy, category size, or luck.
- Thompson calls free with in-app purchase the only viable monetization model for independent developers, yet the wiki contains paid-app and subscription cases with different outcomes; viability remains category-, cost-, and customer-dependent.
- The article reports bundle prices, relative Android revenue, and an increase in basic-bundle net revenue without absolute sales, margin, retention, refund, licensing-cost, or development-time data.
- Pleco's 2001 start, early handwriting-recognition advantage, exclusive publishing agreements, and accumulated reputation may be difficult for a new entrant to reproduce.
