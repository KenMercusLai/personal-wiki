## Summary
[[MichalTurjeman]] argues from designing [[TwelveUp]] without being an American-sports fan that a designer need not resemble the target audience, provided personal preferences are replaced by questions, personas, and explicit comparison of alternatives. Her account frames [[UserCenteredDesign]] as disciplined perspective-taking: understand the unfamiliar domain, separate "I would" from what intended users need, and test intuition by honestly listing the pros and cons of a second solution.

## Key Claims
- Domain unfamiliarity is manageable when designers openly ask basic questions until they understand the problem they are trying to solve.
- A designer's own reaction to an interface is not a substitute for the target user's needs, habits, or preferences.
- Personas can help restore the intended audience's perspective when a designer becomes attached to a favored solution.
- Intuition can generate an initial direction, but producing a second option and comparing their pros and cons makes the decision more explicit and challengeable.
- Designing outside one's own identity or interests can be productive when the product's purpose is compelling and the work remains grounded in users.

## Key Quotes
> "There’s you and there’s the user, and you are designing for the USER." — on separating personal preference from the audience's perspective.

> "Asking questions is the first step to understanding the problem you’re trying to solve." — on working responsibly in an unfamiliar domain.
