## Summary
This PMInsider article frames early [[ProductManagement]] as pre-greenlight persuasion: product managers must sell ideas internally, understand company KPIs, make ideas visual, and prioritize proposals before asking the organization to spend real development money. Its [[ProductIdeaPrioritization]] model scores ideas by KPI impact and build difficulty, then buckets them into growth, activation, engagement, reactivation, and revenue. The repeated local images are decorative product-idea illustrations and do not add independent evidence.

## Key Claims
- A product manager needs to sell ideas to stakeholders with different personalities before a product is greenlit.
- Product ideas should connect directly to company KPIs and business goals; otherwise they may not deserve prioritization.
- PMs do not need to be production designers, but rough visual design and prototyping help busy decision makers understand and support an idea.
- Product ideas should be prioritized by expected KPI impact and difficulty to build.
- Growth ideas include referral and invite loops, including two-sided incentives such as [[Uber]]'s "Give $10, Get $10" pattern.
- Activation depends on identifying a product's magic moment and getting new users to it quickly through onboarding.
- Engagement, reactivation, and revenue ideas should increase existing-user activity, bring dormant users back through well-timed channels, or create money without harming the core user experience.

## Key Quotes
> "Think of yourself as an entrepreneur who is trying to get funding for your ideas." - on internal product persuasion.

> "Your scoreboard should be your organizational KPI's" - on aligning product ideas with company strategy.

> "Busy decision makers respond best to visuals." - on rough design as a PM communication tool.
