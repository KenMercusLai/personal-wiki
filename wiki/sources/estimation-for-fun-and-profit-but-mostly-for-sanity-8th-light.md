## Summary
[[BjornJohnson]] combines vertically sliced [[UserStories]] with [[PERTEstimation]] and simultaneous team estimates as a workflow for making large software projects more deliverable and uncertain schedules more discussable. A story should cross the layers needed for one user-visible outcome and carry acceptance criteria; its estimate should consider optimistic, realistic, and pessimistic cases, then invite independent team input that can reveal assumptions and low confidence. The article is practical guidance rather than measured evidence, and neither referenced image could be opened, so no visual evidence was used or retained.

## Key Claims
- Horizontal slices organized around Model, Template, or View can complete a technical layer without producing anything a user can evaluate.
- A vertical slice narrows work to one logical user interaction that crosses the necessary layers and can be delivered with acceptance criteria.
- Software estimates should be treated as rough timelines and expressions of confidence rather than concrete commitments, especially for unfamiliar features.
- [[PERTEstimation]] requires optimistic, realistic, and pessimistic inputs so teams consider favorable, ordinary, and adverse conditions explicitly.
- Pessimistic scenarios can reveal blockers and risks that a single idealized estimate misses.
- In “Flying Fingers,” team members choose estimates individually and reveal them simultaneously so one early or loud opinion is less likely to dominate.
- Divergent estimates are useful prompts for discussing hidden assumptions and uncertainty, although the article suggests averaging them after discussion.

## Key Quotes
> "there are too many moving parts and unknowns" - on why estimates should not be treated as concrete commitments.

> "everyone shows their numbers at the same time" - on reducing domination during team estimation.
