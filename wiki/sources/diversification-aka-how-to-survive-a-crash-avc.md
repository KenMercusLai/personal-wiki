## Summary
[[FredWilson]] uses his family's roughly 90% net-worth loss after the dot-com bubble to argue that crash protection must be arranged before a market breaks. Two real-estate holdings preserved the remaining wealth when concentrated venture-capital and Internet-stock exposure collapsed, some public companies went to zero, and the instinct to wait for recovery delayed selling. He proposes a personal target of roughly equal cash, blue-chip-stock, low-leverage income-producing-real-estate, and high-risk buckets while presenting the weights and crypto exposure as personal judgments rather than a universal plan.

## Key Claims
- Diversification protects capital before a crash; attempting to sell only after prices break is vulnerable to denial, continued declines, illiquidity, and bankruptcy.
- Wilson's family lost roughly 90% of its net worth after concentrating wealth created from 1996 to 2000 in venture capital and Internet stocks.
- Real estate purchased by selling Yahoo and other shares before the crash represented about 10% of pre-crash net worth and became the family's remaining net worth afterward.
- Wilson's desired long-run allocation is roughly 25% each in cash-like instruments, blue-chip stocks, low- or unleveraged income-producing real estate, and a high-risk bucket containing venture capital and cryptocurrency.
- Although Wilson had previously suggested crypto allocations as high as 10–20% for young people or strong believers, he estimated his own direct and fund-mediated exposure at about 5%.
- Investors with crypto-concentrated fortunes should consider realizing gains, paying taxes, and reallocating outside the sector rather than treating conviction as a substitute for capital preservation.

## Key Quotes
> "Selling long before it blows up and diversifying your assets is a much better way." - Wilson's core crash-survival lesson.

> "Preservation of capital is about diversification" - advice to investors whose crypto gains dominate their net worth.

## Contradictions
- Qualifies [[PortfolioConcentration]]: concentrated exposure may accompany active professional investing, but Wilson's household experience shows that extending the same concentration across net worth can create ruin-level personal risk.
- Complements [[TechnologyBubbles]] by replacing crash-timing confidence with ex-ante diversification; unlike the Wenger source, this essay does not argue that bubbles themselves finance useful infrastructure.
- The 25/25/25/25 target and roughly 5% crypto exposure are Wilson's 2017 personal judgments, not an empirically tested or universally suitable allocation.
