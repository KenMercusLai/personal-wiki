## Summary
[[JoeLonsdale]] argues for timing senior startup hires around stage, not prestige: very early technology startups need a small, intense, product-building culture supported by advisors, while scaling companies need experienced operators once repeatable business processes exist. The article treats [[ProductMarketFit]] and revenue traction as the boundary where CEOs should add senior sales, HR, engineering, marketing, finance, strategy, and other leaders to reduce direct-report overload and scale the business machine. The three inspected local images are alternate crops of a purple-and-white bird illustration and add no separate factual evidence beyond article presentation.

## Key Claims
- Early startups should prioritize brilliant, hard-working builders who fit the tight founding culture, move quickly, and learn by building rather than relying on veteran operators.
- Advisors, strategists, early users, and product thinkers can support an early founding team without becoming full-time management too soon.
- Senior executives become valuable when the startup needs repeatable business processes that have been run many times elsewhere.
- Post-Series A, revenue generation, established product direction, and market clarity are common signals that a company may need a C-suite or several experienced leaders.
- Founders can make the opposite mistake after early success by dismissing experienced business people exactly when sales, HR, engineering management, marketing, finance, or strategy expertise becomes necessary.
- Bringing veteran operators too early can slow a culture of innovation, while hiring them too late can leave scaling processes underbuilt.
- Investors should evaluate early technology culture, metrics, and strategy rather than using "adult" executive presence as a proxy for safety.

## Key Quotes
> "Innovation is not a business process." - on why early startups should not over-import mature-company operators.

> "Bring in the adults!" - closing phrase for the moment after the company has moved from product invention into scaling.
