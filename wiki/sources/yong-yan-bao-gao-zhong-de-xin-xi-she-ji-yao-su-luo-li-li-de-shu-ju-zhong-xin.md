## Summary
RORIRI argues that UX research reports are not neutral dumps of observations but acts of [[UXResearchInformationDesign]]: the researcher must connect methods, data layers, and contradictions into an explanatory structure. Using a [[Jamovi]] usability study, the article shows how [[MixedMethodUXResearch]] can reconcile apparently conflicting questionnaire, behavior, PURE walkthrough, and [[HeuristicEvaluation]] results by placing design elements, [[CognitiveLoadInUXResearch]], subjective feeling, and behavior into one causal chain.

## Key Claims
- UX data does not speak for itself; reports become useful only when researchers design relationships among observations, measures, methods, and conclusions.
- [[MixedMethodUXResearch]] works best when each method occupies a clear explanatory layer rather than appearing as a pile of disconnected findings.
- The article's four-layer chain is design elements, cognitive load, subjective feeling, and observed behavior; the order can change when the research question changes.
- User and expert perspectives are complementary: users reveal lived behavior and confusion, while experts can infer design-principle violations and hidden cognitive burdens.
- The [[Jamovi]] case shows that subjective ease, high observed error, PURE predictions, and heuristic problems can look contradictory until invisible cognitive failure is treated as the mediating layer.
- The embedded Sankey-style chart shows observed impact flowing through PURE task phases to expert ratings: many user errors entered the T2A Trap phase, while much of the corresponding expert structure mapped to Complete with Ease.

## Key Quotes
> "数据不会自己说话" - the article's compact statement of why research reporting needs interpretation.

> "四层模型不是发明，是发现" - the author frames the model as emerging from the dataset rather than as a reusable template.
