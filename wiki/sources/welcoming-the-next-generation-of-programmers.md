## Summary
[[ArminRonacher]] argues that people who create programs with [[ChatGPT]] or coding agents should be treated as programmers and welcomed into established communities rather than dismissed as inauthentic. He predicts that AI will expand participation in programming, while warning that an AI-mediated first step may lack the human guide, community awareness, mentorship, and engineering lessons that earlier entrants often received through spaces such as the [[Python]] community.

## Key Claims
- Creating a program, whether by hand or through an agent, is sufficient grounds to identify someone as a programmer.
- [[VibeCoding]] and agentic tools can lower the entry threshold enough for people without a prior programmer identity to automate real problems.
- The author expects AI to increase rather than reduce the number of programmers, although the article supplies no participation data or forecast model.
- Established communities should welcome AI-first newcomers and create visible on-ramps into conferences, peer relationships, and [[EngineeringMentorship]].
- An AI can be the proximate guide into programming without supplying the human connection, communal norms, and professional learning that a person or community once provided.
- The [[Python]] community may benefit because generated solutions often use Python, but it also has to reach newcomers who may not know that programming communities exist.
- The author expects many vibe coders to move toward more traditional software engineering, making community support and engineering education more important than gatekeeping.

## Key Quotes
> "if you create a program, by hand or with the aid of an agent, you are a programmer" - on defining programmers by practice rather than tool choice.

> "We need to turn a solitary interaction with an AI into a shared journey with a community" - on the missing social on-ramp.

## Connections
- [[ArminRonacher]] - author reflecting on the Python community and AI-mediated entry into programming.
- [[Python]] - community and language context for the proposed newcomer on-ramp.
- [[ChatGPT]] - example of an AI system that can become a person's first guide into programming.
- [[VibeCoding]] - entry mode in which a person delegates implementation heavily to a machine.
- [[TechCommunityParticipation]] - conferences and communities can turn solitary tool use into social learning and belonging.
- [[EngineeringMentorship]] - experienced programmers can transmit engineering judgment that initial AI use may not provide.
- [[AICodingPractice]] - the hoped-for transition from first generated programs toward more deliberate engineering practice.
- [[PublicKnowledgeCommons]] - related social infrastructure that AI-mediated programming can use without automatically replenishing.

## Contradictions
- The source counters definitions of [[VibeCoding]] that treat non-programmer participation mainly as a production risk: Ronacher accepts quality concerns but argues that exclusion would forfeit a major learning and community opportunity.
- The forecast that AI will create more programmers and the claim that AI often selects Python are first-person observations without adoption counts, language-frequency data, longitudinal learning outcomes, or evidence that AI-first entrants move into traditional engineering.
- Welcoming people into a community does not by itself address generated-code security, maintenance, verification, tool lock-in, unequal access, or the capacity cost of mentoring a larger beginner population.
