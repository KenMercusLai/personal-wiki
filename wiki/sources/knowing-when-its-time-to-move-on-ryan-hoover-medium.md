## Summary
[[RyanHoover]] explains why he chose to leave [[PlayHaven]] despite its recovery from near-failure to reported growth, substantial revenue, and an 85-person team. His first-person account frames [[StrategicJobMobility]] around declining domain interest, a role that no longer supplied the broad challenges he valued, and a desire to accelerate learning, while acknowledging that the move was emotionally difficult and economically counterintuitive.

## Key Claims
- A successful company can cease to be the right workplace for an individual even when compensation, colleagues, growth, and belief in the company's vision remain strong.
- Hoover treats challenge, discomfort, and learning rate as primary career returns and says PlayHaven's early constrained rebuild forced rapid product, support, marketing, debugging, and company-scaling development.
- Company growth shifted his generalist product role toward narrower responsibilities and transferred some of the design work he enjoyed to a specialist.
- A two-week vacation did not restore his declining interest in gaming, which he interpreted as a fit problem rather than temporary fatigue.
- Building a product for a problem one personally experiences may improve user understanding and persistence, but the article offers this as practitioner reasoning rather than comparative evidence.
- Hoover evaluates new opportunities by asking whether he truly wants the work, feels strong passion for the product, market, and vision, and expects to be challenged and grow.

## Key Quotes
> "Will I be challenged, learn, and grow to my full potential?" - [[RyanHoover]]'s final test for a new opportunity.

## Connections
- [[RyanHoover]] - author and departing PlayHaven product manager reflecting on his own career decision.
- [[PlayHaven]] - startup whose turnaround and organizational growth created both Hoover's learning opportunity and the later specialization of his role.
- [[StrategicJobMobility]] - leaving is framed as a conditional fit and learning decision rather than a response to company failure or a fixed tenure rule.
- [[CareerPlanning]] - Hoover compares opportunities through desired work, passion, challenge, and growth.
- [[WorkplaceLearning]] - the early turnaround supplied cross-functional, high-responsibility learning under severe constraints.
- [[ProductMarketFit]] - PlayHaven's rebuild, first major client, customer growth, and reported revenue form the company context rather than proof that the role remained personally suitable.

## Contradictions
- The case qualifies the idea that people should leave mainly when an employer is failing: Hoover left after a successful turnaround, while preserving confidence in the company and affection for the team.
- The article's emphasis on passion and building for oneself does not establish that passion is necessary for competence, that founder-user identity reliably produces good products, or that workers with financial, health, immigration, or caregiving constraints should make the same choice.
