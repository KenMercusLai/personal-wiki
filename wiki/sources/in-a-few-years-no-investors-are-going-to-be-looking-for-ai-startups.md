## Summary
This 2017 essay predicts that investors would soon stop treating AI as a distinct startup category because state-of-the-art AI would become an assumed part of important software, much as databases, graphical clients, web front ends, cloud infrastructure, and mobile support had before it. The argument connects [[TechnologyNormalization]] to [[DifferentiationStrategy]]: once a capability becomes baseline infrastructure, founders must distinguish the customer problem, business, or experience rather than advertise the enabling technique. Later wiki sources qualify the forecast's timing because AI remained both an explicit marketing label and an investment theme well beyond the proposed two-year horizon.

## Key Claims
- Startups were already adopting AI language broadly enough that the label risked becoming weak evidence of substantive differentiation.
- Investors would increasingly assume that software companies use the best available AI techniques where relevant rather than fund them merely for being "AI-powered."
- Databases, rich desktop clients, web interfaces, cloud-native systems, and mobile apps are presented as precedents for once-distinctive technologies becoming ordinary software expectations.
- The proposed adoption path runs from enthusiast experimentation and research attention through conferences, bootcamps, MOOCs, IDE support, and standard computer-science curricula.
- Platform shifts temporarily inflate salaries, valuations, and pitch-deck attention before the underlying technique becomes routine.
- As AI becomes a baseline capability, founders must find a different basis for [[DifferentiationStrategy]].

## Key Quotes
> "software without AI will be unthinkable." - the essay's strongest statement of the normalization thesis.

> "All those things are just assumed." - on databases, cloud-native software, and mobile support no longer functioning as startup categories.

## Connections
- [[TechnologyNormalization]] - central process by which a novel technical capability becomes an expected software baseline.
- [[DifferentiationStrategy]] - founders need a customer-visible basis for distinction after AI implementation becomes assumed.
- [[AIMarketingHype]] - the essay observes startups dressing themselves in AI language before predicting that the label will lose signaling value.
- [[AIInvestmentTheme]] - the forecast directly qualifies the idea that AI will remain a durable, explicit investment category.
- [[ProductCommoditization]] - adjacent pressure in which technical capabilities become easier to obtain and less defensible by themselves.

## Contradictions
- The stated two-year forecast was not borne out by the wiki's later evidence: [[ces-2019-a-show-report-learning-by-shipping]] still found AI used widely as an explicit product label, and [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] still framed AI as a structural investment theme in 2025.
- The source displays both a June 27, 2017 article date and an imported Chinese publication date of October 9, 2017; this note uses the date attached to the article itself.
