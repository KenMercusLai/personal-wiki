## Summary
[[DanielKarlsson]] argues that early release is an epistemic tool: paper mockups, customer conversations, and proofs of concept expose both false certainty and [[UnknownUnknowns]] before a team commits heavily to the wrong product. The practical obstacle is often not knowing how to test, but accepting criticism, imperfection, and possible failure early enough for reality to redirect the work.

## Key Claims
- Product teams are especially vulnerable to assumptions they treat as settled facts, because those beliefs become foundations rather than questions.
- Non-experts also face unknown unknowns: missing domain knowledge can hide requirements and failure modes that research alone may not reveal.
- Releasing before a complete product exists can test willingness to pay and problem understanding through conversations, paper mockups, or a small proof of concept.
- Delaying exposure to users can turn every additional build hour into waste if a later discovery invalidates the product's premise.
- Fear of criticism and failure encourages perfectionism and makes an unreleased product feel emotionally safe.
- Early-release techniques are often already familiar; repeated practice is needed to change the avoidance mindset.

## Key Quotes
> "But what is even harder emotionally, and financially, than not releasing is discovering you built the wrong thing." - on the cost of postponing reality checks.

> "No, it's not the techniques you're lacking. It's mindset." - on emotional avoidance as the central barrier.
