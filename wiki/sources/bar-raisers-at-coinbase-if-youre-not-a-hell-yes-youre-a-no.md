## Summary
[[BrianArmstrong]] describes how [[Coinbase]] tried to prevent hiring quality from falling during hypergrowth by screening for both skill and values and placing a trained bar raiser with veto power on every hiring panel. The process deliberately prefers occasionally rejecting a good candidate to accepting a bad one, while asking all interviewers to judge whether a candidate would raise the company's average capability and energy. Four local images were inspected: three duplicate or resized versions of a decorative "Raise the bar with every hire" poster and one tiny prose-repeated illustration; none supplied independent evidence, so no image was retained.

## Key Claims
- Hypergrowth creates pressure to fill roles quickly while newer interviewers may not yet understand the company's culture or hiring standard.
- Coinbase requires candidates to meet both role-skill and company-values criteria; strength on only one dimension is insufficient.
- A trained bar raiser sits on every hiring panel with veto power, preventing the hiring manager from making the decision unilaterally.
- The bar raiser functions as an independent check and balance intended to protect the company-wide standard from local staffing pressure.
- Coinbase explicitly prefers false negatives to false positives: the process may reject some good candidates to reduce the risk of a damaging hire.
- Interviewers are coached to test whether a candidate creates energy, teaches them something, is clearly stronger in at least one area, can add value quickly, and would raise the organizational average.
- Any "no" or "maybe" on those prompts is rounded down to rejection, although every employee—not only the trained bar raiser—is expected to take responsibility for hiring quality.

## Key Quotes
> "If you're not a hell yes, you're a no." - Coinbase's decision rule for hiring panels.

> "Round down to a no." - instruction for uncertain interview judgments.
