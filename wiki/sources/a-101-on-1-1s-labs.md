## Summary
This [[Spotify]] practitioner account argues that recurring manager-engineer one-on-ones become more useful when both participants share a concrete purpose instead of relying on unstructured conversation. After an A3-guided review, the author used five goals—trust, a private feedback channel, career development, team and happiness reflection, and product-direction discussion—to focus conversations and surface previously hidden disagreements, interpersonal problems, and boredom. The reported improvement is immediate but anecdotal, and the article leaves facilitation, confidentiality, escalation, and outcome measurement unresolved.

## Key Claims
- Relationship building alone was too vague to help the author or senior engineers understand what a useful one-on-one should accomplish.
- Open-ended questions and silence did not automatically produce candid discussion, while conventional goal setting failed to engage much of the team.
- An A3 process helped the author and two colleagues clarify the present problem and agree on a desired future state for one-on-ones.
- The resulting meetings had five purposes: trust, private discussion and feedback, career planning, team and happiness reflection, and influence over product direction.
- A purpose-based agenda reportedly surfaced a product-owner disagreement, interpersonal conflict absent from retrospectives, and an engineer's boredom within the first week.
- Shared goals gave the manager prompts for focused discussion and a stopping rule once the relevant areas and spontaneous topics were exhausted.
- One-on-ones remain a context-dependent conversational practice; naming goals does not by itself determine how to discuss them well.

## Key Quotes
> "You just talk to them for half an hour." - the vague guidance that prompted the author's search for a clearer model

> "Working with 1:1s is about people, and as such the practice itself still seems to be more an art than a science." - qualification on the proposed framework

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction with existing wiki pages. The source qualifies the assumption that enough unstructured space will naturally elicit concerns: that approach produced silence in this team, while explicit purposes produced more focused discussion.
- The evidence is one manager's short first-person trial at Spotify, with no employee accounts, comparison group, sustained outcomes, or measures of performance, retention, promotion, trust, or well-being.
