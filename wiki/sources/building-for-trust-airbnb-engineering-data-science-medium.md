## Summary
[[RileyNewman]] and [[JuddAntin]] explain how [[Airbnb]] designs for trust between guests and hosts who would otherwise face stranger-danger bias. The article distinguishes platform confidence from interpersonal trust: profiles, payments, guarantees, customer support, and reputation systems create a scaffold, but hosts and guests still enact trust through evidence, reviews, and repeated positive interactions. The inspected images are mostly illustrative, while the trip-adjacency visualization adds evidence that Airbnb travel connects origin and destination countries across a broad matrix rather than only within local or same-country routes.

## Key Claims
- [[Airbnb]] treats trust as core marketplace infrastructure because democratized hospitality asks people to host or stay with strangers.
- Identity products such as profiles, photos, descriptions, social links, and trip feedback reduce anonymity and help new guests decide whether to book.
- Platform confidence is a scaffold for [[MarketplaceTrust]]: payments, delayed host payouts, customer support, and host guarantees reduce uncertainty without replacing interpersonal trust.
- Retention is used as an imperfect trust proxy; the article says negative guest experiences can reduce retention substantially, while customer support intervention nearly cancels that loss.
- [[CommunityReputationSystems]] are central marketplace data products: voluntary reviews help future participants, hosts with at least one review are much more likely to get bookings, and double-blind reviews increased review rates and negative-review disclosure.
- [[Homophily]] affects traveler trust decisions, but the article reports that enough positive reputation information can counteract similarity bias.
- Trust-rich marketplace communities can produce broader effects, including cross-cultural stays, host income support, and disaster response capacity.

## Key Quotes
> "confidence is the scaffold" - on platform mechanisms making interpersonal trust easier without creating it by themselves.

> "encapsulated interest" - Russell Hardin's definition of trust used by the article.

> "reputation system" - Airbnb's term for the review data product that makes trust evidence visible.
