## Summary
[[JonGrall]] explains the planned closure of [[JustLanded]], a paid iOS airport-pickup app that had reached hundreds of thousands of users, received strong reviews, and been featured by [[Apple]]. He attributes the decision not to one failure but to the interaction of costly and inaccurate flight-data suppliers, replacement costs after supporting services changed or disappeared, heavy professional use under a pay-once model, falling app prices and crowded discovery, and limited development time. The five-month notice and continued service for existing users frame shutdown as a stewardship decision intended to avoid a later abrupt failure.

## Key Claims
- [[ExternalServiceDependency]] can make a successful product operationally fragile when critical data comes from a concentrated supplier market with limited quality, reliability, feature, and pricing pressure.
- Just Landed depended on commercial flight-data intermediaries because contracting with individual airlines and normalizing fragmented formats was impractical for a small team.
- Costs rose as StackMob, Urban Airship, Bing Maps, traffic, and mapping arrangements disappeared, restructured, or became more expensive and restrictive.
- [[UnitEconomics]] broke across user segments: professional drivers, airline workers, and travel companies reportedly consumed 100–500 times as much flight data as casual users while the one-time purchase produced no corresponding recurring revenue.
- [[MobileAppStoreEconomics]] can squeeze paid independent software through crowded discovery, falling prices, high expectations, and continuing service costs even when an app has meaningful usage and favorable reviews.
- Limited resources turned those structural pressures into an opportunity-cost decision: Grall judged that redesign, repricing, localization, and broader device or country support were unlikely to justify a Just Landed 2.0.
- [[ProductLifecycleTrust]] can be protected during a warranted closure through advance notice, continued service for existing users, a definite end date, explanation, and refund guidance.

## Key Quotes
> “The odds of financial success for Just Landed 2.0 are slim to none.” — Grall's opportunity-cost judgment

> “No app lives forever.” — the retrospective boundary placed on product continuity

## Contradictions
- The article qualifies success narratives that treat large usage, good reviews, press coverage, or App Store featuring as sufficient evidence of a sustainable software business.
- The shutdown account is a founder-authored retrospective without audited revenue, cost, cohort, complaint, supplier, or alternative-model data. Its claims about a flight-data duopoly, industry-wide innovation, app-price decline, piracy, and the future of independent development are not independently established.
- Heavy professional users may have supported a subscription, usage-based plan, or enterprise tier, but the article does not report willingness-to-pay tests or quantify migration, rewrite, localization, and support options.
- All four local images were opened. Three are duplicate or thumbnail variants of Just Landed airplane branding, and the fourth is a 44-by-60-pixel phone/app promotional image; they add no recoverable evidence beyond the prose and were omitted.
