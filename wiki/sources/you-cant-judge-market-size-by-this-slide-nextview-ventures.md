## Summary
NextView Ventures argues that a large market-size number is among the least informative pitch-deck claims because experienced investors have seen the same headline repeatedly and will investigate the opportunity themselves. Founders create more confidence by showing assumptions, sources, and calculations, while distinguishing an existing measurable market from an adjacent expansion opportunity or a genuinely created market.

## Key Claims
- A headline market-size slide can provoke skepticism rather than excitement when it presents a large number without a derivation.
- [[MarketSizing]] should classify the opportunity before estimating it: an existing market can be measured from public data, an expanding market begins with an established segment and reaches adjacent customers, and a created market unlocks latent value through a new product.
- Top-down, bottom-up, or blended analysis can estimate an existing market, but the source prefers bottom-up reasoning.
- Expansion and market creation are harder to forecast than an established category because future customers and adjacent use cases are not yet fully observable.
- A startup may combine existing, expanding, and created-market characteristics rather than fit exactly one type.
- Founders should show sources, assumptions, and calculations because the reasoning demonstrates how carefully they have examined the customers they intend to reach.
- Investors will conduct their own diligence, so a market-size slide is better treated as an auditable argument than as proof by authority.

## Key Quotes
> "Show your work." - the source's central instruction for presenting market size.

> "Every VC has seen this slide a million times." - on why an unsupported large number does not create surprise.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction was found. The article sharpens the wiki's existing statement that early-stage valuation depends partly on estimated market size by explaining why the estimate's construction matters more than its headline magnitude.
- The three market types are a practitioner framework rather than a demonstrated exhaustive taxonomy, and the source supplies no worked calculation or outcome data showing that its preferred presentation predicts startup performance.
