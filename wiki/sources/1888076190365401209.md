## Summary
This Chinese-language social-media summary presents hunger as the output of complex, redundant neuroendocrine pathways that evolved to preserve energy balance under uncertain food supply. It distinguishes food-deprivation-driven homeostatic hunger from hedonic hunger without an immediate caloric need, and argues that agriculture and modern access to affordable, palatable, energy-dense food shifted the consequences of a system biased toward positive energy balance. The post also notes possible gut-microbiome involvement and new treatment targets for obesity and anorexia nervosa, while acknowledging that the microbiome's exact role remains unresolved.

## Key Claims
- [[HungerPhysiology]] uses overlapping neuroendocrine, endocrine, metabolic, sensory, psychological, behavioral, cultural, and socioeconomic signals to regulate food intake and body weight.
- Homeostatic hunger is triggered by food scarcity or metabolic need, whereas hedonic hunger can arise without an urgent caloric deficit.
- Human appetite regulation evolved under unpredictable food supply and tends to permit excess intake and fat storage, which could aid survival under scarcity.
- Agriculture and the later abundance of affordable, energy-dense, palatable foods altered the environment acting on this older physiology and may allow hedonic drives to outweigh homeostatic need.
- Relative body-weight stability suggests that regulation integrates predictable expenditure such as basal metabolic rate with variable intake, food composition, thermic effect, and physical activity.
- Gut-microbiome composition and function may affect hunger circuits, but the post says their precise role in controlling intake is not established.
- Better mechanistic knowledge may reveal targets for regulating food intake in obesity and anorexia nervosa.
