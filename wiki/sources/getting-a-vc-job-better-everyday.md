## Summary
This first-person practitioner essay argues that an early-career venture-capital candidate is valuable chiefly when they can help a firm discover and access investments it would otherwise miss. It presents deep emerging-domain expertise and unusually strong founder access as the main differentiators, adds structured analyst sourcing as a training route, and recommends treating the job search itself as evidence of sourcing ability. The advice is practical but anecdotal, early-stage-oriented, and untested against hiring or investment outcomes.

## Key Claims
- An early-career investor candidate should demonstrate incremental deal-finding value, not only financial modeling or due-diligence competence.
- One route is credible expertise in an emerging market: a differentiated point of view, knowledge deeper than surface research, and continued public authority in the field.
- A second route is trusted founder access built through sustained ecosystem participation, memory for companies and people, relationship skill, and a strong reputation; visibility without authenticity can instead become a liability.
- Structured growth-stage sourcing teams can train junior investors through persistent outbound contact and long-term company tracking, but the work demands grit and a high tolerance for rejection.
- The recruiting process should resemble investment sourcing: map target firms, earn warm access, cultivate relationships over a long and uncertain cycle, and demonstrate judgment by surfacing relevant under-the-radar companies without indiscriminate introductions.
- Firm fit and the quality of observed deal flow matter because early investor judgment develops through apprenticeship and repeated exposure; recently raised funds may be more plausible hiring targets, though timing remains unpredictable.
- Candidates can build investing judgment through small personal investments or operating work at a high-growth company, but the source rejects taking an unwanted role purely as a credential and urges candidates to test whether venture capital actually suits them.

## Key Quotes
> “Is this person going to help me to invest in companies that I otherwise would not have invested in without him/her?” — the essay's proposed hiring question

> “Start doing the work.” — advice to demonstrate value by finding relevant companies before being hired

## Contradictions
- The essay values network reach and suggests elite-school analyst pipelines, yet these signals can reproduce the access and homophily problems that broader investor-development efforts seek to reduce.
- Its examples and recommendations are anecdotal and centered on early-stage US venture capital; it provides no hiring data, performance comparison, founder perspective, or evidence that domain visibility, social reach, personal speculation, or analyst sourcing predicts durable investor returns.
- The recommendation to practice through technology stocks or cryptocurrency does not reproduce private-market sourcing, illiquidity, governance, portfolio construction, or fiduciary responsibility, and it may expose candidates to concentrated financial risk.
