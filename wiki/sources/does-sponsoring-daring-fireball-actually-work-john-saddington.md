## Summary
[[JohnSaddington]] reports that a US$9,000 [[DaringFireball]] sponsorship for his Mac blogging app [[Desk]] produced nearly US$16,000 in first-week net proceeds, compared with just over US$2,100 in the preceding seven days. He argues that the larger return came from a [[DirectSponsorship]] halo: App Store rank, review requests, press testimonials, and Apple's Best of 2014 recognition extended the campaign beyond directly attributable sales. The second sponsorship was less successful, and the account stresses audience-product fit, timing, product quality, experimentation, and readiness to absorb follow-on attention.

## Key Claims
- Saddington estimated that a US$9,000 sponsorship needed about 300 additional app sales to break even, but evaluated the campaign over direct sales plus longer-term awareness rather than expecting all conversions on launch day.
- The first campaign reportedly produced nearly US$16,000 in net proceeds during its first week versus just over US$2,100 in the preceding seven days, recovering the sponsorship cost within that week on the author's accounting.
- Desk reportedly peaked at No. 4 in Mac App Store Productivity, No. 3 in Productivity sales, No. 16 overall, and No. 11 in overall sales after the campaign.
- The sponsorship prompted many review-copy requests and supplied press quotations for a redesigned landing page, showing how a paid placement can create secondary distribution and social proof.
- Saddington says he was unprepared for the response and wait-listed more than 30 prominent blogs because he lacked review codes, turning fulfillment capacity into a constraint on the campaign's halo.
- Apple subsequently named Desk among its Best Apps of 2014, but Saddington explicitly says the sponsorship's contribution to that outcome cannot be isolated.
- A second Daring Fireball sponsorship produced similar but smaller effects, so one strong campaign did not establish a repeatable return at the same magnitude.

## Key Quotes
> "Yes, DaringFireball worked for me." - Saddington's conclusion about the first campaign.

> "Audience Matters" - the first condition in his closing sponsorship advice.

## Contradictions
- The article calls the first campaign successful using a before-and-after comparison, not a controlled counterfactual; seasonal demand, existing momentum, App Store ranking feedback, later coverage, and Apple's award may explain part of the observed lift.
- "Net proceeds" and "profit" are used interchangeably without a full cost definition, so the reported US$16,000 and US$32,000 figures should not be treated as audited operating profit.
- The second campaign was materially weaker, qualifying any claim that the publication, placement format, or prior result guaranteed repeatable performance.
- Five unique remote images and one duplicate were referenced. The origin and CDN returned HTTP 403 during ingest, so rankings, testimonials, and award screenshots could not be independently inspected or retained.
