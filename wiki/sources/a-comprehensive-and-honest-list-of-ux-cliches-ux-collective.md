## Summary
UX Collective's satire catalogs familiar UX sayings and reframes them as social moves in product and design work: avoiding research constraints, defending design authority, ending arguments, delaying deadlines, or sounding strategic. The article is valuable less as literal UX advice than as a map of how design slogans can become substitutes for evidence, accountability, and contextual judgment.

## Key Claims
- UX slogans often compress legitimate design concerns into phrases that can be used rhetorically rather than analytically.
- User research, testing, accessibility, scrolling, onboarding, content, and process language can all become meeting ammunition when detached from actual evidence.
- The article satirizes designers' status anxieties, especially around having strategic influence, defending expertise, and navigating stakeholder pressure.
- The UI-vs-UX ketchup-bottle image illustrates how common analogies can simplify design distinctions until they become cliches themselves.
- The author explicitly frames the piece as humor, irony, and exaggeration meant to invite reflection.

## Key Quotes
> "You are not your user" - used as the opening example of a real research reminder that can become a stock phrase.

> "This is a satire article" - the closing disclaimer frames the list as humorous critique rather than literal advice.
