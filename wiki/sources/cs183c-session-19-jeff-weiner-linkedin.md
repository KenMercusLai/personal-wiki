## Summary
This CS183C session records [[JeffWeiner]] in conversation with [[ReidHoffman]] about scaling [[LinkedIn]] from a few hundred employees into a global organization. The discussion argues that scale requires finding the company's core, codifying mission and culture, repeating communication, moving leaders toward coaching, practicing compassionate management, improving decision speed and quality, and preserving product-led company direction. It also introduces LinkedIn's [[EconomicGraph]] vision as an operational extension of its mission to connect knowledge workers and create economic opportunity.

## Key Claims
- [[LinkedIn]] needed a codified mission, vision, values, and objectives because informal language no longer worked once the company moved from village scale toward nation scale.
- [[StartupCulture]] is the organization's collective personality, but it becomes credible only when leaders model values and use them in hiring, management, rewards, and evaluation.
- [[CEOScalingRole]] shifts from problem solving toward coaching people to solve problems and coaching leaders who can coach others.
- [[CompassionateManagement]] is presented as a scale mechanism because it reduces conflict, builds trust, improves decisions, and treats hard personnel transitions as a form of care when someone is failing in a role.
- [[ScalingCommunication]] at LinkedIn relied on transparent every-other-week all-hands meetings broadcast across offices, with an ownership norm that employees say "we" rather than treating "the company" as someone else.
- [[ProductLeadership]] matters for internet companies because product is the primary value-creation surface and many great internet CEOs remain product people.
- The [[EconomicGraph]] vision would represent workers, companies, jobs, skills, educational institutions, and publishing activity so LinkedIn could connect opportunity, talent, and learning at global scale.

## Key Quotes
> "Our mission at LinkedIn is to connect the world's knowledge workers." - Weiner defining LinkedIn's operational mission.

> "The real job of the goalie is to win the game." - Hoffman illustrating global rather than local optimization.

> "The strongest people I know are the most compassionate." - Weiner arguing that compassion is not a weak operating trait.
