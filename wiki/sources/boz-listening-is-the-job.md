## Summary
[[AndrewBosworth]] argues that acquiring the information needed to do one's work is an individual responsibility rather than a service colleagues can be expected to provide automatically. His proposed [[WorkplaceInformationSeeking]] system combines deliberate channel selection, aggressive pruning, feedback to communicators, dependency mapping, surprise-driven follow-up, outbound context sharing, explicit callbacks, and non-defensive listening. The advice offers a practical response to [[InformationOverload]], but it is a first-person prescription from Facebook rather than evidence that exhaustive monitoring improves performance across roles or organizations.

## Key Claims
- Knowledge work depends on acquiring information from other people's observations and imagination, so workers should take responsibility for obtaining what they need.
- Information intake should be designed as a system with explicit channels, review frequencies, and depth rather than left to opportunistic consumption.
- A channel's expected value should justify its attention cost; low-signal groups and mailing lists should be removed or reviewed less often.
- Information users should give feedback when a channel could communicate more usefully instead of remaining passive consumers.
- Workers should periodically map the people and projects on which their work depends, identify missing information flows, and ask for channels to be established.
- Surprising information should trigger recorded follow-up because it may expose an outdated [[MentalModels|mental model]].
- Outbound communication improves inbound discovery by helping other people recognize which information is relevant to the recipient.
- Cross-team relationships should include explicit callbacks describing which future events or facts each side wants to hear about.
- Emotional or defensive reactions should prompt introspection rather than immediate rejection of an imperfectly expressed message.

## Key Quotes
> "The only way to ensure success is to take responsibility for getting the information you need." - Bosworth's ownership principle

> "Look beyond the rhetoric to find the kernel of valuable feedback" - on non-defensive listening

## Connections
- [[AndrewBosworth]] - author presenting listening as an active information-acquisition responsibility.
- [[WorkplaceInformationSeeking]] - central system of channel design, gap discovery, follow-up, callbacks, and receptive listening.
- [[InformationOverload]] - channel pruning and differentiated review cadence are responses to excess information and limited attention.
- [[MentalModels]] - surprise matters because it can reveal that a working model is incomplete or outdated.
- [[ScalingCommunication]] - outbound context and explicit callbacks help relevant information move across organizational boundaries.
- [[WorkplaceCollaboration]] - communication partners jointly improve channels and establish expectations for future updates.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found. The article's practice of reading every email and group post could intensify [[InformationOverload]], but Bosworth makes that workload a signal to reduce memberships, mailing lists, or scope rather than an unconditional demand for exhaustive consumption.
- The source describes one senior Facebook leader's practice. It does not show that the same monitoring depth, note-taking cadence, or channel access is feasible, safe, or beneficial across roles, accessibility needs, frontline work, confidential settings, or organizations with weak psychological safety.
