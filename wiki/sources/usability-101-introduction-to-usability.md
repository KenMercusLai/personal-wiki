## Summary
Jakob Nielsen defines usability as the ease with which people use an interface, decomposed into learnability, efficiency, memorability, errors, and satisfaction. He distinguishes usability from utility while arguing that both are required for usefulness, then presents individual task-based user testing and repeated iteration throughout design as the most practical route to improvement.

## Key Claims
- Usability is a quality attribute of an interface and a family of design methods, not merely visual polish.
- Learnability, efficiency, memorability, errors and recovery, and satisfaction provide five complementary ways to assess usability.
- A product is useful only when it combines utility—having needed features—with usability—making those features easy and pleasant to use.
- Representative users should attempt representative tasks individually while researchers observe without helping or directing attention.
- Small repeated studies, commonly around five participants for finding major interface problems, are more useful for iterative design than one large late-stage test.
- Usability work should begin before a redesign, continue through competitive research, field study and prototypes, and recur after implementation.
- Web usability affects abandonment and conversion, while intranet usability affects employee time and productivity.

## Key Quotes
> "If users cannot find the product, they cannot buy it either." — Nielsen's first law of ecommerce
