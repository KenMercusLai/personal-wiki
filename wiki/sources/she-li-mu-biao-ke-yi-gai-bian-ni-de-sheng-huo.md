## Summary
Piotr Wozniak argues that high goals can focus attention, improve strategy selection, and help learners attach value to knowledge. The article qualifies this claim by saying mature "natural achievers" may be able to follow curiosity without explicit goals, while younger students often need dreams, small decisions, and humane study habits to protect [[LearnDrive]]. Its central learning advice is to seed interest through small acts of exploratory reading and to handle messy domains through [[CreativeAbstraction]] rather than rote detail accumulation.

## Key Claims
- [[GoalSetting]] can focus attention, motivate thinking, and improve learning when goals roughly align with a learner's study plan.
- [[LearnDrive]] grows when goals give knowledge a valuation center, making study feel meaningful rather than merely compulsory.
- Explicit goals and self-discipline are not universal requirements for genius; mature experts may be able to rely on curiosity and accumulated knowledge.
- Young students are more vulnerable to institutional pressure, credential competition, and rote learning, so small non-painful decisions can help protect interest.
- In detail-heavy or unstable fields, [[CreativeAbstraction]] and general principles are more sustainable than memorizing every changing fact.
- Goals can support healthy habits beyond intellectual learning, even when the activity itself is not driven by curiosity.

## Key Quotes
> "目标孕育了对学习的热爱。" - on goals as a way to cultivate love of learning.

> "知识就像是自我推进的机动车，你拥有的知识越多，你渴望的知识就越多。" - on accumulated knowledge increasing curiosity.

> "小步决策可以是坚持不少于 5-10 分钟的启发阅读。" - on low-friction ways to seed interest.
