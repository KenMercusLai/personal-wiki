## Summary
[[EricSeufert]] surveys mobile messaging advertising formats in early 2016 after a leaked document suggested [[FacebookMessenger]] ads would arrive in brand-initiated threads. The article argues that as chat apps move toward platform-like consumer infrastructure, advertising is likely to mature around brand interaction rather than simple banner placement. Its local screenshot of a free Despicable Me 2 sticker pack is substantive evidence for branded stickers as an ad surface inside [[FacebookMessenger]]'s sticker store.

## Key Claims
- [[MobileMessagingAdvertising]] already had several models by early 2016: opt-in content channels, CRM-like brand conversations, unavoidable sponsored content, and branded stickers.
- Opt-in content channels let brands pay for placement in messaging surfaces and then send content only after users engage, as in [[Kik]] Promoted Chats and [[Snapchat]] Discover.
- CRM-like brand conversations are more native to chat because they use conversation itself, but they require labor or automated response tools to scale.
- [[Facebook]]'s possible Messenger ads fit the CRM-like model because ads would reportedly appear only in threads users had previously opened with brands.
- [[WeChat]], [[Tango]], and [[BlackBerryMessenger]] showed that messaging monetization could also use moments/feed placements, promoted channels, or sponsored content.
- [[LINE]]'s branded stickers show that ad inventory can be both a store item and a permission surface for later direct messaging.
- Chat's movement toward a mobile platform makes advertising format design strategically important rather than merely incremental monetization.

## Key Quotes
> "Assuming that brand \"interaction\" is the next big advertising opportunity on mobile..." - setup for the model taxonomy.

> "more \"native\" (in terms of functionality)" - on CRM-like ads fitting chat behavior.
