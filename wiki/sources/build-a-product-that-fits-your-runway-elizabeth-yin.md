## Summary
[[ElizabethYin]] argues that early founders should size the first product to their available [[StartupRunway]] rather than attempting to match incumbent feature breadth. The article recommends choosing one sharply differentiated feature, building it quickly with only a fraction of runway, and then pitching both the current narrow product and the larger vision honestly. Its inspected images are mostly runway metaphors, while the Craigslist category map supports the claim that bloated incumbent products can contain many standalone startup opportunities.

## Key Claims
- Product scope should be based on runway; most founders cannot afford to build capital-intensive products or match mature incumbents feature-for-feature.
- In existing markets, differentiation can come from isolating one valuable slice of a broad product, as [[SendGrid]] did with email-sending APIs and [[HubSpot]] initially did with content-marketing help.
- First-time founders with limited access to capital should aim to ship the first useful version in less than two months, using only a fraction of runway.
- Products that initially look like "just a feature" can still be valid if the feature solves a real problem and can expand later.
- Bloated incumbents such as [[Craigslist]] can be decomposed into many standalone product opportunities; the inspected Quora graphic maps Craigslist categories to vertical startups.
- Startup pitches should distinguish the current product from the future vision so investors see both evidence of execution and a credible expansion path.

## Key Quotes
> "based on your runway" - on choosing product scope from available capital and time.

> "take just one feature" - on limiting the first build to a narrow, excellent slice.

> "your product really should feel like just a feature" - on survivable early product scope.
