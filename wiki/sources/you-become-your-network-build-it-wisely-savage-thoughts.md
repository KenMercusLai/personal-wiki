## Summary
Savage Thoughts argues that relationships are not merely useful contacts but an environment that shapes perspective, ambition, work, and personal growth. Because default workplaces and startup ecosystems can assemble that environment by accident, the article recommends [[DeliberateNetworkBuilding]]: choose mentors, colleagues, friends, reading, and media according to the direction in which one wants to grow. The essay connects this personal claim to startup conformity, warning that founders who pass through the same accelerators, investors, and media can reproduce a venture-funded hive mind.

## Key Claims
- A person's recurring relationships and information sources influence perspective, behavior, identity, and possible growth.
- Networks are often assembled passively from whoever happens to be nearby at work or in an industry.
- [[DeliberateNetworkBuilding]] begins by naming the direction of growth, then choosing people and inputs that make that direction more likely.
- Mentorship quality is goal-relative: the useful mentor is someone able to support the particular capability or future the learner wants.
- Startup founders can converge on the same assumptions when they share accelerators, venture capital networks, and media diets.
- Entering a default network without checking its fit can contribute to alienation, burnout, or work that conflicts with one's values.
- Growth also depends on time allocation across conversations, reading, and viewing, not only formal relationships.

## Key Quotes
> "It is a huge mistake to let your network get built for you." - the article's closing warning against passive network formation.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction was found. The source qualifies purely instrumental networking advice by emphasizing that a network changes the participant as well as supplying opportunities.
- The article is a short practitioner essay. It gestures to research on social ties and obesity without naming the study and provides no comparative evidence that deliberate network selection causes the claimed career or personal-growth outcomes.
