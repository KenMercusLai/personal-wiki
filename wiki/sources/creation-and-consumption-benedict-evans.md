## Summary
[[BenedictEvans]] rejects the claim that PCs are for creation while phones and tablets are only for consumption. He argues that this framing mistakes a minority of precision-heavy professional PC work for normal computer use, ignores writing, photography, video, sharing, and other creation already happening on phones, and overlooks how simpler mobile interfaces make capabilities usable by billions more people. The result is both a [[MobileProductivity]] argument and a broader claim about [[CapabilityAccessibility]]: a feature that ordinary users cannot discover or operate is functionally absent for them.

## Key Claims
- The creation-versus-consumption distinction is a poor way to compare PCs with phones and tablets because both software and work practices change around new tools.
- Evans estimates that only about 100 million people used sophisticated professional PC software for complex creation, while most PCs handled email, web use, games, and routine business processes that did not require a file system, precision pointer, or complex multi-window interface.
- Phones already support writing, sharing, photography, video, games, and communication while adding touch, cameras, GPS, connectivity, and app-distributed capabilities that conventional PCs lacked.
- Computing has two meanings of "can't": a feature may not exist, or a person may not know how to use it; for practical purposes, either condition makes the capability unavailable.
- The simpler interface abstraction of iOS and Android may temporarily remove some expert capabilities while making many more capabilities usable by ordinary people.
- Smartphone price, distribution, and personal ownership expand computing from roughly 1.5 billion often-shared PCs toward billions of individual users, including people whose first computer is a phone.
- Precision-heavy professional tasks can remain PC-bound without supporting the general claim that meaningful creation belongs on PCs.

## Key Quotes
> "If you don't know how to do it, the feature might as well not be there." - on practical capability.

> "Vastly more is being created on mobile now by vastly more people than was ever created on PCs." - conclusion.

## Contradictions
- The source qualifies rather than contradicts [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]]: some precise, complex professional work remains PC-bound even as mobile absorbs mainstream creation and computing.
- Its device counts, professional-software estimates, app-user figures, and literacy examples are a 2017 analytical snapshot built from approximate and partly overlapping data, not current market measurements.
- The essay treats simpler mobile interfaces as broadly enabling but does not separately examine disability access, assistive technology, mobile-only constraints, keyboard-intensive work, or the economic limits of connectivity and charging.
