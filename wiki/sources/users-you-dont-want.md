## Summary
[[MichaelSeibel]] argues that early startups should not let every user request redefine the problem they are solving. Off-target users can either reveal a larger, more acute opportunity or pull a product into one-off consulting work, so founders should evaluate the size, economics, and growth potential of the adjacent segment before changing direction. [[JustinTV]] supplies the positive case: its small but persistent gaming-broadcaster segment became strategically attractive because serving it did not materially change costs and improved advertising possibilities.

## Key Claims
- Early products attract people whose needs fall outside the intended customer and problem scope.
- A user with an urgent unmet need is not sufficient evidence for a pivot; founders should ask whether that user represents a larger group.
- Adjacent segments are promising when they offer better growth without breaking the business model or materially changing service costs.
- One-off feature work for isolated users can turn a technology startup into a consulting business and fragment the roadmap.
- Justin.tv's gaming broadcasters were initially a small but consistent segment whose bandwidth-and-salary cost structure fit the existing service and whose content was more attractive to online video advertisers.
- Courteously declining off-target customers can improve service for the intended segment and help the company search for [[ProductMarketFit]].
- Early product focus is a bet on making a small group very happy rather than half-solving many unrelated problems.

## Key Quotes
> "You don’t have to pivot your business because a customer needs something that you don’t offer." — Seibel on protecting problem scope

> "Being good at customer service doesn’t mean serving every potential customer." — Seibel on saying no courteously

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction was found. The source qualifies broad customer-led product advice: user demand is an input to evaluate, not an instruction to serve every requester.
- The babysitting example uses dated language about disability and simplifies the care requirements of infants and disabled teenagers; it illustrates scope and economics rather than establishing facts about those groups.
- The Justin.tv account is Seibel's retrospective interpretation and does not independently establish that cost compatibility and advertiser preference were the only reasons gaming became the right focus.
