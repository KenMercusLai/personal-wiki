## Summary
[[PriceIntelligently]] argues that SaaS discounting can create short-term acquisition wins while damaging long-term willingness to pay, renewal behavior, and unit economics. The source compares minimal and aggressive discounting across 88 SaaS companies, uses a 20% discount example to show longer [[CustomerAcquisitionCost]] recovery, and recommends discrete, segmented, time-limited discounts when discounting is unavoidable. Several referenced chart images appear evidence-bearing, but the source CDN returned "Not Found" during ingest, so this note uses the surrounding prose rather than unseen axis details.

## Key Claims
- Aggressive SaaS discounts can make a quarter look better while hiding later retention and revenue damage.
- Discounted customers enter with lower willingness to pay, higher price sensitivity, higher churn risk, and lower [[CustomerLifetimeValue]].
- A common 20% discount on a $500 monthly customer with $6,000 [[CustomerAcquisitionCost]] can extend CAC recovery by about three months.
- Discounting trains both customers and sales teams to treat the product as less valuable than its list price.
- Discounts should be discrete, segmented, limited in scope and time, and varied so customers do not learn to wait for predictable offers.
- If discounts are used, annual-plan discounts can improve upfront cash flow and may be safer than broad monthly-plan discounting.

## Key Quotes
> "you shouldn't use discounting as a quick fix" - on discounting after pricing work.

> "secret weapon, rather than the first resort" - on how discounts should be positioned.
