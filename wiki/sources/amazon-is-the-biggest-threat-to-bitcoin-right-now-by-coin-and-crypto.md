## Summary
Coin and Crypto argues that [[Amazon]] could threaten [[Bitcoin]]'s leading cryptocurrency position by staying out of cryptocurrency payments, adopting a faster competing cryptocurrency, or creating its own token across Amazon services. The article frames merchant adoption, transaction throughput, and platform distribution as strategic constraints on whether Bitcoin can become a practical currency rather than only an investment asset. The inspected local images are promotional Signal Groups and Telegram banner icons, while the remote header image is decorative; none add substantive evidence beyond the article text.

## Key Claims
- [[Bitcoin]] had speculative momentum but faced volatility, recovery risk after hacks, and competition from newer cryptocurrencies promising stronger anonymity, programmability, or scaling.
- [[Amazon]]'s retail scale made it a potentially decisive merchant-adoption gatekeeper for cryptocurrency payments.
- Bitcoin and [[Ethereum]] transaction throughput was presented as too slow for Amazon-scale checkout demand, while Ripple was cited as a faster alternative.
- Amazon's reported purchase of cryptocurrency-related domains was treated as weak evidence that it was at least monitoring the sector.
- Amazon could hurt Bitcoin by never accepting cryptocurrency, by adopting a competitor, or by creating a proprietary cryptocurrency for Amazon Prime, Twitch, Audible, and purchase discounts.
- The article's strongest durable claim is about [[CryptocurrencyMerchantAdoption]]: large distribution platforms can redirect crypto attention by choosing which payment rail, token, or ecosystem to support.

## Key Quotes
> "Any cryptocurrency Amazon adopted would surely see a huge surge of support." - framing Amazon's platform distribution power.

> "In order to cross the chasm from investment to currency, bitcoin needs to gain widespread adoption from merchants." - article's merchant-adoption premise.
