## Summary
[[NeilCybart]] argues that [[Apple]]'s 2018 iPhone XS, iPhone XR, and [[AppleWatch]] Series 4 announcements reveal a coherent product strategy: Apple keeps making technology more personal by moving tasks from less personal computers to more intimate form factors. The article formalizes this as [[AppleProductTheory]], where Mac desktops, Mac portables, iPad, [[IPhone]], Apple Watch, and possible [[SmartGlasses]] each handle tasks formerly assigned to the larger or less personal product above them. Its embedded diagram is substantive evidence, showing the same product ladder and placing glasses below Apple Watch as a device meant to handle tasks formerly given to Apple Watch and iPhone.

## Key Claims
- [[AppleProductTheory]] says Apple's major products are connected by a shared mission to make technology more personal rather than by a simple replacement ladder.
- The 2018 iPhone XS, XS Max, and XR push the iPhone X experience through larger screens, the A12 Bionic chip, and more intelligent camera systems.
- Apple's custom silicon gives it a competitive advantage because more of the iPhone experience depends on Apple-controlled core technology.
- [[AppleWatch]] Series 4 is framed as a proactive digital assistant that can monitor heart rhythm, falls, and other body-centered signals unavailable to the iPhone.
- Apple Watch is expected to become a viable alternative to the iPhone once it decouples further from iPhone dependence.
- [[SmartGlasses]] fit the same theory as a future form factor using glances, voice, custom silicon, miniaturization, and enhanced vision to make technology more personal than Watch or iPhone.
- Cybart expects Apple Watch and possible Apple glasses to eventually reach larger user bases than the iPhone because their lower-friction, more personal form factors can appeal beyond the smartphone installed base.

## Key Quotes
> "Apple's major product categories are interconnected by the roles they play in making technology more personal." - product-theory thesis.

> "Apple Watch Series 4 stole the show." - Cybart's assessment of the 2018 event.
