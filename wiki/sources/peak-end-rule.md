## Summary
[[LAN]] explains the [[PeakEndRule]] as a memory shortcut in which the most emotionally intense moment and the ending disproportionately shape retrospective evaluation. Using [[TheLastOfUsPartII]] as a narrative case, the essay argues that emotionally punishing peaks and an unrewarding ending can dominate memories of otherwise strong craft, then distinguishes entertainment selection—where a desired emotional destination may legitimately matter—from work evaluation, where predefined criteria, process records, decomposed judgments, delay, and counterexamples can reduce distortion.

## Key Claims
- People often remember an experience through a few salient reconstructed moments rather than a time-weighted average of every moment.
- The “peak” may be positive or negative, while the psychologically relevant “end” may be the last battle, image, question, or feeling that persists rather than the literal endpoint.
- [[TheLastOfUsPartII]] concentrates several negative or conflicted peaks—Joel's death, forced play as Abby, and Ellie's bleak return from the final confrontation—that can dominate recollection of its levels, performance, stealth, combat, and detail.
- Emotional intensity alone does not determine acceptance: painful experiences are easier to value when they can be integrated into a meaningful narrative.
- For entertainment chosen mainly for emotional experience, expected peaks and the ending can be more decision-relevant than complete logical consistency.
- Work decisions should suppress peak-end distortion because a final incident, polished presentation, or dramatic rescue can obscure long-run project or individual performance.
- Precommitted criteria, process records, dimensional scoring, delayed judgment, and active search for counterexamples can make evaluation less dependent on salient recent moments.

## Key Quotes
> “我们常常以为自己在评价一段完整经历，实际上评价的是几个高亮片段。” — on mistaking selected memories for an average of the full experience.

> “在事情开始前写下评价标准。” — on preventing a vivid ending from defining success after the fact.

## Contradictions

- The article is a reflective application, not a review of peak-end experiments or evidence that every audience member evaluates the game this way. Its travel, project, employee, and game examples illustrate plausible mechanisms without establishing prevalence or effect size.
- Treating a preferred ending as a useful entertainment-selection criterion describes a personal goal, not a general standard of artistic quality; logical coherence, challenge, surprise, and deliberately unresolved discomfort may be central to other audiences or works.
