## Summary
[[JeremyABoyd]] gives [[Duet]]-focused practitioner advice for turning a software side project into a small business through lightweight experiments, trial-user onboarding, tiered pricing, and complementary revenue streams. His strongest reported result is that a staged email sequence coincided with free-to-paid conversion rising from 6% to 18% and customer lifetime value reaching $80, but the article supplies no cohort sizes, test design, observation period, or retention breakdown. The supplied Markdown contains no image references.

## Key Claims
- Even a small project can test landing-page variants by assigning new visitors to a version and connecting that assignment to analytics and payment records; sequential before-and-after tests are a cheaper but more confounded fallback.
- A triggered onboarding sequence should help trial users install, use, and buy the product, with messages conditioned on usage and conversion state rather than one generic receipt.
- Boyd reports that the onboarding sequence increased free-to-paid conversion from 6% to 18% and produced $80 customer lifetime value for a $15 product.
- A conspicuous high-priced tier can anchor the comparison, while lower tiers should reduce support, service, integrations, or account capacity rather than only user count.
- A one-month 50% Pro offer can let active non-buyers experience premium value and later downgrade, but the article does not isolate the offer's effect from the rest of onboarding.
- Side-project revenue can expand through payment-processing spread, annual upgrade licensing, premium hosted SaaS, paid add-ons, and third-party integrations.
- Continued licensing revenue depends on useful improvements and product quality; stagnation removes the reason to renew.

## Key Quotes
> "Just know your conversions." - on using measurement even without a dedicated experimentation platform.

> "there is no reason you couldn't turn this into a viable small startup" - on treating a side project as a potential business.

## Contradictions
- The recommendation to split-test everything is qualified by [[david-kadavy-a-a-testing-how-i-increased-conversions-300-percent-by-doing-absolutely-nothing]], which shows that low-powered tests and noisy relative lifts can mislead small operators.
- The 50% Pro offer conflicts with the broad warning in [[a-comprehensive-data-guide-to-why-you-shouldnt-discount]] that discounts can lower willingness to pay and retention; Boyd provides no cohort-level renewal evidence to resolve the tension.
- Frequent automated email can improve conversion while feeling intrusive or eroding trust. The article reports conversion but does not measure complaints, deliverability, long-term retention, or customer sentiment.
