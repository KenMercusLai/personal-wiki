## Summary
[[JoshElman]] argues that product growth starts with a model of purpose, users, discovery, adoption, and habit frequency before teams build a funnel. The article introduces the [[ProductEngagementLadder]] as a way to break a product into staged skills, using [[Twitter]] onboarding as the example: help new users understand tweets, follow people, read timelines, install mobile apps, then gradually move toward replies, retweets, favorites, posting, search, and audience building. No effective image references were present in the source Markdown.

## Key Claims
- A growth model should define the product's core purpose, who cares about that purpose, how people first hear about it, what setup is required before value appears, and how often the product should become a habit.
- A funnel should run from never hearing about the product to being locked into a frequent habit, with frequency determined by the product's natural use case.
- The product hook may match the core purpose, as with [[Uber]], or may differ from it, as with [[Twitter]] news and celebrity discovery leading toward a personalized "what's happening" experience.
- Adoption and onboarding should help users complete the setup required to receive product value, sometimes through a "Learn Flow" that both guides and teaches.
- A ladder of engagement decomposes expert product use into staged skills or tasks, while accepting that not every user must reach the top to get value.
- Twitter's early ladder prioritized understanding tweets, following accounts, reading the timeline, and using mobile apps before encouraging participation, search, posting, and follower-building.

## Key Quotes
> "what is the core purpose of the product" - first question in Elman's growth-model frame.

> "locked into frequent habit" - endpoint of the product funnel.

> "Ladder of Engagement" - name for the ongoing skill and task journey after initial adoption.
