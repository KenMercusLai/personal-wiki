## Summary
This 2017 Official Unofficial Firefox Blog essay argues that [[Chrome]]'s browser dominance made ordinary web use too dependent on [[Google]]'s advertising business, even if Chrome itself remained a good product. It presents [[Firefox]] and [[Mozilla]] as a modern, independent counterweight: leaner on memory, improving in performance through multi-process Firefox, stronger on privacy controls, and mission-driven by a nonprofit commitment to a healthy web. The embedded campaign creative uses Chrome-colored machine imagery and duplicate Stay Puft stills as rhetorical framing, while a small chart supports the article's memory-use comparison between Firefox and Chrome.

## Key Claims
- [[Chrome]] had roughly four times the desktop browser market share of [[Firefox]] in the cited 2016-2017 StatCounter window, giving [[Google]] outsized influence over web behavior.
- The article frames Google, Facebook, Microsoft, Apple, and Amazon as the "Frightful Five" whose ecosystems partition the web into feudal-style domains.
- [[Firefox]] was being repositioned through the "Browse Against the Machine" campaign as an independent, modern alternative rather than a nostalgic fallback.
- The article claims Firefox had become faster, lagged less, used less memory than Chrome, and was approaching perceived performance parity through multi-process Firefox.
- Firefox's differentiation is tied to independence, privacy control, tracking protection in private browsing, the Focus mobile browser, and [[Mozilla]]'s nonprofit mission.
- Mozilla's Google search revenue is acknowledged as a qualification, but the article argues Mozilla was financially healthier, more diversified, and still independent enough to challenge Google.

## Key Quotes
> "I just don't like only being on Chrome." - on browser choice as the article's central concern.

> "And if we can't act fully independent, what are we actually doing here?" - on Mozilla's need to behave independently despite Google revenue.
