## Summary
[[AndrewBosworth]] argues that time is more scarce and valuable than money, so asking for another person's time carries a high burden of responsibility. The article focuses on meetings as the most common professional abuse case: owners should justify the cost, clarify purpose and outcomes, curate attendance, share notes, gather feedback, and expire recurring meetings by default. It also links meeting discipline to [[AttentionManagement]] by warning that interruptions and context switches let urgent demands displace important work.

## Key Claims
- Time is the only truly scarce resource, and interrupting someone spends a resource more precious than money.
- Meetings are especially costly because they combine large time budgets, context switching, and the tendency for urgent topics to overtake important work.
- Meeting owners should define purpose, size, agenda, expected outcomes, notes, accountability, and feedback loops before claiming other people's time.
- Attendance should be actively curated: people can be removed, remove themselves, or leave when the meeting is not the best use of their time.
- Recurring meetings should expire quickly and be reassessed rather than growing indefinitely.
- Makers should protect focus, but they should also respect meetings they choose to attend by giving full attention.

## Key Quotes
> "Time is the only truly scarce resource we possess." - on why interruptions carry a high burden.

> "Anyone should feel free to leave a meeting at any time" - on preserving attendee agency.
