## Summary
[[PauloCaroli]] presents [[ProductBacklogBuilding]] as a collaborative canvas for turning personas, activities, features, and product backlog items into useful [[UserStories]]. The article argues that user-story writing should not collapse into [[ProductManagement]] or Product Owner administration alone: the whole team can contribute when the work is grounded in who the user is, what action they need, and why the action matters. It also connects story quality to [[ExtremeProgramming]] lineage, INVEST, the 3Cs model, acceptance criteria, enablers, and ready/done agreements.

## Key Claims
- [[UserStories]] work best as concise prompts for conversation, confirmation, and shared understanding rather than fixed requirement contracts.
- [[ProductBacklogBuilding]] starts from personas and user activities, turns product interactions into features, decomposes features into PBIs, and then composes those PBIs into user stories.
- The 3Ws structure keeps stories tied to who benefits, what action they need, and why the product should support it.
- INVEST and the 3Cs model give teams practical quality checks for whether a story is independent, negotiable, valuable, estimable, appropriately sized, testable, conversational, and confirmable.
- Acceptance criteria, UI artifacts, tasks, exploratory enablers, and technical enablers add enough detail for implementation without forcing all work into user-story format.
- Definitions of Ready and Done help teams agree when a PBI is prepared for work and when an increment is releasable.

## Key Quotes
> "anyone can (and should) write a User Story" - on team-wide backlog authorship.

> "The best documents help people remember conversations, not replace them." - on the 3Cs model.
