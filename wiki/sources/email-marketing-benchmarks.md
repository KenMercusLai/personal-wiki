## Summary
This March 2018 [[Mailchimp]] benchmark compares unique opens, clicks, soft and hard bounces, abuse complaints, and unsubscribes across self-reported customer industries. It is based on hundreds of millions of tracked emails from campaigns sent to at least 1,000 subscribers and shows why [[EmailCampaignBenchmarking]] needs industry context, while leaving sample periods, industry sample sizes, weighting, and metric definitions unspecified.

## Key Claims
- Email performance varies materially by industry, so a single universal target can conceal meaningful differences in audience and campaign context.
- Among the listed industries, open rates range from 14.92% for daily deals and e-coupons to 27.35% for hobbies.
- Click rates range from 1.06% for restaurants to 4.78% for hobbies, so open-rate rank and click-rate rank should not be treated as interchangeable.
- Construction records the highest hard-bounce rate at 0.98% and the highest abuse rate at 0.018%, while architecture and construction records the highest soft-bounce rate at 1.39%.
- The benchmark covers customers from one-person startups to Fortune 500 companies, but includes only campaigns with tracking enabled, a reported industry, and at least 1,000 subscribers.
- The figures are historical descriptive averages, not causal targets or current deliverability standards.

## Key Quotes
> "The more context, the better." - on comparing a campaign with others in the same industry

> "We only tracked campaigns that went to at least 1,000 subscribers" - on the inclusion threshold

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction identified. The table qualifies generic email-performance targets by showing substantial industry variation.
- The source gives no observation window, per-industry sample size, weighting method, dispersion, or metric definitions, so its averages cannot establish a campaign's causal quality or a timeless standard.
