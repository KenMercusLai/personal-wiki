## Summary
This first-person essay argues that social-media use can become acutely harmful during an existing depressive episode even when evidence that social media causes depression remains mixed. The author describes a loop of anticipatory reward, compulsive refreshing, comparison, depleted attention, and emotional emptiness; [[IsabellaHeuser]] supplies a physiological interpretation, while [[DeannaZandt]] frames curated public identity as a source of depressive dissonance. Removing social apps, notifications, and other reward-seeking apps reportedly made the author's world more manageable and supported recovery, but the account is experiential and interview-based rather than a causal clinical study.

## Key Claims
- [[DepressionAndSocialMedia]] should distinguish social media causing depression from social media aggravating an episode that already exists; the source says research on the latter was missing from the author's search.
- During depression, habitual feed checking can preserve anticipation of a pleasant reward while anhedonia prevents the expected joy or connection, leaving a painful reward-without-relief loop.
- Low energy can weaken resistance to learned checking habits, while impaired concentration makes rapidly refreshed feeds simultaneously attractive and damaging to attention.
- Curated portrayals invite comparison not only with other people but with one's own earlier public identity, intensifying inferiority and the belief that one has become a failure.
- Constant availability and performative posting can add stress when a person has little capacity for interaction, while shallow public exchange may fail to supply the intimacy depression requires.
- Deleting social apps, disabling notifications, limiting access, walking, reading, single-tasking, and focusing scarce energy on close relationships reportedly helped the author recover.
- [[DeannaZandt]] argues that the gap between lived distress and cheerful public presentation can deepen alienation from other people and from oneself, and that products should make room for vulnerability and messy human experience.

## Key Quotes
> “Will has to do with energy.” — [[IsabellaHeuser]] on why depression can weaken resistance to habitual checking

> “focus what little energy I had on real connection” — the author's practical response

## Contradictions
- The source explicitly declines to claim that social media causes depression and describes the then-available causal literature as mixed; its claim concerns use during an existing episode.
- Its physiological account comes from one clinician interview and its broader pattern from self-observation, acquaintances, and reported clinical experience, not a controlled study or representative sample.
- Some people use Facebook during insomnia, and other wiki sources describe valuable support communities and clinician-vetted social spaces; social-media effects therefore depend on condition, purpose, platform design, and mode of use.
