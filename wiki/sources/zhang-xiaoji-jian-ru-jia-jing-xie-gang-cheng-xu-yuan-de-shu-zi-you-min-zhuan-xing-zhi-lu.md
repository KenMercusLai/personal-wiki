## Summary
[[ZhangXiaoji]] recounts being laid off, abandoning a poorly fitting short-video attempt, and moving toward a small portfolio of paid software tools: [[TableToFigma]], [[FitCurve]], and [[GlimmerCard]]. The article argues that a slash programmer's digital-nomad transition depends less on pure coding ability than on validated demand, early monetization, repeated user feedback, platform momentum, and [[BuildInPublic]] distribution. The screenshots materially support the revenue and demand claims, showing paid orders, usage graphs, early user conversations, social-platform feedback, and platform/community traction.

## Key Claims
- A programmer leaving employment can reduce career risk by building several small paid products rather than betting everything on one large product.
- Failed experiments can still produce transferable capabilities; the short-video attempt failed commercially but taught production workflow and reinforced the need to return to a personally strong domain.
- [[TableToFigma]] emerged from a concrete internal work need and differentiated from Google Sheets Sync by broadening data-source and property support.
- Early monetization should be designed into paid products from launch because real buyers care more about solved problems than tiny price differences.
- [[CustomerLedProductDevelopment]] improved both [[TableToFigma]] and [[GlimmerCard]]: user conversations revealed table generation, multi-source import, layout, template, image, and internationalization needs.
- [[BuildInPublic]] is treated as an operating posture for feedback, trust, first users, and reusable distribution rather than a one-time launch tactic.
- Choosing growing ecosystems such as [[Figma]] or Apple's App Store can amplify a small creator's limited resources.

## Key Quotes
> "产品永远不会有最完美的版本，全是权衡的结果。" - on shipping and monetization tradeoffs.

> "公开构建是一种心态，而不是一种策略。" - on build-in-public as a creator posture.
