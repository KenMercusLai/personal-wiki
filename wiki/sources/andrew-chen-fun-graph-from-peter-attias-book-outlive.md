## Summary
[[AndrewChen]] shares a [[VO2Max]] chart from [[PeterAttia]]'s book [[Outlive]] and says it changed his exercise behavior. The chart compares high, average, and low [[CardiorespiratoryFitness]] percentiles across age against everyday activity thresholds, showing that fitness capacity declines with age and that high current capacity creates more room for later-life activities such as brisk stair climbing. Chen interprets the graph as a reason to start endurance training before later-life capacity loss makes large percentile jumps unrealistic.

## Key Claims
- [[VO2Max]] declines with age across high, average, and low fitness percentiles.
- The inspected chart shows high fitness at older ages preserving enough capacity for brisk stair climbing and some jogging tasks, while average and low fitness fall below more demanding activity thresholds earlier.
- Chen's key behavioral inference is that people should build cardiovascular capacity early because moving from average or low fitness to the 95th percentile later may be unrealistic.
- [[Outlive]] is presented as the source that made the longevity implication vivid enough to change Chen's behavior toward running, Peloton, and outdoor cycling.
