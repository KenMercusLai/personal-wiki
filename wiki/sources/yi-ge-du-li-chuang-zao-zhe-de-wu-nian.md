## Summary
[[Hawstein]] reflects on five years as an [[IndependentCreator]], moving from the one-time paid content product [[AlgoCasts]] toward an overseas subscription [[BootstrappedSaaS]] business. The essay argues that independent SaaS became viable through global software distribution, modular services such as [[Stripe]], and a deliberate preference for small, autonomy-preserving operations. It also frames business as a creative practice where product design, marketing, customer support, and customer friendship all shape the work.

## Key Claims
- A domestic, one-time paid content product can create an initial income base, but low average order value, unstable new-customer acquisition, ongoing content production, and piracy make it a difficult long-term model for a small independent creator.
- Immersion in overseas Indie Hacker stories helped [[Hawstein]] notice a Europe-focused SaaS opportunity and commit quickly once the idea kept demanding attention.
- A U.S. bank account, EIN, [[Stripe]] account, and later a U.S. company formed lightweight financial infrastructure for selling subscription SaaS to global customers.
- [[MicroCompany]] work can become viable when third-party services provide enough product, payment, marketing, operations, and support infrastructure for one person to ship a complete product.
- The author chose not to hire support despite exploring options, because preserving autonomy and minimizing social coordination mattered more than delegation at the current stage.
- Technical ability was necessary but insufficient; selling required reviews, direct outreach, content marketing, paid ads, affiliate incentives, and strong support-driven word of mouth.
- [[CustomerLedProductDevelopment]] supplied product ideas and encouraged pruning solutions to solve a real customer's specific problem quickly instead of overbuilding a generalized system.

## Key Quotes
> "代码永不眠。" — the essay's compressed image of always-on global software leverage.
