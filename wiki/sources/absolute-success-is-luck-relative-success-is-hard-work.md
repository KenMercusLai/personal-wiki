## Summary
[[JamesClear]] argues that luck and effort explain different comparisons: birth, timing, genes, connections, and other contingencies loom larger in absolute or extreme success, while choices, habits, preparation, and persistence better explain differences among people with broadly similar starting conditions. Through [[WarrenBuffett]]'s "ovarian lottery" and [[TuYouyou]]'s discovery of artemisinin, the essay turns this distinction into a temporal model: people do not control their starting position, but can influence their trajectory and increase their exposure to useful opportunities.

## Key Claims
- [[LuckAndEffortInSuccess]] separates absolute comparison across unlike starting conditions from relative comparison among roughly comparable peers.
- The more extreme an outcome is, the more cautious observers should be about attributing it entirely to skill or labor, because rare outcomes usually require unusual circumstances as well.
- Buffett's ovarian-lottery thought experiment treats nationality, wealth, sex, health, and social position at birth as consequential advantages or disadvantages outside individual control.
- Tu Youyou's repeated screening, recovery from failed extraction attempts, return to an ancient text, low-temperature redesign, self-experimentation, and continued research illustrate how persistent work can convert an uncertain lead into a consequential result.
- The slope metaphor distinguishes an inherited starting position from the direction created by habits and effort, while acknowledging that illness, financial collapse, and durable advantage can still change or overwhelm the trajectory.
- Preparation and repeated action can increase a person's "surface area" for luck by creating more encounters with possible opportunities, although they cannot guarantee which opportunity appears or whether it succeeds.

## Key Quotes
> "Absolute success is luck. Relative success is choices and habits." - Clear's comparison-scale thesis.

> "You can only control the slope of your success, not your initial position." - Clear's distinction between starting conditions and trajectory.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found. The source complements [[RepeatableLearningFromHistory]] by offering a comparison-scale model, but its slogan risks understating structural differences within any supposedly similar peer group and does not establish causal weights for luck, effort, or habits.
