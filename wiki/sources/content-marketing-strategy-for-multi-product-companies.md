## Summary
This practitioner article argues that multi-product content strategy should remain anchored to purchasable use cases, then organize each relevant product or audience across the full marketing funnel. It contrasts a product-based plan for [[Zendesk]], which sells several support products to a similar team, with an audience-based plan for [[Clearbit]], whose data-enrichment offer serves different teams, and recommends auditing the resulting matrix in [[Airtable]]. The supplied Markdown's sole remote title image returns HTTP 404, while the diagrams mentioned in the prose are absent from the file, so the visual examples could not be inspected.

## Key Claims
- A [[MultiProductContentStrategy]] should begin with use cases tied to something customers can buy rather than expanding into disconnected editorial topics.
- Companies selling several products to one similar audience should segment content by product and give each product top-, middle-, and bottom-of-funnel coverage.
- Companies adapting one similar product for several teams should segment content by audience and give each audience awareness-, consideration-, and purchase-stage coverage.
- A repeatable product-or-audience by funnel-depth matrix makes existing coverage and gaps easier to audit.
- Content inventory can be tagged by product or target audience and by approximate funnel depth, with visualization used to prioritize missing work.
- The matrix can extend from products to use cases, but content volume need not be evenly distributed across cells.
- Funnel assignment and audience assignment are practical approximations because one post may span stages or serve several audiences.

## Key Quotes
> "The business model informs the content strategy." - on choosing product-based or audience-based segmentation.

> "Each 'vertical' (i.e., product or audience) gets the full treatment of top to bottom content." - on applying one repeatable funnel template to each segment.
