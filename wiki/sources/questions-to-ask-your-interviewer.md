## Summary
[[RandallKoutnik]] treats a job interview as two-way diligence and argues that candidates learn more from specific questions, concrete recent examples, and follow-up probes than from broad questions that invite polished reassurance. His checklist tests role reality, expectations for organizational change, work interruption, engineering and deployment practice, decision authority, communication, feedback, and employee development, while remaining practitioner advice rather than a validated predictor of workplace quality.

## Key Claims
- General questions about trust, challenges, or culture often elicit safe abstractions because interviewers have incentives to preserve candidate interest; asking for a recent concrete example makes the claimed practice more inspectable.
- “What are you hoping I’ll change?” can reveal both the real job and wishful expectations that a new hire will repair entrenched practices without authority or organizational support.
- Asking how often people are pulled from their primary task tests whether the advertised role matches day-to-day work and exposes hidden meeting, design, deployment, marketing, or other responsibilities.
- Development and deployment questions can surface missing source control, testing, code review, deterministic releases, change visibility, and rollback capability before a candidate joins.
- Questions about unresolved disagreement, success beyond generic hard work, performance feedback, and professional growth probe decision authority, distinctive norms, warning mechanisms, and investment in development.
- Communication-tool questions should be followed by a real event, such as the last major announcement, because tool availability does not prove that important information reaches remote and colocated workers consistently.

## Key Quotes
> "Getting concrete examples means you get past the marketing fluff to a company's real values." - Koutnik's rationale for example-seeking follow-ups.

> "If I'm not meeting expectations, how will I know?" - A direct test of whether feedback arrives early enough to support improvement.

## Contradictions
- The source does not directly contradict existing diligence guidance; it shifts emphasis from company claims to specific questions, recent examples, and follow-up evidence.
- The essay is one practitioner's interview advice and supplies no sample, comparison group, hiring outcomes, retention data, or evidence that these answers reliably predict later employee experience. Interviewers can select favorable anecdotes, different teams within one company may operate differently, and candidates with less leverage may not be able to ask every sensitive question safely.
- Async tools are not universally superior to synchronous interaction: urgent incidents, ambiguous conflict, relationship building, and high-bandwidth collaboration may benefit from live conversation when interruption and inclusion are managed deliberately.
- Source control, tests, review, rollback, and deterministic deployment are useful operating signals, but their absence or form should be interpreted against system risk, team size, regulatory context, and the actual work rather than used as automatic rejection criteria.
