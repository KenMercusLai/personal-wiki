## Summary
[[HunterWalk]] argues that some technology reporting grants anonymity too readily, especially when an unnamed former employee supplies a negative quotation about a company or founder. His central concern is epistemic: [[AnonymousSourcing]] hides the source's role, access, and possible bias, leaving readers less able to judge a claim. He reserves anonymity for consequential information whose disclosure would genuinely endanger the source and recommends treating anonymous tips as leads to investigate rather than as controversy-ready proof.

## Key Claims
- A reporter can usually find an unnamed former employee or informed person willing to criticize a company or founder.
- [[AnonymousSourcing]] removes identity, access, role, and bias context that readers need to assess a statement's quality.
- Granting anonymity should be a deliberate editorial concession tied to important information and meaningful risk to the source.
- Whistleblowing about illegal activity is the essay's clearest example of a justified anonymity grant.
- Anonymous tips should direct reporters toward evidence and further investigation rather than substitute for verification.
- Technology-community members who offer only negative opinion should generally be willing to stand behind it publicly.
- Background guidance can be legitimate when it helps a reporter locate stronger, independently supportable evidence.

## Key Quotes
> "Use these leads as heat drawing you towards the real fire" - Walk's distinction between an investigative lead and publishable proof.
