## Summary
[[PostHog]] presents [[DeveloperMarketing]] as a stage-dependent operating system for early developer-tool startups rather than a collection of generic growth tactics. Before [[ProductMarketFit]], it prioritizes genuinely useful, deeply researched content, a product-like website, selective outsourcing, peer review, qualitative attribution, and skepticism toward bursty [[HackerNews]] traffic; after fit, it adds a developer-writer, carefully managed paid ads, newsletter sponsorships, and selective event or social experiments. The article is a first-party practitioner playbook, and its channel judgments are not comparative performance evidence.

## Key Claims
- Early developer-tool companies should compete on content depth rather than output volume, combining SEO pieces, tutorials, and freer editorial work while continuing to update the strongest search assets.
- Content should be useful enough that its author would publish it personally, and websites should be governed like products so short-term marketing conventions do not erode the developer experience.
- Outsourcing works best for capabilities the team lacks, not as a substitute for internal capacity on work the team already understands; every article should receive substantive peer review before line editing.
- [[HackerNews]] can produce a large but unreliable traffic spike and only a small signup lift, so it cannot replace repeatable distribution.
- Click and UTM data can miscredit the final measurable touchpoint, while an optional free-text signup question can recover otherwise invisible first-touch influence.
- Paid ads, sponsorships, events, and social media become more plausible after fit, but each requires channel-specific judgment, guarded defaults, focused experiments, and internal technical credibility.
- Developer audiences respond better to authentic technical participation: a developer who writes, engineers at events, and personal social accounts can be more effective than purely corporate marketing voices.

## Key Quotes
> "1 great article >>> 25 mediocre ones." - on competing through depth rather than publication volume.

> "Treat your website like a product" - on protecting the developer experience from generic marketing conventions.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found. The article reinforces the existing PostHog paid-ads source while widening the playbook to pre-fit content, website governance, review, sponsorship, events, and social media.
- The claim that Google is good for conversion is qualified by the article's own attribution example: a Google ad may receive last-touch credit after Hacker News or another channel created the original awareness.
- Channel prices, platform behavior, and the reported one-in-ten Hacker News hit rate are company-specific practitioner estimates rather than stable benchmarks.
