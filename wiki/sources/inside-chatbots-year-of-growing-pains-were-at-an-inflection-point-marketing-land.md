## Summary
[[TimPeterson]] reports that the first Messenger and [[Kik]] chatbot wave had reached an inflection point by March 2017: open-ended expectations collided with narrow scripts, weak discovery, and insufficient usage feedback, while single-purpose utility often failed to create a habit. The article strengthens the wiki's constructive middle ground for [[ConversationalUI]] and [[MessagingAsPlatform]]: bounded natural language, explicit failure recovery, quick replies or menus, platform merchandising, and persistent CRM relationships can work together, but each introduces tradeoffs among conversational character, discoverability, scale, and task fit. Its platform and campaign figures are attributed snapshots rather than independent comparative evidence.

## Key Claims
- Early chatbot adoption was trapped in a feedback loop: weak platform promotion limited usage, which limited the interaction data and feedback available to improve bots.
- Users approached bots as if they were general assistants, while most builders shipped narrow single-purpose scripts; even a simple weather request exposed many unanticipated phrasings.
- Effective first-wave bots often occupied a middle ground between decision trees and broad AI: they recognized varied language inside a bounded domain, admitted failure, and steered users back toward supported tasks.
- Quick replies, suggested responses, and persistent menus reduced guessing and guided users toward goals, but an entirely graphical bot risked becoming a weaker website or app inside messaging.
- Bots could be more valuable as CRM channels than as novelty campaigns by supporting service, personalization, lead retention, notifications, and continuing relationships after an initial conversion attempt.
- Discovery was a platform constraint: Kik foregrounded a bot store, promotion, and inline Mentions despite its smaller and younger audience, while Messenger offered much greater scale but made bots harder to find.
- Reported engagement examples show possibility rather than a universal pattern: Alpha 5 sessions lasted four to five minutes, the American Music Awards bot exchanged more than 1.2 million Kik messages, and Poncho reported about 60% one-week return among conversation starters.

## Key Quotes
> "Discovery is the biggest challenge. Period." - Doug Robinson on the adoption bottleneck for brand bots.

> "You need to put boundaries or limits on the topic domain" - Michael Zdepski on preventing a bot's limitations from overwhelming the experience.

> "we haven't been thinking about it as much as a CRM vehicle" - Matt Tepper on shifting bots from novelty interactions toward persistent customer relationships.

## Connections
- [[TimPeterson]] - Marketing Land reporter who assembled the multi-interview account.
- [[ConversationalUI]] - adds bounded natural language, explicit recovery, quick replies, and menus as competing ways to manage the gap between user expectations and bot capability.
- [[MessagingAsPlatform]] - shows that interface quality, discovery, audience fit, and merchandising jointly determine whether platform distribution becomes actual use.
- [[MobileMessagingAdvertising]] - reframes brand bots as CRM and lead-retention channels rather than only campaign destinations.
- [[FacebookMessenger]] - large bot platform whose scale, hidden discovery, quick replies, persistent menus, and early in-house Mentions illustrate the core tradeoffs.
- [[Kik]] - smaller platform whose Bot Shop, suggested responses, promotion, and Mentions made bots more visible and shareable.
- [[ProductFlowFriction]] - free text removes visible structure, while menus reduce guessing but can erase the distinctive value of conversation.
- [[NaturalLanguageProcessing]] - needed even for bounded domains because users express apparently simple intents in many ways.

## Contradictions
- Qualifies [[chat-is-the-new-browser-ted-livingston-medium]]: lower entry friction and sharing tools did not by themselves create bot awareness, repeat use, or a broad ecosystem; platform discovery and task fit remained limiting.
- Reinforces [[chatbots-deliver-the-worst-customer-service-late-night-coding]], [[chatbots-were-the-next-big-thing-what-happened]], and [[chatbots-what-happened-chatbots-life]] on hidden affordances and brittle open text, while adding contemporaneous evidence that bounded natural language plus structured controls could still sustain some engagement.
- The postscript records a direct dispute over Kik adoption: an interviewee described very few bots exceeding 30 subscribers, while Kik said 10,000 bots exceeded that threshold.
