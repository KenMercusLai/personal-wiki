## Summary
[[BenThompson]] argues in [[Stratechery]] that [[Apple]]'s 2018 strategy reflected corporate middle age: the high-end smartphone market was saturated, so Apple increasingly sought growth from existing customers through higher iPhone ASPs, Services revenue, and more ecosystem-specific devices. The article frames [[AppleMusic]] and [[HomePod]] as a vertical-business-model example: Apple Music was less important as standalone service profit than as a bridge from iPhone ownership to profitable hardware sales. The inspected images add evidence: the Wired June 1997 "Pray" cover reinforces Apple's pre-Jobs-return crisis, while the iPhone sales-per-week chart shows 1Q weekly iPhone sales rising from about 2.7 million in 2012 to about 6.0 million in 2018 after flattening around 2015-2017.

## Key Claims
- Apple after Steve Jobs' 1997 return grew by simplifying products, launching the iMac and iPod, and making iTunes for Windows to expand the iPod market.
- [[AppleMiddleAgeStrategy]] describes a mature Apple whose growth is increasingly inward-facing: higher prices, more Services revenue, and more devices sold to existing users.
- [[AppleMusic]] is presented as a strategic bridge for [[HomePod]], not primarily as a high-profit service; preinstallation and customer loyalty make it likely that iPhone users will choose Apple Music, which can then make HomePod the natural smart-speaker option.
- [[TimCook]] and [[LucaMaestri]] pushed a Services narrative, but Thompson argues Apple remained a vertical hardware company whose services differentiate hardware rather than replacing hardware as the business model.
- Apple's 1Q 2018 iPhone story depended on per-week unit framing and a roughly $100 year-over-year ASP increase, making revenue growth look better than unit growth.
- Thompson argues that measuring active devices rather than users reveals Apple's priorities: users matter because they buy devices, and Apple wants each loyal user to own more devices.
- The article defends Apple's move toward ecosystem exclusivity as strategically rational for an incumbent in a saturated premium smartphone market, even if it differs from the younger iPod-era interoperability strategy.

## Key Quotes
> "services is a means, not an end" - Thompson on Apple's vertical model.

> "Devices > Users" - section heading summarizing the measurement argument.

> "stability-driven growth, or stagnation" - open question about Apple's mature strategy.

## Contradictions
- This source qualifies later [[AppleContentDistributionStrategy]]: Thompson's 2018 HomePod argument treats Apple Music exclusivity as a rational hardware-selling bridge, while the later 2019 Above Avalon source interprets Apple Music on Amazon Echo as rational selective openness. The difference reflects changed facts and different strategic moments rather than a direct logical contradiction.
