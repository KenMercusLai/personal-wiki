## Summary
[[AaronJames]] explains how product designers can use a [[ProductDesignCareerLadder]] and a focused career plan to move toward senior-level work. The article frames seniority as increasing impact across six [[DesignSkillAxes]], larger and more ambiguous project scope, wider influence, better feedback filtering, faster failure cycles, and more autonomy. The embedded images were inspected: decorative cold/author images add little evidence, while the ladder diagrams, influence-radius drawing, failure loop, rubric screenshots, and timeline image reinforce the article's model of staged growth, self-assessment, and three-month check-ins.

## Key Claims
- Product design ladders differ by company, but documented expectations can clarify responsibilities, valued traits, interview calibration, and career progression.
- Ladders can be misused for unhealthy comparison, title-chasing, or stress, so levels should guide personal growth rather than designer competition.
- [[DesignSkillAxes]] organize product-design growth into product thinking, interaction design, visual design, intentionality, drive, and self-awareness.
- Senior designers usually handle larger and more ambiguous projects, influence beyond their immediate team, filter feedback signal from noise, fail faster, and lead with greater autonomy.
- A product-design career plan should assess current level, study the next level only, and set concrete goals rooted in peer feedback and manager or mentor alignment.
- Leveling up usually takes more than a quarter; the source recommends three-month progress check-ins, six-month evaluation conversations, and roughly annual expectations.
- Career stagnation can require resetting the plan, finding new projects, changing teams or companies, or changing career paths entirely.
