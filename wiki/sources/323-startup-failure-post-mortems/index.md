## Summary

This CB Insights source is a time-staggered compilation of startup failure post-mortems. It begins with an original 2014 list and adds updates through October 16, 2019, eventually covering 323 shutdowns across consumer hardware, AR/VR, healthcare, fintech, crypto, transportation, food delivery, ecommerce, media, marketplaces, energy, education, and enterprise software.

The source is useful because it treats failure as a corpus rather than a single anecdote. Its cases include founder farewell letters, investor explanations, bankruptcy reports, regulatory shutdowns, lawsuits, failed acquisitions, and journalistic reconstructions. The strongest pattern is that startup deaths usually cluster several causes together: cash runs out, demand is weaker than expected, a market matures too slowly, a platform or regulator changes the rules, incumbents outspend the startup, or operating complexity destroys margins before the business becomes self-sustaining.

The compilation also adds negative evidence to startup scaling advice. Many companies had real products, strong teams, public attention, or enthusiastic early users, but those signals did not always translate into repeat demand, profitable unit economics, continued financing, or enough time for the market to catch up.

## Recurring Patterns

- Funding and runway failures: many companies closed after a financing round, acquisition, or strategic investment failed late in the process, leaving too little time to find another path.
- Weak or unproven demand: several post-mortems describe products that users liked in principle but did not use frequently, pay for, or recommend enough to sustain a company.
- Premature scaling: startups expanded teams, geographies, hardware commitments, or operational footprints before product-market fit and margins could support the burn rate.
- Market timing and education costs: AR/VR, consumer hardware, solar, healthcare, battery, and deep-tech startups often needed more capital and more market maturity than a small venture-backed company could survive.
- Incumbent and platform pressure: startups were squeezed by better-funded competitors, app-store policies, search and social platform changes, licensing costs, or dependency on a single distribution channel.
- Regulatory, legal, and trust failures: healthcare billing scrutiny, worker classification suits, crypto regulation, aviation rules, food safety rules, music licensing, fraud allegations, and privacy controversies all appear as terminal constraints.
- Operational complexity: logistics-heavy, food-delivery, marketplace, hardware, and local-services companies repeatedly struggled to make quality, utilization, supply, demand, and margins work at scale.
