## Summary
[[CliveThompson]] argues that parking is hidden urban infrastructure whose land, housing, traffic, and emissions costs are sustained by minimum requirements and underpriced curb space. Shared autonomous fleets and digitally coordinated rides could sharply reduce vehicle ownership and parking demand, but private autonomous cars could instead increase empty travel and long commutes, making [[ParkingPolicy]] and governance decisive. The article is a 2016 forecast rather than evidence of later autonomous-vehicle deployment.

## Key Claims
- The United States had roughly four parking spaces per passenger vehicle in the cited 2011 estimate, while parking construction raised housing costs and downtown cruising added traffic, fuel use, emissions, and heat.
- Minimum parking requirements hide parking costs inside housing and other goods, encourage driving, and often produce excess capacity; removing them can release centrally located land for housing, services, parks, and other infill.
- Smartphones reduce ride-sharing's coordination problem, and shared autonomous fleets could increase vehicle utilization, reduce ownership, and move much parking away from valuable urban land.
- These gains are conditional: appealing privately owned autonomous vehicles could weaken the effective commute-time limit, induce longer trips, and cruise empty rather than pay to park.
- Cities can shape the outcome through congestion pricing, integration with public transit, accessibility requirements, data-sharing rules, elimination of parking minimums, and demand-responsive curb prices.
- San Francisco's SFpark pilot is presented as evidence that variable prices can preserve one or two open spaces, reduce search time and cruising, lower emissions, and raise meter revenue.

## Key Quotes
> "Parking has been this sacred cow that we couldn't touch—and now we can touch it." - Gabe Klein on reclaiming parking land.

> "The upside of the mess we've made is that we have a lot of land." - Donald Shoup on parking as potential urban infill.

## Contradictions
- The article's low-parking scenario conflicts with its own rebound case: privately owned autonomous cars can increase vehicle miles even while reducing the need to park downtown.
- The source's autonomy timeline, millennial driving trends, Uber figures, and deployment expectations are historically situated 2016 forecasts and should not be read as current measurements.
