## Summary

This source is Steve Ridout's three-year update on building Readlang, a language-learning web app, as a one-person startup. He presents the product as useful to language learners and still growing, but far from paying a market-rate software developer income.

The article makes the economics explicit. Readlang's new signups grew from 2,800 in year one to 9,300 in year two and 36,800 in year three. Revenue rose from about $700 to $4,100 and then $16,500, while low expenses left about $14,500 in third-year profit.

Ridout frames those numbers as both encouraging and costly. The product is ramen profitable and year-over-year profit growth was strong, but the third-year profit was still below what he could have earned at UK minimum wage for a much smaller weekly workload. The source therefore separates survival-level product revenue from attractive compensation.

The decision to continue rests on two kinds of evidence: compounding traction and genuine user value. Ridout points to revenue growth, user growth, and recent user praise as reasons to keep working on Readlang despite the opportunity cost and uncertainty of extrapolating the growth trend.
