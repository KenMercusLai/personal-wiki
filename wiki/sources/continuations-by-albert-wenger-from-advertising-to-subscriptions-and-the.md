## Summary
[[AlbertWenger]] argues that a trusted consumer brand begins with business-model incentives aligned with customers rather than with age or scale. He traces online publishing from an imported advertising model toward reach, targeting, and platform intermediation that can conflict with both users and advertisers, then presents consumer subscriptions as a more promising alternative because continued revenue depends on continued value. The subscription claim is an investor thesis illustrated by media and [[UnionSquareVentures]] portfolio examples, not a comparative outcome study.

## Key Claims
- Online publishers imported advertising from older media because early websites were understood as publishing businesses gathering audiences for later monetization.
- Abundant online publishing fragmented attention and depressed ad prices, rewarding very large audiences and detailed targeting.
- Those pressures can conflict with users because platforms benefit from collecting data and optimizing targeted reach rather than serving only the user's interests.
- Ad platforms can also conflict with advertisers because a platform preserves its intermediary value by limiting durable direct customer relationships that bypass it.
- Direct websites, email, text, and two-way digital communication make ongoing company-customer relationships more scalable than they were in the pre-internet catalog, mass-media, and call-center environment.
- Consumer subscriptions can align company revenue with continuing customer value: the provider must remain worth renewing, while the subscriber can use more of the service without a per-use charge.
- [[Netflix]] and [[Spotify]] are cited as media breakouts, while USV's portfolio supplies broader 2018 examples across music, health, education, finance, and publishing, including hybrid free/ad-supported and paid/ad-free models.

## Key Quotes
> “have incentives that are aligned with your customers/endusers so you can build trust over the longterm” - Wenger's explanation of “trusted brands.”

> “we all need less advertising and more subscriptions” - the essay's business-model conclusion.

## Contradictions
- No direct factual contradiction was found. The source strengthens the wiki's existing account of ad-funded attention and subscription alignment by adding the advertiser-side conflict: platforms need advertisers while also having reason to keep them from owning durable customer relationships.
- The alignment claim is conditional rather than complete. The essay does not compare prices, conversion, churn, profitability, affordability, underuse, cancellation friction, or the cumulative burden of many subscriptions, and a hybrid model can retain advertising incentives for non-paying users.
- The examples and platform judgments are historically situated in April 2018 and should not be treated as a current portfolio list or current assessment of the named companies.
