## Summary
This polemical 2016 essay uses [[Evernote]] to argue that venture financing can make rapid growth and investor exits outrank reliability, privacy, data access, and long-lived user value. It recommends that people treat note-service continuity as a personal risk, prepare an exit path, and consider the author's [[StandardNotes]] project, while providing anecdotes and outbound complaints rather than evidence that venture capital caused Evernote's cited problems.

## Key Claims
- The author presents [[VentureBackedGrowthPressure]] as a mechanism that can turn continued expansion into an obligation even when a smaller, steadier product might better serve users.
- Evernote is criticized for product bloat, bugs, performance and stability problems, reported note-access failures, and a privacy-policy controversy.
- Because personal notes can preserve identity and memory across decades, service continuity and user control matter more than short-term feature growth.
- The essay frames an IPO, acquisition, or inability to attract further investment as three imperfect paths that can each increase pressure on product quality or continuity.
- Users should maintain a contingency plan rather than assume that a hosted note service will remain available and aligned with them indefinitely.
- [[StandardNotes]] is promoted as an open-source, private, cross-platform alternative intended to preserve notes over a long horizon.

## Key Quotes
> "Some things should just come small." - the essay's compressed argument against treating maximum scale as the default product objective.

> "Notes, to me at least, are the most important byproduct of my existence." - why continuity and access are framed as personally consequential.

## Connections
- [[Evernote]] - central negative example used to connect outside capital with bloat, reliability complaints, privacy concerns, and uncertain continuity.
- [[StandardNotes]] - author's disclosed alternative and commercial interest, proposed around open source, privacy, cross-platform access, and longevity.
- [[VentureBackedGrowthPressure]] - central causal thesis that investor return expectations can displace product stewardship.
- [[ExitAsGovernance]] - user contingency planning makes the ability to leave a service a practical constraint on provider power.

## Contradictions
- The essay's causal claim is not demonstrated: it cites Evernote product and policy complaints but supplies no counterfactual, internal decision evidence, financial analysis, or comparison showing that venture capital produced them.
- Its three-path forecast is not exhaustive and does not establish that IPO, acquisition, layoffs, abandonment, or user harm must follow; profitability, recapitalization, ownership changes, controlled contraction, or strategic repair are not evaluated.
- The author is building and promoting [[StandardNotes]], so the critique is both a product argument and interested positioning for an alternative.
- The source displays both a December 29, 2016 article date and a July 5, 2018 publication label; the article's own dated byline is used in frontmatter, while the discrepancy remains unresolved.
