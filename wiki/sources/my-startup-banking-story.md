## Summary
[[MitchellHashimoto]] recounts how [[HashiCorp]]'s original [[Chase]] account grew from a $20,000 founder loan to roughly $35 million after three funding rounds while he treated banking as a passive utility and largely ignored the relationship manager. After moving the balance elsewhere, the company left the account open; customer payments continued arriving, more than $100,000 was fraudulently wired out over roughly a year, and a later lock made closing the account and withdrawing its remaining roughly $1 million operationally difficult. The first-person retrospective frames the episode as a lesson in [[StartupTreasuryManagement]], delegation, account closure, monitoring, diversification, and bank relationships rather than an indictment of Chase.

## Key Claims
- A startup's bank choice and banker relationship can become operationally consequential as its balances, payment flows, and service needs outgrow the account's original setup.
- HashiCorp's roughly $35 million single-account balance prompted its newly hired VP of Finance to improve safeguarding and cash use, move primary banking to Silicon Valley Bank, and later diversify balances across multiple banks.
- Moving the main balance did not retire the old payment route: the Chase account remained open, some customers kept paying into it, and the company did not notice recurring fraudulent wires until a routine internal audit more than a year later.
- Fraud controls can conflict with account-exit needs: locking the compromised account disabled electronic transfers, leaving a branch-funded cashier's check as the eventual route for withdrawing roughly $1 million and closing it.
- Branch staff reportedly had incentives tied to local inflows and outflows, which helps explain attention to the account and resistance to a large withdrawal but is second-hand testimony, not verified Chase policy.
- The source attributes much of the failure to founder inexperience and disengagement: Hashimoto did not explain the business, use Chase's relationship support, or promptly close the obsolete account.
- Chase's investigation ultimately recovered all of the reported fraudulent transfers, and the story reports no lasting financial loss to HashiCorp.

## Key Quotes
> "A bank is a bank is a bank" - Hashimoto's former assumption that banking providers and relationships were interchangeable.

> "I never gave them the chance." - the author's qualification that Chase might have been an effective partner had he engaged with it.
