## Summary
[[DanahBoyd]] traces how skills first used by [[4chan]] participants to make memes, troll media, and game rankings became a broader repertoire for manipulating networked attention. Marketers, activists, political actors, and harassers can all exploit algorithms, media incentives, decentralized coordination, and encoded meaning, making technique alone a poor guide to intent or legitimacy. The essay is a historically situated interpretation published in early 2017: its cases support a mechanism-level account of [[NetworkedInformationManipulation]], not a measured estimate of prevalence, effectiveness, or later platform behavior.

## Key Claims
- Hacking can mean learning a system deeply enough to stretch or redirect its capabilities, not only breaching technical security.
- [[4chan]]'s ephemeral image-board design encouraged repeated and modified reposting, helping participants learn how memes could propagate through blogs and social platforms.
- Coordinated participants learned to game rankings, trending systems, recommendation logic, and journalistic incentives so that distributed activity could become mainstream visibility.
- The same mechanics serve playful trolling, commercial marketing, activism, propaganda, and political power; labels such as “social media marketing” do not cleanly distinguish acceptable from harmful manipulation.
- The trajectory from Anonymous campaigns to Gamergate shows tactics such as doxing, swatting, and gaslighting moving from transgressive play toward coordinated reputational, psychological, economic, and physical harm.
- Networked manipulation can combine explicit falsehoods with harassment, suggestive framing, dog whistles, and distributed ambiguity that induces self-censorship or doubt in institutions.
- The important shift is not that manipulation or trolling is new, but that networked tools increase its scale, coordination, accessibility, and strategic use for acquiring power.

## Key Quotes
> “A new form of information manipulation is unfolding in front of our eyes.” - Boyd's closing diagnosis.

> “It has now become about acquiring power.” - the essay's distinction between playful disruption and strategic manipulation.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction was found. The essay expands the wiki's economic account of attention by showing that attention-distribution systems can be strategically manipulated for political and social power, not only monetized by platforms and publishers.
- Boyd offers a selected historical narrative rather than systematic measurement. The source does not quantify campaign reach, isolate causal effects, compare successful and failed manipulation attempts, or establish how representative 4chan, Anonymous, or Gamergate were.
- The origin story connecting 4chan's deletion policy to meme culture is interpretive and simplified; the essay does not claim that 4chan alone invented memes or all later manipulation practices.

A lead “gun as megaphone” photograph and its duplicate thumbnail were decorative; the tiny lolcat and Egypt/Facebook protest images illustrated examples already stated in the prose and added no reliable independent detail.
