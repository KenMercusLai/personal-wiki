## Summary
[[JonahPeretti]]'s memo to BuzzFeed employees frames the digital media crisis as a broken value exchange between professional publishers and large technology platforms. It argues that [[Buzzfeed]] should respond by fighting for fair platform revenue, diversifying beyond direct-sold advertising, building a portfolio of consumer-service brands such as [[Tasty]], and organizing work around a three-by-three matrix of brands and revenue streams. The embedded graphics add evidence for rising platform revenue, Tasty's multi-channel business, and the "nine boxes" operating grid.

## Key Claims
- Large platforms such as [[Facebook]] and [[Google]] capture much of the digital ad market while underpaying professional publishers, creating a structural disadvantage for high-quality content.
- BuzzFeed should push platforms to reward valuable content while continuing to invest in strong journalism, lifestyle brands, new formats, and durable audience connection.
- There is no single ideal digital media business model; stronger digital media companies combine advertising, commerce, platform revenue, studio development, licensing, subscriptions, and merchandise.
- [[Tasty]] is presented as the model for a modern media brand because it connects daily consumer service, social distribution, products, partnerships, books, apps, shows, and commerce.
- BuzzFeed's 2018 structure is organized as a nine-box grid that crosses three content engines - BuzzFeed, BuzzFeed Media Brands, and BuzzFeed News - with advertising, commerce, and studio revenue opportunities.
- The memo treats revenue diversification as organizational maturation rather than evidence of a "digital media apocalypse."

## Key Quotes
> "The media is in crisis." - opening diagnosis of platform-controlled digital media economics.

> "there isn't one perfect model for digital media." - rationale for a multi-revenue strategy.

> "Today, brands need to be loyal to consumers." - shift from aspirational brand image to daily consumer service.
