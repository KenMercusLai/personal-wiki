## Summary
[[BryceRoberts]] presents the second version of [[IndieVC]] as an investment program for revenue-generating companies seeking profitability rather than the next venture milestone. After a first cohort in which three of eight companies were reportedly profitable, two were close, and all remained operating, the program moved to a dedicated fund, rolling admissions, investments of $100,000-$500,000, and eligibility filters based on prior fundraising and monthly revenue. Its hybrid terms seek investor return through capped distributions while making equity conversion contingent on later financing or acquisition, but the release supplies only first-year, first-party cohort results rather than comparative survival or return evidence.

## Key Claims
- The program targets the financing gap between lifestyle businesses and venture-scale monopolies by treating sustainable profitability as a valid route to investor return.
- In the first eight-company cohort, three companies were reportedly profitable, two were near profitability, and all eight remained in business after about a year.
- Version 2 replaces a cohort batch with rolling investment because the batch format made Indie.vc look like an accelerator despite contrary messaging.
- Initial investments expand from a fixed $100,000 to a flexible $100,000-$500,000 range, backed by a dedicated pool of capital rather than an existing OATV fund.
- Applicants should have raised no more than $500,000 and should generate at least $10,000 in monthly revenue; software had outperformed inventory-dependent businesses in the pilot, although hardware and ecommerce remained eligible.
- The terms cap distributions at 5× and convert to equity only upon acquisition or follow-on financing; reaching the distribution cap within four years halves the agreed conversion percentage.
- Investors cannot hire or fire the founder under the described arrangement, while quarterly retreats provide workshops, founder discussions, and peer reporting.

## Key Quotes
> "We are not your boss" - on the intended boundary between investment partnership and operating control.

> "their company on their terms" - on the founder segment the program aims to serve.

## Contradictions
- The pilot outcomes are reported by the program itself for only eight selected companies after roughly one year, with no comparison group, audited financials, company-level detail, failure base rate, or realized investor returns.
- Continued operation, current profitability, and proximity to profitability do not establish long-run survival, fund-level performance, or that the financing model caused the results.
- The claim that software companies outperformed businesses handling physical inventory is not accompanied by definitions, sample composition, or performance measures.
- Operational autonomy does not eliminate investor incentives: follow-on funding or acquisition can still convert the instrument into equity, and the source does not provide the complete legal terms or enforcement experience.
