## Summary
Four months after [[Kik]] opened its Bot Shop, [[TedLivingston]] argues that the early emphasis on humanlike conversation was mistaken but that bots still had value as lightweight services inside messaging. He separates immature [[NaturalLanguageProcessing]] from the stronger [[MessagingAsPlatform]] case: lower first-use friction, in-chat discovery and sharing, consolidated service access, social-graph integration, and instant structured interactions. [[WeChat]] is presented as supporting this non-conversational model, while the article remains an optimistic, self-interested August 2016 platform snapshot.

## Key Claims
- [[ConversationalUI]] was overemphasized in early bot discourse because 2016-era AI and NLP could not reliably manage humanlike conversation.
- Bots need not replace native apps to be useful; their platform opportunity comes from putting services where users already spend time in messaging.
- Messaging bots can reduce first-use friction by avoiding a separate download, account, and unfamiliar interface.
- [[Kik]] treated Bot Shop placement, scannable Kik Codes, Mentions, and Invites as discovery and social-distribution mechanisms unavailable or weaker in standalone app flows.
- A single chat app can consolidate access to many lightweight services and give those services immediate access to messaging context and the social graph.
- [[WeChat]] is used as evidence that messaging ecosystems can succeed through QR discovery, shared interfaces, payments, and instant app-like interactions without depending on open-ended conversation.
- The ecosystem was still incomplete: the source reports more than 20,000 Kik bots and almost two billion bot messages since April, while acknowledging no killer bot and naming payments as a missing platform layer.

## Key Quotes
> "The good news is that bots are useful even without the conversational element." - on separating bot utility from humanlike dialogue.

> "Instead, there's just a whole lot of instant interactions." - on the author's reading of WeChat.

## Contradictions
- Qualifies [[chat-is-the-new-browser-ted-livingston-medium]]: this earlier August 2016 article already rejects conversation and near-term app replacement as the core bot thesis, while Livingston's December article again uses broader browser and app-platform language.
- Reinforces [[chatbots-a-misleading-term-we-should-stop-using-botnerds-medium]], [[chatbots-were-the-next-big-thing-what-happened]], and [[chatbots-what-happened-chatbots-life]] on the gap between conversational expectations and first-wave NLP, but predates their stronger ecosystem and usability postmortems.
