## Summary
This essay introduces [[ObjectOrientedOntology]] through [[MartinHeidegger]]'s broken hammer example and [[GrahamHarman]]'s claim that objects withdraw from every relation. It then uses Harman's real/sensual object and quality schema to explain art as a tension between hidden reality and sensuous experience. The essay's central claim is that [[MetaphorAesthetics]] is theatrical: because the real object named by a metaphor still withdraws, the reader becomes the substitute real object that performs the metaphor's sensuous qualities.

## Key Claims
- [[ObjectWithdrawal]] means objects cannot be exhausted by use, perception, theory, or relation.
- Harman's schema distinguishes real objects, sensual objects, real qualities, and sensual qualities so that object relations can be discussed without reducing things to appearances.
- [[ArtAsObjectTension]] arises when art joins a withdrawn real object with sensuous qualities, creating tension between what appears and what remains hidden.
- [[MetaphorAesthetics]] does not fully reveal the object named by the metaphor; instead, the experiencer performs the metaphor by temporarily standing in as the real object.
- The article uses [[VincentVanGogh]]'s shoes, Homer's "wine-dark sea," and poetic quotation to argue that aesthetic experience depends on participation rather than detached observation.

## Key Quotes
> "每一个隐喻，都是一个小小的剧场；每一次审美体验，都是一次崭新的出演。" — The coda's concise statement of the essay's theatrical view of metaphor.

> "在这个隐喻之中，作为松树出演的我们，就是真正的物自体。" — The essay's strongest formulation of readerly substitution in metaphor.
