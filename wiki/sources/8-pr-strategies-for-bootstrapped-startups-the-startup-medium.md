## Summary
[[JosiahHumphrey]] argues that bootstrapped startups can still practice disciplined [[PublicRelationsStrategy]] without hiring a PR firm. The article frames PR as goal-led communication work that shapes how customers, investors, talent, government actors, and media publics perceive a company, then turns that into practical founder tactics: define the PR goal, write a one-sentence pitch, personalize media outreach, target relevant publications, keep a journalist list, use non-launch news angles, guest blog, and respond to source requests. Its image evidence is mostly decorative export art; the tiny pitch, guest-post, and HARO screenshots support the article's examples but are too low-resolution to add materially beyond the surrounding prose.

## Key Claims
- Bootstrapped startups should treat PR as strategic communication for specific audiences rather than as generic publicity.
- A PR campaign should begin with an explicit goal, such as product-launch attention, hiring, fundraising visibility, or entering a new location.
- A strong one-sentence pitch should name the customer problem, target group, and distinctive value without buzzwords or technical clutter.
- Media outreach works better when founders personalize short pitches, contact well-matched journalists one at a time, respect timing, test subject lines, and follow up politely.
- Startups should research niche-relevant publications and maintain journalist/editor contact lists instead of defaulting to famous technology outlets.
- PR should be continuous: fundraising, feature releases, industry shifts, customer data, guest posts, and reverse-pitching services can all create newsworthy opportunities beyond launch day.

## Key Quotes
> "What, exactly, do I want to achieve with my company's PR initiatives?" - on defining the campaign goal before outreach.

> "Journalists and editors love data" - on why customer or market data can make non-launch pitches stronger.
