## Summary
This practitioner essay argues that startups should choose acquisition channels from customer economics, buying behavior, and product mechanics rather than test a stream of unrelated tactics. It reduces scalable growth to paid acquisition, virality, search, sales, and rare partnerships, then maps enterprise, SMB, category-search, content, referral, community, and visible-use motions to different products. The framework pairs a long-term channel with scrappy early tactics and warns that a plausible channel usually needs sustained time, focus, iteration, or capital before it can be judged.

## Key Claims
- Scalable acquisition is concentrated in a small set of channel families, so novelty is less important than fit, execution, and economics.
- [[CustomerLifetimeValue]], [[CustomerAcquisitionCost]], payback time, price, margin, and product structure eliminate channels that cannot economically support a business.
- A failed first test does not necessarily invalidate a channel; teams should judge whether performance is within striking range before investing in iteration.
- Enterprise products generally move from [[FounderLedSales]] and warm networks toward a repeatable sales organization, while lower-value products cannot support the same human effort per account.
- Existing search intent favors paid search and SEO, while problem-aware but solution-unaware demand favors answer-oriented content with a direct conversion path.
- Passionate professional audiences can support authority-building content and owned-audience nurture, whereas low-friction mass products more often support direct-response advertising.
- [[ViralLoops]] work best when collaboration, payment, sharing, public use, or another existing social behavior naturally exposes the product; incentives should be tested against LTV, cannibalization, and low-quality cohorts.
- Scrappy tactics can create initial traction while SEO, sales, content, or paid acquisition matures, but those tactics should not be mistaken for indefinitely scalable distribution.

## Key Quotes
> "Your first attempt didn't work." - on distinguishing one failed execution from an invalid acquisition channel.

> "think about how your customers buy" - the article's core channel-selection principle.

## Contradictions
- The source calls cold calling the only proven SMB sales method and says every large social network grew through virality; both are broad 2018 practitioner generalizations rather than exhaustive comparative findings.
- The suggested 3:1 LTV-to-CAC ratio, six-month payback target, 12-18 month SEO horizon, enterprise sales cycles, and dialing volumes are useful planning heuristics rather than universal thresholds.
- Several "scrappy" examples involve spam, manufactured questions, voting manipulation, unauthorized use, or platform gaming. They may violate current policies, ethics, consent expectations, or search rules and are not endorsed as durable practice.
- The framework assumes product-market fit while also acknowledging rare companies that achieved scale through distribution despite weak customer value, so product quality is a strong default condition rather than a logical necessity.
