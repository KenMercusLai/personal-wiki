## Summary
[[ChrisBailey]] argues that [[WorkBreaks]] are not lost productivity time but a way to replenish energy, restore focus, make creative connections, and reassess goals. The article recommends different break rhythms across the day: morning work blocks of about 90 minutes followed by at least 20 minutes off, afternoon Pomodoro cycles, and a general starting heuristic of about 15 minutes of break time per hour worked.

## Key Claims
- Knowledge workers usually need more breaks than they already take because focus and energy deplete during continuous work.
- Morning focus can follow roughly 90-minute wakefulness cycles, so work can be organized into 90-minute chunks with at least 20-minute breaks.
- In the afternoon, a Pomodoro rhythm of 25 minutes of single-task work plus 5-minute breaks, with a longer fourth break, can structure recovery.
- DeskTime's reported top-worker pattern of 52 minutes working and 17 minutes breaking supports a broader rule of about a 15-minute break per hour.
- Breaks improve productivity by restoring attention and energy rather than merely reducing time on task.

## Key Quotes
> "you should take more breaks than you already are" - takeaway

> "productivity isn't about how much we do" - on output versus accomplishment
