## Summary
[[NeilCybart]] argues that [[Apple]]'s 2018 growth story had returned through three uneven levers: higher [[IPhone]] average selling prices, growing Services revenue, and accelerating [[WearableComputing]] adoption through [[AppleWatch]] and [[AirPods]]. The article says iPhone was then the largest contributor to year-over-year revenue growth, but also the most vulnerable to slowing ASP gains, while Services and wearables were likely to become more important growth engines. Its embedded revenue chart supports the return-to-growth claim by showing Apple TTM revenue climbing from roughly $215B in 4Q16 to about $255B in 3Q18, with a callout that revenue was up 17% over seven quarters.

## Key Claims
- [[AppleGrowthLevers]] in 2018 were iPhone ASP expansion, Services growth, and wearables adoption, but those levers were not equally durable.
- [[IPhone]] accounted for 57% of Apple's year-over-year revenue growth for the twelve months ending June 2018, mostly through higher pricing rather than strong unit-demand acceleration.
- Services accounted for 23% of year-over-year revenue growth, driven by digital content distribution, licensing, AppleCare, iCloud storage, and Apple Pay.
- [[WearableComputing]] accounted for 11% of year-over-year growth, with Apple Watch and AirPods moving beyond footnote status.
- Cybart expected iPhone ASP gains to slow because repeating a roughly $100 year-over-year ASP increase would be difficult after the iPhone X and iPhone 8 cycle.
- Services and wearables were framed as more durable future growth engines because Apple's installed base, digital content distribution, and low Apple Watch/AirPods penetration left room for expansion.
- Apple was not betting on iPhone forever; Cybart argues Apple wants to create iPhone alternatives such as Apple Watch and possible glasses that start as iPhone-supported devices and move toward independence.

## Key Quotes
> "Apple's three primary growth levers are not created equal." - central framing of the growth-driver comparison.

> "Apple wants to be the one to disrupt the iPhone." - summary of the self-disruption thesis.
