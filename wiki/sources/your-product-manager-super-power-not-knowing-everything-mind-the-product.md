## Summary
[[MindTheProduct]] argues that a product manager's advantage is not encyclopedic expertise but enough system-level judgment to ask useful questions, challenge assumptions, and help specialists align around product value. The article turns that humility into [[ValueBasedProductScoping]]: interrogate why each component is needed, use repeated "why" questions to reach the underlying motive, separate work into independently valuable deliverables, and prefer releases that create value or learning over risky big-bang delivery.

## Key Claims
- A product manager should act more like a conductor than a performer of every specialist role, understanding the whole without pretending to know every detail.
- Admitting uncertainty creates room to question assumptions and explore the problem before committing to a solution.
- Questions about value and outcomes are often more useful than questions about component mechanics.
- Repeated "why" questions can uncover the root motive behind a requested component.
- Teams should identify the minimum independent deliverables, including their ordering, parallelism, and whether their value is incremental or only emerges when combined.
- A smaller release is worthwhile when it delivers value or produces valuable learning.
- Product scope should begin with the foundational capabilities required by the product vision, then add sophistication in layers.

## Key Quotes
> "If we had half the time, what could we get away with not building?" - on testing whether proposed scope is necessary.

> "Relish being the new person" - Dave Wascha on using unfamiliarity as permission to ask questions.
