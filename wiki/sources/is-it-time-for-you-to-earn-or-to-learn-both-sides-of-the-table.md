## Summary
[[MarkSuster]] proposes the [[EarnLearnCareerFramework]]: before accepting a startup role, decide whether its primary return should be near-term wealth or career capital. Most non-founder and non-executive startup roles should be evaluated for responsibility, skills, network, mission, and future options rather than assumed to be a retirement-making equity event, while anyone seeking wealth-scale upside should scrutinize ownership, seniority, vesting, capitalization, and life-stage capacity for risk.

## Key Claims
- A candidate should distinguish an “earn” role from a “learn” role and judge the opportunity by the return it can realistically provide.
- A small employee option grant can produce modest proceeds even in a positive acquisition because ownership percentage, time to exit, vesting, tax, dilution, and liquidation preferences all reduce realizable value.
- Wealth-scale startup outcomes usually require founding the company or holding one of its most senior roles; ordinary middle-management equity is closer to uncertain upside than a dependable financial plan.
- A worthwhile learning role can provide stronger peers and investors, greater responsibility, specific industry or technical skills, partnership relationships, mission, enjoyment, and a more valuable resume.
- Startup leaders damage culture when they sell employment through speculative option riches rather than work, development, mission, and fair cash pay.
- Candidates should inspect the management option pool and ownership structure, especially in a spinout where a parent company or founder may retain most of the equity while employees do the remaining work.
- Age, dependents, mortgage obligations, skills, ambition, and financial position change whether founding a company or taking another high-risk role is responsible.

## Key Quotes
> "Learn now to earn later." - Suster's summary of career capital as a precursor to future leverage.

> "Stock options are the icing on the cake." - advice not to treat speculative equity as the primary reason to join.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction was found. The source reinforces the wiki's treatment of startup equity as high-variance and illiquid while adding career learning as a separate source of value.
- The numerical examples are illustrative 2009 assumptions, not forecasts or current benchmarks. They simplify dilution, strike price, exercise cost, tax treatment, preference structure, exit distributions, acceleration, and the probability and timing of liquidity; the asserted average exit and funding rates are not sourced in the article.
- The framework can become a false binary because one role may provide salary, learning, ownership upside, mission, and lifestyle value simultaneously. Founders and senior executives also face substantial failure risk, so seniority increases potential upside rather than guaranteeing an “earn” outcome.
