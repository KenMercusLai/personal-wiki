## Summary
[[FredWilson]] recalls [[JeffLawson]]'s 2008 seed pitch for [[Twilio]]: Lawson compressed the product into the claim that telephony had been reduced to five API calls, then answered Wilson's disbelief with a [[LiveProductDemo]] that made Wilson's phone ring within about 30 seconds. The anecdote presents a concise value proposition plus immediate executable proof as a powerful form of [[StartupVisionSelling]], while providing no detail about the rest of the fundraising process or whether this pattern generalizes.

## Key Claims
- Lawson described Twilio's value as reducing the complex world of telephony to five API calls.
- When Wilson challenged that claim, Lawson enumerated the calls and immediately live-coded an application.
- The demo used Wilson's own phone number and produced a ringing phone within about 30 seconds, turning an abstract platform claim into direct evidence.
- Wilson regarded the encounter as the best seed pitch he had received.
- The story links Twilio's early developer-facing simplicity to the company reaching its IPO in 2016, but it does not establish that the pitch or demo caused that later outcome.

## Key Quotes
> "we have taken the entire messy and complex world of telephony and reduced it to five API calls" - Lawson's compressed product claim.

> "you can stop there. that's amazing" - Wilson's response after the live-coded demo made his phone ring.
