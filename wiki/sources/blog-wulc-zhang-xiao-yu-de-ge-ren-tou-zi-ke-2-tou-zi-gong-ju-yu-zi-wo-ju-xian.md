## Summary
[[Wulc]] summarizes the investment-tools and self-limitation sections of [[ZhangXiaoyu]]'s personal investing course. The article argues that ordinary investors should prefer diversified, low-cost, understandable products but must still respect valuation, fees, market context, behavioral overconfidence, incentive conflicts, and outside-life constraints. Wulc adds important qualifications: index-fund advice is more directly applicable to U.S. markets than to A-share markets, and any single course should be treated as one perspective rather than a complete investing system.

## Key Claims
- [[IndexFundStrategy]] can reduce stock-picking difficulty, but U.S.-market index-fund lessons should not be copied mechanically into A-share markets.
- Good companies or good indexes can still be poor investments when bought at excessive prices, so [[InvestmentRiskDiscipline]] needs valuation and margin-of-safety discipline.
- Trading costs and fund fees compound against investors over long horizons, making cost control a practical edge for ordinary investors.
- [[BehavioralRiskJudgment]] should distinguish luck from skill, record investment logic before acting, and use stop-loss or pause rules when markets invalidate the thesis.
- High-risk investments must justify the possibility of total loss with genuinely asymmetric upside; otherwise negative compounding can erase years of gains.
- Investment advice should be filtered through incentives, emotional pressure, and the investor's own situation, including the need for independent decision space and non-investment income.
- Zhang Xiaoyu's five principles for ordinary investors are diversification, passive-first implementation, lower costs, patience, and not investing in what one does not understand.

## Key Quotes
> "好公司不等于好股票" - course takeaway on why price matters.

> "越不需要靠投资赚钱的人，越有可能靠投资赚到钱" - course framing for outside income as an investing advantage.

> "多元分散，被动为主，降低成本，保持恒心，不懂不做" - the five-principle summary.
