## Summary
Daniel Rodic analyzes [[TonyHsieh]]'s publicly shared 2015 schedule and presents two linked operating ideas: [[Yesterbox]] turns yesterday's email into a bounded processing queue, while [[OrganizationalTransparency]] gives employees more of the context needed to decide without attending every meeting. The schedule figures and year-over-year comparison are useful descriptive observations, but the article does not provide the raw analysis in the saved source or isolate Holacracy as the cause of workload changes.

## Key Claims
- From January through November 2015, the analysis estimates that Hsieh worked about 62.5 weekday hours and 75 total weekly hours, typically starting between 7:00 and 9:00 a.m.
- Email occupied about 35% of his time, or four hours per day, and meetings about 24%, or three hours per day; these are descriptive schedule allocations, not evidence that the pattern is optimal.
- [[Yesterbox]] treats yesterday's inbox as today's finite task list, handles very short replies or delegation immediately, and relies on a chief-of-staff team to triage volume that one executive cannot process alone.
- Calendar blocks complement email triage by reserving time for proactive, long-term, and intensive work while exposing capacity already committed to travel and meetings.
- Responsive managers can reduce execution delay when quick acknowledgment or delegation keeps questions moving, but high responsiveness can also create interruption and volume if norms and ownership are unclear.
- [[OrganizationalTransparency]] can reduce duplicated questions and unnecessary attendance when people can inspect decisions, priorities, calendars, and meeting context directly.
- The article reports 54% fewer emails and 15% fewer work hours per day in October-November 2015 than in the same months of 2014 after Zappos's company-wide Holacracy rollout, but explicitly says the change cannot be fully attributed to Holacracy.

## Key Quotes
> "Basically, his ‘to-do list’ today is yesterday’s inbox." - on bounding email processing through Yesterbox.

> "If you want your team to be able to make the same decisions as you do, it starts by them having access to the same information you have access to." - on transparency as decision infrastructure.

## Connections
- [[TonyHsieh]] - executive whose public schedule, email workflow, meeting load, and transparency practices are analyzed.
- [[Zappos]] - company context for customer service, Holacracy, and radical-transparency claims.
- [[Yesterbox]] - email-processing method built around the previous day's inbox.
- [[EmailTaskManagement]] - broader practice of turning messages into explicit, reviewable obligations.
- [[OrganizationalTransparency]] - access to operating context as a basis for trust and decentralized decisions.
- [[ScalingCommunication]] - durable shared context can substitute for some meeting attendance and repeated questions.
- [[PersonalProductivity]] - calendars, capacity limits, batching, delegation, and response rules shape the described workflow.

## Contradictions
- The source's inbox-zero orientation differs from the Jeff Huang workflow summarized in [[EmailTaskManagement]], which uses flags and bounded daily planning without making an empty inbox the objective.
- The claimed post-Holacracy efficiency change is an uncontrolled before-and-after comparison during two selected months. It does not establish that Holacracy, transparency, seasonality, staffing, delegation, or another factor caused the decrease.
- The article contains inconsistent throughput figures: it says Hsieh claimed ten messages per 30 minutes, then cites 2014 data suggesting roughly nine per hour. Neither figure is reproducible from the saved article alone.
