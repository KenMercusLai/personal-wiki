## Summary
This LatePost interview presents [[WangTao]]'s 20-year account of building [[DJI]] from a flight-control startup into a global hardware company, then spending eight years rebuilding the organization after corruption, internal fragmentation, and founder immaturity became visible. The article's central arc is Wang's shift from product genius, ego, and competition toward reflection, [[EntropyReductionManagement]], disciplined recruiting, bounded growth, and a company mission framed as seeking truth together.

## Key Claims
- [[WangTao]] reframes his old "world is unbelievably stupid" posture into a self-critical view: the world can improve, and he also needed to mature.
- [[DJI]]'s early advantage came from combining flight control, gimbals, image transmission, vision, and cameras into a consumer drone stack that enlarged a previously niche market.
- Wang says product work felt much easier than management; DJI's 2017-2018 crisis exposed corruption, factional "feudal" units, weak oversight, and the limits of a product-only culture.
- The source presents management as continuous entropy reduction: clear goals, transparent processes, measurable results, accountable contribution, better managers, and systems that prevent organizational drift.
- DJI's newer stance toward departed employees, internal incubation, outside investment, and hiring treats talent flow as a reality to shape rather than only a threat to block.
- Wang now frames founder work as aligning personal and company visions around truth-seeking, not merely winning, innovation theater, or founder ego.
- Inspected images reinforce the narrative: early DJI photos show improvised hardware origins, a 2009 email screenshot shows early recruiting and culture concerns, and Sky City sketches connect Wang's imagination to DJI's later headquarters identity.

## Key Quotes
> "世界可以好很多，我也还能好很多。" - on revising his old public posture.

> "做产品对我来说难度是 1 分，管理大概是 10 分。" - on product ability versus management learning.

> "创始人最大的创造，就是把个人的愿景和公司统一。" - on founder-company alignment.
