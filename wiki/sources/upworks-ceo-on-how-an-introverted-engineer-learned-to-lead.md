## Summary
[[StephaneKasriel]] describes moving from an introverted engineer who expected to write code into the CEO role at [[Upwork]]. The excerpt presents leadership as learned adaptation: he made uncomfortable career moves, studied strategy and management, turned large networking events into measurable tasks, and learned that executive work often means listening, creating motivation, empowering employees, and supplying resources rather than immediately solving every problem as an engineer. The inspected image is a studio portrait of Kasriel; it adds no independent evidence and was omitted as decorative portraiture.

## Key Claims
- Introversion need not prevent executive leadership, but high-social-load settings may require deliberate structures such as concrete conversation and follow-up goals.
- Technical professionals can expand into leadership by seeking roles and projects outside their comfort zones rather than treating technical identity as a fixed boundary.
- Wide reading, online courses, and formal business education can supplement experience in strategy, people management, and entrepreneurship.
- CEO work includes helping employees feel excited about their work, empowering them, and giving them needed resources.
- An engineer's instinct to solve a presented problem can be counterproductive in management; listening must come before diagnosis or intervention.

## Key Quotes
> "Getting by in a crowd doesn’t come naturally" - on the social demands Kasriel learned to structure.

> "He must listen first" - on resisting immediate engineer-style problem solving as a CEO.
