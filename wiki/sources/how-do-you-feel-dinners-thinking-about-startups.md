## Summary
The essay proposes a recurring, equal-status founder check-in that moves in order from personal life, to work, to desired changes. By surfacing feelings and personal motivations before debating plans, roles, or strategy, the practice aims to expose the underlying concerns for which operational proposals are often proxies. A closing case also shows the limit of the ritual: clarity can reveal that founders have drifted too far apart to continue, making an orderly separation preferable to another year of resentment and lost friendship.

## Key Claims
- Founder teams become less effective when members drift out of sync, accumulate grudges, or interpret disagreement as a challenge to status.
- The proposed dinner uses three ordered questions: how each person feels about personal life, how each feels about work, and what each thinks the team should do differently.
- One person answers one question at a time while the others listen and ask only clarifying questions; no chair or secretary is appointed, preserving equal participation.
- Starting with personal feelings can reveal that disputes about titles, responsibilities, hiring, travel, fundraising, or technical plans are proxies for envy, family pressure, fear, exclusion, overwork, or unequal responsibility.
- The final change question turns surfaced concerns into discussion and notes only after participants have established personal and work context.
- The format is adaptable: the author's later company used every-second-month lunches when childcare and fatigue made evening wine gatherings impractical.
- A candid alignment conversation may repair coordination, but it may also establish that founders differ irreconcilably on product direction, near-term strategy, personal goals, and motivation.

## Key Quotes
> "How do you feel about your personal life?" - the first question in the check-in sequence

> "titles or responsibilities" - examples of work disputes that may proxy for personal concerns

> "They had drifted too far apart." - the conclusion of the closing founder-separation case
