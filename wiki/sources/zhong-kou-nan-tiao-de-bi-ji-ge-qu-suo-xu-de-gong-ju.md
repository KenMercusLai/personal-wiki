## Summary
[[ChrisGrieser]] argues that note size is not a universal best-practice question but a relationship among [[NoteGranularity]], knowledge goals, and tool affordances. Small notes suit bottom-up idea development through dense links, metadata, graph views, templates, and fast switching, while big notes suit top-down organization of existing knowledge through outlines, headings, folding, visual anchors, and text-moving tools. The article frames [[ZettelkastenMethod]] as one small-note tradition, but concludes that the deeper issue is [[NoteToolFit]]: tools are not neutral because they encourage different writing and organization habits.

## Key Claims
- [[NoteGranularity]] matters less as an abstract rule than as a practical choice between bottom-up idea development and top-down organization of existing knowledge.
- Small-note systems rely on dense internal links, metadata, graph views, templates, quick capture, and fast note switching to turn many atomic notes into usable structure.
- Big-note systems rely on headings, outlines, folding, visual anchors, block or heading links, and text-transport tools to make long notes navigable and revisable.
- Modern note software narrows the operational gap between small and big notes because headings, block links, embedded notes, search, and structured maps can move atomicity above or below the file level.
- [[NoteToolFit]] is central because note methods work better when the chosen software supports their natural structure, navigation, and maintenance patterns.

## Key Quotes
> "能够写更长的笔记并不一定意味着我们应该这样做" - on digital freedom not automatically settling note-size choices.

> "工具并非中立的" - on note methods depending on the tools that enact them.
