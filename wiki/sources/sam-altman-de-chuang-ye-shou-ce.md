## Summary
In a Chinese translation of his Startup Playbook, [[SamAltman]] presents [[YCombinator]]'s early-stage operating doctrine as four linked requirements: a strong idea in a potentially large market, a capable and resilient founding team, a product that a small group genuinely loves, and focused execution that turns that product into a durable company. The playbook makes direct customer contact the bridge between idea testing and [[ProductMarketFit]], then connects retention, growth cadence, hiring, management, profitability, cash control, and fundraising to the startup's stage.

## Key Claims
- A startup should first make something a small group loves intensely; broad mild approval, publicity, registrations, or founder self-deception cannot substitute for repeated use, referrals, retention, and willingness to pay.
- Ideas should be tested through observed behavior: consumer teams should ship the smallest useful experience, business teams should try to sell before building, and hard-technology teams should test the smallest technical subset after speaking with prospective customers.
- Founders need determination, resourcefulness, clear communication, speed, and complementary ability to build and sell; a bad cofounder is worse than founding alone.
- [[DoingThingsThatDoNotScale]] means manually recruiting early users, observing them directly, and serving them closely enough to power a fast product-improvement loop—not postponing viable economics indefinitely.
- Growth should be a company priority, but retained users, transparent operating metrics, unit economics, and repeated customer value matter more than vanity registrations or growth bought at a loss.
- Execution requires focus and speed, while the CEO remains responsible for vision, mission, senior hiring, capital, operating standards, morale, and solving problems without treating missing resources as an excuse.
- Hiring, monetization, cash management, and fundraising are stage-sensitive: hire slowly and maintain quality, reach survival-level profitability where possible, watch cash flow, and raise when capital can accelerate a fundamentally good company.

## Key Quotes
> "做一个少数用户真心热爱的产品，要比做一个很多用户有些喜欢的产品要好很多。" - on prioritizing intensity of early demand over breadth.

> "这一切归根结底是因为执行。" - on why ideas become companies only through sustained operating work.

## Contradictions
- The playbook says co-located work is effectively required for exceptional early-startup outcomes. [[RemoteWork]] documents Buffer, Zapier, 37signals, and Gumroad as counterexamples in which distributed work can function when trust, written communication, boundaries, and operating design are deliberate; neither side supplies a controlled comparison.
- Its recommendation that two founders use near-equal equity with one holding a slight majority is explicitly qualified by the translator as potentially inappropriate in China and is not supported with jurisdiction-specific legal or governance evidence.
- The source sometimes uses categorical or numerically precise advice—such as ignoring competitors 99% of the time, spending about 25% of CEO time on recruiting, or tying direct-sales affordability to a $500 LTV boundary—without datasets, category controls, or outcome comparisons. These are practitioner heuristics, not universal thresholds.
