## Summary
[[JessicaLivingston]] presents a seven-part failure-avoidance framework drawn from observing more than 1,000 [[YCombinator]] startups: make something people want, stay focused on product and users, keep gender-related noise from displacing execution, measure growth, know whether the company is [[DefaultAlive]], keep expenses low, and expect later fundraising to require performance rather than promise. The advice links customer conversation and idea evolution to growth and runway discipline, but it remains an accelerator cofounder's practitioner synthesis rather than comparative evidence that its thresholds or causal claims generalize across startup types.

## Key Claims
- Founders should begin with a problem they experience, then quickly test beyond themselves by building, talking to users, and changing the idea as evidence accumulates; [[Airbnb]]'s move from hosted airbeds to whole-place stays illustrates that evolution.
- Product and user work should outrank investor coffees, acquisition conversations, networking, advisory boards, speculative partnerships, premature PR, social-media disputes, and most conferences unless those activities clear a high opportunity-cost bar.
- Livingston acknowledges real obstacles for women founders but argues that media and social controversy can become an additional distraction; starting and building successful companies is presented as both the individual response and a route to more visible role models.
- Revenue growth is proposed as the strongest test of user demand, with at least 10% monthly growth offered as a startup benchmark and explicit targets used to challenge founder denial.
- Growth alone does not establish survival: a company is default alive only if unchanged expenses and continued revenue growth reach breakeven before cash runs out.
- Overhiring reduces the time available for inevitable delays and can force a company to fundraise while its promise has not yet become visible performance; founders should hire for a pessimistic case.
- Later fundraising is structurally harder than seed fundraising because Series A investors seek demonstrated performance and a path to an outsized outcome rather than promise alone.

## Key Quotes
> "Your users are your guidepost." - Livingston on building, talking to users, and allowing the idea to evolve.

> "Denial is the silent killer of startups." - context for using growth and cash-survival measures as forcing functions.

> "Seed investors are looking for promise. Series A investors are looking for performance." - on why running low on cash is not itself a fundraising strategy.

## Contradictions
- Qualifies [[StartupGrowthSignalQuality]] by treating at least 10% monthly growth and revenue as strong diagnostic signals; the existing YC Demo Day critique shows that base size, duration, retention, acquisition cause, and unit economics are still needed before a rate supports durable-demand claims.
- Qualifies [[DefaultAlive]] because its projection assumes expenses stay fixed and recent revenue growth continues; it is a forcing question, not a reliable forecast under volatile growth, churn, margins, hiring, or financing conditions.
- The advice about women founders acknowledges harder conditions but supplies no gender-disaggregated outcomes or comparison capable of showing that structural barriers rarely determine success or failure. Treating public discussion mainly as noise may underweight discrimination, access, safety, caregiving, capital, and network constraints even when focus remains useful individual advice.
- The 10% monthly benchmark, revenue preference, pessimistic hiring rule, and seed-versus-Series-A distinction come from Livingston's practitioner experience and are not validated here across bootstrapped, enterprise, regulated, hardware, research-intensive, or deliberately slow-growing companies.
