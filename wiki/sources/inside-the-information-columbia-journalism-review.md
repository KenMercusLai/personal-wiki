## Summary
Merissa Marr profiles [[JessicaLessin]] and [[TheInformation]] as an early test of premium, reader-funded technology journalism: fewer exclusive or deeply reported stories, a $399 annual subscription, community benefits, disciplined spending, and expansion from a concentrated professional audience. The 2016 account reports cash-flow positivity and rapid subscription growth while exposing the model's central tension: the relationships and member access that support acquisition and retention can also weaken, or appear to weaken, editorial independence.

## Key Claims
- [[TheInformation]] differentiated itself from ad-supported volume publishing through a hard paywall, roughly two stories a day, exclusive reporting, and deep or data-driven analysis.
- The standard membership combined reporting with forums, a private Slack channel, informal events, dinners, screenings, and direct contact with [[JessicaLessin]], making community part of the paid product.
- The publication was reported as cash-flow positive after nearly three years; its subscriptions had more than doubled in the prior year, although Lessin withheld the count and an outside estimate placed membership near 10,000.
- A $10,000 invitation-only investor tier added private calls and in-person briefings, illustrating price segmentation by professional value and access.
- Lessin used family money, retained ownership, spent conservatively, and planned to finance near-term growth through subscriptions and her own capital.
- The acquisition strategy began with a dense Silicon Valley network, tested the offer with six representative prospects, and aimed to become indispensable to one core audience before expanding by sector and geography.
- Close social ties to technology leaders and subscriber access to reporters create an [[AccessJournalism]] risk even when tough reporting is itself necessary to retain credibility.

## Key Quotes
> "You have to become indispensable to a core audience first and then find related or adjacent audiences." - Lessin on the proposed sequence for scaling a focused subscription publication.

> "You earn respect by being tough, not writing puff pieces." - Lessin on the commercial value of editorial credibility.

## Connections
- [[JessicaLessin]] - founder, owner, editor, chief executive, and central subject of the profile.
- [[TheInformation]] - premium technology-news publication whose early model and growth are examined.
- [[NicheSubscriptionPublishing]] - concrete case of combining scarce professional reporting, premium pricing, and member community for a focused audience.

## Contradictions
- The source complicates simple incentive-alignment claims about reader funding: subscriptions reduce dependence on advertisers, but a membership built around access to the covered industry can create a different form of commercial and social pressure.
- Subscriber count, investment, churn, acquisition cost, margins, and cohort retention are undisclosed; cash-flow positivity and growth are reported as a 2016 snapshot, while the roughly 10,000-member figure is an outside estimate.
