## Summary
The [[ChristensenInstitute]] presents [[JobsToBeDone]] as a lens for understanding the circumstances that move people or organizations toward and away from a decision. Instead of explaining choice mainly through demographics, product attributes, or a direct request for reasons, the framework asks what progress someone is trying to make and examines the functional, social, and emotional forces surrounding that effort. Examples from housing, school technology, milkshake purchases, Nigerian solar adoption, health care, and microschools illustrate the range of application, but the page supplies no methods, comparative studies, or outcome measurements for those cases.

## Key Claims
- People and organizations pull products or services into their lives to make progress toward a goal or aspiration in particular circumstances.
- A Job to Be Done combines functional, social, and emotional forces rather than reducing choice to a product feature or demographic category.
- Jobs can change when circumstances change, as illustrated by a school district moving from compliance concerns toward leadership and educational-outcome aspirations.
- JTBD research seeks a decision story and recurring circumstances across a group, not merely a respondent's direct answer to why a purchase occurred.
- The milkshake example suggests that the relevant job may be stress relief through a convenient snack rather than flavor preference alone.
- The Christensen Institute applies the lens to slow solar adoption in Nigeria, patient and consumer needs in health care, and parental movement toward microschools.

## Key Quotes
> "People don't simply buy or pick products or services; they pull them into their lives to make progress." - the page's central progress framing.

> "Jobs to Be Done is a lens that reveals the circumstances—or forces—that drive people and organizations toward and away from decisions." - definition of the framework.

## Connections
- [[ChristensenInstitute]] - publisher and practitioner applying the theory to education, health care, and economic-development questions.
- [[JobsToBeDone]] - central framework for interpreting choices through desired progress and circumstance.
- [[CustomerLedProductDevelopment]] - JTBD adds circumstance-based decision stories to customer conversations, observed behavior, and demand tests.
- [[ProductUserSegmentation]] - recurring circumstances can define decision-relevant groups that differ from demographic or product-usage segments.
- [[ProductMarketFit]] - understanding the progress sought by customers can refine the problem and value proposition being tested.

## Contradictions
- The source qualifies feature-, demographic-, and direct-question-led research rather than proving those methods useless; demographics and attributes may still matter when they correlate with access, constraints, preferences, or outcomes.
- Its cases are brief illustrations from an institution advocating the framework. The page provides no interview protocol, sampling detail, comparison with alternative research methods, adoption results, or evidence that JTBD analysis caused better innovation outcomes.
