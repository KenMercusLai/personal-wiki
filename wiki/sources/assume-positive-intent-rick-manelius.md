## Summary
[[RickManelius]] argues that beginning with [[AssumePositiveIntent]] can make tense professional relationships more efficient, collaborative, and solution-focused. The article grounds the point in a budget-crisis story where a stakeholder named Chris interrupted defensive contract conflict by asking both sides to assume positive intent. Manelius qualifies the advice by distinguishing trust-first collaboration from low-trust or harmful contexts where skepticism and boundaries are warranted.

## Key Claims
- [[AssumePositiveIntent]] can reduce defensive second-guessing and make hard business conversations more solution-focused.
- Starting from trust can unlock relationship efficiency that skepticism may delay or prevent.
- Positive intent is not a universal rule; prior trust violations, dangerous contexts, and bad-faith actors require stronger boundaries.
- The risk of some people exploiting trust must be weighed against the broader cost of approaching most relationships from distrust.
- [[TheSpeedOfTrust]] is cited as supporting evidence that high-trust relationships improve efficiency and effectiveness.

## Key Quotes
> "assume positive intent instead" — Chris's intervention during a tense project-budget phone call.

> "Both are feasible" — Manelius on skepticism-first versus trust-first approaches to new relationships.
