## Summary
The Better Humans article reframes Wikipedia's long list of cognitive biases as a smaller set of biased mental strategies grouped by the practical problems they solve: too much information, not enough meaning, the need to act fast, and deciding what to remember. It argues that biases are energy-saving tools with predictable tradeoffs: filtering drops useful signal, meaning-making creates illusions, fast action produces unfair or self-serving decisions, and memory processes reinforce earlier errors. The embedded Chainsawsuit comic reinforces the confirmation-bias theme, while John Manoogian III's Cognitive Bias Codex poster turns the taxonomy into a radial visual map.

## Key Claims
- [[CognitiveBiasTaxonomy]] becomes easier to remember when biases are grouped by the mental problem they address rather than by alphabetical or overlapping topical categories.
- Cognitive biases primarily save time and energy under information, meaning, action, and memory constraints.
- Information-overload biases make people notice primed, repeated, strange, changed, belief-confirming, or other-people-focused flaws.
- Meaning-making biases fill sparse evidence with stories, stereotypes, familiar preferences, simplified probabilities, mind-reading assumptions, and projections across time.
- Fast-action biases support confidence, immediacy, completion, autonomy, status preservation, and simple choices, but can also generate flawed decisions.
- Memory-related biases edit memories, discard specifics into generalities, reduce events to salient elements, and encode experiences unevenly.
- Biases are useful in some contexts and harmful in others, so improvement depends on noticing and appreciating the tools rather than imagining bias can disappear.

## Key Quotes
> "Every cognitive bias is there for a reason" - on treating biases as functional shortcuts.

> "Noise becomes signal." - summary of the information-filtering tradeoff.
