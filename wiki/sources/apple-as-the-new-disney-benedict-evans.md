## Summary
[[BenedictEvans]] argues that Apple's March 2019 services event should be read less as a pure TV announcement and more as a subscription, retention, and brand-positioning event. The article frames [[Apple]]'s News+, Arcade, Card, and TV+ moves as incremental services execution that can add revenue and reduce churn, but its stronger claim is that Apple was making trust, privacy, safety, curation, and freedom from scams into a more tangible consumer promise. Evans describes this as [[AppleTrustBrand]]: the old Apple promise was that technology would work, while the new promise was that technology would not exploit the user.

## Key Claims
- [[Apple]]'s 2019 services event was primarily a subscription event spanning news, games, credit, and TV rather than only a television announcement.
- The services can reduce churn and add incremental revenue by making iPhone users more likely to remain in Apple's ecosystem.
- Smartphone saturation made post-iPhone growth harder, so services and future projects became more important strategic conversations.
- [[AppleTrustBrand]] reframes Apple's brand promise around curation, privacy, safety, and protection from scams, tracking, manipulative ads, loot boxes, and confusing charges.
- Evans links the trust promise to a broader evolution of computing: as lower-level technical problems fade, higher-level harms such as adware, scamware, phishing, tracking, fraud, and addictive game mechanics become the problems platform owners try to solve.
- Apple's TV+ proposition was still underspecified in the event, but Evans reads its celebrity-heavy launch and family-friendly tone as another expression of Apple's trust-and-values positioning.

## Key Quotes
> "The new promise is you don't have to worry if the tech is scamming you." - Evans on Apple's updated brand promise.

> "Trusted, secure, private, no ads, no scams..." - Evans' shorthand for Apple as the Disney of tech.
