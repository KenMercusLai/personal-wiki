## Summary
[[AhmadIqbal]] explains how operating [[Nadeef]] led him from selling handheld bidets to building Shopify apps, especially [[Scout]], around the principle of loving the problem rather than the first solution. The source argues that manual customer conversations, especially phone calls to abandoned-checkout customers, produced better learning, higher recovery, and stronger retention than automated recovery tactics alone. It also contrasts validated, systematized workflows with an overbuilt handwritten-card product that failed because the team built the solution before testing merchant demand.

## Key Claims
- Founders should begin with a real market problem they understand personally, then test whether the problem is widespread and painful before committing years of effort.
- Calling abandoned-checkout customers helped Nadeef recover more sales and revealed purchase blockers that automated emails, popups, and retargeting could not explain.
- [[Scout]] began as a minimal alert workflow after the manual callback process had already worked; it was first an email notification with checkout details and a tap-to-call phone number.
- The handwritten-card app failed because the team invested in custom software and a robotic printer before manually validating merchant willingness to buy and operational feasibility.
- [[ProductMarketFit]] starts with market need and value delivery, not with feature breadth; the product is easier to change than the market.
- Shopify App Store discovery depended heavily on merchant support, positive reviews, and relevant keywords, making customer experience part of acquisition.
- Customer conversations increased [[CustomerLifetimeValue]] at Nadeef: eight of the top ten customers had originally abandoned checkout and were recovered by phone.
- [[RavenCallback]] and [[PizzaParty]] extended the same pattern: prove a customer-experience tactic in Nadeef, then systematize it as a Shopify app.

## Key Quotes
> "Fall in love with the problem, not the solution." - framing advice for the whole founder story.

> "Pick up the phone, and ask them one-on-one." - on diagnosing checkout abandonment.

> "Write 0 lines of new code" - retrospective lesson from the handwritten-card failure.
