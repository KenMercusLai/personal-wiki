## Summary
[[SensorTower]] reports that first-time No. 1 U.S. iPhone download-chart positions on the [[AppStore]] were usually short-lived in 2015 and 2016. Among 53 apps that first reached No. 1 during that period, 74% fell out of the top 25 within 30 days, only 8% remained after three months, and the longest-lasting examples were Color Switch, Bitmoji, and [[Snapchat]]. The article argues that developers should treat chart-topping paid acquisition as weak evidence of durable visibility and invest more in App Store Optimization, Apple featuring, platform-specific features, and timely content.

## Key Claims
- [[MobilePlatformDiscovery]] is volatile when it depends on App Store download-chart rank: 74% of first-time No. 1 iPhone apps in the study left the top 25 within a month.
- Chart-top visibility rarely persisted beyond the initial spike: only 25% of the 53 apps stayed in the top 25 for more than a month, six stayed after two months, and 8% stayed after three months.
- The sample covered 22 first-time No. 1 U.S. iPhone apps in 2015 and 31 in 2016.
- Large incumbent apps such as [[Facebook]], [[Instagram]], [[Snapchat]], and [[FacebookMessenger]] made long-term chart presence harder for most other apps because brand recognition and marketing budgets concentrated top-chart positions.
- The article recommends App Store Optimization and Apple featuring work over concentrating resources solely on reaching No. 1 through traditional user acquisition.

## Key Quotes
> "74 percent dropped out of the top 25 within a month" - headline retention finding.

> "merely 8 percent remained there after three months" - long-tail chart persistence finding.
