## Summary
[[NoahJessop]] presents 15 practical rules for founder-led selling, from warm introductions and concise scheduling messages through meeting control, prompt follow-up, qualification, persistence, and closing. The essay's central lesson is that product enthusiasm is not enough: founders must learn whether a prospect can buy within the startup's survival horizon, advance each interaction toward a concrete next step, and combine substantive product value with a compelling account of the customer's future.

## Key Claims
- Double opt-in introductions work best when the requester supplies forwardable context, replies first after the connection, and removes the introducer from the active thread.
- Specific proposed meeting times and questions answerable with yes, no, or concrete data reduce decision effort; short, plain messages should seek the right person or next conversation rather than close the whole deal by email.
- Early responsiveness and reliable follow-through act as trust signals when a prospect has little other evidence about the founder.
- A sales meeting should establish its purpose, the information needed, the obstacles to progress, and the best achievable next step; once that step is secured, continuing can put the gain at risk.
- Founders should qualify purchase timing and budget early, schedule follow-up while the meeting is fresh, send promised material promptly, and ask directly for the sale so hidden blockers become visible.
- Product substance needs a credible account of the customer's changed future; features alone may not create urgency for customers or investors.
- Persistence and slightly unconventional effort can distinguish an upstart, but tactics should remain proportionate to the prospect's context and should not become intrusive.
