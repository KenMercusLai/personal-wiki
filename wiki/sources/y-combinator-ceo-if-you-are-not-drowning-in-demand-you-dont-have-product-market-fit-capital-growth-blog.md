## Summary
An edited Capital & Growth AMA with [[MichaelSeibel]], who is introduced as CEO of [[YCombinator]] Core, the founder who sold Twitch to [[Amazon]] for $1 billion in 2014, and a founder of SocialCam before that. Seibel argues that product-market fit has one practical test — demand that overwhelms the team's capacity to serve it — and that YC companies most often fail by believing they have reached fit and scaling on that belief. The interview also gives YC's application filters, a rejection of contract developers for technology companies, a persistence-versus-stubbornness distinction, and a warning that most great companies never joined an accelerator.

## Key Claims
- Product-market fit means the product is used by more customers than the team can handle; without that, a founder does not have fit, because most people use the term too loosely.
- For YC companies the major cause of failure is believing that product-market fit has been attained and scaling expenses accordingly; for startups in general the leading cause is founder disputes.
- YC evaluates applications by looking for negatives as a quick filter, so the absence of negatives reads as a strong positive.
- The three turn-offs are poor communication (jargon that fails the "explain it to your grandmother" test), no technical founder who can build the MVP, and lack of speed, which usually signals lack of commitment.
- Building a billion-dollar technology company with contract developers is unacceptable; YC will accept missing business experience but not an inability to build good technology.
- Foolish stubbornness is refusing to iterate on the solution, while persistence keeps the customer and the problem fixed and changes the solution; passion for the problem is what makes fast pivoting possible.
- Accelerators carry real disadvantages, program quality varies enormously between the best and the tenth best, and most great companies never went through one.

## Key Quotes
> "My definition of product-market fit is: you are drowning in demand — your product is being used by so many customers that you cannot handle all the new people knocking at your door! Absent that you do not have product market fit." — Seibel on why the term is used too loosely.

> "I 1,000% disagree with using contract developers to try and build a billion-dollar company." — Seibel on the technical-founder requirement.

> "Foolish stubbornness comes not from working on the same problem, but from an unwillingness to iterate on the solution." — Seibel on persistence versus stubbornness.

> "We like backing people who've jumped off the ledge because it is a necessary condition for success." — Seibel on commitment as a founding signal.

> "If you want to be a successful founder, build a product your customers love!" — Seibel's closing advice.
