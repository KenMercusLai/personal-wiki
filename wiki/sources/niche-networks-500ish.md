## Summary
[[MGSiegler]] argues that smartphone and app saturation made it unusually difficult for a new general-purpose social network to displace established habits at massive scale in 2017. His alternative is [[NicheSocialNetworks]]: build around a narrowly defined passion, win a meaningful share of a small segment within the enormous smartphone population, and pursue monetization suited to that focused audience rather than treating billion-user advertising scale as the only viable outcome.

## Key Claims
- The obstacle facing new social networks is not merely product quality; saturated smartphone adoption and established social-app habits leave little discretionary time for another broad network.
- A major platform transition can reset incumbent advantages, but tablets, watches, televisions, virtual reality, bots, chat-based micro-apps, and voice had not yet created a mobile-scale reset in the article's 2017 view.
- App-store featuring and paid downloads cannot substitute for the sustained work required to establish a durable social habit.
- A narrow network can become substantial by capturing a small fraction of a massive smartphone-installed base rather than seeking broad appeal from the outset.
- Targeted passion is proposed as the mechanism for competing for limited user time: relevance and intensity may create a stronger habit than generic breadth.
- Focused audiences may support specialized advertising or non-advertising revenue, although the essay does not demonstrate which alternatives work or at what scale.
- Starting deliberately small requires patience and focus but does not preclude later expansion into adjacent audiences or products.

## Key Quotes
> "go granular. Niche, even." - Siegler's central product recommendation.

> "the true competition here is time" - the constraint behind the niche strategy.

## Contradictions
- The claim that the road to massive social-network scale was closed is a 2017 strategic judgment, not a demonstrated market law; later entrants, new formats, geographic expansion, regulation, or platform shifts could invalidate it.
- The essay infers app saturation and competition for time without supplying usage cohorts, retention data, market-share thresholds, or comparative outcomes for broad versus niche launches.
- Audience focus may improve relevance but does not itself solve cold start, moderation, retention, distribution, willingness to pay, advertising demand, or expansion without dilution.
