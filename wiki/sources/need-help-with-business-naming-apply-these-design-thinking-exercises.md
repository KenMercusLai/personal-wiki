## Summary
This 99designs article applies a four-stage design-thinking sequence—define, discover, develop, and deliver—to [[BusinessNaming]]. It begins with company mission and values, adds customer language, competitor patterns, and naming conventions, then uses high-volume collaborative ideation before filtering candidates for clarity, pronunciation, availability, and audience response. The method extends [[StartupBrandStrategy]] by treating the name as an early, durable brand choice that should fit the wider visual and non-visual system rather than operate as an isolated creative artifact.

## Key Claims
- A company name is both an introduction and a relatively durable brand element, so the process should begin with the company's mission, values, and intended character.
- Research should collect customer and industry language, emerging themes, competitor names, and examples that reveal which category conventions a candidate follows or deliberately breaks.
- Common forms—real words, compounds, human names, mash-ups, suffixes, removed vowels, and intentional misspellings—are prompts rather than rules; simpler names tend to face greater domain scarcity.
- Early ideation should maximize quantity, defer judgment, involve varied collaborators, use words and sketches, and repeat across sessions or settings.
- Shortlisting should remove duplicates and candidates that are hard to understand, spell, type, or pronounce, then compare unfamiliar listeners' impressions with the original mission and values.
- Operational screening should check relevant domains and social handles; alternative extensions or spellings are compromises whose branding and usability costs still need evaluation.
- Finalists can be tested through matched landing pages and targeted traffic or through social polls, although either method can reflect audience, sampling, and measurement bias.

## Key Quotes
> "the name is not a standalone thing" — on evaluating a name as part of the broader brand system.

> "Quantity, not quality." — on separating divergent idea generation from later selection.
