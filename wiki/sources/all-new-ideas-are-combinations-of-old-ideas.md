## Summary
[[TomTunguz]] argues that innovation often comes from recombining existing ideas across fields, using [[EastgateCentre]]'s termite-inspired climate design and [[BenjaminJones]]'s burden-of-knowledge argument as anchors. The article then applies the same idea to SaaS organizations: sales, marketing, customer success, finance, and data teams create better hypotheses when they share observations and connect fragmented datasets. The embedded Eastgate photograph shows the building facade and supports the architectural example, but it does not add independent measurements beyond the prose.

## Key Claims
- [[InnovationAtIntersection]] treats new ideas as recombinations of older knowledge, with some combinations more valuable than others.
- [[EastgateCentre]] is presented as an architectural example where termite-mound temperature regulation informed building design.
- [[BenjaminJones]]'s burden-of-knowledge argument implies that modern innovation depends on teams with complementary expertise rather than lone generalists.
- In SaaS companies, customer success, sales, marketing, and finance each hold partial observations that can improve product and go-to-market hypotheses when combined.
- [[OrganizationalDataSharing]] becomes a practical form of intersectional innovation as company data splinters across specialized tools and databases.
- The combination of [[TwilioSegment]] Sources and [[Looker]] Blocks is cited as enabling end-to-end funnel analysis across marketing, sales, customer success, lifecycle marketing, and upsell activity.

## Key Quotes
> "All new ideas are combinations of old ideas" - the article's opening innovation frame, attributed to [[FransJohansson]].

> "data within organizations seems to be splintering geometrically" - on why cross-team data sharing becomes necessary.
