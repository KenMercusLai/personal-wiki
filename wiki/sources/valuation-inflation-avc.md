## Summary
[[FredWilson]] reviews 55 seed, Series A, and Series B investments led or co-led by [[UnionSquareVentures]] and finds that early-stage pre-money valuations rose substantially between the 2012 and 2017 timeframes. Seed round sizes also increased, while Series A round sizes remained roughly stable despite higher valuations. He treats the result as directional rather than universal because the sample is one firm's portfolio, spans several geographies, contains little recent Series B data, and is too young to reveal the return effects of the higher prices.

## Key Claims
- Across 55 new USV portfolio companies since its 2012 third early-stage fund, the firm led or co-led 16 seed rounds, 31 Series A rounds, and 8 Series B rounds.
- Average seed pre-money valuations rose from roughly $5–10 million in the 2012 timeframe to $10–15 million in the 2017 timeframe.
- Average seed capital raised rose from about $2.5 million to more than $4 million over the same comparison.
- Average Series A pre-money valuations rose from roughly $10–15 million to $22–27 million, while average Series A round size remained around $5–7 million.
- USV's recent Series B sample was too small for Wilson to draw a conclusion about that market.
- Prices varied geographically within the combined sample: the Bay Area was the most expensive investment market and Europe the least expensive.
- Realized returns could not yet show what the higher entry prices meant because contemporary venture returns still largely reflected 2005–2010 investments, while later cohorts remained immature.

## Key Quotes
> "The average pre-money valuation for a seed round has gone from $5-10mm in the 2012 time frame to $10-15mm in the 2017 time frame" - Wilson's central seed-market comparison.

> "What this means for returns for angel and early-stage investors remains to be seen." - the article's explicit limit on translating price inflation into performance conclusions.

## Contradictions
- Qualifies [[StartupValuation]] by showing that valuation benchmarks vary by stage, period, geography, and investor sample rather than forming one general startup price.
- Qualifies [[StartupFundingRound]] by showing that valuation and capital raised need not move together: Series A valuations rose while average Series A round size stayed roughly flat.
- No direct contradiction is established about investor returns because the relevant 2010–2020 investment cohorts had not matured when Wilson wrote.
