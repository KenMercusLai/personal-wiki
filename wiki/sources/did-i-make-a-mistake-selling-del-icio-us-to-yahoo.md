## Summary
[[JoshuaSchachter]] recounts how a personal Unix text file and Muxway became [[Delicious|Del.icio.us]], a multi-user bookmarking service built around low-friction tagging and social discovery. Unable to keep the fast-growing service reliable or raise a satisfactory second financing round, he sold it to [[Yahoo]] in 2005 for an undisclosed amount below the widely reported US$30 million figure. He does not regret securing financial stability, but describes weak technical follow-through, slow approvals, absent strategic direction, and eventual understaffing as reasons the acquisition stopped the product's momentum and left its broader potential unresolved. ## Key Claims
- Del.icio.us evolved from Schachter's personal practice of appending terse hash-marked notes to saved URLs, then from single-user Muxway into a shared bookmarking service.
- Saving with one or two tags reduced the cognitive burden of hierarchical bookmark filing while letting users observe, share, and contribute to one another's collections.
- Rapid traffic growth, frequent downtime, limited founder operating capacity, and an unsuccessful attempt to raise a second round made an acquisition materially more attractive.
- Yahoo was selected because it made the first serious offer, appeared committed to Web 2.0 after acquiring Flickr and Upcoming, and promised search-indexing and infrastructure help.
- The reported US$30 million purchase price was, according to Schachter, an unsupported press estimate; the real amount remains under NDA and was lower.
- Selling exchanged product momentum, autonomy, and uncertain upside for financial security, but the founder describes the outcome as intellectually defensible rather than emotionally triumphant.
- After acquisition, Yahoo supplied some technical help but imposed slow approval paths, lacked a decision-guiding mission, shifted staff toward Yahoo Answers, and ultimately left Del.icio.us with about one engineer.

## Key Quotes
> "It takes away all of your forward momentum." - Schachter on why a founder's sale may not feel like the victory outsiders expect.

> "Before you're acquired, you're an important visionary. Afterward, you're a crazy person who just wants to burn money." - Schachter's account of the change in organizational standing after a deal closes.

> "You can be rich or be king." - the founder-control tradeoff Schachter uses to frame an exit, while noting that neither outcome is absolute.

## Contradictions
- The account deepens rather than resolves the acquisition tension in [[xavier-amatriains-answer-to-what-lessons-can-silicon-valley-tech-executives-learn-from-what-went-wrong-at-yahoo-quora]]: Yahoo could genuinely offer infrastructure that a small product lacked while still failing to allocate enough attention, autonomy, and staff after closing.
- Schachter's claim that he invented tags is a first-person priority claim without comparative historical evidence in the supplied article; the source establishes his role in popularizing the Del.icio.us tagging interaction, not a comprehensive history of tagging.
- This is a retrospective founder interview without deal documents, operating metrics, employee testimony, Yahoo's response, or a counterfactual showing how Del.icio.us would have performed independently.
