## Summary
RORIRI explains [[VarianceAdditivity]] by starting from a [[DataGeneratingProcess]] view of weight as the output of many interacting causes. The article argues that variance can be decomposed cleanly only when component variables are [[Orthogonality|orthogonal]]; otherwise [[Covariance]] terms are part of the total variation and must be included.

## Key Claims
- A measured outcome such as weight should be understood as the product of a complex [[DataGeneratingProcess]], not as the effect of one isolated factor.
- Variance measures how dispersed observations are, making it possible to ask how much of total variation comes from different factors.
- [[VarianceAdditivity]] is incomplete if it is taught as "variance of A+B equals variance of A plus variance of B" without the covariance term.
- [[Covariance]] captures two variables' joint dispersion and makes variance a special case of covariance with itself.
- Independent data-generating processes do not guarantee a sample correlation of exactly zero; [[Orthogonality]] is a mathematical sample relationship affected by noise.
- Statistical learning with LLMs is risky when models blur independence, orthogonality, covariance, and sampling noise.

## Key Quotes
> "数据生成过程" - the source's frame for complex causal systems behind observed data.

> "协方差" - the missing shared component in naive variance-additivity explanations.

> "独立" and "正交" - the distinction the source says many LLMs fail to handle.
