## Summary
[[TerribleSoftware]] argues that the distinguishing skill of senior engineers is [[AmbiguityReduction]]: turning vague requests into clear problems, explicit assumptions, bounded choices, and executable work. The essay connects that front-loaded reasoning to project de-risking and contrasts it with hiring practices centered on technologies, tenure, or coding puzzles. Its single-skill thesis is useful as a corrective to checklist seniority, but it is a practitioner argument rather than evidence that ambiguity reduction alone is sufficient for senior performance.

## Key Claims
- Senior engineers distinguish themselves by reducing ambiguity before implementation, not merely by writing code or accumulating years of experience.
- Useful clarification starts with the underlying problem, a specific user and pain, hidden assumptions, and the downside of shipping while wrong.
- Separating signal from noise, deciding what belongs now or later, and cutting unnecessary work turns fuzzy initiatives into smaller executable projects.
- Ambiguity reduction de-risks delivery by preventing avoidable surprises, production incidents, and emergency coordination, although the work is often invisible when successful.
- Technology checklists and algorithm interviews do not directly test whether a candidate can turn an incomplete product requirement into a shippable plan.
- Architecture and communication become more valuable after the team has established what it is actually building.

## Key Quotes
> "They first make the problem clear. Then, and only then, they go to solve it." - on sequencing clarification before implementation.

> "If you can't reduce ambiguity, all your other skills are just elegant ways of solving the wrong problem." - on the essay's central, deliberately strong thesis.

## Connections
- [[TerribleSoftware]] - publication and attributed author of the essay.
- [[AmbiguityReduction]] - the central practice of converting fuzzy requests into bounded decisions and work.
- [[EngineeringCareerArchitecture]] - the essay proposes ambiguity reduction as a behavioral signal that complements formal level descriptions.
- [[UnderstandDesignBuild]] - both approaches require understanding the business problem before committing to implementation.
- [[PrematureImplementation]] - starting to code before clarifying the problem is the failure mode the essay warns against.
- [[ProductDesignCareerLadder]] - product-design seniority similarly includes ambiguity tolerance, autonomy, judgment, and wider influence.

## Contradictions
- The claim that one core skill separates senior engineers is narrower than [[EngineeringCareerArchitecture]] and [[ProductDesignCareerLadder]], which treat seniority as multidimensional and organization-dependent. The strongest synthesis treats ambiguity reduction as an important behavioral signal, not a sufficient or universal definition.
- The essay gives practitioner examples but no comparative hiring, promotion, delivery, or incident data, so its causal claims about smoother projects and fewer production fires remain unmeasured.
