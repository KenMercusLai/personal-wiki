## Summary
This Rands in Repose article presents [[StaffMeetings]] as recurring leadership forums that should exist only for a concrete coordination need and should combine a minimal metrics story, a team-sourced rolling agenda, and explicit correction of gossip, rumors, and falsehoods. It assigns separate runner and historian roles, recommends broad distribution of curated notes, and treats silence, stale metrics, exhausted agendas, and habit-driven recurrence as signals that the meeting may lack value. The larger argument is that meetings can help people interpret organizational change, but discussion is only useful when it produces follow-up work, reduces information gaps, and helps resolve the underlying problem.

## Key Claims
- A staff meeting becomes plausible with three or more direct reports, substantial interdependence, management layers, rapid growth, recurring coordination failures, or a recent organizational shock.
- A meeting runner should set the agenda and end conversational threads when their incremental value falls too low, while a separate meeting historian captures themes, decisions, action items, and relevant context.
- After removing personal or clearly confidential material, meeting notes should be shared broadly to reduce status games and prevent information gaps from being filled with damaging speculation.
- A durable agenda combines a small set of story-producing metrics, topics contributed by the team, and a final safe space for checking gossip, rumors, hallway talk, and chat-based claims.
- Useful metrics generate questions and frame debate; metrics that never do so may be wrong for the group or may indicate that the recurring meeting has outlived its purpose.
- Early signs of meeting health include useful conversational detours, unsolicited agenda items, and a leader who talks less as participants begin to contribute their own experience and judgment.
- Meetings are a response to organizational discomfort, not a cure by themselves; progress depends on actionable follow-up that addresses the problem beneath the conversation.

## Key Quotes
> "in the absence of information, humans fill the gap with the worse possible version of the truth." - on why meeting notes should be shared beyond the room.

> "Meetings are a symptom of a disease, they are not the cure." - on the difference between discussing change and resolving its underlying cause.

## Contradictions
- [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] recommends deleting default scheduled meetings in favor of useful informal interaction. This source agrees that purposeless recurring meetings are harmful but argues that interdependent leadership teams can justify a scheduled forum when it has explicit roles, decision-relevant metrics, team-owned topics, transparent notes, and continuing evidence of value.
