## Summary
The unnamed first-person author presents [[GetOutOfTheWayDesignPhilosophy]] through [[Path]]'s shift from private photo sharing to a broader private life journal. After growth stalled near 10,000 daily active users, research found that people were posting screenshots of music, notes, running maps, and health apps; Path 2 turned those observed behaviors into first-class photo, music, location, thought, and health posts, after which the source reports growth to more than 500,000 daily active users within two weeks. Early [[Facebook]] profile-photo changes and user-created conventions on [[Twitter]] provide analogous examples of products incorporating behavior their creators did not initially design.

![Original Path interface centered on private photo sharing with close friends](../../wiki-assets/get-out-of-the-way-design-philosophy-medium/original-path-photo-sharing-interface.png)

## Key Claims
- Founders' initial use-case beliefs can become dogma that hides how users actually understand and adapt a product.
- Path's users treated a private social graph as a journal for everyday life, not only a place to share camera-roll photos.
- Screenshots of music, notes, Nike+ Running maps, and health apps were behavioral evidence for broader posting needs.
- Path 2 made photos, music, locations, thoughts, and health first-class content and added a Nike+ Running integration.
- The source reports that Path remained near 10,000 daily active users through much of 2011, then grew to more than 500,000 within two weeks of the Path 2 launch.
- Facebook's repeated profile-photo changes and Twitter's user-created @mentions and hashtags illustrate how user workarounds can reveal product direction.
- Product teams should search for recurring user adaptations, support the valuable pattern, and then reduce constraints that keep users from pursuing it.

## Key Quotes
> “The book you wrote may not be the book people read.” - Michael Lewis, used to frame the gap between creator intent and audience use

> “Users hack your product to make it do what they want it to do.” - the author on behavior that should be investigated rather than dismissed

## Connections
- [[Path]] - central product case that expanded from private photo sharing into a private life journal.
- [[GetOutOfTheWayDesignPhilosophy]] - named framework for following recurring user behavior instead of protecting the initial product dogma.
- [[UserBehaviorDrivenProductDiscovery]] - primary research into posted screenshots revealed the broader journal use case.
- [[EmergentProductIdentity]] - users assigned Path a broader meaning than its founders initially intended.
- [[ProductMarketFit]] - the reported post-redesign daily-active-user jump is presented as evidence of stronger fit, though not a complete fit test.
- [[Facebook]] - early profile-photo behavior is offered as an analogous user-led product signal.
- [[Twitter]] - @mentions and hashtags are offered as conventions users created before the product formalized them.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction was found. The source strengthens behavior-led discovery while remaining compatible with [[TargetUserDiscipline]]: teams still need to distinguish recurring, strategically useful behavior from isolated requests.
- The reported 50-fold daily-active-user increase is a first-party historical claim without cohort retention, acquisition-channel, causal, or independent verification data; the article does not establish how much of the increase came from the redesign itself.
- Facebook and Twitter are brief retrospective analogies rather than documented comparisons, and the article does not examine unsuccessful cases where following user behavior harmed focus, economics, safety, or trust.
- Six local embeds were opened. The original Path interface was retained as product evidence; the lightbulb hero was decorative, while the small Path, Facebook, Twitter, and Path 2 thumbnails were omitted because their legible content was duplicated by the prose or by the retained interface image.
