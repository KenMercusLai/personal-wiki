## Summary
[[JoshLaurito]] records a quantitative retrospective of [[Gawker]] at the end of the site's original operations, covering publishing volume, contributors, traffic, topics, and [[Kinja]] comments. The account reports 202,370 posts, roughly 7 billion pageviews across more than 3 billion visits, 420 publishing identities, and more than 16 million comments, while linking declining output after 2008 to layoffs, the loss of direct homepage traffic, and the end of per-post writer compensation. Its figures are valuable historical operational evidence but remain author calculations with acquisition, tagging, tracking, and archive-boundary caveats.

## Key Claims
- Gawker and its sub-blogs published 202,370 posts through August 2016, including 3,311 automated Public Pool posts.
- Annual output peaked in 2008 at 33,047 posts, then fell sharply after editorial layoffs, declining direct and homepage traffic, and the end of per-post writer compensation.
- Across its history, the site had 420 human, group, and bot publishing identities.
- The author estimates roughly 7 billion lifetime pageviews across more than 3 billion visits, with an average post receiving about 30,000 lifetime pageviews.
- Gawker covered a broad mix of media, politics, television, and celebrity topics, but tag counts understate coverage because of misspellings and other tagging errors.
- Kinja comments formed a major part of the product and community: the site accumulated more than 16 million comments and used recommendations to surface notable contributions.
- Historical totals include Cityfile posts merged after Gawker acquired the site, while per-day and author calculations exclude some retroactively added material.

## Key Quotes
> "the comments were a big part of the reason many of us enjoyed the sites so much" - on Kinja's community value beyond article publishing.

> "tracking historical, all-time traffic is a challenge" - qualification on the traffic estimates for a site that predates modern analytics.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction was found. The source adds historical evidence to [[PlatformDistributionDependence]] by showing that a loss of direct traffic can change publishing incentives, but it does not isolate that effect from layoffs or compensation changes.
- The captured Markdown announces several rankings and tag tables without preserving their underlying values, so only figures explicitly present in the prose can be incorporated.
