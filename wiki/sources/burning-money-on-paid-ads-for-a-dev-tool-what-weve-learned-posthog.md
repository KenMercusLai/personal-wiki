## Summary
[[PostHog]] argues that paid ads can work for [[DeveloperTooling]], but only as a small, carefully measured subset of marketing rather than the whole go-to-market system. The article distinguishes conversion campaigns from awareness campaigns, recommends qualitative signup attribution for developer audiences, and gives channel-by-channel lessons across search, social, developer communities, newsletters, and software-review venues. The inspected local image is a decorative newsletter mascot and does not add independent evidence beyond the surrounding subscription callout.

## Key Claims
- Paid ads are only one part of [[SaaSMarketing]]; PostHog spends roughly 10% of marketing-team time on ads and more than 80% on writing.
- Developer-tool campaigns should split spend between demand capture and demand creation rather than putting everything into directly attributable conversions.
- Quantitative click attribution can undercount developer ad influence, so signup and demo "where did you hear about us?" answers should supplement [[MarketingAttribution]].
- Agencies can improve execution efficiency, but founders still need enough channel fluency to critique work, give feedback, and propose experiments.
- Google Search is useful for conversion, brand defense, and intent keywords, but teams should avoid overlapping paid bids with top-ranking SEO pages.
- Google Display produced bots and irrelevant conversions for PostHog and is treated as a channel to avoid despite low apparent CPA.
- Twitter, LinkedIn, Quora, Product Hunt, newsletters, G2, and [[StackOverflow]] vary by awareness, conversion, targeting quality, audience fit, cost, and technical focus.
- Early paid-channel learning should come from two to three small experiments at a time, with about $500 and two weeks per experiment.

## Key Quotes
> "Outsource the legwork, not your critical thinking." - on working with agencies.

> "Experiment, experiment, experiment." - on starting with small paid-channel tests.
