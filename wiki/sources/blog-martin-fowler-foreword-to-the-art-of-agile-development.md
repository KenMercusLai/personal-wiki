## Summary
[[MartinFowler]] argues that mainstream "agile" often diverges from the original [[AgileSoftwareDevelopment]] vision because teams copy ceremonies without the interconnected management and technical practices that make agility work. The foreword presents [[ExtremeProgramming]] and the [[AgileFluencyModel]] as ways to recover that practice base: focus on value through teamwork, planning, and customer collaboration, and deliver reliably through testing, refactoring, design, collaborative development, DevOps, and [[ContinuousDelivery]].

## Key Claims
- [[AgileSoftwareDevelopment]] is not a ceremony set; it depends on a web of connected management and technical practices.
- Many teams believe they are agile while lacking the technical practices needed for high productivity and reliability.
- [[ExtremeProgramming]] is presented as a central pillar of the Agile movement and a foundation for teams that want to execute agile processes properly.
- The [[AgileFluencyModel]] distinguishes basic customer-focused project management from higher levels of value delivery that require stronger technical skill.
- [[InternalSoftwareQuality]] can decrease cost and increase delivery speed when supported by testing, refactoring, design, DevOps culture, and [[ContinuousDelivery]].
- Observing software in production helps teams learn what is valuable, connecting reliable delivery to product learning.

## Key Quotes
> "working in an Agile way requires a web of interconnected practices" - on why ceremonies alone are insufficient.

> "building software with a high internal quality decreases cost" - on the counterintuitive economics of technical excellence.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradictions found. The source qualifies generic agile adoption by arguing that process vocabulary and ceremonies are weak substitutes for disciplined technical practice and production learning.
