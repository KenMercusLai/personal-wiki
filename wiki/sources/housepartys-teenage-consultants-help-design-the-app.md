## Summary
[[YirenLu]] reports how [[Houseparty]] and [[MusicalLy]] reduced the demographic gap between their adult builders and teenage users through school visits, early-adopter networks, group conversations, mockup review, and observation of everyday behavior. The article presents [[ParticipatoryProductDesign]] as both product discovery and distribution: involving socially connected young users shaped interaction details while helping the apps spread through dense school networks. Its adoption and usage figures are historical company- and investor-reported snapshots, and the article does not examine participant compensation, child safety, privacy, or failed comparison products.

## Key Claims
- Houseparty sought feedback from high-school and college users on room size, presence notifications, and other concrete interaction choices because its adult founders did not share the core audience's lived norms.
- Houseparty deliberately visited schools outside coastal technology hubs and recruited through alumni, family, and early-user networks in an effort to hear from more typical American teenagers.
- [[AlexZhu]] describes Musical.ly's continuous WeChat and WhatsApp groups as participatory design: the company discussed major ideas, mockups, and wireframes with users before coding.
- The source says Musical.ly chose young American early adopters deliberately, joining observed music listening with mobile video creation and editing in a lip-sync product.
- [[JoshElman]] frames user visits as learning rather than merely marketing, with favorable sessions also seeding product awareness.
- Dense school relationships amplified social-network adoption, while Houseparty's 2016 ranking decline during the school break suggested that in-person contact could stimulate rather than substitute for online use.
- Teen-led adoption did not imply permanent demographic confinement: the article argues that socially expressive products can be reinterpreted by older users and different relationship groups.

## Key Quotes
> "We always first present the idea, have a conversation with users, share the mock-ups and wireframes, before we do any coding." - Alex Zhu on Musical.ly's design process.

> "It wasn't just, hey we need to go market this." - Josh Elman distinguishing Houseparty's school visits from promotion alone.
