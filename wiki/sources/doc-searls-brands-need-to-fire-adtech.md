## Summary
[[DocSearls]] argues that the 2017 YouTube brand-safety boycott exposed a category error: tracking-based [[Adtech]] follows targetable people toward cheap inventory, while [[BrandAdvertising]] sponsors media whose own reputation contributes to the advertiser's message. He links adtech to direct-response marketing, surveillance, fraud, malware, low-quality content incentives, and weak publisher control, then proposes reputable-media sponsorship, deliberate customer opt-in, and user-controlled interest signaling as alternatives. ## Key Claims
- Automated ad systems optimize for reaching selected people cheaply rather than for sponsoring a known media environment, creating brand-safety failures when ads follow audiences onto objectionable inventory.
- Tracking-based adtech and brand advertising have different objectives: direct response seeks measurable individual action, while brand advertising buys contextual association and population reach through a chosen publication or broadcaster.
- Adtech's tracking, intermediaries, and impression incentives can impose privacy, fraud, malware, and editorial externalities while rewarding cheap attention regardless of the quality or provenance of the surrounding content.
- Publishers adopted third-party ad systems partly because they lacked practical online alternatives, but outsourcing sales also weakened their direct relationship with advertisers and control over placement.
- Ad blocking and tracking protection are framed as legitimate market responses to surveillance rather than merely as costs or threats imposed on publishers.
- Interest-based advertising would be more legitimate if individuals could express preferences through an open, reusable, user-controlled system instead of repeatedly opting out of tracker-defined profiles.
- Advertisers are urged to replace surveillance-led placement with direct sponsorship of reputable media, while direct-response campaigns should focus on people who consciously opted in.

## Key Quotes
> "With real advertising, you have brands supporting brands." - Searls's summary of sponsorship-oriented brand advertising.

> "Brands should engage customers by their grace" - the article's case for deliberate opt-in rather than inferred interest.

## Contradictions
- The article predicts that GDPR enforcement beginning in May 2018 would be an "extinction event" for adtech, but it supplies no later outcome evidence; this must be treated as a historical forecast rather than an established result.
- Its claim that adtech works only "0.0x%" of the time is rhetorical and unsupported by campaign definitions, comparative measurements, attribution methods, or business outcomes.
- The sharp opposition between adtech and "real advertising" clarifies incentives but can overstate the boundary: programmatic systems can buy known inventory or guaranteed placements, and contextual sponsorship can still use automation.
