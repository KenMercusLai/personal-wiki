## Summary
This GV Library essay argues that self-directed fictional design projects can build skill, confidence, technical range, and portfolio evidence before paid opportunities exist. Its examples move from repeated album-cover practice to a working mock e-commerce site, an unsolicited Verizon redesign, and voluntary Firefox branding work, while insisting that simulations preserve realistic constraints and ideally include [[UserTesting]]. All four local images were opened: three duplicate versions of a decorative “FAKE” apple hero and one tiny Mozilla/Firefox logo pair repeat the prose and were omitted, so no asset manifest was created.

## Key Claims
- [[FakeDesignPractice]] creates low-permission opportunities to accumulate repetitions and attempt work beyond one's current résumé.
- A fictional but functioning project can become portfolio evidence that helps resolve the experience-before-opportunity problem.
- Simulated design should preserve real constraints, data, copy, recovery paths, and stakeholder pressures rather than producing an artificially easy ideal screen.
- The Verizon redesign taught the author that apparently poor interface choices can encode organizational constraints and reduced an expected 200% improvement to a self-judged 20% improvement.
- Testing a simulated design with representative users is stronger evidence than designer applause or the creator's own judgment.
- Self-directed fake work differs from speculative work requested by a prospective client, while freely volunteering for a respected project can be legitimate when participation is informed and uncoerced.

## Key Quotes
> "Fake design work let me get in thousands of reps." - on repeated self-directed practice.

> "Design to learn, not to get accolades." - on making customer evidence, rather than peer approval, the objective.
