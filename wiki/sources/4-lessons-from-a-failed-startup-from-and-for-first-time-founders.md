## Summary
The author reflects on shutting down [[Maderight]], a software-powered apparel manufacturer accepted into [[YCombinator]] and [[StartX]] that failed to find [[ProductMarketFit]] after three years. The retrospective argues that first-time founders should manage early startups as systems for testing assumptions, not merely conserving runway or servicing existing revenue. It connects [[StartupHypothesisTesting]], [[FounderLedSales]], [[DoingThingsThatDoNotScale]], and [[StartupFocus]] through a concrete failure case in service-heavy apparel manufacturing.

## Key Claims
- Startups begin as assumptions about a problem and willingness to pay, so founders need a repeatable framework for documenting, testing, and evaluating hypotheses.
- Runway should be judged by how many meaningful hypotheses it lets the company test, not only by how many calendar months it preserves.
- Before [[ProductMarketFit]], sales should be founder-led because sales conversations are discovery loops for customer problems and solution tests, not merely revenue generation.
- Choosing both a high-touch service business and a new technology product split Maderight's time and resources, making both efforts suffer.
- Manual service work can produce customer learning and revenue, but it becomes dangerous when founders cannot decide whether the service is the business or a bridge to software adoption.

## Key Quotes
> "The sale is just a byproduct of a successful test." - on pre-fit founder-led sales.

> "maximize the number of hypotheses you can test" - reframing runway as learning capacity.
