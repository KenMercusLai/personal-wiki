## Summary
[[ExceptionNotFound]] argues that developers can mistake user confusion for laziness, hostility, or incompetence because builders are far more fluent in their own software than ordinary users are. The essay reframes frustrating support requests through [[HanlonsRazor]]: users usually are not trying to anger developers, they are trying to learn how to do their jobs with an unfamiliar tool. The repeated embedded image was inspected and is a decorative warning-sign illustration of someone kicking a computer, reinforcing the emotional tone without adding separate evidence.

## Key Claims
- Developers can overread user mistakes as intentional annoyance when the real cause is unfamiliarity with the application.
- [[BuilderUserFluencyGap]] explains why instructions, links, combined search boxes, and other interface options can seem obvious to builders but foreign to users.
- In-person support can reveal that users are often grateful and motivated to learn once someone explains the tool without judgment.
- [[HanlonsRazor]] is a practical support stance: interpret confusion as ignorance or missing context before assuming malice.
- Developers have a responsibility to transfer their product knowledge where users need it, because they are the ones with deeper system knowledge.

## Key Quotes
> "They weren't trying to make me angry, they were trying to learn." - the author's central reframing of user frustration.

> "The issue was that I live and breath code, and they don't." - on why developer obviousness did not transfer to non-developers.
