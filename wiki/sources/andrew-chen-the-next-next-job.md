## Summary
[[AndrewChen]] presents the [[NextNextJobFramework]] for big career decisions: define the job after the next job, identify why it is not reachable now, then choose current opportunities by how well they close the gap. The article argues that career options should be compared by the skills, network, experiences, mentors, ideas, and "superpower" they build rather than by immediate role attractiveness alone. The inspected header image is text-bearing but mainly repeats the setup that recent tech changes have made company-by-company job evaluation feel chaotic and that the initial "what do you think of XYZ company?" question is the wrong starting point.

## Key Claims
- The [[NextNextJobFramework]] asks what someone wants as their next next job and why they cannot get it now.
- Career evaluation should work backward from 2-3 plausible future roles rather than only comparing immediate companies, titles, cultures, or compensation packages.
- Researching people who already hold the desired future role can reveal repeated gaps in skills, network, experiences, mentors, and ideas.
- Some future roles are already attainable but feel blocked by imposter syndrome, over-preparation, or risk aversion.
- When a future role is not yet attainable, the best current job is the one that closes the most important gaps and helps build a decisive superpower.
- For aspiring investors, a superpower such as access to founder dealflow can matter more than generic analytical credentials.
- Chen's own Uber choice is presented as a stepping-stone decision because it could create future-founder networks, scale experience, and relevance for later startup investing.

## Key Quotes
> "What do you want to be your next next job? And why can't you get it right now?" - on the framework's central prompt.

> "Think offense (building up a superpower), not just defense (filling in gaps)." - on weighting standout advantage alongside gap closing.
