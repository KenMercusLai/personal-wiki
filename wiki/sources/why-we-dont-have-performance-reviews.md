## Summary
[[CourtneySeiter]] explains why [[Buffer]] replaced annual performance reviews with frequent, employee-led coaching conversations. The company paired mentor-style one-on-ones with optional peer masterminds, using both to surface achievements and challenges while favoring attentive questions over prescriptive problem-solving. The account is a 2016 practitioner description of an evolving system, not comparative evidence that abolishing ratings improves performance.

## Key Claims
- Annual reviews compress too much time into one retrospective conversation, make recall difficult, let ratings displace useful dialogue, and deliver feedback later than employees can act on it.
- Buffer used weekly feedback and at-least-fortnightly one-on-ones between each teammate and team lead as its core coaching rhythm.
- One-on-ones were primarily the teammate's meeting: the employee set the agenda and used the time for achievements, challenges, advice, and feedback.
- Optional masterminds paired peers with related work for structured celebration and challenge discussion across professional and personal growth.
- One-on-ones and masterminds served different relationships: mentor-to-mentee guidance versus peer-to-peer reflection, so neither format was treated as universally necessary.
- Buffer restored one-on-ones after dropping them during a self-management experiment, concluding that peer sessions did not replace the value of mentorship.
- Both formats emphasized close listening and questions that help the speaker form a solution rather than immediately solving the problem for them.

## Key Quotes
> "The key to both: Listening, not solving" - Seiter's shared rule for one-on-ones and masterminds

> "The aim is to help the other person to think about the challenge differently and come up with their own solution." - coaching objective

- No direct contradiction with existing wiki pages.
