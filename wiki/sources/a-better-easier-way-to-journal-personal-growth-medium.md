## Summary
The article proposes the T.L.C. method as a lightweight [[JournalingPractice]] for people who want the benefits of journaling but stall at a blank page. Each day, the journaler writes briefly under three prompts: gratitude for a specific event, something learned, and intellectual or interpersonal connections noticed during the day.

## Key Claims
- Journaling becomes easier to sustain when the prompt is simple, memorable, and short enough to avoid psychological resistance.
- The T.L.C. method asks for three daily observations: what to thank, what to learn, and what to connect.
- [[GratitudePractice]] should focus on specific events from the day rather than generic affirmations.
- Daily learning can come from ordinary interactions and small observations, not only from life-changing insights.
- Connection includes both conceptual linking and interpersonal follow-up, making journaling a bridge between [[ActiveLearning]], [[PersonalKnowledgeManagement]], and social attention.
- A daily prompt can change attention during the day because people begin looking for the things they expect to record.

## Key Quotes
> "Just remember TLC." - introducing the article's three-part prompt.

> "Write a few sentences for each heading." - keeping the habit small enough to start.
