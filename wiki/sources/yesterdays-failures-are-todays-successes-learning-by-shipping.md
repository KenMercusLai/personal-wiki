## Summary
[[StevenSinofsky]] argues that watching an idea you developed fail and then become someone else's success is easier to accept once two things are accounted for: innovation is mostly recombination of older ideas rather than isolated invention, and the technology context of the day decides what a concept can actually execute. He makes the case by contrasting the dot-com bust with the startups of the mid-2010s and by listing what changed in between - mobile compute, battery life, touch interfaces, cellular bandwidth, location and maps, an authenticated social graph, flash storage, web payments, and a culture that finally accepted sharing. The essay closes on execution: the complete mix of product, price, place, and promotion is what it equates with [[ProductMarketFit]], so a remembered idea is not the same as having built and sold one.

## Key Claims
- Products and services that failed spectacularly in the dot-com era reappeared as successful startups once the surrounding technology foundation matured, so an earlier failure is not evidence that the idea was wrong.
- Innovation is a stream of recombination rather than a series of inventions: the essay claims there are few true inventions and that each innovation builds on the past in novel recombinations.
- Hindsight understates context, because today's technology changes how a concept is executed and how broadly it can diffuse, which makes the shift more than "what is old is new again".
- The mid-2010s foundation was concrete: two billion people carrying roughly ten times the compute of early-2000s PCs, 16-20 hours of phone battery against 4-6 hours for laptops, touch interfaces whose design constraints force simplicity, cellular bandwidth treated as an effectively unlimited resource, and GPS-plus-maps location that replaced printed street directories for on-demand services.
- Identity, storage, and payments matured into infrastructure: an authenticated profile replaced the pseudonymous screen name that people once used as [[SocialProof]], flash storage removed moving parts and space anxiety, and [[PayPal]] made it reliable and secure to pay and accept money on the web.
- Sharing was as much a cultural change as a technical one, since society was skeptical of user-generated content until Wikipedia and [[YouTube]] made sharing beyond one's immediate social circle ordinary.
- Execution is the whole commercial arrangement rather than the idea: the essay defines it as product, price, place, and promotion, equates that with product-market fit, and dismisses the notion that a person who thought of something first had therefore executed it.

## Key Quotes
> "It is super tough to see something you thought you dreamed up and developed fail only later go on to be someone else's big success." - on the feeling the essay starts from.

> "There are few true inventions, but rather a technology stream of innovations, each one building on the past in novel recombinations." - on innovation as recombination.

> "We should always remind ourselves that execution is not just an idea and code, but the complete set of Product, Price, Place, Promotion - what we today think of as Product-Market Fit." - on what execution has to include.

> "Perhaps the biggest change is not technology but culture - people share and find value in sharing what they create, think, and feel beyond their immediate social circle." - on sharing as a cultural shift.

> "The best thing is to be the person, team, or product that makes today happen and not dwell on why today didn't happen yesterday." - closing advice.
