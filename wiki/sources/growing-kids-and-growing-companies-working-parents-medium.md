## Summary
[[EvWilliams]] reports an informal anonymous survey of professionally successful parents, mostly in technology and predominantly founders, to examine how they combine family life with demanding work. The selected sample most commonly reported 55-60 focused work hours, widespread but limited weekend work, more work outside the office among founders, and frequent household childcare assistance. The results complicate 100-hour startup mythology without defining a healthy or representative norm, because the article provides no sample size, recruitment frame, demographic breakdown, outcome comparison, or validation of self-reported time.

## Key Claims
- The largest reported work-hours band was 55-60 hours per week at 29%; 2% reported 90 or more hours and 4% reported 35-40 hours.
- More than 60 hours was reported by 21% of respondents at companies below 50 employees, 46% at companies with 50-200 employees, and 55% at companies above 200 employees.
- At companies younger than five years, 25% of all respondents and 28% of founders reported more than 60 hours, compared with 50% of all respondents and 44% of founders at older companies.
- Although 85% reported working six or seven days, about half worked only two to five weekend hours; 38% of founders reported at least five weekend hours.
- Founders reported less office-bound work: half did at least 75% of their work in the office, compared with 86% of non-founders.
- Household support was common: half lived in dual-working-spouse households, 73% had childcare assistance, and the reported rate was 77% among founders versus 64% among non-founders.
- The author rejects a single prescribed balance and presents his own coexistence of an office dinner and a ski weekend with his children as one personal arrangement.

## Key Quotes
> "when your work is your baby and you have actual babies at home" - Williams on competing demands from company leadership and parenting.

> "There are many ways to live your life." - the article's deliberately non-prescriptive conclusion.

## Contradictions
- The survey challenges the strongest version of 100-hour startup mythology: only 2% of this selected successful-parent sample reported 90 or more hours, while 55-60 was the largest band.
- The apparent association between company size or age and longer hours does not establish causation; role seniority, sector, company stage, respondent selection, and correlated characteristics are not controlled.
- The sample was recruited from the author's friends and professional acquaintances, mostly in technology, with 71% founders and too few women for gender analysis. The article gives no sample size or response denominator, so its percentages should not be treated as population norms for founders, parents, or workers.
- The two supplied images were opened and omitted: the lead is a contextual family-skiing photograph whose information is repeated by the closing sentence, and the final image is a duplicate thumbnail.
