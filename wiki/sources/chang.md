## Summary
[[Wulc]] argues that people are shaped less by isolated willpower than by the invisible "fields" created by incentives, people, family patterns, intimate relationships, creative conditions, and moral situations. The essay treats work, life, creation, and human nature as variations on one thesis: because humans are vulnerable to their surroundings, freedom begins with perceiving and choosing the [[EnvironmentalField]] one inhabits.

## Key Claims
- People are products of their environments: a "field" consists of rules, incentives, people, moods, routines, and norms that gradually become character and fate.
- Work fields are shaped by incentives and colleagues; short-term metrics, low feedback, and value mismatch can drain vitality, while strong teams and open discussion can make growth almost automatic.
- [[FamilyOfOrigin]] gives people their earliest emotional field, and economic independence can make separation and self-reparenting possible.
- A long-term partner creates a [[ThirdField]] that can either heal old wounds through shared daily practices or amplify inherited damage.
- [[CreativePresence]] depends on becoming receptive: absorbing good material, staying sensitive, finishing imperfect drafts while inspiration has momentum, and cultivating relaxed focus.
- [[SituationalMorality]] matters more than abstract claims about fixed human goodness or evil: scarcity, role assignments, incentives, and community norms can redirect ordinary people toward cruelty or care.

## Key Quotes
> "人是环境的产物" — the essay's core thesis.

> "选择自己的场，就是选择自己的人性走向。" — the closing moral formulation.
