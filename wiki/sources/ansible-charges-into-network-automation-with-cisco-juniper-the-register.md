## Summary
The Register reports that [[Ansible]] moved network-infrastructure automation into its core platform through Ansible 2.0 network modules for command, configuration, and templating. The launch positioned [[NetworkAutomation]] as a multivendor DevOps bridge for networking teams, with support for [[AristaNetworks]], [[Cisco]], [[Juniper]], [[CumulusNetworks]], and [[OpenSwitch]], while [[Huawei]] was notably absent.

## Key Claims
- [[Ansible]] expanded from systems automation into core [[NetworkAutomation]] by adding network modules to Ansible 2.0.
- The initial network framework targeted multivendor environments rather than a single vendor stack.
- Playbooks were presented as the mechanism for automating network command, configuration, and template tasks.
- The practical goals included configuration automation, testing and validation of existing network state, and continuous compliance against configuration drift.
- The pitch was cultural as well as technical: bring network teams into DevOps workflows without requiring network engineers to become programmers or programmers to become network specialists.
- [[Huawei]]'s absence from the launch partner list was treated as notable.

## Key Quotes
> "we're giving customers the ability to solve their complicated networking automation and workflow problems in a new way." - [[ToddBarr]] on bringing Ansible to networking.

> "We're not asking the networking guys to become programmers" - [[PeterSprygada]] on the intended boundary between networking and programming roles.
