## Summary
[[ZachHolman]] uses his 2015 dismissal from [[GitHub]] and conversations with other former employees to argue that firing should be treated as a consequential organizational process rather than compressed into “fire fast.” He connects clear expectations, humane treatment, deliberate access removal, truthful internal communication, separation terms, and continued alumni relationships into [[EmployeeTermination]], while emphasizing the asymmetry between a company that performs terminations repeatedly and a worker making decisions under shock.

## Key Claims
- Employment termination is common but socially concealed, which makes its emotional, financial, legal, and organizational consequences harder to improve.
- Clear expectations and a good-faith performance improvement plan can reduce damaging surprise, give an employee a real chance to recover, and provide time to prepare if recovery fails.
- A person being dismissed should avoid making immediate commitments while emotionally compromised, obtain terms in writing, and seek independent legal, tax, or financial advice where appropriate.
- Employers should automate access revocation and credential rotation while checking downstream effects on personal data, healthcare, payroll, equity, and other services tied to company accounts.
- Internal departure communication should protect legitimate privacy without falsely implying that a unilateral firing was mutual; misleading language damages trust among remaining employees.
- Severance, healthcare continuity, tax documents, stock-option exercise terms, and a reliable post-employment contact channel are part of the termination process rather than administrative afterthoughts.
- Coworker support and alumni networks can preserve relationships, aid recovery, support recruiting, and carry culture beyond formal employment, but former employees may need boundaries around company gossip.

## Key Quotes
> “The point is: surprise firings are the worst types of firings.” - on clear expectations and meaningful warning.

> “How we treat employees, past and present, is a reflection on the company itself.” - on termination as an expression of organizational culture.

## Contradictions
- Holman's defense of good-faith performance improvement plans qualifies [[ManagerialResponsibility]] and [[CompassionateManagement]] sources that reject PIPs categorically. A sham plan used only to manufacture a record is harmful, but clear expectations, documentation, response opportunity, accommodation, and a genuine recovery path can reduce surprise and bias.
- The article's warning that HR protects the company captures an incentive conflict but is too categorical as universal guidance; reporting duties, legal protections, union or representative support, external regulators, and organizational practice vary by jurisdiction and case.
- The statement that vested unexercised incentive stock options disappear after 90 days “by law” compresses a tax-and-contract issue. The source is useful evidence of employee pressure, not current legal or tax advice.
- This is an opinionated first-person U.S. technology-startup account. Holman's dismissal rationale remained uncertain, other people's stories are summarized rather than independently documented, and the legal, healthcare, tax, severance, equity, and at-will-employment claims are historical and jurisdiction-specific.
