## Summary
[[Wulc]] summarizes [[ZhangXiaoyu]]'s personal investing course section on market rules for ordinary investors. The article argues that individuals should use their freedom from institutional pressure to avoid obvious mistakes: do not overfit investment preferences to youth-era memories, do not rely on [[MarketTiming]], do not overuse macro forecasts, judge risk by consequences, and diversify across assets and geography. Its practical center is a conservative [[PersonalInvestorStrategy]] built around [[AssetAllocation]], [[IndexFundStrategy]], and clear sell reasons.

## Key Claims
- Individual investors have fewer elite resources than professionals, but they also have less career and client pressure to chase fashionable assets.
- Early market memories can create asset-class preferences that cause investors to miss later opportunities, so [[AssetAllocation]] should deliberately avoid one-asset attachment.
- [[MarketTiming]] is difficult both at short horizons and cycle horizons; being right too early can still fail under pressure, as the article illustrates with shorting before the 2000 technology crash and the 2008 crisis.
- [[MacroForecastingHumility]] matters because even sophisticated macro thinkers such as [[JohnMaynardKeynes]] struggled when investing from macro predictions and later shifted toward company-level value investing.
- [[InvestmentRiskDiscipline]] should evaluate what happens after a loss rather than pretending risk can be cleanly summarized as a single probability or percentage.
- [[HomeBias]] is risky because stock returns do not mechanically track domestic GDP growth, and investors often overallocate to familiar countries.

## Key Quotes
> "尽量忘掉你的投资偏好，多元配置你的投资组合" - course takeaway on avoiding asset-preference lock-in.

> "个人投资是一个无限游戏" - course framing for why selling solely after a gain can be a weak reason.

> "不要去想象风险的概念，而是要直接去衡量风险的结果" - practical risk framing.
