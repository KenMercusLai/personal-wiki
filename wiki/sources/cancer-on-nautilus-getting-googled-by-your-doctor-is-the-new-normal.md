## Summary
This Nautilus article argues that [[PatientTargetedOnlineSearch]] is becoming a common but ethically uneasy part of mental healthcare, especially in emergency settings where patients may be psychotic, intoxicated, suicidal, or unable to provide reliable collateral history. It uses a Facebook-triggered involuntary hospitalization, survey data from [[LiliyaGershengoren]], and ethics commentary from [[PaulAppelbaum]] and [[JohnLuo]] to show how online information can improve risk assessment while threatening patient privacy, trust, and legal expectations. The source also distinguishes searching from constructive online engagement: cancer-care clinicians may use social platforms to support patient communities if professional boundaries are maintained. The inspected lead image shows a silhouetted person facing a Google search page; it is illustrative of searching rather than independent evidence.

## Key Claims
- [[PatientTargetedOnlineSearch]] can provide useful collateral information when patients cannot or will not communicate clinically important facts.
- Emergency psychiatry is the strongest use case because acute intoxication, psychosis, suicidality, self-harm evidence, substance use, and family supports may be visible online.
- Clinicians who search without consent risk damaging privacy, trust, and the therapeutic relationship, especially with paranoid or involuntarily hospitalized patients.
- Repeated clinical use of online search may shift the [[StandardOfCare]] and create malpractice expectations around online warning signs.
- Saving online material into charts creates [[MedicalRecordPersistence]] because ephemeral social posts can become durable, third-party-accessible medical-record content.
- Online platforms can also support [[SocialMediaClinicalCare]] when clinicians use them for patient communities, coping strategies, and vetted information while maintaining boundaries.

## Key Quotes
> "Anecdotally, I know we do it all the time" - [[LiliyaGershengoren]] on psychiatrists searching for patients online.

> "medical records are forever" - [[PaulAppelbaum]] on preserving online material in charts.
