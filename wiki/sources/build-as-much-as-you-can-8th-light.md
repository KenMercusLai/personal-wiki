## Summary
This 8th Light essay argues for [[ProlificPractice]]: craft improves when people make many small things, learn from specific failures, and shorten the gap between thinking and doing. Using a pottery-class parable and 8th Light's apprenticeship culture, it reframes prolific output as a skill built through bounded daily making rather than a generic rejection of perfectionism. ## Key Claims
- [[ProlificPractice]] improves quality because repeated making creates more feedback than planning one perfect artifact.
- 8th Light's apprenticeship model treats building more things as the practical route to craft progression.
- The barrier to prolific output is often fear of starting, fear of building incorrectly, or over-research meant to avoid failure.
- Failure becomes useful information when the work unit is small and specific enough that the fallout does not swamp the lesson.
- A fixed, short work session can lower starting friction and gradually create an identity as someone who makes things.

## Key Quotes
