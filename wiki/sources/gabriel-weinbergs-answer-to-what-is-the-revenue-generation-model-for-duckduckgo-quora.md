## Summary
[[GabrielWeinberg]] explains [[DuckDuckGo]]'s business model as primarily current-query keyword advertising, supplemented by a smaller stream of anonymous Amazon and eBay affiliate commissions. He argues that profitable search does not require persistent personal profiles and contrasts this model with cross-site [[BehavioralTargeting]] by Google and Facebook. The account is first-party advocacy rather than an audited revenue or privacy report, and the saved copy ends before the promised list of actions for other companies.

## Key Claims
- Weinberg says DuckDuckGo had been profitable since 2014 without storing or sharing personal information about search users.
- Search advertisers bid on keywords, so an ad can respond to the current query without using a person's search history; DuckDuckGo presents this as its primary revenue model and as evidence for [[PrivacyPreservingSearch]].
- DuckDuckGo also receives a smaller revenue share when users reach Amazon or eBay through its results or !bang shortcuts and then buy, with no personally identifiable information reportedly exchanged and no effect on result ranking.
- Weinberg argues that large platforms' audience and reach, rather than maximal tracking, generate most of their advertising value, while broader profiling adds incremental revenue and enables invasive retargeting.
- The article connects cross-site behavioral profiles with risks including political manipulation, discriminatory exclusion, and disclosure of personal data to unfamiliar companies.

## Key Quotes
> "search advertisers buy search ads by bidding on keywords, not people" - Weinberg's distinction between current-query intent and person-level targeting.

> "Their business models don’t need to be this invasive." - the article's broader judgment about audience scale and tracking.

## Contradictions
- The claim of profitability since 2014 slightly sharpens the prior secondary account's statement that DuckDuckGo was profitable by 2015; the underlying financial results are not supplied in either source.
- Claims about Google and Facebook's tracker reach, indefinite profile storage, revenue dependence, and social effects are advocacy claims or second-hand statistics in this copy, not independently verified measurements.
- The saved article has no original publication date and stops immediately after promising actionable recommendations for other companies, so it should not be treated as a complete or current statement of DuckDuckGo's business model.
