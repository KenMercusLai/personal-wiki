## Summary
The author argues that speed cannot substitute for elapsed time in creating trust, maturity, quality, community, or durable commitment. Applying the metaphor of a decades-old tree to AI-assisted software, startups, compliance, and open source, the essay says some friction is valuable because it forces reflection or demonstrates reliable behavior over time. It also questions whether productivity tools truly return time to people when competition immediately converts saved hours into additional output.

## Key Claims
- [[TimeDependentValue]] describes goods such as trust, maturity, community, and durable commitment whose value is created or demonstrated through sustained experience rather than compressed production.
- [[ProductFlowFriction]] should distinguish incidental burden from safeguards such as compliance checks, permission systems, reviews, and cooling-off periods that support deliberation, accountability, or repeated correctness.
- Faster AI code generation can push pressure downstream to remove design, infrastructure, review, checklist, and permission controls even though those controls may protect software quality and organizational reliability.
- [[ProductLifecycleTrust]] is damaged when startups disappear without explaining what happened, helping customers transition, or acknowledging the relationship created by adoption.
- [[OpenSourceProjectMaintenance]] requires persistence, succession planning, or a community capable of carrying the project beyond its creator's initial enthusiasm; a brief burst of commits does not establish durability.
- [[FounderTimeLeverage]] does not automatically produce leisure, reflection, or quality because competitive pressure can capture freed capacity as a demand for more output.
- Long-term projects become commitments when maintainers keep showing up after momentary enthusiasm fades, allowing relationships and communities to develop around them.

## Key Quotes
> "Because some things just take time." - the essay's central claim that some forms of value cannot be accelerated.

> "Nobody is going to conjure trust, or quality, or community out of a weekend sprint." - on the limits of production speed.

## Connections
- [[TimeDependentValue]] - central concept that some value is created or verified only through sustained elapsed time.
- [[ProductFlowFriction]] - distinguishes removable process burden from deliberate pauses and controls that create safety or judgment.
- [[ProductLifecycleTrust]] - abrupt startup disappearance breaks the continuing relationship implied by customer adoption.
- [[OpenSourceProjectMaintenance]] - durable projects depend on persistence, succession, or a community that can outlast initial enthusiasm.
- [[FounderTimeLeverage]] - questions whether nominal time savings remain available for rest, reflection, or quality work.
- [[StartupExecutionSpeed]] - qualifies speed as a useful operating capability rather than a substitute for commitment, trust, or long-lived outcomes.

## Contradictions
- The source qualifies [[FounderTimeLeverage]] by arguing that saved time can be captured by competition and refilled with more work rather than becoming usable personal capacity.
- It qualifies [[StartupExecutionSpeed]] and low-friction product practice rather than rejecting them: rapid iteration can support learning, but speed becomes harmful when it removes controls or is mistaken for evidence of durability.
- The essay is a first-person practitioner argument without comparative measurements of software lifespan, company survival, compliance outcomes, AI-tool adoption, workload, or customer trust. Its claims about recent startup and open-source behavior are illustrative and unquantified.
