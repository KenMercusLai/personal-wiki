## Summary
INDIGO describes a practical learning and publishing workflow built around the [[FeynmanTechnique]]: choose a target, understand it through structure and comparison, explain it simply, review gaps, and internalize the result. The article extends that method into an information pipeline of random reading, [[FocusedReading]], note and short-opinion output, long-form articles, and courses. It also argues that modern note tools and LLM-powered assistants may reduce manual organization by summarizing, linking, and retrieving knowledge inside a personal knowledge base.

## Key Claims
- [[FeynmanTechnique]] treats teaching-oriented output as the test of whether learning has actually been internalized.
- [[ActiveLearning]] improves when learners explain ideas in simple language, revisit weak points, and form their own knowledge system.
- [[FocusedReading]] turns broad information intake into goal-directed filtering once the learner has one to three near-term creative topics.
- [[KnowledgeOutput]] can progress from notes and short opinions to structured long articles and finally courses that require deeper internalization.
- [[PersonalKnowledgeManagement]] depends on reliable capture, tagging or classification, retrieval, and reuse of reading notes, bookmarks, and drafts.
- [[AIKnowledgeAssistant]] tools could shift note organization from manual filing toward automated summarization, association, classification, and contextual recall.

## Key Quotes
> "学与用是互为一体的" - on treating use and output as inseparable from learning.

> "无法对外输出的学习就不能称为学习" - on output as a threshold for real learning.

> "最好的整理就是不要整理" - on the desired direction of AI-assisted knowledge organization.
