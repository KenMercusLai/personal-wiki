## Summary
[[Wulc]] summarizes a 得到 course on [[SpeedReadingMethod]] as a three-stage process: evaluate whether a nonfiction book deserves attention, speed-read for structure and key concepts, then deep-read only the parts worth turning into memory and output. The article reframes fast reading as selective attention rather than faster word consumption, connecting [[FocusedReading]] with [[ActiveLearning]] and [[LearningByWriting]].

## Key Claims
- [[SpeedReadingMethod]] starts by reducing reverence for books: nonfiction should be judged by value, relevance, and usable insight rather than treated as sacred text that must be read linearly.
- Evaluation is part of reading because prefaces, publisher copy, tables of contents, and argued reviews help decide whether a book is good and suitable for the reader.
- Speed reading means reading fewer words with better aim: visual scanning, semantic-unit navigation, and concept hunting reveal structure, intent, and high-value parts.
- [[FocusedReading]] improves when the reader looks for new concepts, underweighted old concepts, and concepts that may replace previous assumptions.
- Deep reading is justified when the book can become the reader's own understanding, memory, conversation material, writing, or teaching.
- [[ActiveLearning]] and [[LearningByWriting]] make deep reading effective because notes, reactions, drafts, and teaching force interaction with the material.

## Key Quotes
> "读书读三遍，从大往小看" - on moving from value judgment to structure to selected deep reading.

> "所谓的快速阅读，就是指快速地找出一段话的关键信息" - on fast reading as finding key information.

> "能记住、能输出" - on the test for effective reading.
