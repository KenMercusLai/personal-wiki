## Summary
[[Xiaoyong|小泳]] proposes that remembered sexual dreams may end because REM sleep is transitioning, an external or bodily stimulus causes awakening, physiological excitement crosses an arousal threshold, or anxiety and conflict disrupt the dream. The post also distinguishes an actually interrupted dream from a continuous experience that only appears fragmented because parts were not retained or recalled after waking. The author explicitly labels the explanation as personal speculation rather than scientific evidence, so [[DreamInterruption]] records a set of hypotheses and a useful interruption-versus-recall distinction, not an established account of dream physiology.

## Key Claims
- Dreams are described as most active during rapid eye movement (REM) sleep, which alternates with non-REM stages across the sleep cycle.
- A dream may stop when REM ends, when sleep shifts to another stage, or when the sleeper awakens near the transition.
- Noise, light, discomfort, the need to turn over, or bladder pressure may interrupt a sexual dream by waking the sleeper.
- Strong emotion or sexual arousal is proposed as another possible trigger for awakening.
- An apparently incomplete dream may reflect failure to remember intervening or later material rather than an actual break in the dream experience.
- Anxiety, stress, guilt, or discomfort with dream content are proposed as possible psychological disruptions.

## Key Quotes
> “我们所回忆的梦境缺乏连贯性可能是梦境片段被遗忘，而非实际停止。” — the post's distinction between dream interruption and later recall.

> “以上解释纯属作者个人推测，不属于科学依据范畴。” — the author's explicit evidence disclaimer.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction with an existing wiki page was identified.
- The post calls REM sleep “lighter” and treats sexual dreams as most common during REM, but supplies no study, definition, comparison with non-REM dreaming, or frequency data.
- The proposed arousal threshold and psychological “reset” mechanisms are not operationally defined or supported with measurements, and the post gives no way to distinguish them from ordinary awakening or incomplete recall.
- The final disclaimer explicitly prevents the proposed mechanisms from being treated as scientific findings, diagnostic guidance, or an explanation of any individual's sleep experience.
