## Summary
[[HitenShah]] argues in [[ProductHabits]] that SaaS acquisition became easier as tools, knowledge, and distribution costs improved, while retention became harder because competition, switching ease, and customer selectivity increased. The article reframes growth around [[SaaSRetention]] and [[ProductLedRetention]]: companies keep valuable customers by building products that compound usefulness, encourage deeper usage, and align marketing with the features retained customers actually value.

## Key Claims
- In modern SaaS, cheap acquisition can fill the funnel, but retention determines whether growth compounds.
- More competitors make customer attention scarce, so companies need differentiated products that become more useful over time.
- Freemium models, subscriptions, and portable data lower switching costs, making every retained customer a continuing choice rather than a locked-in account.
- Low distribution costs can attract weak-fit customers; product and marketing should emphasize the value that best customers keep using.
- [[Intercom]] illustrates retention through suite expansion around customer communication, [[Front]] illustrates account expansion and negative churn, and [[CrazyEgg]] illustrates marketing aligned to a specific sticky feature.
- The inspected images reinforce the article's claim: a SaaSFest tweet says acquisition is "dead," a chart shows acquisition getting easier while retention gets harder over time, and the Crazy Egg screenshot centers heatmaps as the marketing promise.

## Key Quotes
> "Today, getting tons of new users isn't hard. What's hard is keeping them." - on the source's acquisition-to-retention shift.

> "you win or lose based on the strength of your product" - on competition in the second wave of SaaS.

> "you're wasting time and money on customers who are going to churn." - on misaligned lead chasing.
