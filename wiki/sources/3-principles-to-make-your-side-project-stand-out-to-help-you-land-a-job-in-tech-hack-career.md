## Summary
[[HackCareer]] argues that side projects help tech job applications only when they are legible evidence of relevant ability rather than generic resume filler. The article distinguishes rare viral "Field of Dreams" projects such as [[YouTubeInstant]] from ordinary but useful "A for effort" projects, then says strong [[JobApplicationSideProjects]] should be relevant to the target role, show depth, include at least two iterations, and be made obvious through resume placement, metrics, and public documentation.

## Key Claims
- Side projects are not automatically useful for a tech job search; they create value only when they answer doubts about a candidate's experience.
- Rare attention-generating projects can produce unsolicited opportunities, but most candidates should not treat viral success as the standard.
- [[JobApplicationSideProjects]] need relevance, depth, and at least two iterations to demonstrate applied ability and care.
- Learning a skill is not itself a side project; the candidate needs to ship an artifact that represents their work quality.
- A side project must be easy for resume screeners or interviewers to notice, understand, and evaluate through description, credibility indicators, and public process documentation.

## Key Quotes
> "Just having a side project doesn't mean it'll actually help you land a job." - on the source's core warning.

> "learning a skill isn't a side project, you have to ship something." - on the difference between study and demonstrable work.

> "A good side project that adds value to your job application is relevant, has depth, and has at least two iterations." - on the three-part standard.
