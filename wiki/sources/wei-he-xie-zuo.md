## Summary
[[Laixintao]] argues that writing is valuable less as text production than as a human practice of recording change, clarifying thought, expressing oneself, and finding other people. The essay treats diaries and blogs as time capsules, describes explanation as a test that exposes incomplete understanding, and argues that AI-generated prose cannot substitute for those functions when it bypasses the writer's own memory, reasoning, voice, and direct relationship with readers.

## Key Claims
- Writing creates a durable record through which a later self can recover earlier beliefs, circumstances, emotions, questions, and changes that unaided memory would lose.
- [[LearningByWriting]] turns a vague mental outline into inspectable language, revealing gaps that require further research before the writer can explain a subject clearly.
- Repeated blogging can improve explanatory skill even without an audience, and old technical posts can later help their author reconstruct knowledge that has become rusty.
- Public writing supports expression and direct human connection because a writer's own words let like-minded readers recognize the person and viewpoint behind the text.
- Low-pressure blogging can satisfy expression without school-style length, structure, audience, or traffic constraints.
- [[ProfessionalBlogging]] may create technical influence and friendships, although the author predicts that blogs will lose influence as fewer people use them to learn technology.
- The source rejects AI-generated articles because they do not preserve the author's lived record, perform the author's thinking exercise, or supply the author's direct voice; it does not separately evaluate bounded AI assistance within a human-owned writing process.

## Key Quotes
> “写作就像创作时间胶囊” — on writing as dialogue with one's past and future selves.

> “水中的月亮还是水中的月亮” — on a vague idea remaining vague when generation replaces the writer's attempt to explain it.

## Contradictions
- The essay's categorical claim that AI cannot replace thought qualifies workflow accounts in [[AIAssistedWriting]] that use models for brainstorming, rough drafts, transcription, checking, or revision while retaining human judgment. The apparent conflict narrows when replacement of the central cognitive and expressive act is distinguished from bounded assistance, a distinction this source does not develop.
- The prediction that blogs will no longer create technical influence sits in tension with the same essay's claim that blogs can still create friendship and with existing [[ProfessionalBlogging]] retrospectives that report delayed discovery and career benefits. The source supplies personal experience and prediction rather than readership, learning, influence, or career-outcome data.
- Claims that writing improves thought, skill, recall, freedom, happiness, influence, or connection are plausible mechanisms and first-person observations here, not controlled comparisons across media, AI-use patterns, writers, or readers.
