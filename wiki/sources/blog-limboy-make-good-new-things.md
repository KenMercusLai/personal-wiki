## Summary
[[Limboy]] reflects on [[PaulGraham]]'s "make good new things" advice and [[SteveJobs]]' "make something wonderful" philosophy, arguing that creation is a lifelong way to express care for the world. The essay connects writing, apps, visual creation, and AI-assisted tools into a practical ethic: AI lowers the threshold for making, but the flood of average output makes high standards, quality inputs, and cognitive curiosity more important.

## Key Claims
- [[MakeGoodNewThings]] frames creation as a meaningful life orientation, not only a career tactic or productivity goal.
- AI coding assistants and image tools expand who can create software, writing, and visual work, making creation more accessible to non-specialists.
- Creation has a value-investing profile: even failed projects can return learning, self-solved problems, and satisfaction, while successful projects may help others and attract peers.
- In an AI-abundant world, [[AIEraCreativeStandards]] become a survival line because average work is cheap and easily copied.
- [[CognitiveCuriosity]] supplies creative direction, while high standards determine how far the creator is willing to push the work.
- Making things can anchor attention in the present and create a traceable record of life that passive consumption cannot provide.

## Key Quotes
> "创造美好的新事物" - central phrase borrowed from Paul Graham and used as the essay's core thesis.

> "Average is over" - the essay's shorthand for why AI-era creators need standards above the new cheap average.
