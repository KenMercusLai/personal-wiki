## Summary
The New Yorker profiles [[TenXManagement]], a talent agency that adapted music-management practices to represent elite freelance technologists in negotiation, contracting, invoicing, and project matching. Through [[AltayGuvench]] and the agency's clients, the article presents the [[TenXEngineer]] as a scarce, unusually productive programmer whose value combines technical range, readable and efficient code, communication, and business judgment. It also shows the model's limits: clients may misunderstand scope, startups may need a future employee rather than a freelancer, critics question agent-driven selection, and exceptional-talent economics do not generalize to most workers.

## Key Claims
- Demand for experienced programmers across technology and non-technology firms created a 2014 market in which startups struggled to compete with large companies on pay, equity, and amenities.
- [[TenXManagement]] represented programmers rather than employers, took a 15% share, vetted code and communication, and handled negotiation, contracts, invoicing, collection, and project matching.
- [[AltayGuvench]] argues that good code is readable by humans, avoids repetition, and performs work efficiently; the agency also screens for client-facing communication.
- The article's [[TenXEngineer]] frame treats top programmers as capable of disproportionate output, but its examples are anecdotes and projected business value rather than controlled productivity measurements.
- Agency support can make [[ProgrammerFreelancing]] more viable by raising rates, reducing administrative work, smoothing project flow, and clarifying scope.
- The proposed Hollywood-style packaging model would assemble design, engineering, testing, branding, and marketing around a client project without full-time hiring.
- The model remains selective: critics argue that the strongest engineers already have access to work, while economist David Autor says rock-star representation fits scarce, indivisible expertise rather than labor generally.

## Key Quotes
> "The first quality of good code is that it's readable" - Guvench on code review.

> "part of our goal is to de-risk freelancing" - Guvench on the agency's role.

## Connections
- [[TenXManagement]] - the profiled agency representing freelance technologists.
- [[AltayGuvench]] - engineer, musician, early client, and later partner who vets technical talent.
- [[TenXEngineer]] - the article's contested model of disproportionate individual technical value.
- [[ProgrammerFreelancing]] - the work arrangement the agency tries to make more stable and administratively manageable.
- [[TeamProductivity]] - later team-centered evidence qualifies the article's individual-superstar framing.
- [[EngineeringTeamMotivation]] - competition for scarce engineers links compensation, purpose, opportunity cost, and working conditions.

## Contradictions
- The individual-centered [[TenXEngineer]] model conflicts with [[10x-ing-your-team-the-end-of-superstar-developer-culture]], which argues that team output and downstream cleanup are better measures than visible individual speed.
- The quoted 2014 hourly rates, salaries, company valuations, perks, and talent shortages are historical observations, not current market benchmarks.
- The article reports client earnings doubling from 2013 to 2014 but supplies no client-level distribution, comparison group, billable-hours data, or separation of rate growth from workload growth.
