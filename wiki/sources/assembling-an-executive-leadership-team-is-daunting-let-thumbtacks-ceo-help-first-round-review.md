## Summary
[[FirstRoundReview]] interviews [[MarcoZappacosta]] about how [[Thumbtack]] learned to recruit an experienced executive team after years of first-time-founder mistakes. Zappacosta's process narrows each executive role to three essential strengths, splits early founder conversations into mutual-interest and fit sessions, relies heavily on broad reference checks, and ends by comparing reference themes with the candidate's own self-awareness. The inspected local illustration is a portrait-style drawing credited to Alejandro Garcia Ibanez and adds no separate factual evidence beyond article presentation.

## Key Claims
- First-time founders often cannot directly evaluate senior functional expertise, so executive hiring should use a learnable process rather than founder confidence alone.
- Executive job descriptions should identify the three most essential role strengths instead of listing every desirable skill.
- Founder interviews should separate mutual selling and context-setting from deeper fit assessment around ambition, motivation, failure, vulnerability, and leadership style.
- Reference checks should be broad, candid, cumulative, and targeted to the role's required strengths rather than limited to formal references.
- The final executive-hiring conversation should test self-awareness by presenting reference themes, asking why the candidate thinks those themes appeared, and making the evaluation reciprocal through 360-degree feedback.
- Zappacosta treats executive hiring as high-beta: a successful hire can become a force multiplier, while a mismatch may take several quarters to reveal itself.

## Key Quotes
> "I trust my gut less and references more" - Peter Currie's hiring advice as relayed by Zappacosta.

> "A misevaluation of fit... is usually the reason executive hires don't work out." - Zappacosta on executive-hiring failure.
