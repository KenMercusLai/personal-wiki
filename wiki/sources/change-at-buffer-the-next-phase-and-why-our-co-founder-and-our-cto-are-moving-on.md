## Summary
[[JoelGascoigne]] announces that [[LeoWidrich]] and [[SunilSadasivan]] are leaving [[Buffer]] after a period of strategic misalignment about the company's future. The post reframes Buffer's next phase around long-term sustainable growth, full remote work, product reliability, transparency, diversity and inclusion, and an empowering culture rather than faster hiring, further fundraising, or a more traditional board-led scaling path. The four remote images referenced in the source currently return 404 from this environment, so they could not be inspected or used as evidence.

## Key Claims
- By February 2017, Buffer had grown to 65,000 paying customers, an 80-person team, and more than $13 million in annual revenue.
- Buffer's earlier path involved pendulum swings between bootstrapping and fundraising, self-management and traditional management, slow hiring and rapid hiring, and product/culture focus versus growth-rate focus.
- Gascoigne says Buffer was recommitting to one path: a long-term, sustainable, fully remote company focused on reliable social-media tooling, transparency, culture, freedom, diversity and inclusion, creativity, learning, innovation, and joy at work.
- Leo Widrich and Sunil Sadasivan were leaving because their preferred company-building paths differed from Gascoigne's vision, especially around senior leadership timing, future fundraising, board structure, product quality, culture investment, and performance expectations.
- Widrich would remain a non-executive board member and advisor while exploring a new SaaS product around HR or diversity; Sadasivan planned to take time, possibly start another company, join a high-impact engineering organization, and experiment on product ideas with Widrich.
- Buffer communicated the news progressively in January and early February 2017 through executive-team conversations, one-on-one team announcements, investor updates, and then a public post.
