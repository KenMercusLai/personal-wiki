## Summary
[[AlexDanco]] applies a scarcity-shift framework to media, arguing that the web moved strategic value from content and distribution toward pull and push, while front-facing cameras and ephemeral messages then made varied, momentary self-expression abundant. He interprets [[Facebook]]'s feed as a bargain exchanging distribution for one persistent public identity and proposes that the next scarce resources are situated presence—being “here” and “now”—with [[Snapchat]] as a camera-and-ephemerality wedge, [[Twitch]] as a stronger participatory-presence example, and [[AugmentedReality]] as a location-bound possibility. The essay is a speculative 2016 strategy argument rather than measured evidence of adoption, identity effects, market structure, or later winners.

## Key Claims
- The web made traditional media content and distribution more abundant, shifting advantage toward pull—destination and search intent—and push—feeds and publication into an audience.
- Facebook's “grand bargain” combined a diverse ranked feed with persistent identity, broad audiences, and constrained variation in how each person could present themselves.
- Front-facing cameras increased the expressive bandwidth of social communication, while ephemeral messages reduced the cost of presenting temporary moods and personas.
- Snapchat's momentary communication increased access to varied self-expression but, in Danco's account, did not fully create a durable sense of shared place.
- [[HereAndNowMedia]] becomes more distinctive when synchronous participation, location, audience interaction, or mutable works make presence part of the content rather than merely a delivery mode.
- Twitch is presented as combining active play, passive viewing, and audience participation, while Pokémon Go illustrates location-bound AR and *The Life of Pablo* illustrates streaming media that can change after release.
- A technology that makes an old scarce resource abundant does not automatically capture the next profit pool; value may move to the new bottleneck organized around presence.

## Contradictions
- The essay's claim that persistent feeds enforce a “one-self” identity is interpretive and overbroad: users can segment audiences, operate multiple accounts, vary posts, or use pseudonyms, while ephemerality does not guarantee authenticity.
- Danco explicitly says he did not use Twitch, so the claim that it had found the new paradigm rests on secondhand observation rather than participant research, product data, or economic evidence.
- “Here and now” is proposed as a future profit center without definitions, comparison cases, market measurements, or a falsifiable test; the named products may succeed for other reasons.
- The article's Instagram Stories, Pokémon Go, Snapchat, Twitch, and streaming-album examples are bounded to 2016 and do not establish their later trajectories.
