## Summary
[[MathildeCollin]] describes the preparation and execution of [[Front]]'s $10 million Series A process, which began after the company reached $100,000 in monthly recurring revenue with low churn and a clear roadmap. Her retrospective connects advance investor relationship-building and a deliberately compressed raise with the deck evidence investors valued—capital efficiency and customer expansion—and the gaps they challenged in acquisition scalability, financial projections, engagement data, and buyer definition.

## Key Claims
- Founders should build investor relationships before a round because a lead investor may become a long-term board member, while limiting how much company and market information they disclose during informal conversations.
- Collin considered Front ready to raise after it reached $100,000 in MRR, low churn, and a clear roadmap; she then shifted decisively into fundraising rather than running an indefinite partial process.
- Collin spent about 90% of her workdays building the deck, gathering data, making projections, and iterating from investor feedback; Front received three term sheets within ten days.
- The team accepted one term sheet and ended other meetings because the concentrated process was already imposing a visible productivity cost on a 15-person company.
- Investors responded strongly to Front spending less than its ARR and to customers spending 50% more after 12 months net of churn.
- The deck was weak on scalable acquisition channels and did not contain a model explaining how the company would deploy the new capital.
- Collin later judged that the deck also needed engagement measures such as time in app, DAU, MAU, and DAU/MAU, plus a better-defined buyer to support outbound and paid acquisition.

## Key Quotes
> "Raise or don’t raise. But don’t ‘kind of raise’" - Collin's argument for a concentrated process.

> "10 days later we had 3 term sheets." - the reported result of the fundraising sprint.
