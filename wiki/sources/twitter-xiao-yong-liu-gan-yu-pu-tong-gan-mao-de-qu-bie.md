## Summary
[[Xiaoyong|小泳]] distinguishes influenza from the common cold by pathogen, typical symptom pattern, anatomical scope, and potential severity. The post describes a common cold as a usually mild, self-limited upper-respiratory syndrome caused by several non-influenza viruses, whereas influenza is specifically caused by influenza viruses and can become severe through pneumonia, worsening underlying disease, acute respiratory distress syndrome, encephalopathy, or multiorgan dysfunction. This is a concise educational social-media post without citations, diagnostic rules, treatment advice, or explicit red-flag guidance, so [[InfluenzaCommonColdDistinction]] should not be used to diagnose an individual illness.

## Key Claims
- Influenza and the common cold are not synonyms: influenza names illness caused by influenza viruses, while “common cold” describes an acute upper-respiratory syndrome caused by several non-influenza viruses.
- The Chinese translation “流行性感冒” can encourage a misleading association between influenza and an ordinary cold despite their different risk profiles.
- A typical cold begins acutely with nasal symptoms such as sneezing, congestion, and watery discharge; cough, throat irritation, postnasal drip, and several additional local symptoms may also occur.
- Common colds usually cause no fever or systemic symptoms, or only low fever and mild malaise, chills, or headache, and uncomplicated cases generally resolve in five to seven days.
- A respiratory virus can affect sites from the nose to the alveoli, so lower-airway involvement should be described more specifically as viral pharyngitis, laryngitis, bronchitis, or pneumonia rather than remaining under the loose label “cold.”
- Influenza is self-limited in many cases but can cause substantial suffering, pneumonia, worsening of underlying disease, critical illness, and death.

## Key Quotes
> “流感不是感冒” — the post's central correction to everyday terminology.

> “尽管是自限性疾病，但患病期间会给患者带来极大的痛苦” — on why spontaneous recovery does not make influenza trivial.

## Contradictions
- The post calls the common cold “not really a disease” while also describing it as an acute upper-respiratory infection with symptoms, examination findings, and a typical course. It is clearer to treat “common cold” as a syndrome label rather than deny that affected people are ill.
- The text says colds usually lack fever and systemic symptoms but does not provide a validated symptom rule that can reliably distinguish influenza, another respiratory infection, or a complication in an individual patient.
- The severe influenza outcomes named are clinically consequential, but the archived post provides no references, incidence estimates, risk-group definitions, diagnostic criteria, or guidance on when to seek care.
