## Summary
[[JustinJackson]] describes a morning loop in which checking Product Hunt, Twitter, and Medium turns competitor and peer updates into jealousy, stress, and reduced capacity to create. He argues that founders and creators should redirect attention toward understanding and helping their users while improving their own craft and product. The essay connects comparison-driven [[CreatorAnxiety]] with the customer and skill priorities of [[StartupFocus]], but offers personal reflection rather than clinical or comparative evidence.

## Key Claims
- Monitoring competitors and peers can turn comparison into jealousy, anger, sadness, or despair before productive work begins.
- Worry consumes energy without changing competitors' behavior and can reduce the attention available for making useful work.
- Competitor obsession does not itself improve customer service, product quality, or creative skill.
- A more actionable focus is to understand users, audiences, customers, and fans, then build deeper connection and trust with them.
- The complementary inward focus is deliberate improvement of one's skill, expertise, competence, craft, and product.

## Key Quotes
> "Focus on how you're helping people." - Jackson's alternative to monitoring peers and competitors.

> "Agonizing over your competition doesn't help you serve your customers better." - on the opportunity cost of comparison.

## Connections
- [[JustinJackson]] - author describing his own comparison-and-stress loop.
- [[CreatorAnxiety]] - peer and competitor monitoring turns uncertain external signals into emotional and creative pressure.
- [[StartupFocus]] - the proposed response prioritizes users, product improvement, and skill over competitor fixation.
- [[CustomerLedProductDevelopment]] - learning what users want is one of the essay's two actionable priorities.
- [[AttentionManagement]] - morning feeds can allocate attention to uncontrollable external events before creative work begins.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction was found. The essay reinforces existing customer-over-competitor and morning-attention claims while making the comparison-to-stress-to-displaced-work sequence more explicit.
- The account is a short personal and motivational essay. Its statements about adrenaline, cortisol, emotion, and creative impairment are not supported here with clinical evidence, and selective competitor monitoring can still provide useful market intelligence when it serves a defined decision.
