## Summary
This [[Drift]] interview summary distills [[PatGrady]]'s advice from [[SequoiaCapital]] on building durable startups. It argues that important companies solve real problems in non-conventional ways, enter large markets through narrow wedges, preserve culture and communication as they approach 150 employees, and avoid trying to buy scale with hype or excess capital.

## Key Claims
- Venture-scale companies should be "fundamental companies" that can define and dominate a category over decades rather than follow a conventional playbook.
- Companies exist to solve durable customer problems, not primarily to enrich founders or investors.
- [[SequoiaCapital]]'s funnel is highly selective, with roughly 500 first meetings per investment in the period described.
- Large markets are best entered through a narrow slice that earns the right to expand.
- Around 150 employees, informal shared context weakens and companies need explicit systems, cadence, and culture.
- Founders should avoid chasing unicorn hype, raising more money than needed, or hiring people attracted only to growth theater.
- In more competitive SaaS markets, companies need a genuinely superior value proposition because spending alone cannot force scale.

## Key Quotes
> "Conventional wisdom builds conventional companies." - on why category-defining startups cannot simply follow the default playbook.

> "You can't brute force your way into scale." - on competition and product-market fit in crowded SaaS markets.
