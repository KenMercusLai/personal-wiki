## Summary
[[AlexisMadrigal]] argues that [[Snapchat]]'s description of itself as a camera company identifies a broader shift: a phone camera is becoming an input and transformation interface for social communication rather than only a device for faithful image capture. Snapchat Lenses exemplify [[AugmentedReality]] at consumer scale by editing the captured face in real time, making self-presentation playful and plastic; the essay contrasts this with the older expectation that AR would overlay useful annotations on external space through glasses.

## Key Claims
- In picture-based communication, the camera can help generate the social graph because captured and processed images become messages that sustain the network.
- Snapchat Lenses do more than superimpose graphics: they process the video base layer in real time by smoothing skin, enlarging eyes, or transforming a face into a character.
- Treating the image sensor as an input method points toward new interaction models rather than merely better digital photography.
- The article frames successful consumer [[AugmentedReality]] as camera-mediated self-transformation, contrasting it with older visions of data glasses annotating rooms, landscapes, and nearby people.
- Rotating Lenses can support repeat use because the available transformations change daily, while sponsored Lenses create a commercial surface.
- Looksery's reported US$150 million acquisition is presented as the technical foundation for Lenses, but the article supplies no architecture, internal attribution, or measured return.
- Filters let people communicate a chosen mood or identity rather than claim visual fidelity, making the camera part of narrative self-construction.

> "the image sensor as an input method" - Benedict Evans's framing of the camera as an interface rather than only a capture device.

> "Filters don’t always blur out the facts; they can work as storytellers" - Rachel Syme on filters as part of personal narrative.

## Contradictions
- The essay challenges but does not invalidate older spatial-overlay models of AR; it establishes Snapchat Lenses as a large-scale counterexample, not that glasses, environmental annotation, or utility interfaces would fail.
- Claims about teenage preference, daily use, emotional expression, social-graph ownership, and the future plasticity of online selves are interpretive and lack comparative adoption, retention, wellbeing, or identity evidence.
