## Summary
[[SaschaFast]] argues that notes intended to last for decades should be edited for a future reader who no longer shares the writer's immediate context. His [[DurableNoteWriting]] method combines simple prose with explicit, content-specific templates, then puts a descriptive title and a short opening summary before the detail. This sharpens [[ZettelkastenMethod]] by treating each note's independent intelligibility as a maintenance requirement rather than assuming that storage and links can rescue unclear writing.

## Key Claims
- A feeling of present clarity does not guarantee that the written note will remain intelligible years later.
- Notes should be edited before archival because a long-lived knowledge system preserves weak formulations as readily as useful ones.
- Short sentences, paragraphs, and chunked prose reduce the effort required to reconstruct an old note.
- Explicit templates should match the note's subject: an entity description, a stock-and-flow analysis, a debate map, and a formal argument need different fields.
- A clear, content-bearing title should state the note's substantive point rather than merely gesture at a broad topic.
- The first sentences should summarize the note so a future reader can quickly judge its relevance.
- [[ChristianTietze]] adds that unclear notes are also harder to retrieve and connect because they may omit useful search terms, titles, tags, and links.

## Key Quotes
> "Write simple and in chunks." - on sentence and paragraph structure for later comprehension.

> "Make it easy for your future self." - on writing for a reader separated from the original context.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction with existing wiki content. The source makes the existing requirement for independently understandable permanent notes more operational by specifying prose, template, title, and opening-summary practices.
- The recommendations come from two practitioners' retrospective experience rather than comparative evidence; the article does not establish that short sentences, front-loaded summaries, or templates improve comprehension for every note type or reader.
