## Summary
[[JasonFried]] presents [[37signals]]' internal communication system as asynchronous by default, writing-led, deliberately slow, and attached to the work being discussed. The company reports putting almost all internal communication in [[Basecamp]], using automatic daily and weekly check-ins, six-week Heartbeats and Kickoffs, durable announcements, and project-local comments to share context without making everyone follow everything. The supplied Markdown contains no effective image references.

## Key Claims
- [[AsynchronousWorkplaceCommunication]] should be the default for substantial work, with real-time conversation reserved for cases that genuinely benefit from synchronized attention.
- Complete written thoughts preserve decisions, include people who were absent, support future employees, and reduce dependence on meetings or immediate replies.
- Urgency and response pressure can worsen judgment, interrupt focused work, and turn the sender's timing into everyone else's obligation.
- Communication becomes more useful when factual context and spatial context stay together: task discussion belongs with the task, document discussion with the document, and project work inside its project.
- [[Basecamp]] reports centralizing 98% of 37signals' internal communication so announcements, discussions, decisions, project work, and social exchanges share one durable system of record.
- Predictable prompts and cadences can produce lightweight awareness without universal monitoring: daily reflection, weekly projection, optional social questions, six-week retrospective Heartbeats, and forward-looking Kickoffs.
- Meetings are treated as a costly last resort because their nominal duration understates the combined participant time and interruption they consume.

## Key Quotes
> "Real-time sometimes, asynchronous most of the time." - the guide's channel default.

> "Writing solidifies, chat dissolves." - on using complete written thoughts for consequential decisions.

## Contradictions
- The guide tensions sources that treat recurring meetings and all-hands as standard alignment infrastructure: 37signals instead makes meetings a last resort and relies on durable writing and scheduled asynchronous prompts. This is a contextual disagreement about operating design, not evidence that either model is universally superior.
- The article is a first-party statement of 37signals' norms. It reports no comparative productivity, decision-quality, inclusion, retention, or employee-experience evidence, and the claimed 98% Basecamp share is not independently measured here.
