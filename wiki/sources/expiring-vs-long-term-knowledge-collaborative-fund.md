## Summary
[[MorganHousel]] distinguishes short-lived facts about recent events and performance from durable explanations of mechanisms, incentives, and recurring patterns. He argues that [[KnowledgeDurability]] matters because long-term knowledge accumulates, transfers across domains, and supplies the [[MentalModels]] needed to interpret news and other expiring information rather than simply replacing it.

## Key Claims
- Expiring knowledge reports what happened, while long-term knowledge asks why it happened and whether the mechanism is likely to recur.
- Current figures such as revenue growth, vacancy rates, or one-year manager performance can inform present decisions but lose value quickly when detached from durable causal context.
- Long-term knowledge can compound because mechanisms and frameworks connect with knowledge from other domains and improve later interpretation.
- A company's periodic results are easier to understand through the durable sources and defenses of its competitive advantage than as isolated numbers.
- Management quality is better studied through recurring capacities such as motivation, tenacity, and execution than through any single problem a company encounters.
- Books are not automatically superior to news, but enduring frameworks can help a reader decide which news deserves attention and how to interpret it.
- Asking whether information will matter in one, ten, or eighty years is a selection heuristic, not a requirement to avoid all short-lived information.

## Key Quotes
> "Expiring knowledge tells you what happened; long-term knowledge tells you why something happened and is likely to happen again." - Housel on durability and recurrence.

> "Will I care about this a year from now? Ten years from now?" - Housel's reading filter.

## Contradictions
- The essay qualifies medium-based reading hierarchies: books can carry durable knowledge and news can be transient, but durability depends on explanatory content rather than format alone.
- The argument is a practitioner heuristic supported by examples and personal recall, not a measured comparison of retention, transfer, or decision quality across books, news, and other media.
