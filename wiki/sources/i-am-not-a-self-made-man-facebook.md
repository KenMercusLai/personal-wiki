## Summary
In a foreword for [[TimFerriss]]'s *Tools of Titans*, [[ArnoldSchwarzenegger]] rejects the label “self-made” and attributes his achievements to personal drive interacting with family, teachers, coaches, shelter, mentors, role models, career sponsors, and voters. He turns that autobiography into a broader argument for humility and continuous learning: acknowledging help does not erase effort, but claiming solitary authorship hides the social and material conditions that made effort productive.

## Key Claims
- [[LuckAndEffortInSuccess]] should include social support and institutional participation alongside birth, timing, preparation, judgment, and effort when attributing an outcome.
- Schwarzenegger credits his parents, teachers, and weightlifting coaches with supplying discipline, useful knowledge, and foundational skills.
- Reg Park gave him a model connecting bodybuilding with film, while Joe Weider helped bring him to the United States, promoted his bodybuilding career, and taught him about business.
- Lucille Ball supplied an early Hollywood break, and Schwarzenegger treats the 4,206,284 Californians who voted for him in 2003 as necessary participants in his election as governor.
- [[DeliberateNetworkBuilding]] includes actively seeking wisdom from mentors, idols, and people in different fields, not merely receiving help passively.
- Treating the world as a classroom requires admitting knowledge gaps, remaining open to unexpected lessons, and continuing to learn.
- The saved Facebook rendering includes audience disagreement about whether “self-made” means solitary success or self-directed advancement, showing that the label itself is contested.
- Four embedded Facebook CDN photographs could not be inspected because every URL now returns HTTP 403; their captions identify mentor and hero contexts, but no visual details were inferred or retained.

## Key Quotes
> “I am not a self-made man. I got a lot of help.” - Schwarzenegger's attribution thesis.

> “Never stop learning. Ever.” - the foreword's closing imperative.

## Contradictions
- The source does not contradict the wiki's existing luck-and-effort synthesis; it corrects accounts that treat achievement as either pure personal effort or pure contingency by adding specific forms of social assistance.
- Several saved commenters defend a narrower definition of “self-made” as pursuing one's own goals from humble circumstances. This is a semantic qualification rather than evidence that Schwarzenegger succeeded without help.
- The account is autobiographical and rhetorically selected. It does not independently verify every causal contribution, measure the relative weight of effort and assistance, or establish that every reader can access comparable mentors, sponsors, institutions, or public support.
