## Summary
In an excerpt from *Behind the Cloud*, [[MarcBenioff]] describes how [[Salesforce]] crossed an early trust barrier by recruiting pioneering customers through personal networks, giving them free access, and treating them as product-design partners. [[BlueMartiniSoftware]] became the first customer after its sales team accepted a hosted CRM that could be running in two weeks without a large enterprise-software investment. The account joins [[FounderLedSales]], [[CustomerLedProductDevelopment]], and [[FreemiumAcquisition]] into one loop: find a user with an urgent problem, collect and prioritize feedback, ship quickly, verify the result, and convert demonstrated value into low-commitment monthly payment.

## Key Claims
- Prospects were reluctant to place sensitive customer lists on an unproven hosted service, so Salesforce targeted pioneers willing to participate in something new rather than trying to persuade the whole market at once.
- [[BlueMartiniSoftware]] needed CRM but could not afford or operationally absorb traditional enterprise software; Salesforce reportedly deployed its service within two weeks and avoided a million-dollar upfront investment.
- Before Salesforce had a formal sales organization, team members used their personal networks to find prospects; product manager Diane Mark reportedly found the second customer through a chance conversation at a market.
- Salesforce called its five free pilot customers “design partners,” contacted them frequently, and treated their requests and usage as inputs to engineering rather than as passive beta feedback.
- An in-product idea button and the “bugforce” request database created a fast, prioritized feedback loop based partly on request frequency, while the small development team reportedly shipped added functionality within weeks.
- Salesforce hired its first dedicated salesperson only after the service had measurable customer value, then converted free users to monthly paying accounts by combining prior product experience with plan flexibility and low exit cost.
- Benioff summarizes the operating loop as staying close to customers, tracking and responding to requests, checking whether the response met the need, and observing actual product use.

## Key Quotes
> "treat them as partners" - Benioff's summary of the relationship he recommends with early customers.

> "they became the eyes and ears of the engineering team" - on design partners translating use into product requirements.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction was found. The source strengthens the wiki's founder-led sales and customer-led product material by showing the practices operating as one early SaaS loop.
- The account is a favorable founder retrospective excerpt. It reports selected successes but provides no rejected-request record, conversion denominator, retention cohort, security evidence, comparison group, or independent basis for attributing later success to the design-partner or free-pilot strategy.
