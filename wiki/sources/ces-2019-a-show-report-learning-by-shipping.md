## Summary
[[StevenSinofsky]] reads CES 2019 as a show where many consumer-electronics ingredients had matured, but too many products still felt one product-management iteration away from reliable mass-market utility. The report's central question is where the "smarts" should live across connected devices, cloud services, voice assistants, TV runtimes, radios, sensors, and apps. Its strongest reusable lesson is that cheap connectivity and broad APIs create impressive demos faster than they create coherent, trustworthy, and maintainable consumer experiences.

## Key Claims
- [[ConsumerElectronicsIntegration]] is now the core product problem: cheap radios, cloud APIs, and commodity processors let almost every device connect, but users still experience fragmented systems and fragile setup.
- [[VoiceAssistantUX]] was omnipresent at CES 2019, yet simple command APIs shifted too much burden onto users who must remember flat vocabularies, device names, and awkward phrases.
- [[SmartHomeInteroperability]] improved materially in security, cameras, appliances, lighting, and infrastructure monitoring, but the practical home still requires multiple apps, hubs, standards, and first-party systems.
- [[StreamingAppUX]] extends beyond app interface polish into the broader TV runtime question: discovery, search, subscriptions, casting, voice, and device software all compete to become the receiver of television.
- [[AIMarketingHype]] was visible in "AI" labels applied to TVs, washing machines, cameras, and gadgets, while a smaller number of examples such as EMG-trained prosthetics had clearer learning-model substance.
- CES 2019 showed display, camera, wireless, PC-gaming, and startup energy, but many of these advances remained bounded by standards timing, physical infrastructure, product ecosystem fit, or unclear consumer need.

## Key Quotes
> "Where should the smarts be?" - framing question for connected consumer products.

> "AI was everywhere." - setup for the report's critique of AI as marketing language.

> "USB-C has won." - shorthand for the shrinking but still confusing connector landscape.
