## Summary
[[JoshElman]] argues that early consumer startups should not be categorically afraid of building distribution on large platforms such as [[Facebook]], [[Google]], [[Twitter]], or [[Instagram]], but they must use those platforms as a path toward independent demand rather than as permanent dependency. The Greylock article frames [[PlatformDistributionDependence]] as a needle-threading problem: borrowed reach can accelerate early growth, while API, feed, ranking, and policy changes can break products that never build their own user relationship. The attached deck is listed as the presentation "Launching a Rocketship Off Someone Else's Back / How to Build on Other Platforms"; the local embedded slide images after the lead rocket photo are only 60x34 thumbnails, so their detailed text could not be reliably extracted.

## Key Claims
- Platform distribution can be useful for early startups because large platforms already aggregate the users that a new product lacks.
- Startups should build their own durable product and direct demand rather than expecting a missing feature or partnership with a platform owner to become the whole company.
- Platform risk is real because owners can change APIs, feeds, rankings, or data access without warning.
- The practical goal is to convert users who arrive through a platform "side door" into users who intentionally return through the startup's own "front door."
- Consumer founders should treat Facebook, Google SEO, Twitter, Instagram, iOS, Android, and similar platforms as distribution environments with different stability and control tradeoffs.

## Key Quotes
> "Founders should not be afraid of building on top of other platforms." - central recommendation.

> "thread the needle on their path to building an enduring company" - on balancing borrowed distribution with independence.
