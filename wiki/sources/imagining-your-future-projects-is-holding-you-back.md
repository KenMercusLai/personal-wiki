## Summary
[[JessicaAbel]] defines [[IdeaDebt]] as accumulating fantasies, plans, identity, and expectation around a creative project while doing little of the work itself. Drawing on [[KazuKibuishi]]'s “take the jump or skip it” analogy and [[IraGlass]]'s account of the gap between taste and early ability, she argues that creators should either begin imperfectly and improve through volume or consciously abandon stale projects so present work can receive their attention. The essay joins [[Perfectionism]], [[ProlificPractice]], and [[SunkCostFallacy]] into a practical model of creative avoidance and release.

## Key Claims
- [[IdeaDebt]] grows when imagined outcomes, status, lore, research, or planning substitute for making a concrete artifact.
- Idealizing a large future project raises the emotional stakes and makes the inevitable gap between imagination and current skill feel like failure.
- [[KazuKibuishi]] recommends acting before expectation compounds: take the jump now, or deliberately pass it and move on.
- [[IraGlass]] argues that creators close the gap between good taste and disappointing early output by finishing a large volume of work on deadlines.
- Old projects can become [[SunkCostFallacy]] anchors when commitment to an earlier self overrides whether the work still matters now.
- Explicitly abandoning stale ideas can release attention for the project a creator currently wants to make.

## Key Quotes
> "But there is no book. There is only Idea Debt." - Abel's contrast between extensive imagined lore and an absent artifact.

> "Just take the jump, or skip it." - Kibuishi's rule for avoiding compounding expectation.

## Connections
- [[JessicaAbel]] - author who synthesizes the idea-debt model and applies it to her own abandoned projects.
- [[KazuKibuishi]] - source of the term and the snowboarding analogy for acting or moving on.
- [[IraGlass]] - supplies the volume-of-work account of closing the gap between taste and skill.
- [[IdeaDebt]] - central concept joining fantasy, perfectionism, creative sunk costs, and attention.
- [[Perfectionism]] - idealized future work can make present ability feel too inadequate to begin.
- [[ProlificPractice]] - repeated finished work is the proposed route from current skill toward ambition.
- [[SunkCostFallacy]] - attachment to an earlier project's identity can distort present allocation of effort.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found. The essay strengthens existing anti-perfectionism and practice-through-making material while adding deliberate abandonment as a valid alternative to persistence.
- The account is a practitioner essay built from analogy, quotation, student observation, and autobiography rather than comparative evidence. It does not establish that all planning is avoidance, that output volume alone improves quality, or that abandoning a difficult project is always preferable to sustained commitment.
