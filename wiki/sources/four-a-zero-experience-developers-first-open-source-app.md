## Summary
[[SketchK]] describes leaving energy-sector work at age 27, entering iOS development without a computer-science background, and turning the 10,000-hour idea into [[FOUR]], a four-goal time-tracking app. In about three weeks he moved from a rough concept through competitor and typography research to App Store release and open-sourced the code, using a finished product as both a learning record and public evidence of capability. The account is a motivating single case, not proof that elapsed hours, resignation, or one shipped app reliably produces expertise or employment.

## Key Claims
- A shipped [[JobApplicationSideProjects|career-entry project]] can make an aspiring developer's ability more legible than background or intention alone.
- The author's conference experience made software community membership feel output-mediated: unfamiliar people could recognize one another through GitHub projects and App Store products.
- Product quality was treated as more than code; competitor review, interaction simplicity, visual composition, and typography shaped the app users actually experienced.
- [[FOUR]] was built in Objective-C to avoid anticipated Swift 3 migration churn and to create a later comparison point for a planned Swift rewrite.
- Open-sourcing the released app was intended to preserve a technical-growth record and invite collaboration with stronger practitioners.
- The app's four-goal limit came from a motivational calculation: four hours per day would require about ten years per 10,000-hour pursuit, leaving room for roughly four such goals by age 60.

## Key Quotes
> "用户不会直接看到代码写得多漂亮，他们只看到手机上展示的东西" - on treating interface design as part of product quality.

> "这不是一个靠关系、靠背景的地方，而是一个靠实力、靠能力来说话的地方。" - on the author's interpretation of recognition through public work.

## Contradictions
- The article treats 10,000 accumulated hours as a motivating planning device, while [[DeliberatePractice]] rejects any universal hour threshold and emphasizes task design, feedback, correction, recovery, and unequal opportunity.
- The author's claim that software is governed by ability rather than relationships or background reflects one encouraging encounter, but the same story depends on conference access, community recognition, GitHub and App Store distribution, and the ability to leave paid work.
- The three-week release demonstrates that a narrow product can be shipped quickly; it does not establish product-market fit, sustained use, maintainability, expertise, or a successful career transition.
