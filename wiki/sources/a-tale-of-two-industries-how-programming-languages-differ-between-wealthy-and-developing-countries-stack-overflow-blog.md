## Summary
[[DavidRobinson]] uses 2017 [[StackOverflow]] question-visit data to argue that global software development looks different when segmented into high-income countries and the rest of the world. The analysis finds Android and PHP traffic negatively correlated with GDP per capita, while Python, R, C, C++, and scientific data tools are more associated with high-income countries. The article treats these as non-causal signals shaped by education, research intensity, software-industry age, outsourcing, and English-language Stack Overflow usage.

## Key Claims
- [[StackOverflowTrafficAnalysis]] can reveal geographic differences in developer attention, but only for the subset of developers represented by English-language question visits.
- [[DeveloperEconomySegmentation]] is useful because high-income countries generated 63.7% of Stack Overflow traffic in the measured period, while India, Brazil, Russia, and China drove much of the non-high-income segment.
- [[ProgrammingTechnologyDemand]] differs sharply by country income: Android and PHP skew toward non-high-income countries, while Python, R, C, C++, and scientific packages skew toward high-income countries.
- [[DataScienceTechnologyAdoption]] appears more concentrated in high-income countries, where Python and R traffic is higher and scientific Python/R package traffic is disproportionately represented.
- The author explicitly avoids causal claims: language choice is not claimed to determine national income, and national wealth is not claimed to directly determine language choice.

## Key Quotes
> "we're not suggesting any causality here" - caveat after the GDP correlation plots.

> "separating them can sometimes give us more informative answers" - conclusion on segmenting developer data.
