## Summary
This article is a practitioner catalog of growth-hacking case studies across consumer apps, SaaS, marketplaces, media, e-commerce, travel, crowdfunding, and social platforms. Its central claim is that growth hacks are not one tactic but a family of distribution mechanisms: built-in sharing, referrals, free or freemium entry, content and SEO, stunts, product-led user experience, trust systems, partnerships, scarcity, and offline visibility. The examples are uneven as causal evidence, but together they form a pattern library for [[GrowthHacking]] and early [[SaaSMarketing]].

## Key Claims
- Growth hacks work best when distribution is built into the product behavior itself, as in [[Shazam]] users visibly holding phones to speakers, [[Dropbox]] file sharing, [[LinkedIn]] public profiles, [[TripAdvisor]] review badges, [[Spotify]] embeds, and [[Airbnb]] cross-posting.
- Incentive systems can accelerate adoption when the reward matches the product loop, as with [[PayPal]] cash referrals, [[Groupon]] sharing and friend referrals, [[WPEngine]] affiliate payouts, [[Firebox]] affiliate networks, and Tastecard bulk partnerships.
- Free trials, freemium models, and low-friction entry reduce adoption risk for users and can build large audiences before monetization, as shown by [[Shopify]], [[Spotify]], HideMyAss, and [[PokemonGo]].
- Content and community can become durable acquisition channels through guest blogging, inbound tools, editorial search traffic, quizzes, viral media, and useful industry reports, as shown by [[Buffer]], [[HubSpot]], [[Moz]], [[Buzzfeed]], InVision, ShopStyle, [[OneDollarShaveClub]], and Poo-Pourri.
- Growth can come from trust repair and market-enabling infrastructure, including [[Zappos]] returns, [[EBay]] ratings and escrow, [[Kickstarter]] community legitimacy, [[TripAdvisor]] reviews, and [[BookingCom]] paid-search scale tied to repeat direct bookings.
- Offline visibility and cultural participation can reinforce digital growth through [[Uber]] event rides, the Ice Bucket Challenge, Paddy Power stunts, Moonpig jingles, Compare the Market characters, and The Body Coach's media ubiquity.
- The article treats metrics such as downloads, users, traffic growth, referral spend, and ad budgets as evidence of traction, but it often infers causality from retrospective success rather than isolating tactic effects.
