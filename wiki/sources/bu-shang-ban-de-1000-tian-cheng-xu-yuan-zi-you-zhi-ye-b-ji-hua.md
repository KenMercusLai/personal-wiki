## Summary
[[ZhangXuan]] describes three years outside conventional employment as a programmer's "B plan": building [[EasyCV]], selling courses through [[Imooc]], and growing a personal audience across social platforms. The source frames [[ProgrammerFreelancing]] as viable only when financial pressure is low enough to survive slow early income, when a stable revenue base comes before experimental products, and when [[PersonalBranding]] becomes a distribution channel rather than vanity.

## Key Claims
- [[ProgrammerFreelancing]] needs financial runway because early income may be low and pressure can force bad decisions.
- [[EasyCV]] gave the author product, full-stack engineering, CI/CD, copywriting, and [[SaaSMarketing]] practice, but its early revenue was not enough to fund three years of living.
- Selling technical courses on [[Imooc]] provided the main stable income because courses can compound when updated and distributed through a large platform.
- [[PersonalBranding]] is strategically valuable because audiences on Twitter, Bilibili, YouTube, Zhihu, and WeChat can become traffic sources for products.
- Solo work requires deliberate [[PersonalProductivity]] habits: regular schedule, dedicated workspace, milestones, exercise, social connection, stable revenue first, and a time-boxed exit option.

## Key Quotes
> "就是钱" - on the prerequisite for trying freelancing.

> "先以赚钱为目的" - on prioritizing stable income before dream products.
