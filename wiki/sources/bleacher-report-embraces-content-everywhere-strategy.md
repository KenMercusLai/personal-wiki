## Summary
Digiday profiles [[BleacherReport]] and [[RoryBrown]] as examples of publishers moving from destination-site traffic toward [[DistributedPublishingStrategy]]. The article argues that Facebook, Google, Snapchat, social channels, search, and apps require platform-native editorial work, channel hedging, advertiser integration, and interaction metrics rather than only traditional audience totals. ## Key Claims
- Publishers that do not embrace content across platforms risk falling behind as audiences spend more time inside Facebook, Google, Snapchat, and similar channels.
- [[BleacherReport]] treats channel mix as a hedge: it wants owned audience relationships, but still expects audiences to arrive through search, social, apps, and platform-hosted content.
- Platform-native editorial requires different missions by channel; the article contrasts Instagram's visual "wow" mindset with Twitter's breaking-news and snark posture.
- Bleacher Report's mobile app strategy converted search traffic into email newsletters and then into Team Stream app adoption, which the article reports at 15 million downloads.
- Facebook is treated less as a referral source and more as a direct video and Instant Articles distribution surface.
- Social interactions such as views, likes, and shares become a more relevant success measure than site uniques when content lives across many surfaces.

## Key Quotes
> "We don't go all-in on one audience channel" - Brown on Bleacher Report's hedging strategy.

> "Social interactions are the new uniques." - article framing of the metric shift.
