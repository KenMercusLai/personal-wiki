## Summary
This article argues that joining an early-stage startup should be treated as a diligence decision, not a lottery-ticket career bet. Candidates should question founders about financing, runway, investors, founder qualifications, board structure, early team quality, achieved business milestones, and whether the startup's idea is personally credible enough to commit years of work.

## Key Claims
- Startup employment upside is partly assessable: candidates can improve their odds by evaluating company fundamentals instead of treating equity as a random bonus.
- Funding amount must be interpreted together with burn rate and remaining runway; two startups with the same cash balance can have radically different survival windows.
- Investor quality, founder quality, board composition, and early employee quality are signals about governance, judgment, and future operating capacity.
- Business milestones such as recurring revenue, paid customers, and well-defined active users are more meaningful than press mentions or raw website traffic.
- Early employees should believe in the startup's idea; if founders cannot sell the vision to a prospective core team member, that is itself evidence about the company's ability to sell customers.
- The embedded TechCrunch chart shows a visible jump in Series A fundraising likelihood once seed funding crosses roughly $1.5M, supporting the article's claim that seed amount is a signal but not sufficient by itself.
