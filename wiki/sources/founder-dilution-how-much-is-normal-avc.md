## Summary
[[FredWilson]] gives a 2009 practitioner rule of thumb for cumulative [[CapTableDilution]] across a venture-backed company's path to sustainability: three to four equity rounds plus management equity can leave the founder team with roughly 10-20% ownership. He contrasts that path with earlier exits, where founders may retain more ownership but sell a less-developed company for a lower total value, and emphasizes that capital needs, financing valuations, and timing can produce materially better or worse outcomes.

## Key Claims
- Founders begin with all company equity, then lose percentage ownership when the company raises capital or issues shares and options to managers and employees.
- A company built through sustainability or profitability may require three to four equity rounds and roughly 20-25% of its equity to recruit and retain a management team.
- Wilson's representative endpoint assigns roughly 20% to the founder team, 20-25% to management, and 55-60% to investors, while his broader founder range is roughly 10-20%.
- The outcome is not a benchmark guarantee: Wilson reports founder stakes below 5% as well as the Google founders retaining more than 25% after IPO dilution.
- Capital requirements, financing valuations, and the timing of rounds determine how much dilution accumulates.
- Earlier exits can preserve more founder ownership because fewer financing and hiring events occur, but usually pair that larger percentage with a lower total company value.
- The article calls for survey data because its numerical ranges come from venture-practice experience rather than a systematic dataset.

## Key Quotes
> "It all depends on the amount of capital a company needs to raise and the valuations it can raise it at and the timing of those financings." - Wilson qualifies the rule of thumb with the variables that drive actual dilution.

> "Those exits are generally for less money so its more ownership times less total value." - the source's compact statement of the early-exit ownership tradeoff.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction identified. The article extends [[CapTableDilution]] from one simplified financing case to cumulative founder outcomes across multiple rounds, hiring grants, and possible exit timing.
- The numerical ranges are Wilson's 2009 experience-based estimates, not current market benchmarks or population statistics; the proposed survey underscores the evidence gap.
- The ownership examples do not provide cap tables, transaction values, preference terms, or outcome comparisons, so they illustrate possible ranges without establishing representative founder returns.
