## Summary
[[BenedictEvans]] argues that the smartphone had become the central computing and internet ecosystem by late 2015, with [[Apple]] and [[Google]] both winning different sides of the mobile platform transition. The article treats "mobile" less as screen size or walking-around use than as an ecosystem shift toward ARM, [[IOS]], [[Android]], cloud software, app distribution, discovery control, messaging, sensors, cars, watches, and living-room devices. Its core strategic question is how discovery, runtime, and user behavior change when the internet is no longer centered on the desktop browser.

## Key Claims
- [[MobileEcosystem]] had become the new center of technology investment because smartphone scale was moving toward an order of magnitude larger than the PC industry.
- [[MobileInternet]] should replace the old "mobile versus desktop internet" frame because smartphones are often the primary and more capable internet platform.
- [[Apple]] and [[Google]] both won mobile in different ways: Android had more users, while Apple's ecosystem concentrated unusually large economic activity and high-value users.
- [[MobilePlatformDiscovery]] became the strategic bottleneck because search solves known intent but not awareness, curation, traffic allocation, or demand generation.
- [[MobileRuntime]] remained unsettled after the browser-native-app split, with Siri, Google Now, messaging, maps, and notifications competing to become new service surfaces.
- [[MessagingAsPlatform]] could become an alternative development, discovery, and user-acquisition layer, especially where [[WeChat]] had already shown a mobile-first platform model.
- [[InternetOfThingsData]] would matter less as one standards category than as cheap sensors and mobile supply chains creating new data surfaces for redesigning systems.

## Key Quotes
> "Mobile is the new central ecosystem of tech" - thesis heading.

> "Do people want to put your icon on their home screen?" - the app-versus-web product test.
