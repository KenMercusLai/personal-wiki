## Summary
Kristina Dell presents [[MarcoArment]] and [[Instapaper]] as a case of a deliberately small, profitable internet business that grew from a five-hour personal prototype into a full-time one-person operation. The supplied extract links [[SideProjectIncubation]] to an immediate personal problem, offline commuter use, App Store timing, and mixed revenue, while linking [[SoloFounding]] and [[BootstrappedCompanyBuilding]] to low overhead and freedom from investor growth expectations. Its user and revenue details are a journalistic snapshot from 2011 rather than evidence of the product's current scale or a representative solo-business outcome.

## Key Claims
- [[MarcoArment]] built the first simple version of [[Instapaper]] in about five hours after repeatedly losing articles he wanted to read later.
- After friends responded positively and Apple announced the iPhone App Store, Arment spent evenings adding offline mobile reading for his subway commute.
- Instapaper reportedly reached one million users and enough income to support Arment while he still worked at [[Tumblr]], allowing him to leave that job in September 2010.
- The 2011 revenue model combined a $4.99 app, website advertising, and a purely optional $1 monthly website subscription.
- The article describes Instapaper as a profitable one-person operation with 1.8 million users, low overhead, no employees, and no investors.
- Arment presents outside investment as a poor fit because investor expectations for growth and return would create a job he did not want.
- Paul Kedrosky argues that cheaper company and marketing tools let small standalone products compete without necessarily expanding into conventional venture-backed firms.

## Key Quotes
> "It didn't need to do that much and there was so much value right there." - Arment on the first narrow version.

> "It would lead to the kind of job that I don't want right now." - Arment on investor-driven growth expectations.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction with the later Instapaper retrospective: the fall 2007 prototype can precede its January 2008 public announcement. The source's one-million and 1.8-million user figures describe different points in the same early growth period.
- Calling the business recession-proof is stronger than the evidence supplied. The article provides one successful case and no cost detail, failure comparison, continuity analysis, or proof that low overhead eliminates market and founder risk.
