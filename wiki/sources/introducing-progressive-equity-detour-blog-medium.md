## Summary
[[AndrewMason]] introduces [[ProgressiveEquity]], an employee-equity design intended to spread more of an exceptional startup exit beyond founders and early employees. [[Detour]] planned to split employee grants between ordinary and capped progressive RSUs, then route value released above a chosen financial-independence threshold into a kicker pool distributed pro rata to eligible employees at a major liquidity event. The post provides illustrative mechanics and reusable legal paperwork, but no operating results, adoption evidence, actual threshold, or analysis of tax, accounting, securities, and distributional effects.

## Key Claims
- Conventional startup equity can produce extreme outcome concentration: founders may receive dynastic wealth, a few early employees may become financially independent, and most employees may receive only a modest bonus.
- Progressive Equity applies a 50% reduction to participating employee value above a company-defined financial-independence threshold and redistributes the released amount to employees pro rata.
- Detour's proposed implementation divides the employee equity pool equally between normal RSUs and Progressive RSUs; the Progressive RSUs stop appreciating at half the threshold while the normal half continues appreciating.
- A third kicker pool begins empty and accumulates redistributed value as participating employees cross the threshold.
- Redistribution occurs once, at a large secondary sale, acquisition, or IPO, after which the company returns to a conventional grant process.
- Investors do not participate, employees cannot opt out, and employees who leave before the event do not share in the kicker pool.
- Mason published the legal template for others to use and improve while acknowledging that the design does not solve every equity-distribution problem.

## Key Quotes
> "maximize the number of employees that achieve financial independence, and minimize the number of newly-minted dynasties" - the program's stated distributional objective.

> "The employee equity pool is divided into two equal pieces" - the implementation begins with normal and progressive RSU pools.

## Contradictions
- The illustrative 1,000-megadonk exit, 50-megadonk threshold, 50% founder ownership, 50% rate, and 225 employees demonstrate arithmetic rather than document Detour's actual terms or a realized outcome.
- The statement that employee percentage ownership rises with company value is an incentive hypothesis, not evidence of motivation, retention, fairness, or performance.
- Pro-rata distribution is not necessarily equal distribution: the post does not specify the kicker denominator, grant weights, tenure treatment, or resulting employee-level payout spread.
- Excluding investors, removing employee opt-out, and denying the kicker to leavers preserve important power and risk asymmetries; the post does not analyze involuntary termination near the trigger.
- The post provides no independent legal, tax, accounting, securities, governance, dilution, or jurisdictional assessment. Its comment that only “awful people” would object is rhetoric, not evidence about employee preferences or legitimate financial concerns.
