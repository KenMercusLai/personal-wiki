## Summary
Mission.org surveys Apple advertising from the 1977 Apple II "Simplicity" ad through 2017-2018 Siri celebrity spots, using each campaign as a marketing lesson. The article frames [[Apple]]'s advertising strength as a long-running ability to make unfamiliar technology legible through simplicity, cultural association, product demonstration, contrast against alternatives, social proof, demand signals, and emotionally charged ideal states. Its embedded images are mostly illustrative; the early black-and-white [[SteveJobs]] and [[SteveWozniak]] workshop photo reinforces the humble-origin narrative, while repeated Mission Studios promo graphics and tiny ad thumbnails add little evidence beyond the prose.

## Key Claims
- New or unfamiliar products need simple explanations, concrete use cases, and memorable contexts before buyers can understand why they matter.
- [[SocialProof]] appears repeatedly in Apple ads, from Apple II buyer claims to celebrity, influencer, product-placement, and demand-signaling campaigns.
- [[BrandDistinctiveness]] is built through recognizable slogans, cultural references, historical figures, silhouettes, music, and the choice to associate Apple with creativity and courage.
- Apple repeatedly contrasts its products with a dominant alternative, a customer misconception, or a feared absence, such as IBM, the PC, Y2K anxiety, or a world without apps.
- Strong Apple ads often demonstrate one remarkable attribute or before-and-after transformation instead of explaining many features at once.
- Advertising can overreach when it insults the audience or exaggerates what a product can do.
