## Summary
[[BrieCode]] argues that many supposed non-gamers reject the narrow tastes, assumptions, controls, and conflict-heavy conventions of existing games rather than interactivity itself. Drawing on her cousin's partial attachment to *Skyrim*, she proposes designing with underserved audiences around their lived concerns, cultural references, desired depth, and need for care, using conversation, play-testing, and co-design instead of treating exclusion as a customer defect.

## Key Claims
- A homogeneous workforce of committed gamers can mistake its own tastes and design theory for the full possibility space of interactive entertainment.
- People who say they dislike games may be rejecting hostile representation, unfamiliar cultural references, inaccessible controls, shallow themes, or mandatory violence rather than the medium itself.
- Emotional attachment to a character and experimentation with identity can reveal value even inside a game whose combat and fantasy conventions otherwise repel a player.
- Removing disliked features is insufficient; new games should begin with problems, aspirations, relationships, and questions that intended audiences already care about.
- Audience conversation, play-testing with non-gamers, customer development, and co-design can reduce the risk of creating for an unfamiliar market.
- Games centered on care, compassion, creation, and social connection could help people understand and adapt to difficult lives rather than add more shock and stimulation.
- Reader responses qualify the thesis by pointing to discoverability, control complexity, production economics, existing nonviolent genres, the continuing value of linear media, and the possibility that some people simply prefer other forms.

## Key Quotes
> "It wasn't that she didn't like video games, it was that she didn't know what they were." — Code on her cousin's discovery of emotional attachment in *Skyrim*

> "It wasn't about answering them; it was about asking them." — Code on shifting from recommendations to audience inquiry

## Contradictions
- Reader responses dispute the implication that interactivity is more relevant than linear books or films, arguing that fixed form and helpless observation can themselves create emotional depth.
- Some responses challenge the diversity diagnosis, pointing to existing women players and nonviolent genres and reframing the problem as incumbent publishers serving known markets.
- The source presents co-design for new audiences as risk reduction, while readers note that unfamiliar game categories remain commercially risky because production, discovery, distribution, and proven revenue paths constrain experimentation.
