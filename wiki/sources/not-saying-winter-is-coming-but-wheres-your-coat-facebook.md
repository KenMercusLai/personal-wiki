## Summary
[[KentBeck]] uses his post-dot-com loss of consulting demand, depression, and panic-driven decisions to argue for [[DownturnPreparedness]] before a technology slump combines with an external shock. He recommends modest, reversible defenses—cash reserves, lower fixed expenses, sustainable work, explicit downside scenarios, and small learning investments—while stopping short of predicting that a crash will occur.

## Key Claims
- Financial damage can come less from a downturn itself than from pressured decisions made after reserves and options have disappeared.
- Industry weakness and an external shock can reinforce each other even when neither would independently cause collapse.
- As downside risk rises, a current job and liquid savings become more valuable relative to assumed job mobility, future earnings, equity compensation, investments, and housing appreciation.
- A six-month cash reserve and lower recurring commitments can create time to respond, when feasible.
- Sustainable performance and broad, low-cost learning can strengthen employment options without requiring an immediate drastic career change.
- Downside questions about unemployment, a 90% equity-compensation loss, or a 40% home-price decline are preparation exercises, not forecasts.

## Key Quotes
> "It's not the bad times that wipe you out, it's the bad decisions during bad times" — on preserving decision quality under pressure.

> "Knowing where to find your coat suddenly becomes valuable when the snow starts falling." — on preparing before circumstances force action.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found. The essay qualifies its own severe scenarios as possibilities rather than forecasts and recommends awareness and incremental preparation rather than immediate retreat.
- The six-month reserve is a heuristic whose feasibility and adequacy depend on income, household obligations, benefits, debt, and the duration or breadth of a downturn.
