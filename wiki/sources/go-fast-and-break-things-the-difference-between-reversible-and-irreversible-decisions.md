## Summary
[[FarnamStreet]] presents [[JeffBezos]]'s one-way-door/two-way-door heuristic as a way to match decision process to reversibility. Reversible choices can be made by small groups with incomplete information, then corrected using the evidence action produces; consequential and irreversible choices deserve slower analysis and consultation. The article connects that distinction to organizational learning, [[AnalysisParalysis]], and [[SunkCostFallacy]], while acknowledging that speed is not permission for recklessness.

![Circular two-way arrows beside a one-way arrow contrasting reversible and irreversible decisions](../../wiki-assets/go-fast-and-break-things-the-difference-between-reversible-and-irreversible-decisions/reversible-and-irreversible-decision-paths.png)

## Key Claims
- [[ReversibleDecisionMaking]] matches deliberation to consequence: move quickly on genuinely reversible choices and slow down for irreversible or nearly irreversible ones.
- Waiting for near-perfect information can cost more than it improves a reversible decision; the article attributes to Bezos a practical threshold of acting around 70% certainty and correcting course.
- Acting on a reversible choice produces information, so decision speed can increase learning velocity when feedback and rollback are real.
- Large organizations tend to apply heavyweight one-way-door process to two-way-door choices, reducing experimentation and invention.
- Small failures can be treated as information, making it easier to pivot instead of continuing only because of sunk costs.
- More information is not automatically better: the article's second-hand medical example argues that irrelevant variables can obscure a useful short-term signal.

## Key Quotes
> "If a decision is reversible, we can make it fast and without perfect information." - the article's central process rule.

> "Reversible decisions are not an excuse to act reckless or be ill-informed" - boundary on the speed heuristic.

> "Most decisions aren't like that - they are changeable, reversible - they're two-way doors." - Bezos, reproduced from a shareholder letter.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction found. The source strengthens the wiki's reversible-choice material but leaves a critical classification problem unresolved: decisions that look reversible may still impose lasting safety, trust, financial, data, or third-party costs.
- The medical example is a popular account quoted through Malcolm Gladwell rather than clinical guidance or a review of the underlying evidence; it should not justify withholding medically relevant information or testing.
