## Summary
[[TimUrban]] presents a first-principles [[CareerPlanning]] framework for replacing inherited career scripts with a more honest audit of wants, fears, constraints, and options. The article argues that careers are too identity-shaping, time-consuming, and impact-bearing to choose by conventional wisdom, so people should examine their inner motives, test beliefs about the career landscape, choose a next "dot" as an experiment, and keep revising as they and the world change. The inspected images are central framework diagrams rather than decoration: they show the childhood river/college pond path, the Want Box and Reality Box overlap, the [[WantRealityCareerFramework]] option pool, the Yearning Octopus, the yearning hierarchy, pace-and-persistence distance models, the chef strategy loop, opportunity cost, inertia spectrum, and feedback loops for a changing self and world.

## Key Claims
- [[CareerPlanning]] should be done from first principles because inherited social scripts are often outdated and poorly matched to the individual.
- The [[WantRealityCareerFramework]] says viable career options sit where authentic wants overlap with a realistic assessment of the career game, starting point, success threshold, pace, and persistence.
- Career wants are internally conflicted: personal, social, lifestyle, moral, and practical yearnings can each push toward different jobs and fears.
- A person should audit whether career motives are authentic or implanted by parents, peers, culture, heroes, past selves, or conventional wisdom.
- Reality assessment should treat careers as evolving games rather than fixed ladders; success depends on improving at the whole game, not only on talent or credentials.
- Career choices are better understood as experimental dots than as irreversible tunnels, because future selves and future labor markets will know things the present self cannot.
- After choosing a next dot, people need to manage inertia, [[OpportunityCost]], restless impulses, and the difference between mission-enhancing and mission-changing jumps.
