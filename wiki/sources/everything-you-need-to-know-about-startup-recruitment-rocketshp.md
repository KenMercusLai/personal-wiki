## Summary
ROCKETSHP presents startup recruitment as an end-to-end system: confirm that a permanent hire is necessary, define the role from near-term goals and daily outcomes, choose sourcing channels, screen and interview against relevant work, make a deliberate selection, and retain the employee through culture and growth. Its strongest contribution is the connection among role clarity, candidate attraction, evidence gathering, pipeline management, and retention; several specific tactics, statistics, tools, and equity claims are dated or insufficiently qualified. The source references 23 remote images, including equity charts, an inbound-recruiting process, screening and outreach screenshots, and a job-change-factor chart, but the origin returned inaccessible responses and no local copies were present, so none could be inspected or retained.

## Key Claims
- A startup should first test whether it needs a permanent employee at all; a contractor can be a lower-commitment fit for bounded short-term work.
- Role selection should follow the startup's highest-impact three-to-six-month goal, and the job should be described through responsibilities, daily work, and outcomes rather than a vague title.
- Early-stage companies often benefit from adaptable generalists, while experience, speed, training burden, salary, and equity create role-specific trade-offs rather than one universally superior candidate profile.
- Candidate acquisition should combine employer culture and benefits, inbound content, direct outreach, recruiters, and targeted job boards, with an employee persona guiding message and channel choice.
- Screening forms, job-relevant questions, skills tests, interviews, work samples, references, and short auditions can reduce weak matches when each method is relevant, proportionate, and consistently evaluated.
- Applicant tracking, source measurement, and a reusable talent pool can make recruiting more repeatable, while onboarding carries the system into employment.
- Retention depends on credible culture, transparency, visible values, internal growth, shared activities, and regular one-to-one attention—not compensation alone.

## Key Quotes
> "If all the candidates left in the selection stage don't feel right for your company - don't hire." - on refusing a forced choice.

> "Tracking candidates is particularly helpful." - on learning which recruiting channels produce useful candidates.

## Connections
- [[HiringSystemDesign]] - extends hiring-system design upstream into need and role definition, outward into multi-channel sourcing, and downstream into onboarding and retention.
- [[StartupHiringAtScale]] - distinguishes early-stage generalist hiring from the more specialized needs of a later organization.
- [[InclusiveHiring]] - the source warns about selection bias but also recommends subjective cultural-fit and personality screens that need stronger job-relevance controls.
- [[StartupCulture]] - treats culture as both an employer-value proposition and a retention mechanism.
- [[EmployeeEquityRisk]] - presents equity as a recruiting incentive while only partially addressing vesting, dilution, legal complexity, and employee downside.
- [[LinkedIn]] - primary directory and outreach channel in the source's self-recruiting model.

## Contradictions
- The source's “get a drink” test, unease-based rejection, personality quizzes, and broad cultural-fit screening conflict with [[HiringSystemDesign]] and [[InclusiveHiring]], which require observable, job-relevant evidence and warn that affinity and unsupported impressions can reproduce bias.
- Its statement that dilution makes existing shares “worthless” is incorrect: issuing additional shares reduces an existing holder's ownership percentage and may reduce per-share value if enterprise value does not rise, but it does not inherently reduce the shares to zero. This overstatement conflicts with the more qualified treatment in [[EmployeeEquityRisk]].
- Multi-day or week-long “auditions” may provide useful work evidence, but the source does not address candidate burden, compensation, confidentiality, accessibility, or employment-law constraints.
- The generalist-over-specialist rule is stage-sensitive rather than universal; an early startup can still require narrow expertise when safety, regulation, or a specific bottleneck dominates.
- The article is a 2017 practitioner handbook. Its platform feature lists, job boards, tool recommendations, and externally cited candidate statistics should not be treated as current without fresh verification.
