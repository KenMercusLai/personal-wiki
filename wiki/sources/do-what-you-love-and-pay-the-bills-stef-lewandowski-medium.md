## Summary
[[StefLewandowski]] describes how he and [[EmilyQuinton]] turned her photography teaching and audience into [[Makelight]], a family-centered, bootstrapped education, community, and software business. The case links a no-code course test, preorders, consulting income, customer listening, membership revenue, and lightweight automation to reported growth of $300,000 in turnover, while warning that monetizing a passion can turn it into an obligation. It supports bootstrapping as a way to preserve strategic optionality, but ends with unresolved financing choices because cash-flow constraints were limiting growth.

## Key Claims
- "Do what you love" is not universal career advice: ordinary paid work can legitimately fund life outside work, and monetizing a valued pursuit can damage the enjoyment that sustained it.
- [[Makelight]] began by combining [[EmilyQuinton]]'s photography teaching and social audience with [[StefLewandowski]]'s product, design, coding, and company-building experience.
- The first offer was tested without custom code through Squarespace, Mailchimp, Stripe, Instagram, and Facebook, reducing the cost of learning whether customers would pay.
- Self-funding preserved future choice but concentrated risk: Lewandowski used consulting income while building at night, and the founders used course preorders to finance production and household cash flow.
- The founders report that going all-in was followed by a first $30,000 month, roughly 2,000 paying customers, several hundred members, and a Net Promoter Score near 80; these are first-party historical figures without independent financial detail.
- Continuous customer conversation supported a repeating loop of identifying community problems, teaching solutions through courses, and building useful tools around color analytics and hashtag research.
- Bootstrapping did not eliminate the need for capital; after initial traction, the founders were considering angel investment, crowdfunding, or debt because internal cash flow constrained expansion.

## Contradictions
- The source qualifies celebratory side-project narratives: turning a loved activity into income can replace intrinsic enjoyment with cash-flow, support, and fulfillment obligations.
- The reported revenue, audience, customer, membership, and NPS figures are a founder's 2016 snapshot, not audited results or evidence that the approach typically works; the source omits margins, ad spend, labor accounting, retention, debt, and longer-term outcomes.
- Five of six local images were opened and omitted as decorative or duplicate photography. The retained Makelight Insights screenshot is very low resolution, so it supports only the visible product concept—source images paired with a color palette—not detailed interface or performance claims.
