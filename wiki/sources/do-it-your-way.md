## Summary
This [[CollaborativeFund]] essay argues that success should be judged by whether a person gets what they want from life, not by whether they reproduce another person's methods or outcomes. Through examples from writing, investing, and spending, it introduces [[PersonStrategyFit]]: learn from others while choosing strategies compatible with one's own personality, skills, goals, and tolerances.

## Key Claims
- A talented independent writer can become mediocre when a successful institution replaces the writer's voice and intuition with a standardized editorial system.
- A universally "best" method is unlikely when people differ in personality, background, goals, and skills.
- Reasonable financial plans can still cause harm when they do not fit the person using them.
- Investment and spending differences need not imply that either person is wrong; treating another person's choices as a verdict on one's own can create needless imitation or defensiveness.
- Learning from others is compatible with choosing a strategy aligned with one's own personality and skills.
- Personal benchmarks can encourage progress toward one's own potential while reducing pursuit of someone else's goals.

## Key Quotes
> "The ultimate success metric is whether you get what you want out of life." - the essay's criterion for evaluating a personal strategy.

> "What was right for the company was wrong for the writer." - the distinction between institutional success and person-strategy fit.
