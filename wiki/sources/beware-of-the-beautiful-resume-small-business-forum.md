## Summary
A founder warns that impressive resumes from admired companies can mislead startups into hiring people who value status, security, and organizational territory more than company-level momentum. The article distinguishes "startup people" from "big-company people" by motivation and behavior rather than employer name, urging founders to test whether candidates created success or merely rode an already-successful organization. The inspected local images are repeated meeting-room cartoons captioned that no one accomplished anything, reinforcing the essay's warning that process and meetings can replace decisive execution.

## Key Claims
- Founders can overpay for prestige by assuming name-brand company experience will automatically translate into startup impact.
- Startup fit depends more on motivation and behavior than on whether someone currently works at a large company or a startup.
- Big-company behavior is characterized here by status sensitivity, security seeking, visible-error avoidance, territoriality, and reliance on budget, title, and headcount.
- Startup behavior is characterized here by company-first winning, ownership, impact seeking, comfort with excellent peers, and lack of territoriality over who gets important work done.
- Candidate evaluation should ask whether the person created success in a prior role or benefited from a company whose momentum already made success easier.
- Wrong senior or prestige hires can create meetings, delay, politics, drama, opportunity cost, and cultural drag that outweigh the extra work of rigorous hiring.

## Key Quotes
> "Startup people are missionaries, not mercenaries" - on mission-driven fit.

> "Did they create success where they came from or did they ride the rocket ship?" - on separating contribution from pedigree.
