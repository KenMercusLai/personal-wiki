## Summary
[[MindTheProduct]] presents outsourced development as risky but potentially useful when it is treated as a bounded product-validation strategy rather than a cheap substitute for an invested internal team. The article argues that product managers must manage references, fit tests, scope, communication workflow, quality assurance, technical specification, and project tracking more explicitly when working with external developers. It connects [[OutsourcedProductDevelopment]] to [[MinimumViableProduct]], [[StartupHypothesisTesting]], [[CustomerLedProductDevelopment]], and [[ProductEvolution]] by framing throwaway or refactored prototype code as an acceptable cost when it cheaply validates market demand.

## Key Claims
- Outsourced development can be cheaper than local in-house development, but its value depends on knowing whether the work is a market-validation prototype, a maintainable long-term system, or code that will later be refactored or discarded.
- Product managers should hire external developers through trusted references and ask for balanced accounts of both strengths and weaknesses.
- A small warm-up project can test culture, skill, communication, and domain-understanding fit before committing a larger product build.
- Outsourced teams often deliver less polish than expected, so managers should focus effort on the most critical components and functionality.
- External teams should usually work through their preferred communication and tracking system rather than being forced into the product manager's internal tool stack.
- Product managers need to perform their own QA and provide granular technical documentation because outsourced developers have less business-context knowledge.
- A simple tracked workflow of Issues, Committed, Rejected, and Done helps maintain a clear picture of product state and viable next steps across distance and time zones.

## Key Quotes
> "you might just throw away the prototype code once it's done it's validation job" - on treating outsourced prototype code as a validation artifact rather than permanent architecture.

> "The only way to assure quality is to perform your own testing." - on the product manager's QA responsibility.
