## Summary
An anonymous Reddit founder uses three alleged disputes over design royalties, unpaid sweat equity, and a disclosed product invention to argue that business relationships need contemporaneous written records from their first day. The practical advice spans royalty terms, time and expense ledgers, counterparty identity, acknowledged discounts, scoped confidentiality, disclosure records, and limits on what a prototype reveals. The file's `updated` value supplies the date above but does not establish the post's publication date; the export also contains unrelated Reddit posts after the titled discussion, which are excluded from the claims below.

## Key Claims
- Written, mutually acknowledged terms provide a fallback when trust, incentives, personnel, or legal entities change; even a confirmed email can be more useful than an undocumented handshake.
- A royalty license should define the calculation base, reporting or audit cadence, retained ownership, anti-circumvention terms, performance obligations, and the conditions under which exclusivity ends.
- Work performed for deferred payment, discounts, or equity should be tied to the exact counterparty and tracked at full economic value through hours, expenses, invoices, and explicit discount reasons.
- Confidential inventions need a disclosure strategy as well as formal rights: a focused NDA, signed access records, deadline awareness, and demonstrations that prove function without unnecessarily revealing implementation.
- Documentation reduces ambiguity but does not create bargaining power by itself; enforcement cost, counterparty resources, contract quality, and the founder's ability to act still matter.

## Key Quotes
> "Document EVERYTHING in writing." - the author's central lesson from the three disputes.

> "Even a clear, concise email between everyone involved with some confirmation would have been at least SOMETHING on which to lean." - the author's lower-cost fallback when professional drafting is unaffordable.
