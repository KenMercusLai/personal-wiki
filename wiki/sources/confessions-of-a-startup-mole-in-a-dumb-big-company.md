## Summary
After moving from a decade of startups into [[Square]], [[SaumilMehta]] revises his caricature of large companies without denying their structural disadvantages. He reports unusually direct executive access, similarities between venture and public-market storytelling, far richer employee infrastructure, slower execution caused by strategy and dependency constraints, and waste emerging from specialization and resource abundance rather than presumed laziness. The essay's useful contrast is therefore not smart startups versus stupid incumbents but speed and generalism versus scale, coordination, specialist support, and a larger installed business.

## Key Claims
- A well-run large company can preserve direct executive access and candor: Mehta describes senior Square leaders reading individual-contributor work, probing details, accepting unvarnished feedback, and remaining accessible across levels.
- Venture investors and public-market investors can misunderstand young companies in similar ways; both require a coherent story, social proof or conviction, and repeated traction that makes an initially unfamiliar business legible.
- Large-company perks and specialist support can materially improve work while also turning luxuries into expectations and making unnecessary work easier to request.
- A startup should not assume a large competitor can copy and execute at startup speed unless the incumbent aligns company strategy, customer fit, scarce engineering capacity, hiring, product dependencies, and culture behind the attack.
- Large organizations are slower even with more people because engineers have fewer degrees of freedom and product managers coordinate across a much larger peer and dependency set.
- Organizational waste can emerge from a rational shift toward specialization, infrequent hierarchical budgeting, and resource abundance; small amounts of optional work compound across thousands of people.
- The contrast remains conditional: the author misses startup velocity and cohesion while accepting that large-company work need not represent lower ambition or an identity failure.

## Key Quotes
> "big companies have their own constraints" - the essay's answer to founders who treat an incumbent's resources as equivalent to execution capacity.

> "we just can't go as fast as a 10 person startup even if we have well over 10 people on the case" - Mehta's distinction between headcount and organizational speed.

> "larger organizations trend towards specialization while scrappy startups trend towards generalization" - the proposed mechanism behind both expert support and some avoidable work.

## Contradictions
- The source challenges a blanket assumption that large-company executives are insulated from individual contributors or candor, but its evidence comes from one employee's observation of one well-run company and may not generalize to other firms, periods, teams, or leaders.
- It supports the wiki's incumbent-fragility material by identifying concrete coordination constraints, while qualifying any stronger claim that incumbents are harmless: a large company that aligns the whole organization behind a market can still pose a serious threat.
- The comparison is retrospective and selected. It provides no measured delivery-speed, staffing, waste, investor-perception, employee-satisfaction, or business-outcome data, and it explicitly omits politics, tools, and much of culture.
