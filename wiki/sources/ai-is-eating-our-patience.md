## Summary
[[Innei]] argues that AI-assisted programming can multiply output while eroding the patience, exploration, flow, and hands-on practice through which developers build understanding and professional identity. The first-person essay links [[AIDependencySkillAtrophy]] to fragmented multi-agent supervision, weaker recall of design decisions, dependence on frontier-model access, and a harder talent market for newcomers. Its claims are experiential rather than measured, and its preserved 24-vote poll is too small and self-selected to establish prevalence.

## Key Claims
- AI can shorten the path from requirement to product while removing the intermediate research, design deliberation, implementation, and testing through which a developer learns and develops ownership.
- Shipping more projects does not guarantee deeper competence: the author reports being unable to explain many design decisions in AI-built projects and feeling substantially disconnected from programming after nearly a year of vibe coding.
- Agent supervision replaces sustained flow with repeated prompting, waiting, and switching across terminal sessions, sometimes causing instructions to be sent to the wrong conversation.
- When broadly available AI compresses implementation time, coding speed becomes less differentiating and access to frontier models, prompting, business understanding, and context-switching capacity become more consequential.
- Dependence on paid or geographically fragile model access can turn tool availability into a career and competitive risk, particularly for students and new graduates.
- AI complicates talent assessment because polished projects and competent architecture can be generated without demonstrating the builder's underlying understanding or independent ability.
- The author does not argue that efficiency gains are imaginary; the tension is that faster output may weaken patience, craft identity, and the ability to work without AI.

## Key Quotes
> "You do it for me, I just want the result." - on replacing learning with delegation.

> "the process itself is the meaning — not just the result." - on what efficiency can remove.

## Connections

- [[AIDependencySkillAtrophy]] - central concern that substituted practice weakens skill, patience, understanding, and confidence without AI.
- [[AICodingPractice]] - the essay challenges practices that optimize output while leaving developers unable to explain design decisions.
- [[AttentionManagement]] - many concurrent prompt-wait cycles fragment attention and impede sustained flow.

## Contradictions
- The essay strengthens the cautionary side of [[AIDependencySkillAtrophy]] but tensions [[blog-antirez-dont-fall-into-the-anti-ai-hype]], which argues that manual code writing can become optional while human building and direction remain meaningful.
- The account is a translated first-person reflection with no timed comparison, skill test, project audit, defect data, hiring study, or independent evidence that patience loss is irreversible.
- The closed poll reports 24 votes split 25%, 25%, and 50% among three self-descriptions; its tiny, self-selected audience cannot support population claims.
- Claims about universal access, model quality, competitive convergence, entry-level barriers, and the future obsolescence of human work are forecasts or impressions rather than established results.
