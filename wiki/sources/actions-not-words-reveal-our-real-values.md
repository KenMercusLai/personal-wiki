## Summary
[[DerekSivers]] argues that repeated conduct is stronger evidence of a person's operative priorities than what they say they want. Through a coach's challenge to his unlaunched company idea, he concludes that inaction can expose a stronger preference for one's current life and presents [[RevealedPriorities]] as a choice between honestly accepting that ranking and beginning the claimed goal. The essay usefully challenges self-deception, but it treats action too conclusively: constraints, conflicting duties, fear, addiction, disability, and missing capability can separate sincere values from observed behavior.

## Key Claims
- Saying that a goal matters does not establish its priority when behavior repeatedly allocates time and effort elsewhere.
- Sivers's years of not launching another company suggested that he valued a simple life of learning, writing, and parenting more than the company idea, even though he wanted both to some degree.
- Inaction can reveal competition among wants rather than the total absence of desire: the person may want one outcome but want another condition more.
- A discrepancy between words and conduct supports two responses: admit the priority currently expressed in behavior, or begin acting on the professed goal and test whether the commitment persists.
- Actions are evidence about values under current conditions, not infallible proof of a person's deepest values or unconstrained preferences.

## Key Quotes
> "Yes, I wanted it a little bit, but I wanted something else more." - Sivers's reinterpretation of his unlaunched company.

> "Your actions show you what you actually want." - the essay's action-first test for professed priorities.

## Connections
- [[DerekSivers]] - author who applies his coach's action-first interpretation to his own unrealized company plan.
- [[RevealedPriorities]] - central distinction between professed wants and the ranking expressed through behavior.
- [[ActionGroundedIdentity]] - parallel Sivers argument that current conduct should support present-tense self-description.
- [[SelfDiscipline]] - beginning the claimed goal is the proposed test of whether declared intent can become sustained practice.
- [[IntentionalLifeDesign]] - honest recognition of competing wants can clarify whether to accept or change one's current allocation of life.

## Contradictions
- The essay's absolute claim that anyone who truly wanted an outcome would already have acted conflicts with cases where sincere desire is blocked by coercion, poverty, illness, disability, caregiving, addiction, fear, discrimination, missing knowledge, or other constraints. Behavior more safely reveals effective priorities within a person's present options and capacities.
