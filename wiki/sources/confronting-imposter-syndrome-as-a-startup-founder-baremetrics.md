## Summary
[[JoshPigford]] describes [[ImposterSyndrome]] as a recurring gap between visible success and an internal sense of not being good enough, using negative Hacker News comments about [[Baremetrics]] as the immediate trigger. He argues that founder work becomes increasingly psychological as a company grows and recommends a trusted founder community, regular distance from the startup echo chamber, direct customer contact, and openly naming difficult days rather than hiding them behind “fake it till you make it.”

## Key Claims
- [[ImposterSyndrome]] can persist despite clear business success because external achievement does not automatically resolve self-doubt or make criticism emotionally neutral.
- Company growth can increase founder pressure: more revenue and apparent success may bring greater responsibility, scrutiny, and stress rather than relief.
- A small community of founders can reduce isolation by making difficult experiences discussable with people who understand the role.
- Regularly leaving the startup echo chamber can weaken status comparison and restore perspective on which company problems actually matter.
- Direct customer contact can correct a negativity-skewed information flow in which leaders mainly encounter escalations while routine positive experiences are filtered out.
- Calling a difficult day what it is can be healthier than suppressing it or performing permanent confidence.
- [[FounderSuccessDefinition]] matters because “fake it till you make it” has no stopping rule until the founder defines what making it means personally.

## Key Quotes
> "despite any clear successes you've had, that you're not good enough" - Pigford's concise description of imposter syndrome.

> "Making more money has not equated to having less stress" - on growth amplifying rather than resolving mental pressure.

- [[ImposterSyndrome]] - central psychological pattern the essay names and attempts to mitigate.
- [[SoloFounding]] - concentrated founder responsibility helps explain the loneliness and mental pressure described.
- [[FounderSuccessDefinition]] - a personal definition of “made it” gives success a stopping rule beyond startup comparison.

- [[CustomerLedProductDevelopment]] - direct customer contact is valuable here as an emotional and informational corrective, not only a product-discovery method.

## Contradictions
- The essay challenges the assumption that financial growth or visible business success reliably reduces stress or produces an internal feeling of success.
- Its coping suggestions are a first-person founder account rather than clinical guidance or comparative evidence; they may improve perspective without addressing depression, anxiety, harassment, financial strain, or an objectively unsustainable workload.
