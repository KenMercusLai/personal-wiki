## Summary
[[FredWilson]] argues that a fast-growing technology company should establish human-resources leadership early, keep it directly connected to the CEO, and treat it as culture and leadership infrastructure rather than primarily as a recruiting pipeline. He pairs that organizational model with explicit behavioral rules, substantial onboarding, company-wide feedback, reporting training, a known whistleblower hotline, carefully bounded anonymous feedback, and recurring all-hands discussion. The essay is practitioner advice prompted by a reported sexual-harassment complaint at [[Uber]], but Wilson says he knew only the engineer's account and that an investigation was pending.

## Key Claims
- HR leadership should be hired early and its experience level revisited as a company grows from tens to hundreds, thousands, and tens of thousands of employees.
- The HR leader should report directly to the CEO because hierarchy communicates organizational priorities and because the CEO needs an unfiltered view of culture and leadership.
- HR should support hiring and departures without being reduced to a recruiting and offboarding pipeline.
- Culture, leadership, dignified performance and development feedback, a behavioral handbook, and multi-week organizational onboarding are core HR responsibilities.
- Effective whistleblower infrastructure combines recognition and reporting training, an accessible hotline, appropriately governed anonymous feedback, and repeated company-wide communication.
- Rapid headcount growth requires repeated instruction because a workforce can change substantially between annual policy discussions.

## Key Quotes
> "Your management hierarchy says a lot about what you value and what you do not." - Wilson's reason for a direct CEO reporting line.

> "Your employees are watching you." - the essay's closing reminder that organizational response is itself a cultural signal.

## Contradictions
- Qualifies recruiting-centered descriptions of HR by treating recruiting and offboarding as necessary but insufficient parts of the function.
- The direct-to-CEO reporting recommendation and claims that strong onboarding and feedback improve performance are practitioner judgments, not comparative findings.
- The essay's Uber setup is explicitly provisional: Wilson knew only the engineer's blog post and did not claim to have established the full facts.
