## Summary
[[MorganHousel]] argues that durable strategy combines technological change with customer desires that remain stable over time. Using [[Amazon]], [[JeffBezos]], [[WarrenBuffett]], [[GEICO]], and [[MarcAndreessen]] as examples, the essay says enduring companies compound by investing in timeless customer values such as low prices, fast delivery, selection, convenience, trust, transparency, entertainment, and status while using change mainly as the competitive delivery mechanism.

## Key Claims
- [[Amazon]] succeeded by using the Internet to change distribution while anchoring its strategy in old retail values: selection and low prices.
- Bezos's strategic question is not only what will change in the next decade, but what will not change enough to justify long-term investment.
- Change can create bursts of opportunity, but durable compounding usually requires a stable customer demand that still matters years later.
- Successful investments combine change that drives competition with timelessness that drives compounding.
- [[WarrenBuffett]]'s long GEICO investment and venture-backed insurance software both rely on old customer values such as lower cost and convenience, even when the mechanisms change.
- Extremes fail: Sears underweighted Internet change, Beenz overestimated behavioral change, and Amazon found the overlap between new distribution and timeless customer desires.

## Key Quotes
> "You can build a business strategy around the things that are stable in time." - Bezos on why Amazon invests in low prices, fast delivery, and vast selection.

> "Every successful investment is some combination of change that drives competition and things staying the same that drives compounding." - Housel on the change-plus-timelessness pattern.
