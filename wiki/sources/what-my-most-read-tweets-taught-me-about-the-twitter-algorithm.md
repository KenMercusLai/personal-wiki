## Summary
[[MaiYang]] groups three unexpectedly high-reach [[Twitter]]/X posts into opinion, learning, and genuine product use, then proposes [[PracticeLedContent]] as an alternative to manufacturing posts for performance. The retained screenshots add point-in-time view and engagement counts for posts about [[Cursor]], [[OpenClaw]] and context engineering, and [[Raycast]], but the three self-selected examples do not identify a ranking mechanism or establish that authenticity caused their reach.

## Key Claims
- Direct, experience-based opinions about tools can invite reach and debate without being planned as viral content.

![Mai Yang Cursor opinion post showing 65,000 views and its visible engagement counts](../../wiki-assets/what-my-most-read-tweets-taught-me-about-the-twitter-algorithm/cursor-opinion-post.jpg)

- Publicly documenting active learning can turn a podcast, a repeated project pattern, and a useful explanation into shareable material.

![Mai Yang learning post about OpenClaw, Pi, and context engineering showing 23,000 views](../../wiki-assets/what-my-most-read-tweets-taught-me-about-the-twitter-algorithm/openclaw-context-engineering-post.jpg)

- Repeated use supplies concrete product testimony: Mai Yang says Raycast reduced dependence on both mouse and trackpad and prompted internal sharing and a demo site.

![Mai Yang Raycast usage post showing 20,000 views and an embedded user example](../../wiki-assets/what-my-most-read-tweets-taught-me-about-the-twitter-algorithm/raycast-usage-post.jpg)

- The author interprets opinion, learning, and real usage as signals of active building and exploration rather than a formal content strategy.
- The screenshots show different engagement shapes despite similar subject matter: the Cursor opinion drew far more replies, while the learning and Raycast posts had comparatively high bookmark counts.
- The proposed algorithm lesson is a personal hypothesis from three selected posts, not evidence that Twitter/X ranks authenticity, lived experience, or these three categories directly.

## Key Quotes
> "They simply came from three things: opinion, learning, real usage." - the author's retrospective grouping.

> "just document what I actually use, learn, and build." - the resulting publishing rule.

## Contradictions
- The article's claim that Twitter may amplify people who document reality is not established by three self-selected successful posts; it lacks unsuccessful comparisons, impression sources, audience size, timing, content-format controls, and platform-side ranking data.
- Screenshot counts are point-in-time platform displays, not final or audited outcomes. Views do not establish attentive reading, and replies, reposts, likes, and bookmarks represent different behaviors whose motives are not observed.
- The Cursor post's 65,000 views, 164 replies, 9 reposts, 289 likes, and 10 bookmarks differ materially from the learning post's 23,000 views, 2 replies, 12 reposts, 108 likes, and 215 bookmarks and the Raycast post's 20,000 views, 3 replies, 2 reposts, 62 likes, and 90 bookmarks. Those differences support content-type comparison but not a common causal explanation.
- All four remote images were opened. The decorative title graphic was omitted; the three text-bearing post screenshots were retained because they document wording, context, dates, view totals, and engagement counts not fully repeated in the prose.
