## Summary
This GV Library essay argues that [[InterfaceCopywriting]] is a core design practice: labels, buttons, descriptions, and supporting text shape whether people understand and trust a product. It prioritizes specific, front-loaded, action-oriented language over brevity or personality alone and recommends writing real copy throughout prototyping and [[UserTesting]] rather than inserting it after the interface is designed.

## Key Claims
- Clarity is the primary standard for interface copy: writers should distinguish actions precisely, avoid unexplained jargon, front-load important words, and replace generic controls such as “OK” with explicit outcomes.
- Brand personality is secondary to comprehension in high-value interface elements such as headlines and buttons; supporting text is a safer place for restrained voice.
- Direct labels and previews of the next step reduce ambiguity, especially for unlabeled content and multi-page flows.
- People read prominent and interactive text, and clear explanations of matters such as security or privacy can contribute to credibility and trust.
- Copywriting belongs throughout the design process, because prototypes with real text let teams test and improve what the product says alongside interaction and visual design.

## Key Quotes
> “Clarity is king.” — the essay's governing standard for interface language.

> “Writing is a core part of the design process” — on treating copy as design material rather than final-stage decoration.
