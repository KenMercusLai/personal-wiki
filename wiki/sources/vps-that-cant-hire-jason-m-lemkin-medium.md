## Summary
[[JasonLemkin]] argues that a startup VP's ability to recruit strong direct reports is a core executive qualification, not a secondary management skill. He recommends checking whom a candidate personally hired, speaking directly with the candidate's two best hires, and evaluating their role-specific outcomes before making the appointment. If an otherwise capable VP proves unable to recruit, Lemkin recommends quickly hiring a stronger executive above that person rather than waiting for repeated failures.

## Key Claims
- After roughly $1 million in annual recurring revenue, hiring strong functional VPs becomes one of a SaaS company's most consequential scaling decisions.
- Smart, driven, successful individual contributors or team leads may still lack the ability to recruit a strong team beneath them.
- Reference diligence should distinguish people a candidate personally recruited from people they merely inherited as a manager.
- Founders should speak directly with at least two strong hires made by the candidate and test role-specific outcomes: sales quota, shipped product, engineering quality, marketing leads, or customer-success retention and satisfaction.
- Recruiting is presented as every VP's first job because a leader who cannot attract strong reports constrains the whole function.
- The damage appears at different speeds by role: sales failure may become visible quickly, while weak product, marketing, or other functional hiring may take several releases or months to surface.
- Once a VP demonstrates that they cannot hire even one strong report, Lemkin recommends adding a stronger leader above them promptly rather than waiting for a third failed hire.

## Key Quotes
> "This is job #1 for all your VPs. Recruiting." - Lemkin's central executive-accountability claim.

> "Check who they hired. Not inherited as a manager." - on separating personal recruiting evidence from team inheritance.
