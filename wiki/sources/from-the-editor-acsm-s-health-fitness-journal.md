## Summary
[[StevenKeteyian]] argues that health and fitness professionals should help clients distinguish evidence from diets, gimmicks, and fads by translating correctly conducted research into practical guidance. He presents the [[AmericanCollegeOfSportsMedicine]] and [[ACSMHealthAndFitnessJournal]] as institutions for that [[ResearchToPracticeTranslation]], then previews an annual fitness-trends survey and features on hydration and cognition, waist circumference and cardiometabolic risk, and other practitioner topics.

## Key Claims
- Health and fitness professionals operate amid a persistent stream of advertisements making health and wellbeing claims about diets, gimmicks, and fads.
- Practitioners should warn clients that claims which seem too good to be true are likely unreliable.
- The profession has a responsibility to translate correctly performed research into practical help for patients and clients seeking weight loss and improved fitness.
- ACSM's certifications, programs, and practitioner journal are presented as channels for moving science into health and fitness practice.
- The issue's annual Worldwide Survey of Fitness Trends for 2014 is intended to orient practitioners to the next year's field, while features on hydration and cognition and waist circumference and cardiometabolic risk turn narrower research topics into applied guidance.
- Regular columns extend the issue's practitioner scope to probiotics, lifelong bone health, Pilates, professional ethics, injury prevention, foot blisters, and energy management.

## Key Quotes
> “translating correctly performed research” — Keteyian's standard for converting science into useful professional guidance.

> “sort out fact from fiction” — the editorial's stated purpose for the issue's practitioner-facing material.

## Connections
- [[StevenKeteyian]] — editor-in-chief and author of the editorial.
- [[AmericanCollegeOfSportsMedicine]] — professional organization credited with certifications and programs that help translate health and fitness research.
- [[ACSMHealthAndFitnessJournal]] — practitioner publication whose purpose is described as translating science into usable guidance.
- [[ResearchToPracticeTranslation]] — the editorial's central professional responsibility and institutional rationale.

## Contradictions
- This is a brief promotional editorial, not a research study. It supplies no methods, evidence hierarchy, translation process, client outcomes, or evaluation showing that the journal, ACSM programs, or featured articles improve practice.
- The editorial names the fitness-trends, hydration, and waist-circumference articles but does not reproduce their evidence or findings; its favorable descriptions should not be treated as support for their underlying claims.
- “Too good to be true” is a useful skepticism heuristic, not a test of truth; plausible claims can still be false, and surprising claims can be supported by strong evidence.
