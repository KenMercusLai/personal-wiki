## Summary
[[DavidLieb]] reflects on the ten-year path from the original idea for [[Bump]] through [[YCombinator]], viral growth, server crises, product pivots into photo sharing, acquisition by [[Google]], and later work on [[GooglePhotos]]. The thread treats the company's most durable outcomes as people, cofounder trust, scrappy learning, and product discovery through real user behavior. It adds a positive startup case where an initially interesting app did not directly become the final product, but still led to a team, capabilities, and ideas that carried forward.

## Key Claims
- [[Bump]] reached 2 million users during [[YCombinator]] with five people and $16,000, supporting [[SmallTeamLeverage]].
- The Apple commercial spike showed that startup outcomes can depend on both product quality and luck, while the YC network helped Bump recover operationally.
- Calling Bump's top 100 users revealed that people were really sharing photos, making [[UserBehaviorDrivenProductDiscovery]] more important than the founders' initial framing.
- [[Flock]] did not grow because it lacked both an immediate tool reason and a network-spreading reason, while the unreleased Photoroll preserved ideas that later influenced [[GooglePhotos]].
- [[StartupTeamBond]] mattered as much as the product path: Lieb emphasizes cofounders, coworkers, humor, burnout care, and unusually high post-acquisition retention.

## Key Quotes
> "Talk to your users. They'll tell you what they want." - Paul Graham advice recalled by Lieb

> "Choose your cofounders wisely. That bond is everything." - Lieb on surviving startup pressure
