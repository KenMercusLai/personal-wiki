## Summary
This article profiles [[EdParsons]] and Google's Geo organization as [[Google]] pushes [[GoogleMaps]], [[GoogleEarth]], and [[StreetView]] from shared digital maps toward personalized, user-authored, and location-aware platforms. It argues that modern digital maps resemble billions of personal Chonhado-style maps, centered on the user but controlled by a platform whose data, moderation, privacy, advertising, and geopolitical choices shape what the world looks like. The source also contrasts Google's proprietary, utility-driven cartography with [[OpenStreetMap]]'s volunteer model and with older national or local mapping conventions.

## Key Claims
- [[DigitalCartography]] has shifted from static, shared paper maps toward dynamic, personalized maps that can change by user, zoom level, country, law, and commercial context.
- [[GoogleMaps]] is strategic infrastructure for [[Google]] because location data, search, ads, local business pages, crowdsourced corrections, and connected devices all meet inside the map.
- [[GoogleEarth]] is framed as a future "browser for the planet" and storytelling platform, bringing user-authored content, feeds, and dashboard-like planetary information into maps.
- [[StreetView]] makes mapping immersive and close to real-world presence, but its history also shows how cartography can become surveillance when collection systems gather sensitive data.
- [[LocationDataPrivacy]] is a central risk because even anonymized movement tracks can become identifying over time.
- [[UserGeneratedMapping]] expands map coverage and freshness through public photos, corrections, and volunteered data, but requires moderation and editorial judgment.
- [[OpenStreetMap]] offers a more democratic and open-data alternative to Google's proprietary map stream, while still facing cartographic abstraction and quality tradeoffs.

## Key Quotes
> "your map is literally your map" - Parsons on personalized digital maps.

> "Your location on the planet is one of the most sensitive pieces of information that anyone can hold on you." - Parsons on location privacy.
