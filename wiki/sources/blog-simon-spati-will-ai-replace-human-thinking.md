## Summary
[[SimonSpati]] argues that AI is useful for bounded support tasks but dangerous when it replaces the manual practice of writing, coding, reading, and thinking. The article frames [[AIDependencySkillAtrophy]] as a long-term risk: people can gain short-term productivity while losing attention, craft competence, learning, originality, and willingness to maintain what they produce. The embedded charts and screenshots reinforce the argument with an AI-productivity/error curve, declining reasoning-skill charts, overreliance writing examples, AI-web-content share data, seniority-biased employment data, and a productivity-illusion sketch.

## Key Claims
- [[AIDependencySkillAtrophy]] happens when AI use substitutes for the very practice that builds writing, coding, reading, memory, and judgment.
- AI use should be task-fit: autocomplete, diagrams, discovery, historical overviews, and well-defined functions are safer than architecture, long-term planning, final prose, or work whose consequences extend far into the future.
- [[AIAssistedWriting]] should preserve human insight, voice, conviction, research depth, and manual drafting rather than outsource the writing itself.
- [[AICodingPractice]] should account for maintainability, ownership, architecture risk, and the user's ability to understand generated code.
- AI-generated abundance may make genuine knowledge, pressure-tested facts, writing skill, and firsthand experience more valuable rather than less.
- The article treats junior labor and junior learning as exposed: AI-adopting firms may favor seniors while juniors lose both jobs and apprenticeship pathways.

## Key Quotes
> "Use it, yes, but not for every task." - on selective rather than total AI adoption.

> "Someone needs to add knowledge or new insights; AI cannot train itself." - on why human-originated thought still matters.

> "Stop Writing -> Stop Thinking -> Stop Learning -> Lose Competitive Edge" - on the article's compressed skill-atrophy chain.

## Contradictions
- Partly qualifies [[PracticalLLMUse]] and pragmatic [[AIAssistedWriting]] sources: those sources emphasize bounded usefulness and blank-page reduction, while this source argues that even helpful use becomes harmful when it displaces deliberate practice, research depth, and personally owned insight.
- Partly qualifies optimistic [[AICodingPractice]] and [[VibeCoding]] material by stressing maintainability, architecture risk, and responsibility for code the human did not understand deeply.
