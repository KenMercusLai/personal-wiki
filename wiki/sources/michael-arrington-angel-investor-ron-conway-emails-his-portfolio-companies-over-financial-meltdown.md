## Summary
[[MichaelArrington]] publishes [[RonConway]]'s October 2008 warning to portfolio-company CEOs together with two closely related emails from the 2000 technology downturn. Conway argues that a sudden contraction in capital availability should trigger immediate runway extension, realistic financing and valuation expectations, broader investor outreach, and—in sufficiently cash-constrained companies—early consideration of partnerships or M&A. Arrington adds an important boundary: Conway remained optimistic about technology and intended to keep investing, so the advice concerns portfolio survival under adverse financing conditions rather than a loss of confidence in innovation.

## Key Claims
- Conway treated the Wall Street-centered 2008 crisis as a direct Silicon Valley financing shock and reused the operating advice he had sent during the 2000 technology-market collapse.
- Startups should cut burn enough to add at least three to six months of runway, even when that requires staff reductions and lower marketing or general-and-administrative spending.
- Cost reduction functions like an internal financing round because it delays the next external raise; the useful goal is survival until fundraising conditions become more favorable.
- A company already raising should seek capital immediately and raise as much as feasible, while recognizing that failure to close requires corresponding cost cuts.
- The 2000 emails broaden the financing response: meet investors beyond the top tier, begin earlier, accept lower valuations, and seek corporate investment when traditional venture capacity contracts.
- Conway advised companies with limited cash to explore M&A early because a transaction can take more than 90 days and may combine funding, customers, market share, relationships, and operating capacity.
- Arrington reports that Conway was still investing and remained optimistic about technology; his concern was the ability of thinly capitalized portfolio companies to outlast the market disruption.

## Key Quotes
> "The message is simple. Raising capital will be much more difficult now." - Conway's 2008 diagnosis of the financing environment.

> "This is the equivalent to 'raising an internal round' through cost reductions" - Conway on extending runway by lowering burn.

> "the name of the game in this environment in some respects is survival" - Conway on preserving the company until conditions change.

## Contradictions
- The advice creates a productive tension with sources that warn against treating runway as calendar preservation alone: cutting burn buys time, but [[StartupRunway]] remains useful only if the company can still learn, serve customers, and reach material milestones.
- Conway's recommendation to raise as much as possible during a downturn differs from capital-discipline advice against taking excess funding. The combined judgment is conditional on financing scarcity, survival risk, terms, and whether additional capital supports a credible operating plan.
- The emails are investor-authored crisis guidance, not comparative evidence that the recommended cuts, fundraising, partnerships, or acquisitions improved survival. They do not quantify portfolio outcomes, distinguish company stages, or address how layoffs and reduced marketing may impair execution.
