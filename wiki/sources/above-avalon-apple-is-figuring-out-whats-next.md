## Summary
[[NeilCybart]] argues that Apple's WWDC 2018 announcements pointed beyond the devices on stage: ARKit, Siri Shortcuts, and machine-learning features make more sense on future body-worn form factors than on handheld iPhones and iPads alone. The article frames [[Apple]]'s post-iPhone strategy as making technology more personal, with [[AppleWatch]] and [[AirPods]] already proving momentum on the wrist and ears while [[SmartGlasses]] become the likely next battle for the eyes. Its embedded chart supports the argument by showing iPhone unit sales flattening in the low 200-million range while wearables rise toward iPad-like annual unit volume in the 2019 estimates.

## Key Claims
- Apple's WWDC 2018 software announcements are interpreted as preparation for form factors that did not yet exist, especially [[SmartGlasses]].
- [[Apple]] is not trying to replace the iPhone's revenue directly; the source says the post-iPhone goal is more personal technology and workflows the iPhone cannot handle.
- Plateauing iPhone unit sales make the search for the next personal-computing form factor strategically important.
- [[AppleWatch]] and [[AirPods]] show that Apple was already winning body real estate on wrists and ears, creating a bridge toward eye-worn computing.
- [[ARKit]] gives Apple a large installed base and developer surface for seeding augmented-reality behavior before glasses are ready.
- [[AppleWatch]] is framed as a possible support device for future glasses, carrying body-worn technology and proactive assistant learning.
- Apple is presented as unusually well positioned for AR glasses because of hardware/software integration, custom silicon, miniaturization, developers, retail demos, fashion, and health.
- [[MagicLeap]] is used as a contrast case: ambitious in AR, but lacking Apple's installed base, developer ecosystem, and wearable-learning base.

## Key Quotes
> "Apple is setting the stage for smart glasses." - central product-strategy claim.

> "This is Apple's game to lose." - conclusion about Apple's position in AR glasses.
