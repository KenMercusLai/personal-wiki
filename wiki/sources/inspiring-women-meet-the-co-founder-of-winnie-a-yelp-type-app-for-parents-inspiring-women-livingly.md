## Summary
Livingly interviews [[SaraMauskopf]] about co-founding [[Winnie]] with [[AnneHalsall]] after motherhood exposed fragmented information about childcare, child development, and family-friendly places. Mauskopf credits [[SiliconValley]]'s engineers, investors, and relationships for helping the company form, while arguing that a product for families must work beyond that ecosystem's bubble. Her account also connects [[WorkLifeBalance]] and [[StartupCrisisLeadership]]: during her husband's cancer treatment, family help, a self-sufficient team, and Winnie's own local recommendations let her combine company leadership with solo parenting, but the profile does not independently test the company's reach or outcomes.

## Key Claims
- Mauskopf says becoming a mother revealed the need for one place where parents could find age- and stage-relevant information, local childcare and venues, and advice from other parents.
- She presents parenthood as both the origin of Winnie's problem selection and a source of confidence and determination to build the company.
- The profile reports a community of more than 25,000 parents and users in more than 3,000 U.S. cities, while describing the product as an intended long-term companion from diaper needs through camps and adolescence.
- Mauskopf credits [[SiliconValley]] with access to engineers, early investors, her co-founder, and professional relationships, but calls it a bubble and says useful products should address people beyond it.
- After her husband was diagnosed with aggressive cancer, Mauskopf initially expected to stop working; two weeks of strong team execution showed her that the company could operate without her constant presence.
- Family support and Winnie's recommendations for playgrounds, museums, and child-friendly restaurants helped Mauskopf maintain routines while parenting largely alone during treatment.
- Mauskopf attributes her progress to hard work interacting with privilege, upbringing, education, a supportive spouse, professional relationships, and persistence rather than to individual effort alone.

## Key Quotes
> "People matter the most." - on fundraising relationships and being someone others want to work with.

> "Don’t wait for the perfect time to follow your dreams." - advice to prospective women entrepreneurs.
