## Summary
[[TomWhitwell]] collects 52 surprising facts from 2017 across startups, translation apps, robotics, health technology, insurance, energy, poverty, platform behavior, media economics, and customer research. The list functions less as one thesis than as a curiosity-driven scan of weak signals: old infrastructure persists, new technology enters unexpected contexts, behavioral evidence can overturn intuition, and striking anecdotes often need source-scope qualifications. The three local image embeds are duplicate/thumbnail versions of a decorative illustration of people walking through barriers and add no independent factual evidence beyond the article's playful presentation.

## Key Claims
- Cross-domain scanning can reveal useful patterns by juxtaposing small facts from technology, markets, health, institutions, climate, and everyday behavior.
- Technology adoption often appears in unexpected or informal settings, including smartphone translation in Shenzhen markets, DIY artificial-pancreas work, QR-code donations, home battery building, pagers in the NHS, and voice search.
- New systems coexist with old infrastructure and social constraints, as shown by radioactive ATM vouchers, transparent prison typewriters, divining rods at water companies, iron lungs, and paper-mail retargeting.
- Behavioral and social evidence can challenge common assumptions, including startup founder age, WEIRD sampling bias, cash-transfer spending, customer objection research, sports-car risk signals, and bad cellphone-market forecasts.
- Several claims are intentionally source-scoped snapshots from 2017 rather than durable current facts.
