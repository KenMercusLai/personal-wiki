## Summary
[[AndrewChen]] argues that [[DAUMAU]] is a useful engagement ratio for products whose value naturally recurs every day, especially communication and social products, but a poor universal test of [[ProductMarketFit]]. Episodic products can remain valuable through high-value transactions, monetization, or unique data even when daily use is structurally low. Teams should therefore fit their [[ProductMetricLadder]] to the product's natural cadence and examine hardcore cohorts, network effects, value per interaction, and retention rather than trying to force every category toward Facebook-like frequency.

## Key Claims
- [[DAUMAU]] divides daily active users by monthly active users; the article describes more than 20% as commonly considered good and more than 50% as world class, benchmarks popularized by [[Facebook]].
- Daily-use communication and social products fit the ratio best, while travel, recruiting, enterprise software, rides to airports, and infrequent commerce can be valuable despite low daily frequency.
- Low-frequency products need to create enough money or distinctive data per interaction to compensate for fewer occasions, while high-frequency products have more opportunity for viral or advertising-supported audience models.
- Product categories have a natural usage cadence: communication can combine high frequency and retention, social games can be frequent but short-lived, and weather can be infrequent yet persistently needed.
- Email and push reactivation can increase monthly actives more than daily actives and therefore lower the ratio even when they increase total use.
- Teams should not assume that effort can turn a naturally 10% DAU/MAU category into a 40% category; they should instead study consistently active users and test whether frequency rises with network size, content created, or other accumulated value.
- The right metric should show that the product creates user value at its real cadence, not reward daily use as an end in itself.

## Key Quotes
> "Not everything has to be daily use to be valuable." - on separating frequency from product value.

> "Maybe DAU/MAU is just not for you." - on choosing a metric that fits the product category.
