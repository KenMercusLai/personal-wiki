## Summary
The GrowthBot article argues that the 2015-2017 chatbot boom overpromised because early bots lacked a real ecosystem, used brittle decision-tree logic, hid useful graphical affordances, and could not match human conversational expectations. It qualifies optimistic [[MessagingAsPlatform]] and [[ConversationalUI]] claims: bots can still help with repetitive, bounded tasks and can extend apps, but they should not be treated as wholesale replacements for apps, GUIs, or human support. The inspected images make the argument concrete through a hype-cycle chart, a rule-based dialogue architecture diagram, canned-response failure screenshots, and a Penny screenshot showing chat advice embedded beside account dashboards and transaction lists.

## Key Claims
- The first chatbot wave was a classic hype cycle: expectations around messaging, WeChat, conversational marketing, Slack, and Facebook Messenger ran ahead of actual consumer adoption, developer engagement, and platform maturity.
- [[ConversationalUI]] should be treated as an extension layer rather than an app replacement, because complex apps and GUIs remain better for many rich, multi-step, visual tasks.
- Most first-wave bots were brittle because decision-tree and keyword logic only handled cases their builders anticipated.
- [[NaturalLanguageProcessing]] and broader AI were not mature enough for human-like support, sales, or marketing conversations in the period the article describes.
- Visible GUI affordances often reduce effort because users can scan options, click or tap directly, and understand information visually instead of guessing what a bot can do.
- Bots work best when scoped to one bounded job, repetitive low-level queries, or multimodal/hybrid experiences that combine conversation with traditional interfaces.

## Key Quotes
> "Because there isn't even an ecosystem for a platform to dominate." - on why the expected chatbot platform race failed to appear.

> "A bot that does ONE thing well is infinitely more helpful" - on the need for narrow scope.

> "Computers are good at being computers." - on assigning bots bounded computational work rather than human emotional work.

## Contradictions
