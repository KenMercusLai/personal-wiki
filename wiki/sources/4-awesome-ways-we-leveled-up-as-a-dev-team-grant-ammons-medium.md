## Summary
[[GrantAmmons]] argues that technical leaders should deliberately protect work time for team learning rather than treating growth as something engineers must do only outside delivery work. Drawing on [[PipelineDeals]], he recommends weekly lunch-and-learns, shared video watching, book clubs, and meetup or conference speaking as practical [[WorkplaceLearning]] routines. The inspected local images are decorative abstract teamwork illustrations and do not add source evidence beyond the article text.

## Key Claims
- Technical leaders should deliberately allocate work time for engineers to learn together.
- Lightweight lunch-and-learns can mix current events, show-and-tell, process retrospectives, and informal social conversation without requiring heavy planning.
- Shared conference talks, Clean Coders, Destroy All Software, and similar videos can create team discussion and real-time "aha" moments.
- Book clubs let engineering teams discuss technical, business, and leadership books in manageable recurring chunks.
- Encouraging engineers to attend or speak at meetups and conferences builds learning culture, career skill, and team brand.
- Public speaking forces deeper subject learning because preparing a talk requires enough command of a topic to teach it.

## Key Quotes
> "Knowledge workers need sanctioned time to develop themselves" - on making learning legitimate at work.

> "That is what leveling up feels like." - on recognizing a changed coding mental model.
