## Summary
[[MarkSuster]] argues that candidates seeking a role in a city where they intend to relocate should commit to the place before searching for the strongest local opportunities. His [[InMarketJobSearch]] case combines sustained face-to-face networking, access to unadvertised roles, and employer confidence that the candidate will actually relocate, while acknowledging that moving first creates real financial and family risk.

## Key Claims
- The best local opportunities often circulate through personal networks before reaching job boards or executive recruiters.
- A few exploratory trips or old LinkedIn contacts do not provide the sustained local presence needed to build a high-quality opportunity pipeline.
- Candidates who hedge between multiple possible cities can weaken their prospects in each because neither their network nor their relocation commitment becomes credible.
- Hiring managers may prefer an equivalent local candidate when a remote applicant's house sale, children's schooling, partner's education, or long commute makes relocation uncertain.
- A committed mover should settle the household and learn the city before adding the demands of a new role and intensive networking.
- Someone unwilling to accept the relocation risk should acknowledge that constraint and postpone the move rather than present an uncertain commitment as settled.

## Key Quotes
> "move there first and job search second." - Suster's central recommendation for a candidate committed to a particular city.

> "Sure, you can land 'a' job, just not 'THE' job." - the article's distinction between obtaining employment and maximizing fit or quality.

## Connections

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction was found. The source concerns candidates seeking location-bound work after relocating, so it does not by itself refute the wiki's examples of organizations designed for remote work.
- The article is a forceful 2016 practitioner opinion, not comparative hiring research. Its "100x" probability claim is rhetorical and unsupported, and its advice may not transfer to remote-first roles, standardized hiring pipelines, scarce-specialist searches, or candidates constrained by money, visas, disability, caregiving, housing, or a partner's career.
- The sole local image was inspected. It is a decorative photograph of a paper map and was omitted without creating an asset manifest.
