## Summary
This 2016 Going Concern essay argues that [[Uber]]'s on-demand marketplace model is a poor fit for most accounting engagements because accounting is scheduled, complex, long-lived, advice-heavy, and grounded in personal trust rather than a short standardized trip. It distinguishes commoditizable tax preparation and transaction-categorization work from higher-value client relationships, while arguing that automation and accounting software pose a more direct threat to the low end than a marketplace for interchangeable accountants. The source supplies a useful [[ServiceMarketplaceFit]] comparison, but it is a practitioner argument without market data or later outcome evidence.

## Key Claims
- An on-demand marketplace is not a universal service template; fit depends on the service's frequency, standardization, duration, relationship structure, trust mechanism, provider capacity, and required assets.
- Uber benefits from frequent short transactions, an easily understood destination, spare provider capacity, and reputation signals that help strangers transact.
- Most accounting engagements are scheduled, long-term, personal, technically complex, and partly diagnostic: clients may need help deciding what outcome to pursue, not only execution toward a known destination.
- Ratings are weaker quality signals for expert services when buyers cannot independently assess technical work, making referrals, testimonials, case studies, and accumulated adviser trust more important.
- Low-end tax preparation and bookkeeping are more susceptible to standardization, but software automation may remove human work directly rather than merely route it through an on-demand labor marketplace.
- The author expects accounting disruption but rejects the claim that it must take the form of a phone-based Uber analogue.

## Key Quotes
> "The problem with this doomsday scenario is that it relies on the assumption that any service can be Uberized." - the article's rejected premise

> "the client has no idea where they need to go" - why accounting often includes diagnosis and advice rather than simple fulfillment

## Contradictions
- The source qualifies broad "Uber for X" and marketplace-disruption narratives: a successful interface and matching model in one category does not establish fit in another.
- Its categorical claim that accounting will not be Uberized is a 2016 forecast, not an observed market outcome. The essay provides no adoption, pricing, retention, quality, capacity, or automation data and does not examine hybrid marketplaces, specialized project work, regulatory variation, or later AI capabilities.
