## Summary
[[GabeZichermann]] argues that [[Gamification]] reward design should not default to cash or physical goods because tangible rewards can be expensive, underwhelming, operationally costly, or less motivating than virtual rewards unless the prizes are large. The source introduces the [[SAPSRewardFramework]] - Status, Access, Power, Stuff - as a priority order for rewards that are usually stickier and cheaper before moving to material giveaways. The article is practitioner advice rather than research-backed evidence, and the captured comments include later criticism that SAPS is incomplete and not data-grounded.

## Key Claims
- Tangible rewards can reduce engagement if users quickly judge them as unattractive, cheap, or not worth pursuing.
- Cash, physical gifts, and fulfillment-heavy prizes are expensive relative to many startup or brand-engagement budgets.
- Small-dollar rewards often do not motivate better than virtual items or non-material rewards.
- [[SAPSRewardFramework]] prioritizes Status, Access, Power, and Stuff in that order for gamified engagement.
- Status, access, and power can be cheaper and stickier than stuff because they use recognition, special experiences, and permission rather than external prizes.
- Stuff should be considered after non-material options are exhausted unless an organization has strong prizes and the budget to support them.

## Key Quotes
> "Cash (or stuff)" - problem framing around defaulting to tangible rewards.

> "Status, Access, Power and Stuff" - expansion of SAPS.
