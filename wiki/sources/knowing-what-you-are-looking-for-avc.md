## Summary
[[FredWilson]] argues that a clear investment thesis prepares an investor to recognize and pursue a matching opportunity quickly. He illustrates the claim with [[BradBurnham]] and [[UnionSquareVentures]] identifying a search-style, pay-per-click model for job listings before encountering [[Indeed]], then persistently winning an investment despite the self-funded founders' initial reluctance. The retrospective links preparation to a strong outcome but does not show that thesis fit caused the result or how often similarly compelling matches failed.

## Key Claims
- A defined investment thesis narrows attention and makes a strong fit easier to recognize when it appears.
- Wilson and Burnham formed conviction around an applications-layer job-search model before discovering a company that implemented it.
- A John Battelle post served as the discovery channel that connected their prior model to Indeed.
- Indeed's founders did not need outside money, so recognizing the opportunity was followed by sustained investor pursuit rather than an immediate transaction.
- Wilson retrospectively calls Indeed possibly USV's best company investment, while distinguishing that company judgment from the firm's largest realized return.
- The counterfactual claim that an independent public Indeed might have exceeded a $10 billion valuation is speculation, not an observed outcome.

## Key Quotes
> "It really helps to know what you are looking for." - Wilson's case for thesis-driven preparation before a deal appears.

> "your search engine for jobs is here, it is called Indeed." - Wilson's recognition that a previously discussed model had become a concrete company.

## Contradictions
- The story complements [[VentureCapitalBlindSpots]] by showing a prepared thesis aiding recognition, but it does not resolve the opposing risk that rigid thesis filters can exclude category-defining companies.
- The conclusion that knowing what to seek is usually a recipe for success rests on one retrospectively selected winner and supplies no denominator of thesis-matched investments, missed companies, or failed pursuits.
- Indeed's later quality and hypothetical independent valuation are Wilson's retrospective judgments; the source provides no return data, valuation analysis, or counterfactual evidence.
