## Summary
[[JasonFried]] argues that availability is often constrained by attention rather than clock time. Using a prospective intern as the central example, he says that making room for another person or project can be unfair when the responder would be physically present but mentally occupied elsewhere. The source contains no effective image references.

## Key Claims
- [[AttentionManagement]] should distinguish available hours from available mental capacity.
- Saying "I do not have the time" can obscure the more precise reason for refusing work: insufficient attention.
- Responsible collaboration sometimes means declining even small commitments when the needed preparation, follow-through, and mental presence are unavailable.
- [[PersonalProductivity]] is limited by how many important projects can hold the mind at once, not only by calendar arithmetic.

## Key Quotes
> "I don't have the attention" - on naming the real constraint.

> "Time and attention aren't the same thing." - on the article's central distinction.
