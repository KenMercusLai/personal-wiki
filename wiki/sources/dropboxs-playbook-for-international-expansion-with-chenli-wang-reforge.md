## Summary
This Reforge interview with [[ChenLiWang]] presents [[Dropbox]]'s international growth as a staged operating problem rather than a translation project. It argues that teams should compare international expansion with other post-[[ProductMarketFit]] growth options, follow demonstrated demand where possible, select markets and offices around monetizable opportunity and required capabilities, and learn through one launch cycle before multiplying regions. The case also shows that local presence, language, distribution, data trust, currency, payment methods, and internal legal, finance, and HR capacity can matter as much as the core product.

## Key Claims
- Organic international use depends on the product, customer base, regulation, and distribution model; Dropbox could follow users already spreading the product, while enterprise products such as Workday required more deliberate country adaptation.
- International expansion should compete with adjacent products, platforms, and customer segments for limited post-fit investment rather than being treated as an automatic next step.
- Dropbox found doubling down in penetrated markets more viable than trying to spark growth from a low base, partly because monetization correlated with users' GDP per capita even when product usage was broadly useful.
- The forcing function for a foreign office can be a missing local capability: Dropbox could not deliver German- and Japanese-language, time-zone-aligned phone support from San Francisco.
- Office selection should test the talent pool for the roles actually needed; blinded candidate review favored Dublin for Dropbox's sales and support work.
- A local office can create trust and demand that remote experiments fail to measure, while a landing team of experienced culture carriers can establish shared values without treating the new office as a satellite.
- Market adaptation can extend across discovery, retail distribution, local currency and payment methods, data-ownership trust, regulation, and support; teams should launch sequentially so legal, finance, HR, and operations can learn and codify a reusable playbook.

## Key Quotes
> “Follow the users” — Wang's description of Dropbox's demand-led localization.

> “Don't go from zero to ten” — Wang on sequencing country launches.

## Contradictions
- No direct contradiction was found. The case qualifies generic expansion advice by showing that Dropbox's organic international reach was exceptional and that usage, monetization, trust, and operating capability can diverge by market.
