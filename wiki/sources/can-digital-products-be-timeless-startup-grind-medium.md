## Summary
This [[StartupGrind]] essay asks whether websites and apps can become timeless in the way physical products, watches, cars, logos, and graphic designs can. Using the [[GoogleHomepage]] and [[Porsche911]] as parallel examples, it argues that digital products face a handicap because the web changes quickly, but some digital designs may still become [[DigitalProductTimelessness]] cases when a simple, iconic core endures through careful iteration.

## Key Claims
- Timeless design requires good design, cultural recognizability, and enough time to prove the design remains good decades later.
- Digital products are rarely designed for timelessness because the web is a fast-changing living medium where platforms, paradigms, and business needs shift quickly.
- The [[GoogleHomepage]] and [[Porsche911]] share a focused origin story: each began around one primary experience and kept that core recognizable while improving over time.
- Physical products may get an emotional advantage from tactile materials that age visibly, but digital products can also improve with use through personalization, memory, relevance, and accumulated user value.
- Digital designers prioritize elasticity more than longevity, but mature internet products may eventually be understood as timeless with enough hindsight.

## Key Quotes
> "The web is living medium" - describing digital design's pace constraint.

> "simple, innovative, unobtrusive" - the shared Google/Porsche design comparison.
