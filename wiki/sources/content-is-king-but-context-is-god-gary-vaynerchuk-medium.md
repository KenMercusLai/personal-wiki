## Summary
[[GaryVaynerchuk]] argues that useful content earns the relationship that makes a later sale possible, but only when it fits the audience's mindset, the platform's norms, and the surrounding experience. His three-part practitioner framework is to put the platform and audience ahead of the marketer's agenda, avoid interruptive encounters, and keep every post aligned with a consistent brand goal. The retained illustration depicts one target audience distributed across Facebook and Pinterest contexts, reinforcing the article's platform-specific segmentation example without adding performance evidence.

![People in audience segments marked by Facebook and Pinterest pins around a central target](../../wiki-assets/content-is-king-but-context-is-god-gary-vaynerchuk-medium/platform-specific-audience-targeting.png)

## Key Claims
- Brands can act as media companies by repeatedly giving consumers useful or entertaining material before asking for a sale.
- Content quality depends on context: the same person may have different intentions and expectations on different platforms.
- Marketers should design for what the audience values, not what the marketer personally prefers, and place the sales agenda third behind platform and audience fit.
- Native, value-adding content should meet people inside their activity rather than interrupting it with forced exposure.
- Clicks and impressions can misstate interest when an intrusive interface causes accidental interaction.
- Every public post contributes to a broader brand narrative, so channel-specific execution still needs a consistent goal, story, and personality.
- Leaders must communicate the company's north star throughout the organization if many employees are expected to express the brand coherently.

## Key Quotes
> "Content is king, but context is God." - on platform, audience, and experiential fit determining whether content works.

> "Respecting the platform. Respecting your audience. And finally, taking your agenda, and making it third." - the article's priority order for content decisions.
