## Summary
[[Tw93]] turns an internal share into a high-risk, non-advisory framework for engineers thinking about investing. The article combines AI-and-Bitcoin stock examples, [[DuanYongping]]-style value-investing discipline, financial-statement heuristics, index-fund simplicity, and engineer-specific leverage through code, writing, and sharing. The inspected slides add material evidence through portfolio result tables, downside-recovery math, VIX/RSI timing charts, ETF holding screenshots, finance-ratio examples, and leverage diagrams.

## Key Claims
- Engineers should treat investment as judgment under risk, not as blind copying of AI, Bitcoin, or US-stock winners.
- AI is presented as a structural investment theme spanning chips, cloud, software, applications, energy support, and physical-world automation, but the source repeatedly flags volatility and bubble risk.
- Buying companies should be grounded in business quality, future cash flow, financial statements, and sell discipline rather than short-term price movement.
- Leverage can be labor, capital, or knowledge, but the source argues that engineers usually have the best risk-adjusted leverage in code, writing, independent products, and public sharing.
- Low-cost broad-market ETFs are presented as the simpler default strategy, with concentrated growth or innovation exposure treated as optional and riskier.
