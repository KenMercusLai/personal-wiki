## Summary
[[MathildeCollin]] distills nine pieces of startup advice she found useful while building [[Front]]. The article's strongest throughline is selective judgment: follow customer and growth evidence, hire and fire with cultural consequences in mind, separate reversible from irreversible decisions, repeat the company mission, treat benchmarks cautiously, transmit founder context to executives, and treat outside advice as one data point rather than a command.

## Key Claims
- Founders should "follow your growth" by listening to customers and product evidence instead of outsourcing strategy to advisors.
- Early hires should be evaluated by whether the company would want many more people like them, because early employees shape later hiring.
- Leaving a toxic or badly fit person in place can damage the organization more than operating with an empty seat.
- One-way-door decisions deserve founder involvement, while reversible two-way-door decisions should increasingly be delegated.
- Generous severance can make necessary exits more humane and make managers more willing to make hard calls.
- Repeating the mission at every all-hands keeps work connected to purpose as the company grows.
- Startup benchmarks and outside advice should be treated as context-sensitive data points, not universal rules; founder instinct still has to be taught and exercised.
