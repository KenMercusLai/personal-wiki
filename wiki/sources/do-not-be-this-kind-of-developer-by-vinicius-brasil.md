## Summary
[[ViniciusBrasil]] argues that programming languages should be treated as context-dependent tools for communicating with machines and delivering business value, not as identities or weapons in disputes between developers. The essay connects technical choice to understanding the business problem and warns that habitual language contempt can spread negativity through a workplace.

## Key Claims
- Programming languages resemble spoken languages in their core communicative purpose, while differing in dialect, applicability, and context.
- A language is a tool whose value depends on the problem and business outcome it helps deliver.
- Software developers increasingly need to understand the business problem rather than treat code production as the final objective.
- Python, Java, and JavaScript each have useful application domains as well as limitations; no language is best for every project.
- Repeatedly disparaging languages can turn technical preference into hostility toward colleagues and damage the working environment.
- Developers should respond to an imperfect but relevant tool by studying its fit and tradeoffs rather than relying on blanket complaints.

## Key Quotes
> "And that’s how you want to see programming languages: as a tool."

> "Every programming language has its applicabilities, its pros and cons."
