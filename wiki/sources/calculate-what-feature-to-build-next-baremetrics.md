## Summary
This [[Baremetrics]] article presents DIE—Demand, Impact, and Effort—as a lightweight [[ProductIdeaPrioritization]] score for deciding which feature idea deserves attention next. Teams record ideas, rate demonstrated customer demand, expected effect on the company's current goal, and estimated implementation effort, then treat a lower combined score as a stronger candidate. The framework is explicitly a decision aid rather than an automatic roadmap: software estimates mix data with judgment, and prioritization must balance customer and business value against scarce time and money.

## Key Claims
- Writing every feature idea into a shared list is safer than acting on whichever idea feels exciting most recently.
- Demand should reflect repeated customer requests or observed customer problems; an unsupported founder hunch deserves a low demand rating.
- Impact must be defined relative to the company's current goal, such as revenue, customer growth, or product usage.
- Effort includes design research, customer interviews, engineering time, and the scarce time and money consumed by delivery.
- A coarse XS-to-XL effort scale can prevent false precision, though teams may substitute days or money when those units are useful.
- Lower DIE scores rank more favorably: high demand, high impact, and XS effort scores 3 in the example, while low demand, low impact, and XL effort scores 11.
- The score should guide judgment and reduce uncertainty, not categorically choose the roadmap or claim exact forecasts.

## Key Quotes
> "Building software is a mix of data and gut feelings." - on the limits of quantifying feature choices.

> "The goal here is to help guide your decision-making process and reduce some of the unknowns." - on treating DIE as a decision aid.
