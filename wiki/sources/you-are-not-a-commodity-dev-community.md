## Summary
[[JeffMeyerson]] argues that software engineers should resist being treated as interchangeable maintainers inside large companies and instead learn to prototype and ship products independently. Writing for [[SoftwareEngineeringDaily]], he attributes increased [[EngineerLeverage]] and [[SmallTeamLeverage]] to cheap cloud infrastructure, software-based production, and modular services for distribution, contracting, payments, and collaboration. The essay is an entrepreneurial manifesto rather than comparative career research, and its universal claims are qualified by the wiki's evidence on weak solo-business earnings, concentrated founder risk, and the operating demands of remote and independent work.

## Key Claims
- Large companies can narrow engineers into replaceable maintenance roles, while product ownership offers a possible route to more creative control and value capture.
- Cloud infrastructure and modular internet services reduce the capital and organizational resources needed for an individual or small team to create and distribute software.
- [[ProgrammerAnarchy]] removes product-management and management layers by letting programmers choose work and accept end-to-end responsibility.
- Engineers accustomed to internal corporate tooling may need to relearn greenfield prototyping and deployment; the author treats this as an emotional commitment problem as much as a technical one.
- The essay presents independent product creation as a reversible career experiment because failure can build skills that remain useful in later employment.
- "Machine learning + X" is proposed as a broad startup direction, but no customer, market, or outcome evidence is supplied.

## Key Quotes
> "Software tools are the means of production." - on why access to software infrastructure changes who can build products.

> "The leverage of the small team is massive." - the essay's compressed entrepreneurial thesis.

## Contradictions
- The essay's strong claim that independent builders capture all product value is qualified by [[IndependentCreator]], whose cases still depend on customers, platforms, payment providers, marketing, support, runway, and uncertain earnings.
- Its unconditional small-team optimism is qualified by [[SmallTeamLeverage]], where a five-person success also depended on timing, outside distribution, network support, and emergency operational recovery.
- Its claim that failure is safely reversible is aspirational rather than established; the source gives no evidence about financial runway, health, caregiving, immigration, hiring discrimination, or labor-market conditions.
- No direct contradiction was found on remote work, but [[RemoteWork]] shows that tooling alone does not establish transparency or better communication; trust, boundaries, inclusion, coordination, and reliable connectivity remain necessary.
