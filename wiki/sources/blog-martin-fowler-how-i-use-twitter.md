## Summary
[[MartinFowler]] describes [[Twitter]] as a useful but risky information tool whose value depends on careful account selection, chronological feeds, topical lists, reply avoidance, and saving article pointers into [[Instapaper]]. The article treats Twitter less as a conversation venue than as a curated discovery layer for writing, reading, pandemic and Ukraine-related situational awareness, and article sharing, while naming harassment and content moderation as the platform's major failures.

## Key Claims
- [[Twitter]]'s short-post format makes scanning efficient, but threads can weaken the brevity constraint.
- Online conversations are often low-value or toxic, so Fowler mostly avoids replies and arguments.
- A carefully curated follow graph and topical lists can turn Twitter into a controlled information source.
- [[Instapaper]] extends Twitter's usefulness by letting interesting article pointers become later reading rather than immediate distraction.
- Chronological feeds, default clients, and email for private communication help Fowler preserve control over attention and records.
- Harassment is Twitter's biggest failure, and content moderation cannot be reduced to simple free-speech absolutism.

## Key Quotes
> "Small posts are quick to skim" - on why brevity makes Twitter useful.

> "I only set my following to chronological order." - on resisting algorithmic feed control.

> "Twitter has long-lacked the tools to prevent harassment" - on the platform's major failure.
