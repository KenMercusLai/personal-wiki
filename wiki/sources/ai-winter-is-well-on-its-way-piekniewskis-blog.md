## Summary
[[FilipPiekniewski]] argues in 2018 that deep-learning hype was already weakening and that [[AIWinter]] was likely because the technology's most visible promises were failing in real-world applications. The article focuses on [[DeepLearningScaling]] limits, autonomous-driving failures, the Uber fatal crash, and the gap between benchmark/game performance and robust perception-action systems. Its embedded images include a decorative frozen-AI illustration, a compute-scaling chart from AlexNet to AlphaGo Zero, and an NTSB excerpt showing how the Uber system repeatedly reclassified a pedestrian before impact while emergency braking was disabled.

## Key Claims
- [[AIWinter]] becomes plausible when corporate PR, conference demand, and celebrity claims remain strong while visible application progress slows.
- [[DeepLearningScaling]] is not the same as capability scaling: more compute helped train larger systems and game agents, but did not yield proportionally broader real-world competence.
- [[AutonomousDrivingSafety]] exposes the gap between deep-learning demos and open-world reliability because road scenes demand fast obstacle response, physical prediction, and rare-case handling.
- End-to-end image-to-action learning can skip explicit verbal classification, but its high-dimensional inputs and sparse action labels make spurious correlations especially dangerous.
- The Uber fatal crash is used as evidence that classification-centered perception can fail when a safety system should prioritize not hitting an obstacle over naming it correctly.
- Skeptics such as Gary Marcus are treated as necessary correctives to deep-learning celebrity culture, even where the author does not fully share their proposed technical remedies.

## Key Quotes
> "this narrative begins to crack" - on the author's interpretation of 2018 AI sentiment.

> "A different paradigm is needed" - on the author's proposed break from conventional deep-learning approaches.
