## Summary
[[JeanetteHead]] argues that software-team disagreements should be chosen by likely influence, product value, and interpersonal cost rather than by personal preference. The article treats code review, product-scope negotiation, and design disagreement as normal collaboration pressures where developers should decide whether to press, let go, or start a useful losing battle that broadens team understanding. The remote lead image was inspected and classified as decorative workplace photography rather than independent evidence.

## Key Claims
- Developers should ask whether a disagreement can be won, whether the issue is worth changing, and what noticeable product or team gain justifies raising the stakes.
- In [[CodeReviewPractice]], reviewers should distinguish codebase standards, real performance issues, and readability problems from personal style preferences.
- Conversations are often better than line-by-line critique when onboarding a teammate or correcting a standards mismatch.
- Product-scope challenges are more persuasive when framed around meaningful time savings, reliability, viability, or quality rather than developer preference.
- Some losing battles are still valuable when they lead to user testing, shared learning, or a better third option.
- Making critique personal damages collaboration; stepping away and returning to an objective conversation can repair some hard-lost disputes.

## Key Quotes
> "Can you win?" - first triage question for deciding whether to push.

> "Is it worth it?" - value threshold for deciding whether to keep pressing.
