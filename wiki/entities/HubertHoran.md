## Overview
[[HubertHoran]] is presented as a transportation-industry analyst who critiques [[Uber]]'s early operating economics and dominance thesis from the perspective of cost structure, profitability, and economic welfare.

## Current Profile
The Naked Capitalism source identifies Horan as having decades of management and regulatory experience in transportation companies, primarily airlines, and states that he has no financial links to urban car-service competitors, investors, regulators, or firms working for industry participants. In the Uber analysis, he uses leaked or privately circulated financial figures to argue that Uber's growth did not prove software-like scale economics: the company was losing large amounts of money, passengers were paying below the actual cost of rides, and margin improvement depended heavily on reducing driver compensation.

## Key Characteristics
- Transportation-industry analyst with a management and regulation background.
- Applies industry cost-structure reasoning to ride-hailing economics.
- Treats Uber's valuation as a claim about future profitability and welfare improvement, not only growth.
- Emphasizes audited or GAAP-like profitability over selective contribution metrics.
- Separates customer demand from whether each ride can be delivered profitably.

## Evidence
- Background disclosure: [[can-uber-ever-deliver-part-one-understanding-ubers-bleak-operating-economics-naked-capitalism]] identifies Horan as having 40 years of transportation management and regulatory experience and no stated financial ties to industry participants.
- Profitability critique: [[can-uber-ever-deliver-part-one-understanding-ubers-bleak-operating-economics-naked-capitalism]] reports his argument that Uber's year ending September 2015 showed a roughly $2.0 billion GAAP loss on $1.4 billion revenue.
- Cost-structure critique: [[can-uber-ever-deliver-part-one-understanding-ubers-bleak-operating-economics-naked-capitalism]] presents his claim that urban car service lacks the near-zero marginal-cost scale dynamics of digital platforms.
- Driver-pay interpretation: [[can-uber-ever-deliver-part-one-understanding-ubers-bleak-operating-economics-naked-capitalism]] uses Horan's 2016 counterfactual to attribute margin improvement to lower driver share rather than demonstrated operating leverage.
