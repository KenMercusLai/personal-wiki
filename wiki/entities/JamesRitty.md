## Overview
[[JamesRitty]] is presented as the Dayton saloon owner and inventor whose cash-register work belongs to the ancestry of numeric-key interfaces.

## Current Profile
The source says Ritty invented an early cash register after worrying that employees were stealing from his saloon. His machine was designed to record sales and notify managers, not to act as a calculator, and early register models used preset price keys rather than the later calculator-style 10-key layout.

## Key Characteristics
- Saloon owner in Dayton, Ohio.
- Invented an early cash-register machine after observing a steamboat counting mechanism.
- Built a sales-recording and accountability device rather than a calculator.
- Helps qualify simplistic claims that calculator keypads directly copied cash-register zero-key placement.

## Evidence
- Motivation: [[a-brief-history-of-the-numeric-keypad]] says Ritty suspected employee theft and drew inspiration from a steamboat propeller counter.
- Device purpose: [[a-brief-history-of-the-numeric-keypad]] says the register recorded sales and rang to notify a manager.
- Layout qualification: [[a-brief-history-of-the-numeric-keypad]] says early registers used preset value buttons and did not yet have a separate 0 key or the later vertical digit columns.

## Qualifications
The source uses Ritty mainly to correct over-simple keypad-origin stories. It does not give a full history of the National Cash Register company or later cash-register standardization.
