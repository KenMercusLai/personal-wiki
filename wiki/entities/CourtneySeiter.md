## Overview
[[CourtneySeiter]] is a Buffer author represented in the wiki by practitioner essays on [[RemoteWork]] and recurring employee coaching.

## Current Profile
The sources present Seiter as a Buffer team member explaining workplace systems from lived experience. Her remote-work retrospective argues that distributed work depends on trust, rituals, communication norms, strong tools, intentional social contact, and occasional in-person recalibration. Her earlier feedback essay describes why Buffer preferred regular coaching to annual ratings: employee-led one-on-ones supplied mentorship, optional peer masterminds supported shared reflection, and both emphasized listening and questions over taking control of another person's problem.

## Key Characteristics
- Buffer employee writing from four years of direct remote-work experience.
- Initially uncertain about remote work, then strongly converted by practice.
- Treats remote work as broadly possible but dependent on deliberate cultural design.
- Emphasizes personal routines, communication norms, and trust over office visibility.
- Describes annual reviews as delayed, recall-heavy, and vulnerable to ratings displacing useful conversation.
- Favors employee-led one-on-ones, optional peer reflection, and question-led coaching as complementary development practices.

## Evidence
- Experience base: [[40-lessons-from-4-years-of-remote-work-buffer-stories]] says Seiter was marking her four-year Buffer anniversary and had not previously worked remotely.
- View shift: [[40-lessons-from-4-years-of-remote-work-buffer-stories]] says she began unsure about remote work but later could not imagine working another way.
- Cultural design: [[40-lessons-from-4-years-of-remote-work-buffer-stories]] links remote-work success to trust, rituals, deliberate serendipity, over-communication, and periodic physical presence.
- Visibility critique: [[40-lessons-from-4-years-of-remote-work-buffer-stories]] argues that visibility does not equal productivity and that managers should trust people they hired.
- Feedback model: [[why-we-dont-have-performance-reviews]] describes Seiter's experience of Buffer's at-least-fortnightly one-on-ones and optional peer masterminds.
- Coaching stance: [[why-we-dont-have-performance-reviews]] says Seiter's shared rule for both formats is to listen and ask questions without immediately solving the speaker's problem.

## Qualifications
The page is grounded in two reflective Buffer posts. It does not establish Seiter's full biography, complete job history, or all of her views on distributed work and management. The feedback article is a practitioner account without comparative outcome data and does not explain compensation, promotion, or formal accountability processes.
