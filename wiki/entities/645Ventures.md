## Overview
[[645Ventures]] is the venture-capital firm from which [[NnamdiOkike]] and [[AaronHoliday]] present a seven-part framework for avoiding costly investment blind spots.

## Current Profile
In the source, 645 Ventures describes a product-and-market investment thesis as a prepared-mind discipline while allowing that founder-led and traction-led strategies can also work. The firm's stated emphasis is not on eliminating failed bets but on reducing the chance that cognitive bias, homogeneous judgment, or a static market model causes it to miss an exceptional company in its strike zone.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses product and market focus as a way to define investment competence.
- Treats rare breakout outcomes as more important than minimizing company-level failure rate.
- Identifies seven recurring founder, valuation, customer, consensus, market-size, competition, and behavior-change blind spots.
- Presents diverse perspectives as an operating defense against individual judgment errors.

## Evidence
- Investment frame: [[venture-capital-blind-spots-645-ventures-medium]] describes a defined product-market strategy as one way to improve long-run investment judgment across cycles.
- Error priority: [[venture-capital-blind-spots-645-ventures-medium]] centers mistakes of omission because power-law winners dominate venture returns.
- Decision framework: [[venture-capital-blind-spots-645-ventures-medium]] organizes seven recurring reasons investors miss companies with billion-dollar outcomes.
- Diversity claim: [[venture-capital-blind-spots-645-ventures-medium]] calls a diverse range of perspectives one of the firm's foundational principles for reducing blind spots.

## Qualifications
This profile comes from one firm-authored 2018 strategy essay. It describes 645 Ventures' stated principles rather than independently measured portfolio practice or performance, and the source does not show whether its process reduced omission errors prospectively.
