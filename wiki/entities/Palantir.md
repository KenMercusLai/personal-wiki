## Overview
[[Palantir]] is the data-analysis company examined in William Alden's 2016 investigation of its enterprise model, financial reporting, client outcomes, employee turnover, and secrecy.

## Current Profile
In the source's May 2016 snapshot, Palantir was a privately held, CIA-backed company founded in 2004 and valued at $20 billion after raising more than $2.5 billion. It combined proprietary software with on-site forward deployed engineers serving government and corporate clients. That high-touch model produced rising cash collections and very large commitments, but it also carried high delivery costs, difficult client relationships, uncertain value realization, and a wide gap between booked contracts and cash received.

The company reported $1.7 billion in 2015 bookings and $420 million in cash collections while spending more than $500 million. Its commercial collections had just surpassed federal collections, yet Coca-Cola, American Express, and Nasdaq had left or declined proposed work, and other consumer-goods clients questioned price or value. Internally, departures accelerated while compensation and performance management were reset.

## Key Characteristics
- Hybrid software-and-consulting delivery through proprietary tools and embedded engineers.
- Large, complex enterprise commitments whose booked value did not equal near-term cash.
- A mixed client record spanning durable accounts, valuable operational systems, hesitant pilots, and major departures.
- Strong secrecy practices, including press restrictions and internal customer codenames.
- High private valuation and capital raised alongside continuing losses and employee liquidity pressure.
- A demanding culture whose 2016 turnover prompted broad compensation and review changes.

## Qualifications
This profile is deliberately historical and source-scoped. The investigation relies on internal records, six anonymous current or former employees, selected client statements, and Palantir responses rather than audited financial statements or a representative client-outcome study. Bookings, collections, spending, valuation, headcount, leadership roles, customer relationships, and product performance may all have changed after May 2016. Large commitments such as BP's do not establish collected revenue, while failed or hesitant engagements do not establish that the software lacked value for every client.
