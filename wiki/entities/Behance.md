## Overview
[[Behance]] is a creative-portfolio and professional-network platform used by [[ScottBelsky]] as a historical example of personal utility preceding network and marketplace value.

## Current Profile
In Belsky's first-mile account, Behance's immediate value was the ability to present a portfolio online. That single-user utility gave creative professionals a reason to adopt the product before its networking and marketplace components could become useful at scale. The early team also learned that original terminology such as "Realms" for creative fields and "Circles" for groups imposed unnecessary learning costs, leading Belsky to favor familiar product language except where a novel behavior creates distinctive value.

## Key Characteristics
- Offered portfolio publishing as immediate personal utility.
- Developed broader network and marketplace value around creative professionals.
- Serves as Belsky's example of utility preceding network effects.
- Exposed the onboarding cost of unfamiliar category and group terminology.

## Evidence
- Utility before network value: [[crafting-the-first-mile-of-product-positive-slope-medium]] says online portfolio presentation was Behance's immediate early benefit and a prerequisite for later networking and marketplace use.
- Terminology lesson: [[crafting-the-first-mile-of-product-positive-slope-medium]] reports that "Realms" and "Circles" made an already-new product harder to understand.
- Product-design interpretation: [[crafting-the-first-mile-of-product-positive-slope-medium]] uses Behance to support familiar language and patterns unless retraining is necessary for unique value.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one founder-authored 2016 practitioner essay, not a complete company history, independent product evaluation, or measured onboarding study. It describes selected early-product lessons and does not establish how much portfolio utility or terminology changes caused adoption, retention, network growth, or marketplace outcomes.
