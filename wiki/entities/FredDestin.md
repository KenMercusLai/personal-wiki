## Overview
[[FredDestin]] is represented in the wiki as the investor who wrote Zoopla's 2007 seed check from [[AtlasVenture]] and later reflected on the company through its IPO and announced acquisition.

## Current Profile
In the available source, Destin favors company value over percentage ownership, concentrated follow-on investment when conviction in a team remains high, and management systems built around focus, core technology ownership, fast integration, and a small set of stable KPIs. His account is explicitly retrospective and shaped by an unusually successful outcome.

## Key Characteristics
- Evaluates ownership dilution by the strategic and total-value gains it enables.
- Treats exceptional management quality as a reason to increase investor exposure despite adverse external pricing conditions.
- Looks beyond headline market size to recurrence, churn, concentration, and margin quality.
- Draws operating lessons from board work as well as financing decisions.

## Evidence
- Strategic ownership: [[fred-destin-10-years-to-3bn-ten-things-i-learned-from-zoopla]] credits equity alignment with estate agents and a later merger as important parts of Zoopla's competitive strategy.
- Follow-on conviction: [[fred-destin-10-years-to-3bn-ten-things-i-learned-from-zoopla]] says Atlas Venture funded the Series B alone after a lead investor withdrew during the 2008 market crash, before Octopus Ventures completed the round.
- Business-quality lens: [[fred-destin-10-years-to-3bn-ten-things-i-learned-from-zoopla]] emphasizes recurring revenue, low churn, low concentration, and high net margins over a large headline category estimate.
- Operating perspective: [[fred-destin-10-years-to-3bn-ten-things-i-learned-from-zoopla]] describes board-level KPI design, acquisition integration, technology-stack ownership, and leadership focus.
