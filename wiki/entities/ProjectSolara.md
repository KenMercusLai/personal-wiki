## Overview
[[ProjectSolara]] is [[Microsoft]]'s announced chip-to-cloud vision for an ecosystem of agent-first devices that serve as context-dependent portals to cloud-resident agents.

## Current Profile
The source presents Solara as an enterprise-oriented alternative to smartphone-centered computing. Devices can sit with a user, travel with them, or perceive what the user authorizes; speech and proximity initiate work, relevant information comes forward, and an agent can continue after direct interaction ends. The strategic center is therefore the cloud agent and its work context rather than any single device. This remains a presentation and analyst interpretation, not evidence of shipped hardware, adoption, reliability, security, or economics.

## Key Characteristics
- Described as a chip-to-cloud platform for agent-first devices.
- Treats devices as coordinated portals rather than self-contained computing centers.
- Uses brief interaction to initiate longer-running cloud work.
- Targets enterprise settings where institutional context can bound tasks and productivity can justify spending.

## Evidence
- Platform framing: [[the-iphones-last-stand]] reports Microsoft's description of a new chip-to-cloud platform and device ecosystem for an agent-first world.
- Interaction model: [[the-iphones-last-stand]] and its captions describe proximity, speech, selective perception, relevant-information filtering, and continued execution.
- Market position: [[the-iphones-last-stand]] interprets Solara as an enterprise play aligned with Microsoft's cloud infrastructure and absence from mobile platform ownership.
- Strategic contrast: [[the-iphones-last-stand]] opposes Solara's cloud-resident agent center to Apple's iPhone-centered personal-context strategy.

## Qualifications
Solara is presented as a vision at a developer conference and is explicitly described by Thompson as vaporware. The source supplies no shipping plan, hardware specification, workload benchmark, pricing, security model, customer deployment, or evidence that a multi-device ecosystem is preferable to phones and PCs. Enterprise context can make tasks more tractable while also increasing confidentiality, authorization, audit, integration, and failure costs.

## What Changed
- Created the entity as the cloud-first enterprise-device pole in Thompson's Apple–Microsoft comparison.

## Relationships
- [[Microsoft]] - announced the platform and supplies the enterprise and cloud context behind it.
- [[AgentNativeComputing]] - Solara makes the agent, rather than a standalone device or app, the primary computing center.
- [[Apple]] - pursues the source's contrasting device-centered consumer strategy.
- [[IPhone]] - central device Solara's portal model conceptually deemphasizes.
- [[Siri]] - Apple's context-rich but less autonomous counterpart in the comparison.
