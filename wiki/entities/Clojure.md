## Overview
[[Clojure]] is the Lisp-family programming language [[Appcanary]] initially chose and later left in [[PhillipMendoncaVieira]]'s startup tooling retrospective.

## Current Profile
In this wiki, Clojure currently appears through a sympathetic but frustrated adopter's account. The source presents Clojure as conceptually attractive and associated with [[SimpleMadeEasy]], but hard for Appcanary to sustain under startup pressure because the tooling and ecosystem felt unfriendly to new users who lacked deep idiom, runtime, and debugging context.

## Key Characteristics
- Clojure is treated as a different programming paradigm that can require substantial learning effort.
- Its community is described as personally kind but sensitive to external criticism.
- Its ecosystem is criticized as user-hostile from the author's specific adoption context.
- The language is associated with a strong distinction between conceptual simplicity and ease.
- The source suggests its business case can be unclear when teams face JVM overhead and many alternative JVM options.

## Evidence
- Paradigm shift: [[appcanary-simple-aint-easy-but-hard-aint-simple-leaving-clojure-for-ruby]] summarizes Clojure discussion as noting that Lisps are different and work differently.
- Ecosystem critique: [[appcanary-simple-aint-easy-but-hard-aint-simple-leaving-clojure-for-ruby]] says Clojure tools assumed high tolerance for digging through concepts, idioms, runtime quirks, and failure modes.
- Community sensitivity: [[appcanary-simple-aint-easy-but-hard-aint-simple-leaving-clojure-for-ruby]] says some Clojure community members reacted strongly to the RubyConf talk.
- Simplicity frame: [[appcanary-simple-aint-easy-but-hard-aint-simple-leaving-clojure-for-ruby]] uses Rich Hickey's Simple Made Easy talk as the core conceptual backdrop.

## Qualifications
This page does not establish a general verdict on Clojure. The source is one team's story, includes acknowledged mistakes and hyperbole, and says the choice was context-specific rather than proof that Clojure is bad for all teams.
