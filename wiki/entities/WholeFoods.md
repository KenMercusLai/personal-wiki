## Overview
[[WholeFoods]] is the grocery retailer that Amazon agreed to acquire for $13.7 billion in 2017, interpreted by [[BenThompson]] as the anchor customer for a possible grocery-services platform.

## Current Profile
The source contrasts Whole Foods' integrated store supply chain with Amazon's service-oriented model. Thompson argues that the acquisition could give [[Amazon]] immediate local grocery volume, physical demand, store-brand inventory, and operating knowledge, allowing it to modularize procurement and fulfillment around categories such as meat, produce, bakery goods, and non-perishables. In that interpretation, Whole Foods remains a retailer but also becomes the [[FirstAndBestCustomer]] whose demand helps justify high fixed costs before Amazon serves delivery, other stores, or restaurants.

## Key Characteristics
- Operated physical grocery stores with an integrated shelf-stocking supply chain.
- Had sufficient grocery demand to serve as a scale anchor for Amazon's local infrastructure.
- Brought perishables, store brands, suppliers, and operating knowledge into Amazon's consumer system.
- Is treated in the source as both an acquired retailer and an internal customer of a prospective service platform.

## Evidence
- Acquisition: [[amazons-new-customer-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] states that Amazon agreed to buy Whole Foods for $13.7 billion.
- Anchor-customer role: [[amazons-new-customer-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] argues that Whole Foods could immediately bring Amazon's grocery efforts to meaningful local scale.
- Supply architecture: [[amazons-new-customer-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] predicts that Amazon would decompose Whole Foods' integrated supply chain into reusable grocery primitives.
- Visual model: [[amazons-new-customer-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] includes an inspected diagram placing Whole Foods Market beside delivery and restaurants as customers above Amazon Grocery Services.

## Qualifications
The profile is grounded in one 2017 strategy essay written when the acquisition was announced. It records Thompson's forecast, not verified later integration, economics, supplier outcomes, store performance, labor effects, or restaurant-service expansion.

## What Changed
- Established Whole Foods as a distinct entity and as the anchor-customer case in Amazon's proposed grocery-services architecture.
