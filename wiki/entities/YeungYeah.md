## Overview
[[YeungYeah]] is the author of YeungYeah's Context, represented here through a first-person essay about what remains worth writing when AI can answer technical questions, summarize documents, and edit prose.

## Current Profile
The source presents YeungYeah as a long-time note-taker and blogger whose earlier posts often documented technical problem-solving. He now questions the value of shallow technical explanations and excerpt-heavy archives, while retaining personal frameworks, lived process, opinions, and feelings as worthwhile writing. His own article began as voice input, received model-mediated transcription cleanup, and was polished again with AI; he accepts the workflow provisionally because the ideas and judgments remain his, while acknowledging uncertainty about how much personal voice survives.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats writing and hands-on practice as ways to test whether learning has become usable understanding.
- Distinguishes personally synthesized notes from excerpt collection and automated accumulation.
- Values outlines and indexes as human orientation layers over AI-accessible detail.
- Treats process narrative, lived experience, judgment, and emotion as durable reasons to write.
- Uses AI transcription and revision pragmatically while remaining alert to voice dilution.
