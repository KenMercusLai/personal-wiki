## Overview
[[NoahJessop]] is represented in the wiki as a founder-author turning an enterprise-sales qualification failure into practical rules for introductions, meetings, follow-up, and closing.

## Current Profile
Jessop's source presents him as a technically oriented founder who learned that product knowledge and an enthusiastic feature presentation do not substitute for sales process. A newly hired head of sales exposed the gap by asking whether a promising prospect could buy within the next two quarters; the answer, three to four quarters, was potentially later than the company's runway. Jessop generalizes that experience into a compact operating guide centered on reducing coordination friction, creating trust through responsiveness, directing meetings toward explicit next steps, and asking directly for commitment.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes from first-person startup operating and enterprise-sales experience.
- Treats qualification timing as a survival question rather than administrative pipeline hygiene.
- Favors concise, specific, low-friction professional communication.
- Emphasizes explicit meeting purpose, next steps, prompt follow-through, and direct closing questions.
- Combines product substance with vision, persistence, and relationship care.

## Evidence
- Qualification lesson: [[common-rules-for-uncommon-people-by]] recounts a prospect saying implementation was three to four quarters away when the company might not survive that long.
- Communication method: [[common-rules-for-uncommon-people-by]] recommends double opt-in introductions, forwardable context, specific proposed times, simple words, short messages, and bounded questions.
- Meeting control: [[common-rules-for-uncommon-people-by]] says founders should set context, identify the best next step, prepare useful materials, and end once progress is secured.
- Pipeline discipline: [[common-rules-for-uncommon-people-by]] recommends immediate promised follow-up, booking the next meeting promptly, persistence, phone calls where useful, and directly asking for the sale.
- Persuasion and relationship: [[common-rules-for-uncommon-people-by]] argues that credible future possibility complements product substance and that customer risk should be acknowledged after the sale.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one first-person advice essay rather than an independent biography or evaluation of Jessop's companies and outcomes. The rules are practitioner heuristics, and the essay offers no comparative conversion data; its more unconventional persistence tactics require judgment about consent, physical context, power, and the risk of becoming intrusive.
