## Overview
[[DavidSpinks]] is an entrepreneur and community-building practitioner represented here through his account of founding [[Feast]] and [[CMX]].

## Current Profile
Spinks describes moving from New York to San Francisco, consulting on company communities, and then founding Feast with Nadia Eghbal. Feast's failed accelerator-era fundraising and later sale shaped his next company-building approach: CMX began as a conference for community professionals, funded itself through ticket sales, and expanded without outside capital. His position is pragmatic rather than anti-investor—capital should serve a chosen business model and outcome rather than define them by convention.

## Key Characteristics
- Builds products and institutions around professional communities.
- Learned from both unsuccessful fundraising and customer-funded growth.
- Defines company success through durability, useful impact, profit, meaning, and work balance rather than valuation alone.
- Treats financing as a strategic design choice with consequences for pace, control, and acceptable exits.
- Acknowledges the workload, salary, and personal-capital risks of bootstrapping.

## Evidence
- Founder path: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] traces Spinks from community consulting through Feast and CMX.
- Fundraising lesson: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] says Feast spoke with more than 70 investors and began building to fundraise rather than building for customers.
- Customer-funded case: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] says CMX was cash-flow positive from its first ticket sales and remained bootstrapped two years later.
- Success definition: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] prioritizes a lasting, useful, profitable company whose team finds meaning and balance.
- Cost acknowledgment: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] reports two years without salary and substantial personal financial exposure.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one first-person essay published in 2016. Its revenue projection, team size, ownership, products, and company status are historical self-reports rather than current or independently verified facts, and the source does not provide enough financial detail to compare Feast and CMX on equivalent terms.

## What Changed

- Preserved his conditional openness to future investment rather than labeling him categorically anti-VC.
