## Overview
[[BookingCom]] is a hotel-booking platform used in the source as an example of paid search as high-scale user acquisition.

## Current Profile
The source treats Booking.com as a counterpoint to more clever-sounding growth hacks: large paid-search budgets can still drive growth when the company uses an expensive first booking to acquire a user who may later return through cheaper direct channels.

## Key Characteristics
- High-revenue hotel-booking platform.
- Uses large-scale paid search for acquisition.
- Treats first paid acquisition as a route to future direct bookings and loyalty.

## Evidence
- Ad spend: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] cites Priceline's 2013 digital advertising spend of $1.8 billion as context for Booking.com growth.
- Repeat-channel logic: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says buying a user on an expensive keyword can be rational if the user returns directly later.

## Qualifications
The source does not calculate Booking.com's customer lifetime value, brand effects, supplier inventory, conversion rate, or profitability by keyword.
