## Overview
[[HomebrewComputerClub]] appears as a 1970s informal interaction setting where [[SteveJobs]] and [[SteveWozniak]] showed early Apple computers.

## Current Profile
Barnett uses the club as an example of "random access" interaction that may have shaped Jobs' later preference for workplace designs that increase chance encounters. The source treats the club less as a full institution than as a memory of informal technical exchange before highly scheduled meeting culture.

## Key Characteristics
- 1970s computing club associated with early Apple demonstrations.
- Represents informal, open-ended technical exchange.
- Serves as background for Jobs' later interest in random interactions.

## Evidence
- Random-access period: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] says Jobs remembered the informal random-access period at Homebrew Computer Club.
- Apple demonstration: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] says Jobs and Wozniak showed off their first Apple computers there.
- Campus-design link: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] connects that memory to Jobs' Apple campus vision for increasing random informal interactions.

## Qualifications
