## Overview
[[HoldenKarnofsky]] is represented in this wiki as the author of the Cold Takes article "Learning By Writing," where he describes a writing-centered method for forming detailed views on difficult topics.

## Current Profile
Karnofsky contributes a research-method perspective rather than a general biography. In this source, he presents learning as an iterative investigation loop: choose an important-if-true claim, read or discuss enough to start, write and defend a premature hypothesis, list weaknesses, research the most important subquestion, revise or switch sides, and keep repeating with outside feedback once self-critique slows down. His profile here is that of a writer-investigator who uses explicit provisional claims to direct reading, retain reasoning, and expose what would change his mind.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses writing as the organizing surface for learning and research.
- Treats premature hypotheses as tools for finding the next decision-relevant question.
- Emphasizes adversarial self-review, side switching, and outside feedback as safeguards against premature certainty.
- Acknowledges the method's cognitive and emotional cost, including uncertainty about project duration and repeated exposure of confusion.

## Evidence
- Writing-centered learning: [[blog-holden-karnofsky-cold-takes-learning-by-writing]] says reading is always in service of writing rather than the other way around.
- Provisional hypotheses: [[blog-holden-karnofsky-cold-takes-learning-by-writing]] describes forming and defending a hypothesis before being fully qualified so that the investigation has a target.
- Revision discipline: [[blog-holden-karnofsky-cold-takes-learning-by-writing]] repeatedly uses weakness lists, focused subquestions, draft feedback, and side-switching to change the view.
- Cost of the method: [[blog-holden-karnofsky-cold-takes-learning-by-writing]] stresses that the process is taxing, interruptive, and less pleasant than leisure reading.
