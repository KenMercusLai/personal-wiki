## Overview
[[BillBarnett]] appears as the author of a short organizational critique of scheduled meetings and committee-driven work.

## Current Profile
The source presents Barnett as an academic observer of organizational life who connects everyday calendar overload with sociological theory. His argument is practical rather than anti-collaboration: delete default meetings, protect work time, and prefer informal conversations that continue only while they have value.

## Key Characteristics
- Critiques scheduled meetings as overused organizational procedure.
- Uses [[MaxWeber]]'s [[ProceduralRationality]] to explain why meetings can seem legitimate without producing useful outcomes.
- Values spontaneous conversation as a source of productive ideas.
- Frames calendar design as an attention and work-quality problem.

## Evidence
- Meeting critique: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] says formal meetings often fill whole schedules in both Silicon Valley firms and academia.
- Sociological frame: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] applies Weber's procedural-versus-substantive rationality distinction to meetings.
- Informal alternative: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] credits a spontaneous conversation with [[BillDurham]] for an idea that later led to papers and a book.
- Practical advice: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] recommends deleting calendar meetings, scheduling work time, and meeting informally only while useful.

## Qualifications
The source is a brief opinion essay. It does not test meeting deletion empirically, distinguish meeting types in detail, or address coordination-heavy work that may require scheduled structure.
