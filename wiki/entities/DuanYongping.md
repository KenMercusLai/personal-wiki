## Overview
[[DuanYongping]] is presented in the source as a value-investing reference whose principles emphasize business quality, cash flow, temperament, and avoiding destructive investment behaviors.

## Current Profile
The article uses Duan Yongping less as a biography than as an investing discipline model. His quoted framework distinguishes investing from speculation by whether the investor cares about the asset's own cash flow, highlights business model and culture as company-selection criteria, and gives sell rules such as recognizing a mistaken purchase, finding a better opportunity, or seeing absurd overvaluation. The inspected slides also present his "stop doing list" as a behavioral risk-control system: avoid shorting, margin, unfamiliar assets, shortcuts, frequent trading, short-term speculation, and heavily indebted companies.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames stocks as ownership of companies and future cash flows.
- Emphasizes business model and culture as durable company-quality tests.
- Treats temperament as central because market prices are unstable signals.
- Uses negative rules to prevent avoidable investing failure.

## Evidence
- Cash-flow frame: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] quotes Duan Yongping to distinguish investing from speculation through attention to asset cash flow.
- Company quality: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] says business model determines whether a company can make money and culture determines whether it can do so durably and correctly.
- Temperament: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] invokes the "Mr. Market" idea that investors should use volatile prices rather than be controlled by them.
- Negative rules: [[gong-cheng-shi-ru-he-geng-hao-tou-zi]] lists avoiding shorting, leverage, unfamiliar domains, frequent trading, and short-term speculation as protective constraints.

## Qualifications
The source is a secondary slide summary, not a complete primary archive of Duan Yongping's investing record or philosophy. The investment examples and failure cases are included as the source presents them and should be treated as source-scoped.
