## Overview
[[Salesforce]] appears both as an early hosted-CRM company that joined customer acquisition to product co-development and as a later context where [[BillMacaitis]] built custom [[MarketingAttribution]] capability before the attribution-vendor market matured.

## Current Profile
[[MarcBenioff]]'s account presents early Salesforce as a small team overcoming distrust of hosted CRM through personal-network prospecting, pioneer customers, free pilots, rapid deployment, and design-partner feedback. [[BlueMartiniSoftware]] reportedly became the first customer in August 1999 and moved from spreadsheets to a service running within two weeks. Frequent contact, an in-product idea button, “bugforce” request tracking, rapid shipping, and observation of use linked customer learning to engineering; a dedicated salesperson arrived only after the product had measurable value and free pilots began converting to monthly payment. Years later, Macaitis's account uses Salesforce as an attribution build-versus-buy case: custom event-tracking needs and a thin vendor market led him to build internally.

## Key Characteristics
- Early hosted-CRM company that had to overcome customer security and trust concerns.
- Used pioneer customers as design partners and routed their feedback into rapid product changes.
- Paired free pilots and flexible monthly payment with later paid conversion.
- Delayed a formal sales hire until the service had demonstrated measurable customer value.
- Later SaaS context where custom event-tracking needs pushed toward building attribution internally.

## Evidence
- Early acquisition loop: [[marc-benioff-win-customers-by-treating-them-like-partners]] says Salesforce targeted pioneers, recruited customers through team networks, and used five free pilots as design partners.
- First-customer value: [[marc-benioff-win-customers-by-treating-them-like-partners]] reports that Blue Martini adopted the service within two weeks without the cost and implementation burden of traditional enterprise software.
- Feedback operations: [[marc-benioff-win-customers-by-treating-them-like-partners]] describes frequent contact, an idea button, request-frequency tracking in “bugforce,” rapid engineering response, follow-up, and usage observation.
- Free-to-paid transition: [[marc-benioff-win-customers-by-treating-them-like-partners]] says prior product experience, monthly billing, plan flexibility, and penalty-free exit reduced conversion risk.
- Custom build: [[attribution-marketing-creating-a-growth-engine-at-salesforce-zendesk-and-slack-for-entrepreneurs]] says Macaitis built his own system at Salesforce because of custom event-tracking needs and a thinner vendor market.
- Growth sequence: [[attribution-marketing-creating-a-growth-engine-at-salesforce-zendesk-and-slack-for-entrepreneurs]] places Salesforce alongside Zendesk and Slack as companies where attribution-supported marketing became a competitive advantage.

## Qualifications
The evidence consists of favorable practitioner retrospectives rather than independent company history or causal analysis. Benioff does not provide conversion denominators, retention cohorts, rejected requests, security outcomes, or a comparison with customers outside the design-partner program; Macaitis's account does not document system cost or comparative vendor performance. The page therefore covers two bounded operating cases, not Salesforce's broader CRM, platform, financial, or corporate history.

## What Changed
- Added early hosted-CRM trust, design-partner feedback, rapid deployment, and free-to-paid conversion as a second operating case.
- Reframed Salesforce from attribution-only context into a bounded early-customer and later measurement profile.
