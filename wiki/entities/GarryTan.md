## Overview
[[GarryTan]] appears in this wiki as the author of the TechCrunch article on [[CoFounderConflict]], drawing from his own [[Posterous]] experience and later startup advising.

## Current Profile
The source presents Tan as a founder-investor reflecting on a painful co-founder breakdown rather than as a general biography. He says he resigned from his last startup after disagreement over the company's future became physically and mentally unsustainable. He then uses that experience, plus work advising hundreds of startups and time as a [[YCombinator]] partner, to argue that co-founder relationships need active maintenance, fair conflict, decision boundaries, and outside help when needed.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses first-person founder experience to explain co-founder relationship failure.
- Treats conflict style as a startup operating risk.
- Connects prior friendship, current relationship maintenance, and company direction.
- Brings advisor and investor pattern recognition to the founder-conflict problem.
- Recommends executive coaching and trusted external support when conflict threatens the company.

## Evidence
- Author and date: [[co-founder-conflict-techcrunch]] is Garry Tan's February 2017 TechCrunch article.
- Founder experience: [[co-founder-conflict-techcrunch]] says Tan resigned from his last startup after he could no longer agree with his co-founder over the future of the company.
- Posterous context: [[co-founder-conflict-techcrunch]] says Tan co-founded Posterous in 2008 and that the company grew quickly before growth flatlined by the end of 2010.
- Advisor context: [[co-founder-conflict-techcrunch]] says Tan had advised hundreds of startups and repeatedly saw founders struggle with too little or too much conflict.
- YC context: [[co-founder-conflict-techcrunch]] says that during his time as a Y Combinator partner, co-founder relationship depth mattered beyond functional business/technical pairing.
