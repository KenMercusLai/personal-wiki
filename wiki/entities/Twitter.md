## Overview
[[Twitter]] is a social platform used as an example of high-profile status signals and public metrics creating attention.

## Current Profile
One source argues that verified accounts helped Twitter maintain goodwill among celebrities, politicians, and media figures. The virality source adds an earlier metric-driven loop: public follower counts made status competition legible, as in the 2009 Ashton Kutcher versus CNN race to one million followers, and that public contest generated publicity and new-user onboarding into relevant feeds.

## Key Characteristics
- Social platform shaped by public conversation and influential accounts.
- Uses verified accounts as status and identity signals.
- Benefits from celebrity, political, and media participation.
- Uses public follower counts as a vanity and competition surface.

## Evidence
- Verified accounts: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says Twitter's blue-tick system helped center the community around high-profile users.
- Influencer goodwill: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says Twitter maintained influencer goodwill better than other networks in the author's view.
- Vanity loop: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] describes the Ashton Kutcher and CNN race to one million followers as free publicity caused by public follower counts.
- Feed onboarding: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] says users who signed up during the contest could immediately follow interesting accounts, making the product more likely to stick.

## Qualifications
The sources predate later changes in Twitter's ownership, verification policies, platform name, and social-media competition. Their claims should be read as historical interpretations of earlier Twitter growth dynamics.
