## Overview
[[Grubhub]] is an online food-delivery company represented here through [[CaseyWinters]]'s retrospective examples of finding [[FeatureProductFit]] for its early mobile app.

## Current Profile
The source portrays Grubhub's mobile development as a combination of behavioral diagnosis, user research, incentives, and high-touch support. The team reportedly disabled inaccurate current-location behavior, enriched restaurant search results when mobile users lacked browser tabs for comparison, offered $10 off a first mobile order after observing higher mobile lifetime value, and monitored social media so customer service could intervene quickly.

## Key Characteristics
- Used conversion data to identify a location-accuracy problem in the mobile app.
- Added decision information such as cuisine, delivery time, minimum order, rating, and rating count to search results.
- Used a first-order discount to move web users into mobile ordering.
- Treated persistent lifetime value after the incentive as more meaningful than redemption alone.
- Used social-media monitoring and customer service as an adoption and recovery mechanism.

## Evidence
- Behavioral diagnosis: [[feature-product-fit-casey-accidental]] says current-location users converted worse than address-entry users and attributes the gap to accuracy.
- Search decision support: [[feature-product-fit-casey-accidental]] says richer restaurant-result information improved conversion and retention despite making the page look denser.
- Incentive economics: [[feature-product-fit-casey-accidental]] reports that mobile users had twice the lifetime value of web users and that the difference persisted after a $10 first-order incentive.
- Support intervention: [[feature-product-fit-casey-accidental]] describes monitoring social media and having customer service respond quickly to mobile-app problems.

## Qualifications
All claims come from one former operator's retrospective without dates for each experiment, sample sizes, absolute conversion or retention figures, test designs, cost accounting, or independent verification. “First food delivery app” is the author's claim and is not established comparatively in the source.
