## Overview
[[SteveJobs]] appears as an Apple founder, poetic-cultural influence, ousted executive, Pixar acquirer, returning leader associated with Apple's origin story and "Think Different" era, a campus-design example for increasing informal interaction, and a leader whose design impact and motivational force coexist with serious claims of harmful treatment.

## Current Profile
The advertising article uses Jobs less as a full biography than as part of Apple's advertising mythology. His parents' garage and the early workshop image help establish humble origins with [[SteveWozniak]]. His interest in poets and Bob Dylan supports the article's claim that Apple drew strength from cultural association, not only product specification. The 1988 Pixar collaboration shows his post-Apple influence still touching Macintosh advertising, while his 1997 return coincides with Apple's renewed brand attack on IBM and the "Think Different" campaign. Barnett's meeting article adds a workplace-design angle: Jobs's circular Apple campus vision is presented as an attempt to increase random informal interactions, possibly shaped by memories of the [[HomebrewComputerClub]].

The Apple ATG perspective is more ambivalent. [[JohnLilly]] credits Jobs with making beautiful, human-centered technology culturally desirable, enabling creative work, and proving design's economic value. Lilly remembers Jobs's 1997 return as emotionally galvanizing: a defiant employee talk joined turnaround ambition to repriced options and a renewed vesting commitment, while a rapid firing of four alleged leakers signaled operational control. The same essay refuses to convert achievement into moral absolution. Lilly describes Jobs as capable of dehumanizing and personally destructive behavior, credits the teams behind Apple's results, and argues that the transferable lesson is purposeful individual contribution rather than imitation of Jobs's personality or methods.

## Key Characteristics
- Co-founder figure in Apple's humble-origin and cultural-identity narratives.
- Credited with making human-centered technology desirable and design economically consequential.
- Ousted from Apple before returning as a forceful turnaround leader and operator.
- Remembered as an exceptional motivator whose intensity could inspire commitment.
- Also described as belittling and personally destructive toward people around him.
- Connected to Pixar after acquiring Lucasfilm's struggling animation division.
- Associated with designing workplace space to encourage chance informal encounters.

## Evidence
- Origin story: [[40-lessons-from-40-years-of-apple-ads-mission-org-medium]] says Apple's first office was Jobs' parents' garage and shows Jobs and Wozniak in an early hands-on workspace.
- Cultural influence: [[40-lessons-from-40-years-of-apple-ads-mission-org-medium]] says Jobs was obsessed with poets and that he and Wozniak drew inspiration from Bob Dylan.
- Pixar link: [[40-lessons-from-40-years-of-apple-ads-mission-org-medium]] says Jobs acquired Lucasfilm's struggling animation division after leaving Apple and that Pixar later collaborated on a Macintosh II commercial.
- Return: [[40-lessons-from-40-years-of-apple-ads-mission-org-medium]] ties Jobs' 1997 return to the ramp-up of Apple's campaign against IBM and the "Think Different" campaign.
- Informal interaction design: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] says Jobs envisioned Apple's new campus as a large circle to increase random interactions in the center.
- Homebrew memory: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] connects Jobs' campus thinking to the informal random-access period at the [[HomebrewComputerClub]], where he and [[SteveWozniak]] showed early Apple computers.
- Design impact: [[johns-tumblr-steve-jobs]] credits Jobs with beautiful hardware and software, tools for creative work, and validation of design's economic and cultural value.
- Turnaround motivation: [[johns-tumblr-steve-jobs]] recalls a 1997 employee talk that paired defiance about Apple's future with repriced options, renewed vesting, and a demand for commitment.
- Operating control: [[johns-tumblr-steve-jobs]] recalls four alleged leakers being terminated shortly after Jobs warned employees that greater internal communication required confidentiality.
- Human cost: [[johns-tumblr-steve-jobs]] says Jobs could be dehumanizing and belittling and that friends in his line of fire experienced personally destructive effects.
- Legacy boundary: [[johns-tumblr-steve-jobs]] credits Jobs's teams and rejects imitation or beatification in favor of finding one's own contribution.

## Qualifications
The sources remain narrow and partly retrospective. The advertising survey uses Jobs primarily as a brand-history figure, Barnett uses him as an anecdotal workplace-design example, and Lilly writes a personal remembrance rather than a documented management history. Lilly's speech and leak accounts were written years later, include uncertainty about the stock price, and provide no company records or dismissed-employee perspectives. His claims of harmful conduct are morally important but partly secondhand. The profile therefore supports a dual judgment about design influence and human cost without quantifying either or treating strong results as proof that the methods were necessary.

## What Changed
- Added Lilly's first-person account of Jobs's 1997 return, motivational force, and confidentiality crackdown.
- Added human-centered design and creative enablement as central parts of Jobs's reported legacy.
- Made the conflict between exceptional achievement and dehumanizing treatment explicit.
- Qualified hero-centered attribution by crediting teams and rejecting imitation as the lesson of Jobs's life.
