## Overview
[[SteveJobs]] appears as an Apple founder, poetic-cultural influence, ousted executive, Pixar acquirer, returning leader associated with Apple's origin story and "Think Different" era, and a campus-design example for increasing informal interaction.

## Current Profile
The advertising article uses Jobs less as a full biography than as part of Apple's advertising mythology. His parents' garage and the early workshop image help establish humble origins with [[SteveWozniak]]. His interest in poets and Bob Dylan supports the article's claim that Apple drew strength from cultural association, not only product specification. The 1988 Pixar collaboration shows his post-Apple influence still touching Macintosh advertising, while his 1997 return coincides with Apple's renewed brand attack on IBM and the "Think Different" campaign. Barnett's meeting article adds a workplace-design angle: Jobs' circular Apple campus vision is presented as an attempt to increase random informal interactions, possibly shaped by memories of the [[HomebrewComputerClub]].

## Key Characteristics
- Co-founder figure in Apple's humble-origin narrative.
- Associated with poetic and cultural influences behind Apple's public identity.
- Ousted from Apple before later returning as a central brand-era leader.
- Connected to Pixar after acquiring Lucasfilm's struggling animation division.
- Framed as part of Apple's renewed "Think Different" advertising turn.
- Associated with designing workplace space to encourage chance informal encounters.

## Evidence
- Origin story: [[40-lessons-from-40-years-of-apple-ads-mission-org-medium]] says Apple's first office was Jobs' parents' garage and shows Jobs and Wozniak in an early hands-on workspace.
- Cultural influence: [[40-lessons-from-40-years-of-apple-ads-mission-org-medium]] says Jobs was obsessed with poets and that he and Wozniak drew inspiration from Bob Dylan.
- Pixar link: [[40-lessons-from-40-years-of-apple-ads-mission-org-medium]] says Jobs acquired Lucasfilm's struggling animation division after leaving Apple and that Pixar later collaborated on a Macintosh II commercial.
- Return: [[40-lessons-from-40-years-of-apple-ads-mission-org-medium]] ties Jobs' 1997 return to the ramp-up of Apple's campaign against IBM and the "Think Different" campaign.
- Informal interaction design: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] says Jobs envisioned Apple's new campus as a large circle to increase random interactions in the center.
- Homebrew memory: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] connects Jobs' campus thinking to the informal random-access period at the [[HomebrewComputerClub]], where he and [[SteveWozniak]] showed early Apple computers.

## Qualifications
The sources are narrow: the advertising survey uses Jobs primarily as a brand-history figure, while Barnett's essay uses him as an anecdotal workplace-design example. They omit most of Jobs' management record, product decisions, conflicts, and later Apple strategy.
