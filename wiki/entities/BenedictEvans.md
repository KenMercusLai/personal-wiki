## Overview
[[BenedictEvans]] is a technology strategy analyst represented in the wiki through sources on the mobile ecosystem transition, Apple's services-era brand positioning, second-order consequences of electric and autonomous vehicles, the feature-phone-like interface transition in car software, and the 2017 voice-assistant platform boom.

## Current Profile
The mobile source presents Evans as a technology analyst synthesizing the smartphone transition after the first major phase of the iPhone-era platform war. His focus is not on one device feature, but on how scale, operating systems, apps, discovery, messaging, sensors, cars, TVs, watches, and ordinary user behavior reorganize the technology industry around mobile. The Apple services source extends that style into brand strategy: Evans reads Apple's 2019 News+, Arcade, Card, and TV+ event as an incremental services and churn-reduction move, but more importantly as an effort to turn privacy, curation, safety, and protection from scams into a consumer-facing Apple promise. The transport consequences source shows the same systems habit applied to EVs and autonomy: instead of asking only whether they replace today's cars, Evans asks how they cascade into maintenance, fuel retail, taxation, energy grids, accidents, parking, public transport, labor, urban form, and surveillance. The car-interface source adds a more specific product-strategy pattern: old interface models can collapse when electronics and firmware accrete feature by feature, forcing a platform and UX inversion similar to the phone industry's shift from feature phones to smartphones.

The voice essay applies that method to a boom rather than a device. Evans first establishes why the moment was real, citing speech recognition and intent parsing that finally worked, a smartphone supply chain that made microphones and processors cheap, GAFA-scale money and talent, and platform anxiety at companies that do not control mobile distribution. He then redefines the question by asking what "works" means, and separates a working input layer from the missing execution layer: sound can be transcribed and routed, but the dialogue boxes behind it must be hand-built. His resulting claims, that coverage cannot be hand-built for arbitrary requests without general AI, that command discoverability follows a U-shaped curve, that platforms escape partly by answering the head of the query distribution, and that most companies therefore need a narrow and predictable domain, are the same move as his car and mobile essays: accept the enabling conditions, then locate the structural limit the enthusiasm ignores. The essay is also the wiki's clearest statement of his analogy-driven style, borrowing the computer-animation uncanny valley, feature-phone and carrier-deck comparisons, and the IVR-versus-HAL 9000 image to describe a product-experience failure mode.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames mobile as an ecosystem-scale shift rather than a screen-size category.
- Uses platform strategy to connect hardware, operating systems, software, discovery, and distribution.
- Treats Apple, Google, Facebook, and Microsoft as actors whose strategies must be read through mobile's new center of gravity.
- Pays attention to ordinary user behavior, not only technology-industry enthusiasm.
- Reads services strategy through brand promise, trust, curation, and ecosystem retention rather than revenue alone.
- Uses cross-domain analogies, from feature phones to second-order consequences, to connect a technology shift with adjacent industries, public policy, cities, and interface resets.
- Separates the enabling conditions of a technology boom from the product it still lacks, then asks which structural limit caps the enthusiasm.

- Computing harm migration: [[apple-as-the-new-disney-benedict-evans]] links Apple's trust promise to a broader shift from lower-level technical worries toward adware, scamware, tracking, fraud, phishing, and manipulative app mechanics.
- Systems consequence mapping: [[cars-and-second-order-consequences-benedict-evans]] argues that EVs and autonomy will remake not only car manufacturing but also energy, retail, tax, transport, labor, real-estate, city design, and surveillance contexts.
- Speculative discipline: [[cars-and-second-order-consequences-benedict-evans]] explicitly distinguishes identifying where large effects may happen from predicting exact outcomes in car repair, commercial real estate, buses, or cities.
- Interface transition: [[cars-as-feature-phones-benedict-evans]] argues that car dashboards have accumulated features until the old interface model needs inversion under a software platform.
- Platform analogy: [[cars-as-feature-phones-benedict-evans]] compares automotive software to other device categories where accidental custom computers are eventually replaced by real software platforms.
- Boom analysis: [[voice-and-the-uncanny-valley-of-ai-benedict-evans]] lists four causes of the 2017 voice moment: working speech recognition and natural-language processing, cheap smartphone-supply-chain components and Shenzhen contract manufacturing, GAFA revenue at roughly ten times Wintel's 1990s scale, and platform anxiety about Apple and Google controlling mobile distribution.
- Definition discipline: [[voice-and-the-uncanny-valley-of-ai-benedict-evans]] narrows "voice works" to filling in a dialogue box, then notes that the dialogue box has to be built first.
- Coverage limit: [[voice-and-the-uncanny-valley-of-ai-benedict-evans]] calls the assistant a machine-learned front end on a pre-data, hand-crafted expert system that does not scale, since building every possible query by machine would be general AI.
- Discoverability curve: [[voice-and-the-uncanny-valley-of-ai-benedict-evans]] gives the U-shaped command curve and the Siri-versus-Alexa comparison about communicating what a user may ask.
- Domain conclusion: [[voice-and-the-uncanny-valley-of-ai-benedict-evans]] concludes that most companies need a narrow and predictable domain, naming hotel rooms, music, and maps as domains where it does not matter that you cannot scale.
- Interface limit: [[voice-and-the-uncanny-valley-of-ai-benedict-evans]] says even human voice is the wrong UI for booking a flight or buying clothes, and that adding a screen and icons is simply a graphical user interface with optional voice.
- Habit prerequisite: [[voice-and-the-uncanny-valley-of-ai-benedict-evans]] compares Alexa with the Apple Watch and argues that lower friction arrives only after the user changes their mental model and remembers the option.
- Expectations framing: [[voice-and-the-uncanny-valley-of-ai-benedict-evans]] uses the computer-animation uncanny valley to name the moment when making the tech better produces a worse user experience, and contrasts a better IVR with an unreachable HAL 9000.
- Industry motive: [[voice-and-the-uncanny-valley-of-ai-benedict-evans]] attributes part of the enthusiasm to an industry searching for a Next Big Thing once mobile stopped being the hyper-growth sector.

## Qualifications
The wiki currently knows Evans through a 2015 mobile-strategy article, two 2017 car-related essays, a 2017 voice/platform essay, and a 2019 Apple services/brand article, so it should not generalize his broader body of work or later views from these sources alone. The transport consequences and voice essays are explicitly speculative about downstream effects and structural limits rather than measured outcomes, and the car-interface essay is a strategic analogy rather than current automotive UX or autonomy evidence. The voice essay's coverage claim is also pre-LLM: it argues the back-end dialogue boxes cannot be generated from data, which later generative models partly relaxed even if grounding, action coverage, and reliability limits remain.
