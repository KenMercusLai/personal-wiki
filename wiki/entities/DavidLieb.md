## Overview
[[DavidLieb]] is presented as the cofounder and CEO of [[Bump]], later a product leader connected to [[GooglePhotos]] and a practitioner voice on cognitive simplicity in product design.

## Current Profile
The sources frame Lieb as a founder and product leader learning through persistence, user conversations, product pivots, team experience, and close attention to user comprehension rather than as a solo visionary. His retrospective gives equal weight to product lessons and relationships: early momentum came from [[YCombinator]], luck, small-team execution, and distribution shocks, while the durable career outcome came from the people and ideas that survived the original app. The First Round source adds a sharper product-design philosophy: user control, familiarity, consistency, and visible processing can reduce cognitive burden even when they add apparent friction.

## Key Characteristics
- Founded and led [[Bump]] from an initial app idea into a company acquired by [[Google]].
- Treats direct user contact as transformational product discovery.
- Credits cofounders and teammates as the most lasting startup outcome.
- Connects the Bump, [[Flock]], Photoroll, and [[GooglePhotos]] path as one evolving product-learning arc.
- Uses the Bump/Flock contrast to argue for [[CognitiveOverheadInProductDesign]] as a product adoption constraint.

## Evidence
- Founder path: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says Lieb had the original idea for Bump, contacted [[AndyHuibers]], and later built the company with cofounders including Andy and Jake.
- User discovery: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says calling Bump's top 100 users revealed they were sharing photos.
- Product arc: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] connects Bump, [[Flock]], the unreleased Photoroll, acquisition by [[Google]], and later work on [[GooglePhotos]].
- Team emphasis: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says the most lasting thing about most startups is the people, especially cofounders.
- Cognitive simplicity: [[cognitive-overhead-is-your-products-overlord-topple-it-with-these-tips-first-round-review]] has Lieb explain why Bump was understandable despite physical friction and why Flock's predictive sharing confused many users.
- Product heuristics: [[cognitive-overhead-is-your-products-overlord-topple-it-with-these-tips-first-round-review]] presents Lieb's recommendations to involve users in decisions, start from familiar patterns, stay consistent, sometimes slow products down, and test on users sensitive to cognitive overhead.

## Qualifications
The sources are Lieb's own retrospective thread and conference-talk-derived product advice, so they are strongest as first-person memory and practitioner interpretation. They do not independently verify Bump's metrics, acquisition terms, the detailed lineage from Photoroll ideas to Google Photos features, or the generalizability of the cognitive-overhead heuristics.

## What Changed
- Added Lieb's First Round product-design argument about cognitive overhead, automation, familiarity, consistency, and trust-building time.
