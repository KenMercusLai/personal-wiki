## Overview
[[DavidLieb]] is presented as the cofounder and CEO of [[Bump]], later a product leader connected to [[GooglePhotos]].

## Current Profile
The source frames Lieb as a founder learning through persistence, user conversations, product pivots, and team experience rather than as a solo visionary. His retrospective gives equal weight to product lessons and relationships: early momentum came from [[YCombinator]], luck, small-team execution, and distribution shocks, while the durable career outcome came from the people and ideas that survived the original app.

## Key Characteristics
- Founded and led [[Bump]] from an initial app idea into a company acquired by [[Google]].
- Treats direct user contact as transformational product discovery.
- Credits cofounders and teammates as the most lasting startup outcome.
- Connects the Bump, [[Flock]], Photoroll, and [[GooglePhotos]] path as one evolving product-learning arc.

## Evidence
- Founder path: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says Lieb had the original idea for Bump, contacted [[AndyHuibers]], and later built the company with cofounders including Andy and Jake.
- User discovery: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says calling Bump's top 100 users revealed they were sharing photos.
- Product arc: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] connects Bump, [[Flock]], the unreleased Photoroll, acquisition by [[Google]], and later work on [[GooglePhotos]].
- Team emphasis: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says the most lasting thing about most startups is the people, especially cofounders.

## Qualifications
The source is Lieb's own retrospective thread, so it is strongest as first-person memory and founder interpretation. It does not independently verify Bump's metrics, acquisition terms, or the detailed lineage from Photoroll ideas to Google Photos features.
