## Overview
[[NickRockwell]] is represented in the wiki through a 2017 panel as a New York Times technology executive arguing for subscription-first media economics.

## Current Profile
Rockwell treats advertising-only publishing as difficult outside very large or very small operations and considers subscription or membership more aligned with reader value. He nevertheless says few publishers can sustain a pure paid model, accepts combining ads and subscriptions, and expects media companies to experiment with bundles and aggregated subscriptions. Data is mainly a scorekeeping tool in this account; the longer horizon of subscription revenue, rather than data alone, is what may reduce clickbait incentives.

## Key Characteristics
- Subscription-first advocate for digital media.
- Skeptic of advertising as a sufficient standalone model for mid-sized publishers.
- Pragmatist who accepts mixed advertising and subscription portfolios.
- Proponent of longer-horizon business measurement as a quality incentive.
- Observer of subscription bundling and aggregation as likely industry responses.

## Evidence
- Market structure: [[expert-revenue-models-for-new-media]] says ad-only media is possible at large and small scales but especially difficult in the middle.
- Alignment: [[expert-revenue-models-for-new-media]] describes subscriptions as difficult but better aligned than advertising.
- Portfolio boundary: [[expert-revenue-models-for-new-media]] prefers pure subscription or membership but accepts combined ads and subscriptions because few can sustain the pure form.
- Industry direction: [[expert-revenue-models-for-new-media]] predicts more media subscriptions, bundling, and aggregation while warning that charging for social networks can be dangerous.
- Measurement: [[expert-revenue-models-for-new-media]] argues that subscription businesses need a longer view and that this can discourage clickbait.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on a single 2017 panel and captures a historical strategic position, not Rockwell's complete career or later views. The claims are not supported by publisher financial comparisons, subscription cohorts, quality measurements, or evidence about which bundling structures benefit readers, publishers, and creators.
