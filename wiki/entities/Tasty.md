## Overview
[[Tasty]] is BuzzFeed's food media brand used in the source as the leading example of a modern, service-oriented media brand with multiple revenue streams.

## Current Profile
The sources present Tasty as more than a social-video property. In the 2017 interview it is BuzzFeed's strongest proof that an internet-native brand can cross age groups through short, practical Facebook recipe videos and expand onto mainstream television. The later memo treats it as a daily consumer-service brand built around food and shared interests, then monetized through platform distribution, advertising, commerce, apps, partnerships, books, products, and video development. Tasty becomes the model BuzzFeed wants to extend into adjacent lifestyle brands.

## Key Characteristics
- Food-focused media brand that serves a broad audience through practical, shareable content.
- Represents BuzzFeed's desired move from single-channel media toward consumer-service brands.
- Connects social distribution on platforms such as Facebook, YouTube, and Instagram with direct products and partnerships.
- Provides the template for expansion into adjacent lifestyle verticals.
- Uses short video for reach while extending the brand into television and longer-form formats.

## Evidence
- Brand reach: [[9-boxes]] says Tasty is a highly liked food media brand on Facebook and reaches more people globally than any other food network.
- Consumer-service role: [[9-boxes]] frames Tasty as content that exists outside polarized political discourse, brings people together around food, and improves daily life.
- Revenue breadth: [[9-boxes]] shows Tasty revenue examples including a Walmart and Jet.com partnership, Penguin Random House cookbook deal, Tasty One Top, app adoption, Facebook publishing, YouTube shows, and coming shows such as Mom vs. Chef.
- Portfolio template: [[9-boxes]] says BuzzFeed will expand the model into Nifty, Goodful, and beauty/style.
- Cross-generational reach: [[buzzfeeds-jonah-peretti-news-publishers-only-have-themselves-to-blame-for-losing-out-to-google-and-facebook-the-drum]] describes Tasty as Facebook's most-liked page at the time and evidence that BuzzFeed was not only a millennial destination.
- Format expansion: [[buzzfeeds-jonah-peretti-news-publishers-only-have-themselves-to-blame-for-losing-out-to-google-and-facebook-the-drum]] says recipe clips often ran under one minute for reach while Tasty also appeared on NBC's Today show.

## Qualifications
The memo is an internal strategic statement and the interview repeats company or platform figures without independent methodology. Neither verifies Tasty's profitability, causal contribution, or later performance, and both emphasize strategic promise rather than platform dependency, production cost, brand-extension, or commerce-execution risk.
