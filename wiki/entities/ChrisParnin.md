## Overview
[[ChrisParnin]] is represented here as the author and researcher behind [[gamasutra-programmer-interrupted]], a practitioner-facing account of empirical work on interruptions, cognitive load, and context recovery during programming.

## Current Profile
The source presents Parnin as translating software-engineering and cognition research into operational questions: how long developers take to resume editing, which work states carry high memory load, and what cues programmers use when returning to interrupted code. His account combines programming-session data, a developer survey, observed recovery behavior, and physiological measures, while the supplied local copy remains an incomplete excerpt rather than a full research report.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies software development through observable programming activity and developer surveys.
- Connects interruption recovery to memory load and cognitive state.
- Documents improvised developer strategies such as navigation trails, compile-error reminders, and source diffs.
- Communicates research findings in a practitioner-oriented article while relying on underlying studies for methodological detail.

## Evidence
Empirical interruption study:
- [[gamasutra-programmer-interrupted]] attributes findings to an analysis of 10,000 sessions from 86 programmers and a survey of 414 programmers.

Cognitive-load framing:
- [[gamasutra-programmer-interrupted]] links interruption harm to peak memory load and presents pupil-diameter and subvocal EMG figures as candidate workload signals.

Recovery behavior:
- [[gamasutra-programmer-interrupted]] reports navigation across code locations, intentional compile errors, and source-diff review as ways developers attempt to reconstruct state.

Practitioner translation:
- [[gamasutra-programmer-interrupted]] presents numerical findings and practical observations in a Game Developer article rather than reproducing a complete academic methods section.
