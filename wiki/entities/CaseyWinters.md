## Overview
[[CaseyWinters]] is a product and growth practitioner represented in the wiki through his [[FeatureProductFit]] framework, operating examples from [[Grubhub]] and [[Pinterest]], and an analysis of how Google's mobile search strategy changes organic distribution for aggregators.

## Current Profile
Winters argues that growth strategy must follow the actual source of user value and control. Inside a product, teams should judge features by durable use, scalable adoption, and whole-product outcomes rather than launch attention or raw usage. Across platforms, companies should distinguish temporary acquisition access from a durable customer relationship: as Google moves from external links toward answers and vertical discovery, search-dependent aggregators must adapt their listings while building direct loyalty. Both essays treat a useful growth surface as conditional rather than permanent.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames feature development as a search for fit rather than a sequence of launches.
- Measures success across feature retention, scalable adoption, and core-product retention, engagement, or monetization.
- Uses data analysis and user research as complementary diagnostic methods.
- Advocates targeted cohort exposure before broad distribution.
- Treats feature deletion as valuable product work when fit is absent or lost.
- Treats organic search as a historically durable but declining acquisition dependency for inventory aggregators.
- Recommends adapting to platform-owned discovery while building direct demand and switching costs.

## Evidence
- Framework: [[feature-product-fit-casey-accidental]] defines the three-part feature/product-fit test and distinguishes it from raw feature usage.
- Experiment discipline: [[feature-product-fit-casey-accidental]] recommends testing with only enough relevant users to detect a credible signal.
- Operating cases: [[feature-product-fit-casey-accidental]] describes Grubhub's mobile conversion work and Pinterest's feature deletion and Related Pins adoption tactics.
- Portfolio judgment: [[feature-product-fit-casey-accidental]] argues that old and new features should be removed when they cannot be redeemed.
- Search transition: [[googles-new-strategy-and-how-it-affects-aggregators-casey-accidental]] argues that mobile users' demand for immediate answers and options is pushing Google from referral toward direct aggregation.
- Aggregator response: [[googles-new-strategy-and-how-it-affects-aggregators-casey-accidental]] recommends distributing individual listings into Google's discovery surfaces while developing loyal direct audiences.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on two 2018 practitioner essays and selected retrospective examples. The feature cases do not provide complete experiment specifications, absolute results for most cases, or independent causal verification. The search essay is a strategic forecast without cross-category traffic measurements; its Chrome-data claim is unsupported, its AMP advice is historically situated, and its appended reader comment argues that specialist result pages may still add comparison value and convert better than individual listings.

## What Changed
- Created the initial profile around the feature/product-fit framework and its Grubhub and Pinterest cases.
- Added platform-level growth judgment: organic-search access can decay as the search provider internalizes discovery.
- Added the distinction between adapting listings for platform visibility and building direct audience loyalty.
