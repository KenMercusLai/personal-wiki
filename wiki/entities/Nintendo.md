## Overview
[[Nintendo]] is the Japanese game company represented here through the original [[GameBoy]] and a product strategy that can favor accessibility, portability, and distinctive use over specification leadership.

## Current Profile
In the source's historical framing, Nintendo released the Game Boy in Japan in 1989 and used practical product tradeoffs to defeat technically more sophisticated handheld rivals. The article extends that philosophy to the 2019-era Switch, arguing that Nintendo continued to accept lower raw power than competing home consoles when the compromise enabled a different form of play. The Game Boy's continuing modification and creative reuse also show how users can broaden the meaning of Nintendo hardware after launch.

## Key Characteristics
- Game and hardware company behind the Game Boy family.
- Associated in the source with [[LateralThinkingWithWitheredTechnology]].
- Competes through price, portability, product form, and play experience as well as technical performance.
- Benefits when hardware limitations become recognizable aesthetic and creative materials.

## Evidence
- Launch and scale: [[game-boys-have-infinite-lives-at-30-they-live-on-in-unexpected-ways-los-angeles-times]] dates the Japanese Game Boy release to April 21, 1989, and reports 110 million worldwide sales by 2001.
- Competitive tradeoffs: [[game-boys-have-infinite-lives-at-30-they-live-on-in-unexpected-ways-los-angeles-times]] says price and battery practicality helped Nintendo beat Atari Lynx and Sega Game Gear despite weaker specifications.
- Continuing philosophy: [[game-boys-have-infinite-lives-at-30-they-live-on-in-unexpected-ways-los-angeles-times]] interprets the Switch's lower power relative to contemporary PlayStation and Xbox hardware as a later instance of the same product logic.
- User extension: [[game-boys-have-infinite-lives-at-30-they-live-on-in-unexpected-ways-los-angeles-times]] documents independent modification, music, and photography practices around Nintendo hardware.

## Qualifications
This profile is deliberately source-bounded. One anniversary article cannot establish Nintendo's overall strategy, organization, financial performance, software catalog, platform governance, or the continuity of one philosophy across different product teams and decades. Its Switch comparison is interpretive and does not test alternative explanations for the product's success.
