## Overview
[[DavidSundstrand]] is presented as the inventor whose 1914 adding-machine patent helped establish the calculator-style 3x3-plus-0 numeric keypad.

## Current Profile
The source says Sundstrand rearranged 10-key adding-machine input into a more logical and natural configuration: 7-8-9 at the top, descending to 1-2-3, with a larger 0 below. The article treats this design as the ancestor of calculator keypads that still persist more than a century later.

## Key Characteristics
- Swedish-born American inventor in the source's adding-machine history.
- Filed a 1914 patent associated with Sundstrand Corporation.
- Standardized a 3x3-plus-0 layout with 7-8-9 on the top row.
- Framed the design around one-handed speed and usability for adding machines.

## Evidence
- Patent and layout: [[a-brief-history-of-the-numeric-keypad]] says Sundstrand filed patent number 1198487 in 1914 and arranged keys into the now-familiar calculator order.
- Usability goal: [[a-brief-history-of-the-numeric-keypad]] says the arrangement was intended to be logical, natural, one-hand operable, and fast.
- Durability: [[a-brief-history-of-the-numeric-keypad]] says the layout became the calculator-keypad standard even more than 100 years later.

## Qualifications
The source presents Sundstrand as a decisive standardizer of the calculator layout, not as the sole origin of numeric key input. Earlier calculators, cash registers, and adding machines supplied important predecessors.
