## Overview
[[Telegram]] appears as the host of an early in-chat music bot used to illustrate the 2016 claim that messaging products could become browsers, service runtimes, and distribution surfaces.

## Current Profile
The Inside Intercom essay uses Telegram's `@music` experience as a concrete example of bots acting like dynamic bookmarks. Rather than opening a separate music app or a conventional browser, a user can invoke a bot inside chat, search for music, move through paginated results, select a download command, and start playback. The retained GIF grounds the interface claim but does not establish its usage, reliability, economics, or later evolution.

## Key Characteristics
- Hosts bots inside a mobile messaging environment.
- Supports inline interfaces that can update results within a chat.
- Serves as an early example of messaging absorbing discovery and media interaction from separate apps.

## Evidence
- Bot role: [[browsers-not-apps-are-the-future-of-mobile-inside-intercom]] describes Telegram's `@music` feature as a way to find and listen to music without leaving messaging.
- Interface behavior: [[browsers-not-apps-are-the-future-of-mobile-inside-intercom]] says the bot uses an inline keyboard and updates its own messages as the user moves through search results.
- Visual example: [[browsers-not-apps-are-the-future-of-mobile-inside-intercom]] retains an inspected GIF showing `/start`, a “Bach” query, paginated results, download commands, and in-chat playback.

## Qualifications
The source is a 2016 platform forecast and supplies one product example, not comparative evidence that Telegram bots displaced native music apps or conventional mobile browsing. The GIF shows a successful search-and-playback path but no purchase, error, recovery, retention, or adoption evidence.
