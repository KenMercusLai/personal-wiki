## Overview
[[Coinbase]] is profiled as a U.S. cryptocurrency company that combined an accessible, regulated crypto platform with explicit operating controls intended to preserve talent and culture during hypergrowth.

## Current Profile
Fortune presents Coinbase as both crypto's mainstream bridge and a company under pressure. It began by making [[Bitcoin]] buying and storage feel closer to PayPal, Venmo, or online stock trading than to command-line wallets and offshore transfers. By 2018, it had 25 million customer accounts, had crossed a $1 billion valuation and roughly $1 billion in annual revenue, and was pursuing broker-dealer approval, institutional custody, acquisitions, and possible banking status. Armstrong's bar-raiser account adds an organizational response to that growth: candidates had to clear both skill and values screens, while a trained panel member independent of the hiring manager could veto a hire. The company therefore appears to use checks and balances in both market legitimacy and personnel selection, although its business model remained exposed to crypto prices, trading volume, customer trust, security, and low-fee competition.

## Key Characteristics
- Functions as an easy consumer on-ramp and custodial exchange for cryptocurrency.
- Uses regulatory compliance as a strategic differentiator rather than a concession to be avoided.
- Benefited strongly from the 2017 speculative boom and suffered when crypto prices and volumes fell.
- Faced operational and reputational stress from outages, support backlogs, fraud, the Ethereum flash crash, and Bitcoin Cash controversy.
- Tried to diversify toward institutional custody, professional trading volume, acquisitions, and [[SecurityTokens]].
- Used a trained bar raiser with veto power to keep urgent local staffing needs from unilaterally lowering company-wide skill and values standards.

## Evidence
- Consumer on-ramp: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] says Armstrong designed Coinbase so traditional bank accounts could buy cryptocurrency and Coinbase could store assets on customers' behalf.
- Compliance strategy: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] says Coinbase complied with know-your-customer rules, limited coin listings, pursued broker-dealer approval, and discussed a federal banking charter.
- Boom scale: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] reports 25 million customer accounts, roughly $1 billion in annual revenue, a unicorn valuation, and a chart showing accounts rising fivefold in two years.
- Operational stress: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] describes 2017 outages, unresolved tickets, fraud costs, employee exhaustion, the June 21, 2017 Ethereum flash crash, and the Bitcoin Cash controversy.
- Diversification push: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] reports institutional custody approval, professional/institutional volume surpassing consumer volume, acquisitions under Emilie Choi, and interest in security-token markets.
- Hiring checks and balances: [[bar-raisers-at-coinbase-if-youre-not-a-hell-yes-youre-a-no]] says every panel included a trained bar raiser with veto power, candidates needed both skill and values alignment, and uncertainty was resolved toward rejection.

## Qualifications
The profile is based on two 2018 accounts and should not be treated as a current assessment of Coinbase's public-company financials, regulatory status, leadership structure, product portfolio, or hiring process. Fortune leans on Coinbase and investor sources, while the bar-raiser article is company-authored and reports process intent rather than comparative hiring outcomes. Neither establishes whether subjective signals such as interviewer energy or immediate value predict performance fairly, and neither measures the costs of rejected good candidates, veto concentration, affinity bias, or effects on diversity.

## What Changed
- Expanded Coinbase from a market and operations profile into an organizational-design case where an independent hiring veto protects company-wide standards from hypergrowth pressure.
- Qualified the bar-raiser system as a company-authored process claim without outcome or fairness evidence.
