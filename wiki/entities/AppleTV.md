## Overview
[[AppleTV]] is Apple's first-party video hardware and TV-app context in the source, used to show that broader content availability does not necessarily mean Apple is abandoning premium hardware.

## Current Profile
Cybart presents Apple TV as a high-end accessory for users who want the best all-around Apple video experience. The article rejects the idea that Apple would prioritize services over hardware by making a cheap Apple TV dongle. Instead, Apple keeps selling a premium box while also pursuing video distribution through the TV app, smart-TV iTunes availability, and [[AirPlay2]] support.

## Key Characteristics
- Premium first-party Apple video hardware in the article's framing.
- Coexists with broader video distribution on non-Apple televisions.
- Supports Apple's goal of increasing TV app engagement.
- Functions as an integrated experience rather than a low-margin services conduit.

## Evidence
- Premium positioning: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] contrasts Apple TV's price with cheap TV or dongle alternatives to argue Apple is not merely chasing services revenue.
- Non-exclusivity: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] says AirPlay 2 and smart-TV content support do not kneecap Apple TV because the product serves users seeking the best Apple experience.
- TV app strategy: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] says Apple wants third-party content creators to embrace the TV app and wants original content to elevate it.

## Qualifications
The source predates later Apple TV hardware, app, and Apple TV+ developments. It does not prove Apple TV demand, profitability, or user satisfaction; it only captures Cybart's strategic interpretation of the product's role.
