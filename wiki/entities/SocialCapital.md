## Overview
[[SocialCapital]] is the investment firm presented in the sources as using quantitative operating analysis to evaluate demonstrated [[ProductMarketFit]], support portfolio-company decisions, and automate parts of early-stage investment through [[CapitalAsAService]].

## Current Profile
The 2015 article describes a diligence practice for companies that already have products and users. Within that practice, Social Capital looks past cumulative registrations and rising MAU to the underlying flows of new, retained, resurrected, and churned users, while treating traction as one input alongside team, market, vision, competition, and core technology. The 2018 retrospective extends that approach into CaaS: an online product through which founders submitted transaction data for automated diligence, rapid funding decisions, category comparison, and model-based feedback across geographies.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses standardized traction views while acknowledging that companies and activity definitions differ.
- Decomposes active-user growth to distinguish durable retention from churn replacement.
- Applies diligence concepts to portfolio-company operating reviews as well as investment evaluation.
- Treats quantitative traction as one bounded part of broader company diligence.
- Productized parts of sourcing and diligence through an online, globally accessible workflow.

## Evidence
- Standardized analysis: [[diligence-at-social-capital-part-1-accounting-for-user-growth]] says the firm had standard ways of examining core traction metrics.
- Growth decomposition: [[diligence-at-social-capital-part-1-accounting-for-user-growth]] recommends showing new, resurrected, and churned users rather than MAU alone.
- Portfolio use: [[diligence-at-social-capital-part-1-accounting-for-user-growth]] says portfolio companies used the concepts in their operating processes.
- Bounded scope: [[diligence-at-social-capital-part-1-accounting-for-user-growth]] lists team, market, vision, competition, and technology as other diligence dimensions.
- Automated workflow: [[from-experiment-to-product-capital-as-a-service-one-year-later]] says CaaS accepted founder applications and transaction data online and could produce funding decisions within hours.
- First-year reach: [[from-experiment-to-product-capital-as-a-service-one-year-later]] reports about 5,000 applications considered, 76 approvals, applications from 48 countries, and investments in 24 countries.

## Qualifications
The page reflects a former employee's 2015 methodological account and the firm's own 2018 product retrospective, not a complete or independently verified description of Social Capital's historical or current process. The 2015 numerical examples are fictional. The 2018 throughput and portfolio figures are self-reported, lack a conventional-diligence comparison, and do not disclose model performance, selection errors, investment amounts, rejected-company outcomes, or realized returns. Automation does not by itself establish transparency, consistency, or freedom from bias.
