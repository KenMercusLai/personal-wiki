## Overview
[[Alphabet]] is represented here as [[Google]]'s corporate parent and as the coordinator of a distributed startup-investment system spanning venture, growth, artificial-intelligence, direct corporate, accelerator, and impact vehicles.

## Current Profile
In [[JasonRowley]]'s 2018 account, Alphabet invested at high volume without collapsing every mandate into one fund. [[GV]] supplied broad and prolific venture activity, [[CapitalG]] concentrated on later-stage growth, [[GradientVentures]] focused on artificial intelligence, and Google invested directly while also operating equity-free accelerator and impact programs. The limited reported overlap between the main funds suggests differentiated selection, but the article does not establish formal coordination, portfolio returns, or current structure.

## Key Characteristics
- Corporate parent of Google in the source's organizational model.
- Operated multiple equity-investment vehicles with differentiated stage or sector mandates.
- Reportedly participated through its main entities in 103 startup deals during 2017.
- Combined equity investment with accelerator grants and impact-oriented activity.
- Held a broad portfolio with little reported overlap between GV and CapitalG.
- Connected investment activity to acquisitions and public-company exits.

## Evidence
- Distributed structure: [[jason-rowley-a-peek-inside-alphabets-investing-universe]] identifies GV, CapitalG, Gradient Ventures, and Google as four primary equity-investing entities.
- Scale: [[jason-rowley-a-peek-inside-alphabets-investing-universe]] reports 103 deals across those entities in 2017.
- Differentiation: [[jason-rowley-a-peek-inside-alphabets-investing-universe]] finds only two shared portfolio companies between GV and CapitalG.
- Exit network: [[jason-rowley-a-peek-inside-alphabets-investing-universe]] reports seven Alphabet acquisitions of previously backed companies and twelve portfolio companies that had gone public.
- Breadth: [[jason-rowley-a-peek-inside-alphabets-investing-universe]] lists investments across software, insurance, food, recruiting, genetics, robotics, transportation, and energy.

## Qualifications
This profile is a February 2018 interpretation of 2017 Crunchbase data, not a current organizational chart or an audited portfolio report. Deal counts do not measure invested capital, ownership, risk, strategic fit, follow-on reserves, exits, or returns. Limited overlap can indicate mandate separation, independent teams, incomplete data, or coincidental selection; the source does not distinguish among them.
