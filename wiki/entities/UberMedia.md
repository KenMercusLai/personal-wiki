## Overview
[[UberMedia]] is presented as a third-party Twitter-client consolidator whose acquisitions intensified Twitter's concern that an external company could control a large share of tweet creation and consumption.

## Current Profile
The source says Bill Gross's UberMedia acquired Echofon, UberTwitter, and Twidroyd and considered acquiring TweetDeck. Costa treats the possible combined client base as an existential platform-control risk that helped turn consumer-facing Twitter clients from ecosystem participants into competitors.

## Key Characteristics
- Aggregated multiple third-party Twitter clients.
- Was reported to have considered acquiring TweetDeck.
- Represents the risk that an API-dependent complement can consolidate control over a platform's user interface.

## Evidence
- Client acquisitions: [[a-tale-of-2-api-platforms-ggv-capital-medium]] names Echofon, UberTwitter, and Twidroyd as UberMedia acquisitions.
- Consolidation scenario: [[a-tale-of-2-api-platforms-ggv-capital-medium]] says a TweetDeck acquisition could have put a significant client base under one external owner.
- Strategic consequence: [[a-tale-of-2-api-platforms-ggv-capital-medium]] presents the possible shadow network as a major reason Twitter viewed third-party clients as competitive.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on Costa's 2016 retrospective and does not independently establish UberMedia's intentions, the probability of a shadow network, or the counterfactual effect of the proposed TweetDeck acquisition.

## What Changed
- Added UberMedia as the client-consolidation threat in the Twitter API case.
