## Overview
[[MySQL]] is an open-source relational database developed from [[MontyWidenius]]'s earlier database work and released publicly in the mid-1990s through [[MySQLAB]].

## Current Profile
The article presents MySQL as a fast, reliable, approachable system for storing substantial datasets and retrieving results through SQL. Its design reportedly accumulated from database precursors begun in 1981, years of production use and customer feedback, and an SQL layer added when Widenius and his collaborators decided to productize the work. The name joins “My,” the name of Widenius's daughter, with SQL, Structured Query Language.

## Key Characteristics
- Designed to store and query datasets beyond the practical scale of spreadsheet-style tools.
- Developed incrementally from long-running precursor code rather than as a short greenfield project.
- Shaped by real customer feedback before public release.
- Distributed as open source to invite community improvement.
- Commercialized through a paid-use model for enterprises described by the source.

## Evidence
- Technical purpose: [[founder-stories-a-hackers-hacker-openocean]] contrasts MySQL's fast large-data retrieval with spreadsheet slowdown and unreliability at scale.
- Development history: [[founder-stories-a-hackers-hacker-openocean]] says Widenius built precursors from 1981 and added an SQL layer before the public release.
- Product qualities: [[founder-stories-a-hackers-hacker-openocean]] attributes the database's speed, reliability, and learnability partly to sustained customer feedback.
- Open development: [[founder-stories-a-hackers-hacker-openocean]] presents community contribution as a way to improve the software.
- Commercial model: [[founder-stories-a-hackers-hacker-openocean]] says commercial enterprise users were expected to buy a license.

## Qualifications
The source's introductory 1995 implication and detailed October 1996 release date are not fully aligned. Its description of database scale is introductory rather than comparative, and its “honor system” account is not a sufficient legal or historical description of MySQL licensing. The near-solo creation story also underrepresents later employee, cofounder, and community contributions.
