## Overview
[[DonaldKnuth]] is the computer scientist represented here as creator of [[TeX]], designer of its initial automatic-hyphenation approach with collaborators, and academic context for [[FrankLiang]]'s later pattern algorithm.

## Current Profile
The source recounts that Knuth began TeX after rejecting unsatisfactory typesetting for *The Art of Computer Programming*. Within the article's narrower hyphenation history, he and collaborators designed the first TeX algorithm in 1977 from linguistic rules and an exception dictionary, before Liang developed a more general dictionary-derived pattern method.

This profile does not attempt to summarize Knuth's broader work in algorithms or computer science. Its current evidence supports only his role in TeX's origin, early hyphenation design, and the student-supervisor setting around Liang's contribution.

## Key Characteristics
- Created TeX to gain better control over book and mathematical typesetting.
- Helped design TeX's original rule-based automatic-hyphenation method.
- Used prefix, suffix, VCCV, special-case, and exception mechanisms in the early approach described by the source.
- Served as Frank Liang's academic supervisor context during the development of the replacement algorithm.
- Is represented through a secondary historical account rather than primary archival material.

## Evidence
- TeX motivation: [[lian-zi-fu-duan-ci-cong-yuan-li-dao-shi-jian]] connects dissatisfaction with the typesetting of *The Art of Computer Programming* to Knuth's creation of TeX.
- Early hyphenator: [[lian-zi-fu-duan-ci-cong-yuan-li-dao-shi-jian]] attributes the 1977 rule-based design to Knuth and collaborators and outlines its mechanisms.
- Liang relationship: [[lian-zi-fu-duan-ci-cong-yuan-li-dao-shi-jian]] identifies Liang as Knuth's student and places his work within TeX development.

## Qualifications
The supplied article is not a Knuth biography or a primary history of TeX. Its origin story is compressed, gives no archival citations in the body, and does not separate individual contributions among the early collaborators. The reported performance of the first hyphenator is not independently reproduced, and this page makes no claim about Knuth's many contributions outside the source's narrow typesetting scope.
