## Overview
[[DocuSign]] is represented as an electronic-signature product whose convenience illustrates the security consequences that can accompany removal of deliberation and procedural steps.

## Current Profile
The source describes DocuSign as making contract agreement substantially easier while arguing that the same familiar, rapid flow can make fraudulent signature requests more plausible. Its comparison is temporal and cognitive: a longer manual signing process may give a recipient more opportunities to question a suspicious request, whereas a low-friction electronic flow can compress that pause. This is a product-design caution, not evidence that DocuSign caused a measured increase in fraud.

## Key Characteristics
- Reduces the practical steps required to agree to contracts.
- Uses a familiar electronic signing flow that can also be imitated in phishing attempts.
- Illustrates a tradeoff between transaction convenience and protective deliberation.

## Evidence
- Convenience: [[in-products-as-in-life-not-all-friction-is-bad-better-everyday]] describes DocuSign as making agreement easier than a multi-step manual process.
- Threat observation: [[in-products-as-in-life-not-all-friction-is-bad-better-everyday]] reports [[DavidFrankel]] observing phishing through document-sharing and electronic-signature links.
- Deliberation mechanism: [[in-products-as-in-life-not-all-friction-is-bad-better-everyday]] argues that additional signing steps can create time to question whether a request is fraudulent.

## Qualifications
The source provides an anecdotal 2017 observation without phishing counts, attack samples, control groups, product safeguards, victim outcomes, or evidence separating DocuSign-branded lures from broader email and identity failures. More steps do not automatically create security, and poorly designed friction can train users to click through warnings rather than inspect them.

## What Changed
- Established DocuSign as a qualified example of convenience reducing a potential security pause.
- Distinguished the proposed mechanism from evidence of measured or product-caused fraud growth.
