## Overview
[[EdGlaeser]] is a Harvard economics professor represented here through a *Freakonomics Radio* discussion of cumulative knowledge, governance, and civil-rights change.

## Current Profile
Glaeser argues that large intellectual, artistic, and political changes are usually built through many contributions. He uses the transmission of linear perspective across Renaissance artists, the cumulative character of economics scholarship, and decades of NAACP litigation to oppose leader-centered or silver-bullet accounts of progress. His practical emphasis is everyday governance and sustained work across long periods.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses examples across art history, economics, governance, and civil rights to explain incremental change.
- Treats scholarly advances as series of contributions whose implications require later work.
- Warns that faith in one transformative political leader distracts from everyday decision-making and governance.
- Credits long-lived organizations and sequenced legal challenges with enabling later landmark reforms.

## Evidence
- Artistic accumulation: [[in-praise-of-incrementalism-ep-264-freakonomics-freakonomics]] records Glaeser's account of linear perspective moving through successive Renaissance artists.
- Scholarly accumulation: [[in-praise-of-incrementalism-ep-264-freakonomics-freakonomics]] says economics generally changes through bodies of work and later interpretation rather than one revolutionary paper.
- Political and legal sequencing: [[in-praise-of-incrementalism-ep-264-freakonomics-freakonomics]] attributes civil-rights progress partly to decades of NAACP challenges to racial zoning, restrictive covenants, and school segregation.

## Qualifications
This profile is limited to one edited 2016 interview and is not a comprehensive account of Glaeser's scholarship or present views. His examples illustrate incrementalism but do not measure its contribution, compare it with discontinuous change, or capture the full histories and participants behind Renaissance and civil-rights developments.
