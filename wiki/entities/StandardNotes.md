## Overview
[[StandardNotes]] is the note-taking project promoted at the end of a 2016 critique of [[Evernote]], where it is positioned around longevity, open source, privacy, and cross-platform availability.

## Current Profile
The source presents Standard Notes as a deliberately more standard and durable home for personally important notes. Its stated design identity opposes the essay's picture of venture-driven product expansion: preserve access over a person's lifetime, make the software open source and private, and support multiple platforms. These are the author's claims and intentions; the source does not evaluate implementation, portability, security, sustainability, or actual long-term outcomes.

## Key Characteristics
- Proposed as a long-horizon repository for personally meaningful notes.
- Described by its builder as open source, private, and available on most platforms.
- Positioned as a focused alternative to [[Evernote]] and to [[VentureBackedGrowthPressure]].
- Introduced inside an essay that simultaneously criticizes a competitor and recruits interest in the project.

## Evidence
- Product promise: [[evernote-is-what-happens-when-you-mix-vc-with-a-notes-app]] says the project aims to protect a person's notes for as long as they live.
- Design positioning: [[evernote-is-what-happens-when-you-mix-vc-with-a-notes-app]] describes the project as open source, private, cross-platform, and intentionally standard.
- Competitive context: [[evernote-is-what-happens-when-you-mix-vc-with-a-notes-app]] introduces Standard Notes after arguing that Evernote's financing and growth path make a user contingency plan prudent.

## Qualifications
The essay is promotional and offers no architecture, threat model, export test, funding model, operational history, reliability measure, or independent comparison. Open source, privacy claims, and cross-platform availability can support longevity but do not by themselves guarantee durable access or organizational survival. This page records the project's stated 2016 position, not its current capabilities.
