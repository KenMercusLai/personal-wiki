## Overview
[[JoshuaMerrill]] is identified by [[HenryWard]] as eShares' head of product and as a contributor to the 2016 management FAQ.

## Current Profile
Ward uses Merrill as the essay's concrete example of why senior management depends on influence rather than command. He describes Merrill as an unusually strong product builder with attractive outside options and argues that Merrill's team is more loyal to its direct leader than to the CEO. In that account, company direction depends on earning Merrill's commitment rather than treating hierarchy as sufficient authority.

## Key Characteristics
- Served as head of product at eShares in the source's 2016 context.
- Was presented by Ward as a highly capable product builder with substantial outside options.
- Illustrated the organizational power of trusted lieutenants and the limits of CEO command.
- Helped Ward write and publish the management FAQ.

## Evidence
- Role and capability: [[a-managers-faq-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] identifies “Josh” as eShares' head of product and praises his product-building ability.
- Influence example: [[a-managers-faq-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] says the CEO needed Merrill to want to build a proposed product because command would risk losing him and would not directly control his team.
- Contribution: [[a-managers-faq-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] thanks Joshua Merrill and James Seely for help writing and publishing the essay.

## Qualifications
The profile is limited to Ward's brief, highly favorable characterization in one company-published essay. It contains no account from Merrill, no independent assessment of his product work, and no basis for verifying Ward's comparative Silicon Valley ranking.
