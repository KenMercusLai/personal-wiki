## Overview
[[Yahoo]] appears as a large internet company that faced an underbuilt mobile capability, served as an incumbent in search and question-and-answer, and is diagnosed by [[XavierAmatriain]] as declining through mutually reinforcing failures of talent, focus, acquisitions, research transfer, and culture.

## Current Profile
The scaling source uses Yahoo to show how a large company can still face scale mismatch inside a strategic function. Mayer says Yahoo had only about 30 mobile-team members out of 14,000 employees and needed a much larger mobile organization, so acquisitions brought in teams whose founders could lead new divisions. The competition source uses Yahoo from another direction: its established search and question-and-answer products did not prevent [[Google]] and [[Quora]] from entering.

Amatriain supplies the first corpus-wide failure diagnosis of Yahoo itself. He argues that weaker hiring and retention reduced [[TalentDensity]], broad "web" positioning and an inconsistently executed mobile-ad direction weakened [[StartupFocus]], acquisitions used as talent repair further fragmented focus and culture, Yahoo Labs lacked effective [[ResearchToProductTransfer]], and a comfortable family atmosphere did not create enough accountability for impact or speed. His model is systemic: each weakness made the others harder to correct.

## Key Characteristics
- Illustrates a strategic function being underbuilt relative to company size.
- Used talent, building-block, and strategic acquisitions to address capability gaps, with disputed company-wide results.
- Serves as an incumbent that did not prevent later entry in search and question-and-answer.
- Supplies a negative case where talent loss, strategic diffusion, and cultural inertia are described as reinforcing one another.
- Hosted research associated with important technology while allegedly struggling to convert ideas into business impact.

## Evidence
- Mobile gap: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] cites Mayer that Yahoo's mobile team was about 30 people out of 14,000.
- Acquisition strategy: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] says Mayer classified acquisitions into talent, building-block, and strategic categories and used acquired teams to rebuild mobile.
- Search incumbent: [[yash-gandhi-startup-idea-well-funded-competitor-quora]] cites Yahoo's search engine as already famous before Google stepped in.
- Question-and-answer incumbent: [[yash-gandhi-startup-idea-well-funded-competitor-quora]] cites a Yahoo question-and-answer product as already used by many people before Quora started.
- Talent spiral: [[xavier-amatriains-answer-to-what-lessons-can-silicon-valley-tech-executives-learn-from-what-went-wrong-at-yahoo-quora]] says weaker hiring and the departure of strong employees progressively lowered talent density.
- Focus and acquisition costs: [[xavier-amatriains-answer-to-what-lessons-can-silicon-valley-tech-executives-learn-from-what-went-wrong-at-yahoo-quora]] argues that Yahoo lacked a constraining strategic focus and that acquisitions increased fragmentation and cultural dilution.
- Research transfer: [[xavier-amatriains-answer-to-what-lessons-can-silicon-valley-tech-executives-learn-from-what-went-wrong-at-yahoo-quora]] uses Yahoo Labs and [[Hadoop]] to distinguish strong research from business impact.
- Culture: [[xavier-amatriains-answer-to-what-lessons-can-silicon-valley-tech-executives-learn-from-what-went-wrong-at-yahoo-quora]] says a friendly, permissive culture lacked sufficient pressure toward impact, innovation, and speed.

## Qualifications
The sources provide perspectives rather than a settled causal history. The scaling note presents Mayer's acquisition rationale without measuring its outcome; Amatriain explicitly never worked at Yahoo and relies on conversations with employees, while providing no deal data, research-transfer metrics, retention series, or counterfactual. The competition answer mentions Yahoo only as a foil and gives no dates, market data, or account of Yahoo's decisions. Local mobile-team gains and company-wide focus or integration costs can therefore coexist.

## What Changed
- Added a systemic decline diagnosis spanning talent, focus, acquisition integration, research transfer, and culture.
- Reframed acquisitions as a contested intervention that may solve a local capability gap while worsening company-wide coherence.
- Added Yahoo Labs and Hadoop as a qualified research-to-product-transfer case.
