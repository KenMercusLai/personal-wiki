## Overview
[[HannahLee]] is a product designer represented here through her first-person account of [[Chrome]]'s 2018 mobile interface redesign.

## Current Profile
Lee describes design as stewardship of the space between users and web content, but revises a purely invisible-interface ideal when imitation, hidden capability, security signaling, and brand recognition become product concerns. Her redesign practice begins with implementation reality: audit code and states, map variants, distinguish shared from Chrome- and platform-specific components, and bring engineering into the system work.

The resulting omnibox direction combined subtraction with meaning. Stable rounded geometry reduced state-to-state visual change, increased the touch-oriented character of the control, echoed Chrome's circular brand language, and simplified some transition code. The source positions this as collaborative systems work rather than a solitary visual concept.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats interface restraint as protection for user attention rather than an absence of design.
- Audits implementation variants before consolidating visual styles or components.
- Uses platform, language, accessibility, device, and interaction permutations as design constraints.
- Connects brand form to functional meaning rather than adding decoration alone.
- Works with engineers to turn a redesign into reusable component and process infrastructure.

## Evidence
- Design philosophy: [[unboxing-chrome-hannah-lee-medium]] says Lee initially valued Chrome's “content, not chrome” ideal and later qualified invisibility when deceptive copies and security concerns made distinction consequential.
- System audit: [[unboxing-chrome-hannah-lee-medium]] describes crawling code for text styles, reducing 95 greys to eight, and inventorying hundreds of icon variants.
- Cross-functional method: [[unboxing-chrome-hannah-lee-medium]] describes engineering support for a shared component specification grounded in a code library.
- Form and function: [[unboxing-chrome-hannah-lee-medium]] links the final rounded omnibox to touch input, Chrome's circular geometry, stable transitions, and reported user-study perceptions.

## Qualifications
The wiki currently knows Lee through one self-authored 2018 retrospective, not a broader biography or independent account of her role. The article names extensive team and engineering participation, so its outcomes should not be attributed to one designer. Reported user-study judgments and product-size improvements lack methods and measured effect sizes.
