## Overview
[[GameBoy]] is [[Nintendo]]'s 1989 handheld game system, represented here as a commercially successful device whose practical design and expressive limitations enabled unusually long cultural and technical afterlives.

## Current Profile
The source presents the Game Boy as a product that won without being the most technically sophisticated handheld. Lower cost, long-enough battery operation, true portability, and access to home-console-like games made its tradeoffs useful. Its monochrome screen, pixel graphics, compact sound hardware, cartridge slot, and accessories later became materials for repairers, visual customizers, [[Chiptune]] musicians, and low-resolution photographers rather than merely obsolete constraints.

## Key Characteristics
- Portable 8-bit game system released in Japan on April 21, 1989.
- Competed through price, battery practicality, and usable mobility rather than specification leadership.
- Reportedly reached 110 million worldwide unit sales by the 2001 Game Boy Advance release.
- Supports aftermarket shells, backlights, buttons, artwork, and audio or synthesis modifications.
- Became a music platform through cartridge software such as Little Sound DJ and Nanoloop.
- Expanded into 128-by-112-pixel monochrome photography through the Game Boy Camera and Printer.
