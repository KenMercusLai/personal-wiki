## Overview
[[Gawker]] was a digital publication and blog network represented here through a quantitative retrospective at the end of its original operations and a secondary account of the private campaign financed by [[PeterThiel]] against it.

## Current Profile
Laurito presents Gawker as a high-volume, broad-beat publisher whose scale combined articles, automated posts, many contributors, large traffic totals, and a substantial commenting community. Its publishing peak and later decline show how staffing, writer incentives, direct audience access, acquisition history, and measurement infrastructure shaped the apparent output of a long-running digital publication. Holiday supplies a distinct external perspective: he reports that an unnamed proposer persuaded Thiel to finance a multi-year effort using investigators and lawyers to find actionable claims among Gawker's large archive. That account adds a private-power and legal-strategy dimension but does not independently explain the publication's closure or adjudicate the campaign.

## Key Characteristics
- Published media, politics, television, celebrity, and other broad-interest coverage.
- Produced 202,370 counted posts through August 2016, including automated Public Pool posts.
- Reached peak annual output in 2008 before layoffs and changing traffic and compensation incentives.
- Hosted work from 420 human, group, and bot publishing identities.
- Accumulated an estimated 7 billion pageviews and more than 3 billion visits.
- Treated Kinja comments and community participation as an important part of the product.
- Became the target of a privately financed investigation-and-litigation campaign in Holiday's reported account.

## Evidence
- Publishing scale and boundaries: [[closing-the-book-on-gawker-com]] reports 202,370 posts and explains the inclusion of sub-blogs and acquired Cityfile material while excluding shares from other blogs.
- Output and incentives: [[closing-the-book-on-gawker-com]] reports a 33,047-post peak in 2008 and associates the subsequent fall with layoffs, less direct traffic, and the end of per-post pay.
- Audience and community: [[closing-the-book-on-gawker-com]] estimates 7 billion pageviews, more than 3 billion visits, and more than 16 million comments.
- Contributor breadth: [[closing-the-book-on-gawker-com]] counts 420 people, groups, and bots that published on the site and its sub-blogs.
- External legal campaign: [[heres-the-technique-that-ambitious-people-use-to-get-what-they-want-ryanholiday]] reports a proposal to search Gawker's thousands of articles for causes of action using a dedicated company, former investigative reporters, lawyers, a multi-year timeline, and Thiel's funding.

## Qualifications
The operating profile relies on one farewell retrospective by Gawker's own data team. Historical analytics are approximate, tag counts contain acknowledged errors, the Cityfile acquisition complicates annual and author comparisons, and the captured Markdown omits the detailed writer, tag, and commenter tables described in the prose. Holiday's account is secondary, centered on the proposal's preparedness, and does not independently establish the complete litigation history, causation of closure, or the legality and ethics of either Gawker's publishing or the campaign against it.
