## Overview
[[Gawker]] was a digital publication and blog network represented here through a quantitative retrospective published as Gawker.com ended its original operations in August 2016.

## Current Profile
The source presents Gawker as a high-volume, broad-beat publisher whose scale combined articles, automated posts, many contributors, large traffic totals, and a substantial commenting community. Its publishing peak and later decline also show how staffing, writer incentives, direct audience access, acquisition history, and measurement infrastructure shaped the apparent output of a long-running digital publication.

## Key Characteristics
- Published media, politics, television, celebrity, and other broad-interest coverage.
- Produced 202,370 counted posts through August 2016, including automated Public Pool posts.
- Reached peak annual output in 2008 before layoffs and changing traffic and compensation incentives.
- Hosted work from 420 human, group, and bot publishing identities.
- Accumulated an estimated 7 billion pageviews and more than 3 billion visits.
- Treated Kinja comments and community participation as an important part of the product.

## Evidence
- Publishing scale and boundaries: [[closing-the-book-on-gawker-com]] reports 202,370 posts and explains the inclusion of sub-blogs and acquired Cityfile material while excluding shares from other blogs.
- Output and incentives: [[closing-the-book-on-gawker-com]] reports a 33,047-post peak in 2008 and associates the subsequent fall with layoffs, less direct traffic, and the end of per-post pay.
- Audience and community: [[closing-the-book-on-gawker-com]] estimates 7 billion pageviews, more than 3 billion visits, and more than 16 million comments.
- Contributor breadth: [[closing-the-book-on-gawker-com]] counts 420 people, groups, and bots that published on the site and its sub-blogs.

## Qualifications
This profile relies on one farewell retrospective by Gawker's own data team. Historical analytics are approximate, tag counts contain acknowledged errors, the Cityfile acquisition complicates annual and author comparisons, and the captured Markdown omits the detailed writer, tag, and commenter tables described in the prose. The source measures operational scale and community activity but does not independently assess editorial quality, business performance, social impact, or the causes of the site's closure.
