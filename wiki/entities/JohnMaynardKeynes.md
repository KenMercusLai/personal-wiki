## Overview
[[JohnMaynardKeynes]] appears in the source as a macroeconomics founder whose investment experience is used to warn against overreliance on macro forecasting.

## Current Profile
Wulc's source presents Keynes as both a brilliant economist and a practitioner who changed investing style. The article says his earlier currency and futures trades, based on macro analysis, often underperformed or lost money in the 1920s. It then contrasts that with his later shift toward buying undervalued stocks and holding them, which the source treats as evidence that even macro expertise does not make macro prediction easy to monetize.

## Key Characteristics
- Represents the gap between macroeconomic importance and macro-trading reliability.
- Initially used macro theory in currency and futures investing in the source's account.
- Later shifted toward undervalued companies and longer holding periods.
- Serves as a cautionary example for ordinary investors tempted by broad economic narratives.

## Evidence
- Macro status: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-1-shi-chang-gui-lu]] calls Keynes one of history's great economists and a founder of macroeconomics.
- Early investing trouble: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-1-shi-chang-gui-lu]] says Keynes's macro-based currency and futures trades underperformed or lost money during much of the 1920s.
- Later shift: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-1-shi-chang-gui-lu]] says Keynes later focused on undervalued stocks and long-term holding while managing funds for King's College.
- Lesson: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-1-shi-chang-gui-lu]] uses Keynes to argue that macro factors are important but too complex for most investors to master.
