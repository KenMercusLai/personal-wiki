## Overview
[[SoundCloud]] is represented as an upload and discovery platform with a particular 2018 role in surfacing emerging hip-hop, rap, and trap.

## Current Profile
Jen Malone and Marcus Moore both distinguish SoundCloud from Spotify as a place where emerging rappers and related artists publish early work. Malone also connects the platform to music-supervision licensing, while Moore treats its much-mocked “SoundCloud rapper” category as a possible signal of the next wave.

## Key Characteristics
- Hosts early or emerging artist uploads.
- Has particular discovery relevance for hip-hop, rap, and trap in the source.
- Supplies candidates for professional licensing and editorial attention.
- Complements rather than replaces Spotify, Bandcamp, email, shows, and networks.

## Evidence
- Genre role: [[finding-new-music-in-the-algorithm-age-the-outline]] says artists in hip-hop, rap, and trap may post mixtapes, songs, and records there rather than on Spotify.
- Licensing use: [[finding-new-music-in-the-algorithm-age-the-outline]] reports Malone and her co-supervisor licensing bands found through the platform.
- Emerging-wave judgment: [[finding-new-music-in-the-algorithm-age-the-outline]] records Moore's view that SoundCloud rappers can indicate what comes next.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on two professionals' 2018 accounts and should not be generalized across genres, regions, or the platform's later evolution. No catalog, licensing, audience, or artist-outcome data is provided.
