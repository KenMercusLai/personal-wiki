## Overview
[[JustinBauer]] is identified in the source as vice-president of product at [[Amplitude]] and the author of a practitioner comparison between strong and weak product teams.

## Current Profile
Bauer's available argument treats product-team quality as an operating system rather than individual talent alone. He combines direct customer understanding, product-analytics access, strategy and autonomy, iterative experimentation, cross-functional accountability, and customer-outcome measurement while criticizing requirements handoffs, analyst dependence, review layers, functional silos, and output metrics.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames customer understanding as shared expertise across product management, design, and engineering.
- Advocates analytical self-sufficiency while preserving vision and judgment beyond data.
- Connects strategic focus with autonomy for teams closest to customers.
- Treats small experiments, failure tolerance, and post-launch measurement as conditions for learning.
- Distinguishes customer and business outcomes from delivery activity and feature output.

## Evidence
- Role and authorship: [[good-product-teams-vs-bad-product-teams]] identifies Bauer as Amplitude's vice-president of product and attributes the essay to him.
- Team model: [[good-product-teams-vs-bad-product-teams]] contrasts direct customer contact, shared accountability, strategic autonomy, iterative learning, and outcome measurement with their organizational opposites.
- Analytics stance: [[good-product-teams-vs-bad-product-teams]] says data should inform decisions and help explain user journeys rather than validate predetermined choices.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one 2018 practitioner essay and its author biography. It does not establish Bauer's broader career, later views, the results of teams he managed, or whether the article's prescriptions generalize beyond digital-product organizations. His employer sold product analytics, creating a commercial perspective that should remain visible when evaluating the emphasis on analytics access.
