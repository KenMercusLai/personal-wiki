## Overview
[[500Startups]] is an early-stage investment platform and accelerator represented through both its high-volume portfolio strategy and its role preparing a founder cohort for fundraising.

## Current Profile
McClure describes 500 Startups investing in more than 1,000 companies over five years and presents the platform as a counterexample to traditional small VC portfolios: many failures are expected because broad exposure creates more chances to capture rare centaur and unicorn outcomes. Spinks adds the company-level accelerator view. [[Feast]] joined Batch 6 and spent several months preparing to raise capital, but more than 70 investor conversations produced no durable commitment. Together, the sources show the platform operating on both sides of venture formation: building a broad investment portfolio and coaching individual startups toward fundraising, without proving that every participant fits the venture model or secures a round.

## Key Characteristics
- Operates as a high-volume startup investor in the source.
- Uses a large portfolio to improve exposure to rare power-law winners.
- Treats many failures as expected portfolio behavior rather than evidence that the strategy failed.
- Provides the experiential basis for McClure's recommended early-stage portfolio sizes.
- Runs accelerator batches that prepare participating startups for venture fundraising.

## Evidence
- Investment scale: [[99-vc-problems-but-a-batch-aint-one-why-portfolio-size-matters-for-returns]] says 500 Startups invested in more than 1,000 companies over five years.
- Outcome pattern: [[99-vc-problems-but-a-batch-aint-one-why-portfolio-size-matters-for-returns]] says most investments fail or return less than 1X, while a few become centaurs or unicorns.
- Portfolio-size model: [[99-vc-problems-but-a-batch-aint-one-why-portfolio-size-matters-for-returns]] uses the platform's experience to argue for 100-200 or more seed-stage investments.
- Accelerator role: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] says Feast joined Batch 6 and worked for months to become ready to raise.
- Fundraising limit: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] reports that Feast spoke with more than 70 investors but closed no durable commitment.

## Qualifications
McClure's source is promotional and argumentative, not a full independent audit of 500 Startups' realized fund returns, and its outcome rates are experience-based assumptions rather than complete audited performance data. Spinks supplies one participant's retrospective rather than a representative accelerator outcome study; Feast's failure to raise does not establish the program's general effectiveness or the quality of its advice. The retained cohort photograph conflicts with the article's CMX Summit caption, so it supports the accelerator setting but not a precise event identity.

## What Changed
- Expanded the profile from portfolio construction to include the platform's accelerator and fundraising-preparation role.
- Added a participant case showing that accelerator preparation does not guarantee a completed funding round.
