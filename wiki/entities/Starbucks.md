## Overview
[[Starbucks]] is used in the Vox article as a coffee-service example where visible barista attention and preparation performance can matter to customers even when faster automation is possible.

## Current Profile
The source presents Starbucks as a mass coffee chain that still depends on experience rather than only beverage delivery. Its management reportedly told baristas to stop making multiple drinks at once after complaints that coffee preparation had become too mechanized. The article interprets that choice as evidence that customers buy a performed service, not just caffeine.

## Key Characteristics
- Coffee chain used as a service-experience case.
- Illustrates tension between operational efficiency and perceived personal attention.
- Shows how slower labor can be part of the product's value proposition.

## Evidence
- Efficiency tradeoff: [[automation-is-making-human-labor-more-valuable-than-ever-the-new-new-economy]] says Starbucks baristas were told to stop making multiple drinks at once despite slower lines.
- Experience value: [[automation-is-making-human-labor-more-valuable-than-ever-the-new-new-economy]] argues that customers go to Starbucks partly for the experience and perceived personal attention.
- Human-premium case: [[automation-is-making-human-labor-more-valuable-than-ever-the-new-new-economy]] uses Starbucks to explain why coffee work persists as machines improve.

## Qualifications
The source uses Starbucks as an illustrative 2012-era example rather than a full company profile. It does not evaluate Starbucks' later operations, labor relations, pricing, or store strategy.
