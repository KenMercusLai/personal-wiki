## Overview
[[PaulineRoseClance]] is a clinical psychologist represented here as a co-originator, with [[SuzanneImes]], of the impostor-phenomenon construct.

## Current Profile
The source describes Clance's early women-focused account and her later acknowledgment that men also experience impostor feelings. Her practical contribution centers on the perfectionism mechanism: fear of exposure can lead to procrastination or excessive preparation, and success can then reinforce the belief that extreme effort was indispensable. She recommends accepting “good enough” performance and deliberately celebrating success.

## Key Characteristics
- Co-originated the impostor-phenomenon construct in the 1970s.
- Connects impostor feelings with perfectionistic procrastination and overpreparation.
- Revises the construct's early women-only framing in light of later evidence.
- Recommends bounded standards and rewards for success as ways to weaken the cycle.

## Evidence
- Construct history: [[feel-like-a-fraud]] credits Clance and Suzanne Imes with first describing the phenomenon among high-achieving women.
- Mechanism: [[feel-like-a-fraud]] attributes the procrastination/overpreparation pattern and self-reinforcing cycle partly to Clance's account.
- Practical response: [[feel-like-a-fraud]] reports her advice to do tasks well enough and celebrate completed work.

## Qualifications
The profile is based on one secondary 2013 magazine article. It does not establish the full development of Clance's scholarship, the current psychometric standing of the construct, or treatment efficacy, and the early gender framing was later broadened.
