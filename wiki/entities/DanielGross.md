## Overview
[[DanielGross]] is the author of a 2017 guide for engineers and data scientists evaluating the business quality of prospective ML/AI employers.

## Current Profile
In the available source, Gross approaches an ML/AI job interview as commercial due diligence. He asks candidates to look past technical sophistication and test the customer need, improvement over prior practice, depth of user learning, present revenue model, acquisition economics, derived market size, and business defensibility.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames candidate evaluation around business fundamentals rather than technical novelty alone.
- Uses demanding heuristics, including order-of-magnitude value improvement and explicit acquisition economics, to expose weak answers.
- Treats customer understanding, distribution, and network effects as more durable signals than an algorithm by itself.

## Evidence
- Business-first diligence: [[business-questions-engineers-should-ask-when-interviewing-at-ml-ai-companies]] supplies seven interview questions covering need, alternatives, customers, revenue, growth, market size, and defensibility.
- Comparative-value threshold: [[business-questions-engineers-should-ask-when-interviewing-at-ml-ai-companies]] asks whether the product is newly possible, roughly ten times better, or roughly ten times cheaper.
- Growth and moat reasoning: [[business-questions-engineers-should-ask-when-interviewing-at-ml-ai-companies]] asks for channels, acquisition cost, lifetime value, and an advantage beyond the algorithm.

## Qualifications
The wiki currently contains only this short 2017 source by Gross. It establishes his argument in that publication context but does not provide a broader biography, independently tested success rate for the checklist, or evidence that every heuristic applies across company stages and business models.
