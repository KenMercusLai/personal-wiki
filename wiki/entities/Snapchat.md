## Overview
[[Snapchat]] appears as a camera-centered social product whose Stories, ephemeral messages, Discover, and Lenses combine collection, low-stakes self-expression, present-oriented exchange, brand content, and real-time transformation.

## Current Profile
The sources converge on Snapchat as more than a disappearing-message utility. Basche calls it [[AuthenticallyMobile]] because capture, editing, distribution, and consumption depend on the same always-carried camera-and-network device; its camera default privileges collecting what is happening now over entering a feed. Evans generalizes the product mechanism through [[ImageSensorAsInput]]: Stories relax inherited assumptions about saved and linear media, while Lenses join sensor, screen, and software into an expressive interaction loop. Discover supplied an opt-in brand surface, and acquisitions were associated with mapping, hardware, avatars, search, codes, and computer vision.

Ephemerality is presented as a design mechanism rather than deletion alone. It can reduce archive and persistent-profile pressure, making mundane or temporary selves easier to share and enabling what Spiegel called “jumping into” a friend’s experience. Danco places the same mechanics in a scarcity argument: the front camera and ephemerality made varied expression abundant, giving Snapchat a wedge into [[HereAndNowMedia]]. He nevertheless argues that a fleeting view does not automatically create a durable shared place. Facebook’s copied formats, broad networks, and Onavo telemetry add the competitive boundary.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses a camera-first default and unified sensor-screen device to organize interaction around collection, transformation, and communication.
- Uses ephemerality to lower persistence pressure and support temporary self-expression.
- Originated Stories, whose copied implementations had different fit across Instagram and Messenger.
- Uses Lenses to transform faces and scenes into expressive messages in real time.
- Provides opt-in brand content through Discover and sponsored visual effects.
- Combined internal development with acquisitions associated with mapping, hardware, avatars, search, codes, and computer vision.
- Approaches shared presence without necessarily creating a stable digital “there.”

## Qualifications
The sources are mostly 2016-2017 snapshots and do not cover Snapchat’s complete later history. Claims about authenticity, intimacy, identity variance, product empathy, camera-platform control, causal competitive effects, and presence are outside interpretations without comparative behavioral evidence. Evans's sensor-screen account explains a product form but does not prove that Snapchat originated it, that users understood it that way, or that Snap would control later imaging or AR platforms. Ephemerality can reduce persistence pressure but does not guarantee privacy, safety, truthfulness, beneficial outcomes, or freedom from screenshots and platform retention. Acquisition timing and product resemblance do not establish technical causation or returns, and Onavo reporting lacks raw telemetry.
