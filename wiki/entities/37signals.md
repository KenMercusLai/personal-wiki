## Overview
[[37signals]] is represented in this wiki as the software company where [[DavidHeinemeierHansson]] tested AI customer-support systems and observed both impressive handling of difficult cases and seriously wrong answers.

## Current Profile
The source uses 37signals as a small practitioner case inside a broader argument about [[AutomationErrorTolerance]]. AI support could answer hard questions well, but its occasional severe errors raised an unresolved product threshold: customers may punish authoritative machine nonsense more strongly than a comparable human mistake. The source does not identify the systems, test design, deployment status, volume, or measured error rate.

## Key Characteristics
- Provides the article's direct operational context rather than a hypothetical domain.
- Experimented with AI systems for customer-support interactions.
- Observed a mix of strong difficult-case performance and materially wrong answers.
- Treated customer trust and negative word of mouth as constraints on acceptable automation error.

## Evidence
- Experiment context: [[dhh-its-easier-to-forgive-a-human-than-a-robot]] says 37signals tried several AI customer-support systems.
- Mixed performance: [[dhh-its-easier-to-forgive-a-human-than-a-robot]] reports that the systems handled some hard cases well and got other answers seriously wrong.
- Adoption concern: [[dhh-its-easier-to-forgive-a-human-than-a-robot]] asks whether even a one-percent machine error rate could be psychologically unacceptable despite worse human performance.
