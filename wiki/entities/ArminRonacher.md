## Overview
[[ArminRonacher]] is a programmer and Python-community member represented here through an argument for welcoming people whose first programs are created with [[ChatGPT]] or coding agents.

## Current Profile
Ronacher describes himself as a product of the Python community and credits early learning on Python IRC channels with shaping his career. After conversations with AI-assisted newcomers and a return to EuroPython, he argues for defining programmers by what they create rather than whether they typed the implementation by hand. His main concern is not the existence of low-quality first programs but the missing human connection when an AI, rather than a person, provides the initial route into programming.

He asks established communities to treat AI-first programmers as a new participant cohort, make conferences and communities visible to them, offer mentorship and engineering on-ramps, and resist allowing proprietary vibe-coding tools to become their only environment. This is a community prescription grounded in personal observation rather than a measured account of adoption or learning outcomes.

## Key Characteristics
- Identifies strongly with the Python community and credits peer learning for his early career development.
- Uses an activity-based definition of programmer that includes people building software through AI agents.
- Predicts AI will expand the programmer population rather than simply reduce programming work.
- Advocates welcome, mentorship, conferences, and community on-ramps for AI-first newcomers.
- Treats absent human connection and tool captivity as larger long-term risks than imperfect first code alone.

## Evidence
- Community formation: [[welcoming-the-next-generation-of-programmers]] says Ronacher learned from others on Python IRC and can name the person who brought him into Python.
- Newcomer observation: [[welcoming-the-next-generation-of-programmers]] recounts conversations with people entering programming through AI, including an air traffic controller using ChatGPT to make work easier.
- Identity boundary: [[welcoming-the-next-generation-of-programmers]] argues that someone who creates a program with an agent is a programmer.
- Participation forecast: [[welcoming-the-next-generation-of-programmers]] predicts that AI will bring more people into programming than other recent interventions.
- Social prescription: [[welcoming-the-next-generation-of-programmers]] asks the Python community to reach out, mentor, create on-ramps, and connect solitary AI use to shared engineering learning.

## Qualifications
The available profile is limited to one first-person essay. It does not establish the scale or demographics of AI-mediated programming, compare learning or code-quality outcomes, measure Python's share of generated code, or test whether community participation reduces security, maintenance, or tool-dependence risks. The article's prediction and recommendations should therefore be read as Ronacher's informed community judgment rather than demonstrated population effects.
