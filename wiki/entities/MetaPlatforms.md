## Overview
[[MetaPlatforms]] is represented by one source as the corporate home of Facebook, Instagram, messaging, advertising, Reality Labs, and a proposed large-scale AI compute buildout.

## Current Profile
[[a-script-for-mark-zuckerberg]] is a fictional speech written by [[BenThompson]], not an actual statement from Meta or [[MarkZuckerberg]]. Within that proposal, Meta's durable identity is an advertising-funded consumer company built around human connection, entertainment, discovery, and commerce rather than a general-purpose developer platform, enterprise productivity vendor, or permanent cloud provider.

The strategy couples AI investment to the existing business. Recommendation models can extend attention, ad models can improve targeting and creative production, and generative experiences could expand sellable inventory. Thompson also treats investment as defensive because Meta's products and revenues are digital and therefore exposed to AI-mediated substitution.

The proposed capital-allocation control is temporary external compute rental with recall rights. Rental proceeds would help fund a larger buildout, while observable market prices would establish a [[ComputeHurdleRate]]: Meta should reclaim capacity only when an internal consumer or advertising use is expected to earn more. The mechanism is a recommendation, not an announced program, and its operational feasibility and economics are untested in the source.

## Key Characteristics
- Operates consumer social, media, messaging, advertising, and experimental hardware businesses.
- Is interpreted as an entertainment-and-advertising company whose products remain free to users.
- Uses machine learning for content recommendation and ad performance, with generative AI presented as a further expansion path.
- Can finance capital-intensive AI infrastructure from a high-cash-generation advertising core.
- Is proposed to rent spare compute temporarily while retaining the right to reclaim it for higher-return internal demand.

## Evidence
- Business identity and strategic boundary: [[a-script-for-mark-zuckerberg]] argues that Meta should prioritize connection, entertainment, shopping, and advertising over general platform ownership or enterprise productivity.
- AI revenue mechanism: [[a-script-for-mark-zuckerberg]] links machine learning and LLMs to recommendation, targeting, creative generation, prediction, and larger advertising inventory.
- Capital capacity: [[a-script-for-mark-zuckerberg]] presents the asset-light advertising business as the funding engine for tens of billions of dollars of AI capex.
- Allocation discipline: [[a-script-for-mark-zuckerberg]] proposes short-term compute rentals whose market price determines when internal use creates more value.
- Hardware option: [[a-script-for-mark-zuckerberg]] argues that prior virtual-reality work could support devices suited to ambient AI access, while conceding weak near-term returns from Reality Labs.

## Qualifications
The only source is an analyst's fictional speech. It synthesizes and advocates a strategy but does not establish Meta's actual intentions, internal economics, contractual ability to recall rented compute, model performance, incremental ad revenue, capex returns, or user acceptance. It also makes an affirmative case for advertising without resolving privacy, consent, competition, content-quality, or well-being concerns. Facebook's long historical record is maintained separately in [[Facebook]]; this page should not be read as replacing that entity or proving a clean discontinuity between Facebook and Meta.
