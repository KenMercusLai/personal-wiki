## Overview
[[Roku]] is presented as the independent company that received Netflix's near-launch streaming-player team and technology when [[ReedHastings]] decided [[Netflix]] should not ship first-party hardware.

## Current Profile
The sources trace Roku's origin to [[ProjectGriffin]], an internal Netflix Player effort that had reached late validation and manufacturing preparation by December 2007. Spinning the team out preserved the device project while separating it from Netflix's need to integrate its streaming service across competing hardware manufacturers. [[NeilHunt]] independently reinforces the strategic mechanism: concurrent Xbox work showed the appeal of broad device partnerships, and an in-house player would make Netflix a less neutral counterparty. The retained product images in the Griffin history make the continuity visible, although visual resemblance alone does not establish the complete technical lineage.

## Key Characteristics
- Emerged from the spinout of Netflix's Project Griffin team and player work.
- Preserved a dedicated streaming-hardware effort that Netflix no longer wanted inside its service business.
- Was led by former Griffin leader [[AnthonyWood]].
- Occupied the device layer while Netflix pursued broader cross-device distribution.

## Evidence
- Spinout: [[inside-netflixs-project-griffin-the-forgotten-history-of-roku-under]] reports that Hastings chose to move the Netflix Player team into Roku rather than launch it internally.
- Leadership: [[inside-netflixs-project-griffin-the-forgotten-history-of-roku-under]] identifies Griffin leader Anthony Wood as Roku CEO.
- Product continuity: [[inside-netflixs-project-griffin-the-forgotten-history-of-roku-under]] retains photographs of both the Netflix Player prototype and a Roku player.
- Participant corroboration: [[neil-hunt-on-netflix-and-the-story-of-netflix-streaming-internet-history-podcast]] says Netflix separated the Roku effort to make partnerships with Xbox, PlayStation, LG, and Samsung easier.

## Qualifications
Both sources focus on the Netflix decision rather than a complete corporate history of Roku. The Griffin history is a later reconstruction, while Hunt speaks from memory and is tentative about exact launch order. Neither specifies the spinout's legal structure, financing, intellectual-property terms, ownership, complete product-development sequence, or Roku's later business performance.

## What Changed
- Added Hunt's first-person corroboration of device-partner neutrality as the spinout rationale.
- Preserved uncertainty around exact chronology and the absence of legal and financial spinout detail.
