## Overview
[[JonathanHsu]] is the author of the source's [[SocialCapital]] diligence framework for decomposing active-user growth into acquisition, retention, resurrection, and churn.

## Current Profile
The source presents Hsu as an investor-practitioner sharing one part of a broader quantitative approach to product-market-fit diligence. He positions the method as useful both for evaluating potential investments and for portfolio-company operating reviews, while explicitly limiting it to traction rather than treating it as a substitute for assessing team, market, vision, competition, or technology.

## Key Characteristics
- Applies accounting identities to startup user-growth analysis.
- Treats topline user counts as insufficient evidence without retention and churn decomposition.
- Connects investment diligence to recurring product operating practice.
- Presents metric choice as dependent on product behavior and usage cadence.

## Evidence
- Diligence role: [[diligence-at-social-capital-part-1-accounting-for-user-growth]] says Social Capital used the approach to assess demonstrated product-market fit in companies with existing users.
- Operating role: [[diligence-at-social-capital-part-1-accounting-for-user-growth]] says portfolio companies used the concepts to monitor growth and evolving fit.
- Analytical method: [[diligence-at-social-capital-part-1-accounting-for-user-growth]] derives net MAU change from new, resurrected, and churned users and overlays retention and a user-growth quick ratio.
- Scope boundary: [[diligence-at-social-capital-part-1-accounting-for-user-growth]] says the quantitative traction material covers only one subset of diligence.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one article published in 2015. The source note says the author later left Social Capital, but it does not establish his full career history, the firm's formal investment process, or whether the exact framework remains in use.
