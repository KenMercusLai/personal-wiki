## Overview
[[PiaSilva]] is represented as an entrepreneur and former Forbes contributor who describes redesigning her work around a five-hour daily window she calls “SpainBrain.”

## Current Profile
In the available source, Silva tests whether a business can continue during extended travel with only a few late-afternoon working hours. She later adopts a 1-6 weekday schedule built on delegation, advance planning, narrow task selection, and distraction control, arguing that leisure and unrelated learning can improve business judgment and creativity.

## Key Characteristics
- Challenges early starts and visible busyness as reliable evidence of useful work.
- Uses a hard time boundary to force prioritization and reduce procrastination.
- Treats delegation and documented handoff as prerequisites for owner independence.
- Presents leisure, reading, and unrelated learning as productive inputs rather than residual rewards.
- Writes from first-person entrepreneurial experience rather than comparative research.

## Evidence
- Schedule experiment: [[pia-silva-9-5-is-out-try-the-1-6-instead]] describes a two-month Europe trip followed by seven months of a 1-6 schedule.
- Operating method: [[pia-silva-9-5-is-out-try-the-1-6-instead]] links the shorter day to outsourcing, training videos, explicit objectives, weekly planning, and blocking social-media and news procrastination.
- Reframing leisure: [[pia-silva-9-5-is-out-try-the-1-6-instead]] says free time supported reading, thinking, skill learning, and ideas useful to the business.

## Qualifications
The profile is derived from one self-authored 2018 essay. It contains no business metrics, comparison group, employee or customer perspective, and no evidence that the named schedule rather than autonomy, staffing, travel context, novelty, or other factors produced the reported output.
