## Overview
[[GeoffreyHinton]] is presented as a neural-network researcher whose long commitment to connectionist methods links early Boltzmann-machine and backpropagation work with deep belief networks and the AlexNet breakthrough.

## Current Profile
The source uses Hinton's career as the narrative spine of deep learning's revival. It describes his persistence through a period when neural networks were academically marginal, credits him with important work on Boltzmann machines, backpropagation, and layer-wise pretraining, and follows his lab through AlexNet and the acquisition of DNNResearch by Google. This profile records the article's historical framing while avoiding its implication that any one researcher solely invented methods with broader lineages.

## Key Characteristics
- Pursued neural-network research during periods of strong skepticism toward connectionism.
- Contributed to Boltzmann-machine research and the practical revival of multilayer neural-network training.
- Used deep belief networks and layer-wise pretraining to help make deep architectures trainable in the 2000s.
- Led the group associated with AlexNet's 2012 ImageNet result and later DNNResearch.
- Is portrayed as believing brain-inspired approaches may produce systems exceeding human intelligence.
