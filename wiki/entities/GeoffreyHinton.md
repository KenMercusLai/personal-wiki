## Overview
[[GeoffreyHinton]] is presented as a neural-network researcher whose long commitment to connectionist methods links early Boltzmann-machine and backpropagation work with deep belief networks and the AlexNet breakthrough.

## Current Profile
The sources use Hinton's career as a narrative spine of deep learning's revival. They describe his persistence from 1970s doctoral work through periods when neural networks were academically marginal, credit him with important work on Boltzmann machines, multilayer error correction, and layer-wise pretraining, and follow his lab through AlexNet and the acquisition of DNNResearch by Google. The Fortune feature adds his experimental-psychology motivation, collaboration with Yann LeCun, influence on Microsoft's speech-recognition work, and the 2012 ImageNet result that shifted opinion. This profile records that central role while avoiding the implication that any one researcher solely invented methods with broader lineages.

## Key Characteristics
- Pursued neural-network research during periods of strong skepticism toward connectionism.
- Preferred experience-driven neural learning to logic-first accounts of intelligence, drawing on an experimental-psychology background.
- Contributed to Boltzmann-machine research and the practical revival of multilayer neural-network training.
- Used deep belief networks and layer-wise pretraining to help make deep architectures trainable in the 2000s.
- Led the group associated with AlexNet's 2012 ImageNet result and later DNNResearch.
- Connected academic work to commercial speech and vision adoption through Microsoft, Google, and the post-ImageNet hiring race.

## Qualifications
This profile is derived from two secondary popular histories, not a complete biography or primary publication record. Their language can assign Hinton singular credit where backpropagation, convolutional networks, and other methods have longer, multi-author histories. The Fortune feature also simplifies benchmark metrics, the division of labor in the AlexNet team, and links from a research visit to product improvement. Claims about disciplinary consensus, acquisition bidding, and Hinton's forecasts should be checked against primary records before use as definitive history.
