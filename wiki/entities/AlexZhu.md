## Overview
[[AlexZhu]] is represented as the founder of [[MusicalLy]] who deliberately selected young American early adopters and described the company's continuous user involvement as participatory design.

## Current Profile
The 2017 source contrasts Zhu's background as a Chinese immigrant and enterprise-software builder with Musical.ly's largely young American audience. Rather than treating that gap as founder intuition, he says the team observed teenagers listening to music and creating decorated phone videos, combined those behaviors into a lip-sync product, and stayed immersed in user conversation through large WeChat and WhatsApp groups. Major feature ideas, mockups, and wireframes were presented to users before coding.

Zhu's account treats audience choice as strategic. He argues that American teenagers had time, creativity, dense school connections, and familiarity with video creation, making them attractive early adopters for a new social platform. That is a founder explanation of product strategy, not independent evidence that the audience traits caused Musical.ly's reported growth.

## Key Characteristics
- Founded Musical.ly after an earlier career in enterprise software.
- Framed continuous target-user conversation as participatory design.
- Presented major ideas and design artifacts to users before implementation.
- Deliberately targeted young American early adopters.
- Used observation of adjacent mobile behaviors to form the lip-sync product concept.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one 2017 journalistic source and Zhu's retrospective statements. It does not provide research transcripts, participant selection rules, decision logs, rejected ideas, compensation or safeguarding practices, controlled product tests, or evidence separating participatory design from timing, distribution, capital, celebrity culture, or network effects.
