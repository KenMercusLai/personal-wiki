## Overview
[[HenrikKniberg]] is an agile and lean practitioner represented here as the author of the skateboard-to-car product-development metaphor and the [[EarliestTestableUsableLovable]] vocabulary.

## Current Profile
Kniberg argues for treating uncertain product work as a learning system. Teams should identify the customer's underlying need, deliver the cheapest coherent thing that real users can test, and use feedback to revise both requirements and the envisioned solution. He presents the popular vehicle sequence as a deliberately simplified metaphor, not as a literal method for manufacturing cars or a claim that early users will be delighted by every release.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames iterative delivery around complete customer outcomes rather than partially assembled solutions.
- Treats early releases primarily as learning devices.
- Prefers explicit testable, usable, and lovable thresholds to the ambiguous MVP label.
- Uses Spotify, Minecraft, Swedish police software, and Lego as practitioner cases.
- Qualifies his own vehicle drawing as a metaphor that is often misread outside its original context.

## Qualifications
The profile is based on one first-person practitioner essay and its appended comments, not an independent biography or outcome study. Its examples are illustrative and retrospectively selected, and the source does not establish that early feedback alone caused their outcomes. Commenters also contest whether the skateboard sequence accurately represents Lean Startup's broader MVP definition.
