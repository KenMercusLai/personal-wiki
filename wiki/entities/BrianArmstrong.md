## Overview
[[BrianArmstrong]] appears as the founder and CEO of [[Coinbase]], motivated by Bitcoin's promise of state-resistant money but willing to build through regulated, mainstream financial infrastructure.

## Current Profile
Fortune portrays Armstrong as an introverted, improvement-obsessed founder who arrived early to Bitcoin after reading Satoshi Nakamoto's paper in 2009 and seeing inflation effects in Argentina. His answer was not a purist self-custody tool, but a simpler exchange and custody service that made buying crypto feel familiar to ordinary users. As Coinbase scaled, he had to move from technical founder to operator: hiring experienced executives, absorbing support and market-structure failures, working with regulators, and trying to diversify Coinbase beyond trading fees.

## Key Characteristics
- Saw Bitcoin as digital gold and a way to store or transfer wealth beyond fragile state monetary systems.
- Built Coinbase around usability: bank-account funding, simplified purchase flow, and hosted custody.
- Preferred regulatory legitimacy even when that irritated anti-government parts of the crypto world.
- Learned publicly through self-improvement habits, mentorship seeking, and feedback requests.
- Had to mature as a CEO after 2017 exposed Coinbase's support, engineering, fraud, and market-structure gaps.
- Set a large adoption ambition, saying he wanted crypto used by a billion people within five years.

## Evidence
- Motivation: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] describes Armstrong reading Nakamoto's paper in 2009 and later connecting Bitcoin to inflation he saw in Argentina.
- Product judgment: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] says he left Airbnb in 2012 to let customers use bank accounts to buy cryptocurrency and rely on Coinbase custody.
- Compliance posture: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] describes Coinbase's know-your-customer compliance, conservative coin listings, broker-dealer pursuit, and banking-charter talks under Armstrong.
- Self-improvement style: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] quotes investors and anecdotes about Armstrong's follow-up questions, reading habits, pilot license, and company-wide feedback requests.
- CEO learning curve: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] reports outages, fraud pressure, employee exhaustion, later hiring, and Armstrong's shift toward collaborative truth-seeking leadership.

## Qualifications
This profile is limited to Fortune's 2018 account. It does not cover Armstrong's later public-company leadership, political choices, product decisions, layoffs, regulatory disputes, or later crypto market cycles.
