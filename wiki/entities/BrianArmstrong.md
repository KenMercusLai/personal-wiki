## Overview
[[BrianArmstrong]] appears as the founder and CEO of [[Coinbase]], combining a regulated mainstream-crypto strategy with strong, explicitly risk-averse views about hiring quality during hypergrowth.

## Current Profile
Fortune portrays Armstrong as an introverted, improvement-obsessed founder who arrived early to Bitcoin after reading Satoshi Nakamoto's paper in 2009 and seeing inflation effects in Argentina. His answer was not a purist self-custody tool, but a simpler exchange and custody service that made buying crypto feel familiar to ordinary users. As Coinbase scaled, he had to move from technical founder to operator: hiring experienced executives, absorbing support and market-structure failures, working with regulators, and trying to diversify Coinbase beyond trading fees. His bar-raiser article makes that operating style concrete in hiring: distribute responsibility across the panel, give a trained independent reviewer veto power, test both skills and values, and prefer rejecting an uncertain good candidate to accepting a harmful hire.

## Key Characteristics
- Saw Bitcoin as digital gold and a way to store or transfer wealth beyond fragile state monetary systems.
- Built Coinbase around usability: bank-account funding, simplified purchase flow, and hosted custody.
- Preferred regulatory legitimacy even when that irritated anti-government parts of the crypto world.
- Learned publicly through self-improvement habits, mentorship seeking, and feedback requests.
- Had to mature as a CEO after 2017 exposed Coinbase's support, engineering, fraud, and market-structure gaps.
- Favored a high-conviction hiring system that treated false positives as more costly than false negatives during hypergrowth.

## Evidence
- Motivation: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] describes Armstrong reading Nakamoto's paper in 2009 and later connecting Bitcoin to inflation he saw in Argentina.
- Product judgment: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] says he left Airbnb in 2012 to let customers use bank accounts to buy cryptocurrency and rely on Coinbase custody.
- Compliance posture: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] describes Coinbase's know-your-customer compliance, conservative coin listings, broker-dealer pursuit, and banking-charter talks under Armstrong.
- Self-improvement style: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] quotes investors and anecdotes about Armstrong's follow-up questions, reading habits, pilot license, and company-wide feedback requests.
- CEO learning curve: [[coinbase-wants-to-be-too-big-to-fail-fortune]] reports outages, fraud pressure, employee exhaustion, later hiring, and Armstrong's shift toward collaborative truth-seeking leadership.
- Hiring-risk judgment: [[bar-raisers-at-coinbase-if-youre-not-a-hell-yes-youre-a-no]] presents his case for dual skill-and-values screening, an independent veto, shared panel responsibility, and rounding uncertainty down to rejection.

## Qualifications
This profile is limited to two 2018 accounts. It does not cover Armstrong's later public-company leadership, political choices, product decisions, layoffs, regulatory disputes, or later crypto market cycles. The bar-raiser essay states his intended process but supplies no hiring outcomes, candidate perspectives, calibration data, or evidence that inspiration, interviewer energy, immediate productivity, and perceived superiority are reliable or bias-resistant predictors.

## What Changed
- Added Armstrong's explicit hiring-risk preference and use of an independent veto as part of his hypergrowth operating model.
- Distinguished the program's stated intent from evidence that its subjective tests worked fairly or effectively.
