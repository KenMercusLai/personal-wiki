## Overview
[[MengYan]] is a Chinese investment-industry practitioner and writer represented here through an essay on fund herding, investor behavior, retirement-account design, and fund advisory.

## Current Profile
In [[da-shui-yu-rong-qi]], Meng Yan draws on roughly two decades around the fund industry while explicitly presenting the argument as personal research rather than regulation advocacy or a definitive industry judgment. He analyzes behavior through institutional incentives, connects mutual-fund concentration to the origin and routing of money, and advocates user-aligned defaults that make long-term diversified investing easier without demanding constant expert decisions from savers.

The essay also connects this institutional argument to [[YouZhiYouXing]] and to the “four pots of money” framing that Meng Yan says he and collaborators proposed in 2018. His stated ambition is to reduce Chinese stock-market volatility and help more participants share in long-term economic growth, while acknowledging that the outcome is uncertain.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses incentive and environment analysis to explain repeated group behavior rather than reducing it to individual character or professionalism.
- Frames fund-industry economics through the interaction of investor demand, distribution, assets under management, fees, and compensation.
- Prefers defaults and choice architecture that protect long-term goals without requiring every investor to master product selection.
- Connects product-interface questions to household liquidity needs, forced selling, and downstream market behavior.
- States strong reform preferences while acknowledging personal blind spots, institutional differences, and uncertainty about eventual outcomes.
