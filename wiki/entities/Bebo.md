## Overview
[[Bebo]] is represented as a student-focused social network whose small team deliberately applied lessons from Friendster before the company was sold to AOL in 2008.

## Current Profile
According to first employee [[JimScheinman]], Bebo focused on high-school and college users in six English-speaking countries, began with features intended to build critical mass, and protected site speed before attempting large additions. The team used proven technology, shipped small experiments frequently, A/B-tested viral mechanics, and sometimes disabled those mechanics outside target countries to keep growth aligned with its intended audience.

Scheinman describes a six-person team reaching one billion page views, slow and selective hiring, engineers acting as product managers, two-week feature cycles, stable founder leadership under Michael Birch, and delayed advertising followed by integrated brand deals after scale. These are attributed historical claims from a successful operator retrospective rather than independently verified causal evidence.

## Key Characteristics
- Focused its initial market on high-school and college users in six English-speaking countries.
- Protected performance and used proven technologies while the network grew.
- Shipped small product experiments frequently and removed features that did not work.
- Treated virality as team-wide, measured product work and constrained it geographically when necessary.
- Used a small, selectively hired team with engineers carrying product responsibility.
- Delayed advertising until it had greater scale and engagement.
