## Overview
[[AppStore]] is Apple's iOS app marketplace, presented as a distribution and visibility platform for [[Instapaper]], an economically concentrated marketplace that repeatedly out-earned [[GooglePlay]] despite lower download volume, and a chart-mediated discovery surface where top rank can decay quickly.

## Current Profile
The Instapaper article uses the App Store as a marker of mobile-platform timing: Instapaper joined at launch in July 2008, moved through paid and freemium pricing, and later received iPhone X-era featuring. The App Annie reports add the marketplace-economics layer. In 2015 the App Store had fewer downloads than Google Play but generated 75% more revenue; in Q4 2017 it handled roughly 8 billion new downloads against Google Play's more than 19 billion, yet generated $11.5 billion in consumer spend and was reported at 95% above Google Play. The 2015 source links revenue growth to China, the U.S., and Japan and reports games at 75% of iOS revenue, while the Q4 source says spending grew faster than downloads. Sensor Tower adds ranking durability: 74% of first-time No. 1 U.S. iPhone download apps in 2015-2016 left the top 25 within a month. The store therefore combines distribution, pricing, monetization, and volatile discovery.

## Key Characteristics
- Provided early distribution when Instapaper launched as an iOS app in 2008.
- Created visibility through App Store features and launch-era placement.
- Hosted pricing transitions across free, paid Pro, price drops, and freemium availability.
- Continued to shape product milestones through iOS 11 and iPhone X-specific updates.
- Generated much higher direct spending than Google Play in both the 2015 and Q4 2017 App Annie estimates despite lower download volume.
- Drew revenue growth mainly from China, the U.S., and Japan in that source.
- Produced volatile top-chart visibility for most first-time No. 1 U.S. iPhone download apps in the 2015-2016 Sensor Tower sample.

## Evidence
- Early launch: [[10-years-of-instapaper]] says Instapaper was one of the first apps available after Apple's App Store launched in July 2008.
- Initial traction: [[10-years-of-instapaper]] reports more than 2,000 downloads on the first day and more than 15,000 before the end of that month.
- Pricing surface: [[10-years-of-instapaper]] records Instapaper Pro pricing, the 2011 removal of the free app, later iOS price changes, and the 2014 return to free App Store availability through freemium.
- Later visibility: [[10-years-of-instapaper]] says the iPhone X-specific update was featured at launch on Apple's new App Store.
- Revenue lead: [[app-annie-2015-google-play-saw-100-percent-more-downloads-than-ios-app-store-venturebeat]] reports Apple's App Store generated 75% more revenue than Google Play in 2015.
- Revenue geography: [[app-annie-2015-google-play-saw-100-percent-more-downloads-than-ios-app-store-venturebeat]] says App Store revenue growth was driven primarily by China, the U.S., and Japan.
- Games mix: [[app-annie-2015-google-play-saw-100-percent-more-downloads-than-ios-app-store-venturebeat]] says games contributed 75% of iOS App Store revenue.
- Q4 2017 monetization: [[apples-ios-app-store-users-spent-11-5-billion-in-q4-95-more-than-google-play]] reports roughly 8 billion new App Store downloads and $11.5 billion in consumer spend, 95% above Google Play by the article's comparison.
- Spending momentum: [[apples-ios-app-store-users-spent-11-5-billion-in-q4-95-more-than-google-play]] reports combined store spending growth of 20% year over year against 7% download growth.
- Chart volatility: [[74-of-no-1-iphone-apps-dont-last-a-month-in-the-top-25]] says 74% of first-time No. 1 U.S. iPhone download-chart apps in 2015-2016 left the top 25 within a month.
- Long-tail persistence: [[74-of-no-1-iphone-apps-dont-last-a-month-in-the-top-25]] says only 8% of those apps remained in the top 25 after three months.

## Qualifications
The Instapaper source is not an App Store history and covers only the marketplace features relevant to one product. The 2015 App Annie source is a relative snapshot without absolute totals; the Q4 2017 source adds totals but its rounded figures do not reconcile exactly with the retained chart, and its Google Play comparison excludes much of China's Android app economy. Neither supplies full methodology or covers later policy, alternative payments, regulation, or current economics. The Sensor Tower source is limited to first-time No. 1 U.S. iPhone apps in 2015-2016 and does not cover paid or revenue charts, other countries, acquisition costs, or later ranking algorithms.

## What Changed
- Added the 2015 App Annie/VentureBeat economics layer: fewer downloads than Google Play but substantially higher revenue.
- Added Sensor Tower's 2015-2016 chart-retention finding: No. 1 download rank usually did not preserve top-25 visibility for a month.
- Extended the lower-volume, higher-spending pattern through Q4 2017 with absolute download and consumer-spend estimates.
