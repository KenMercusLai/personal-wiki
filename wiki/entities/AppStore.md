## Overview
[[AppStore]] is Apple's iOS app marketplace, presented in the wiki as a distribution and visibility platform for [[Instapaper]], an economically concentrated mobile marketplace that out-earned [[GooglePlay]] despite lower download volume in 2015, and a chart-mediated discovery surface where top rank can decay quickly.

## Current Profile
The Instapaper article uses the App Store as a marker of mobile-platform timing. Instapaper was among the first apps available when the store launched in July 2008, received launch-day downloads and early features, later changed pricing and freemium status through the store, and was again featured around iPhone X launch support. The App Annie/VentureBeat source adds the marketplace-economics layer: in 2015 Apple's App Store had fewer downloads than Google Play but generated 75% more revenue, with revenue growth driven mainly by China, the U.S., and Japan and games contributing 75% of iOS App Store revenue. The Sensor Tower source adds the ranking-durability layer: for first-time No. 1 U.S. iPhone download-chart apps in 2015-2016, high visibility usually faded quickly, with 74% leaving the top 25 within a month. The App Store therefore appears as a distribution channel, an economic surface where iOS spending could outweigh Android download scale, and a ranking system where chart position alone did not guarantee lasting discovery.

## Key Characteristics
- Provided early distribution when Instapaper launched as an iOS app in 2008.
- Created visibility through App Store features and launch-era placement.
- Hosted pricing transitions across free, paid Pro, price drops, and freemium availability.
- Continued to shape product milestones through iOS 11 and iPhone X-specific updates.
- Generated much higher revenue than Google Play in the 2015 App Annie estimate despite lower download volume.
- Drew revenue growth mainly from China, the U.S., and Japan in that source.
- Produced volatile top-chart visibility for most first-time No. 1 U.S. iPhone download apps in the 2015-2016 Sensor Tower sample.

## Evidence
- Early launch: [[10-years-of-instapaper]] says Instapaper was one of the first apps available after Apple's App Store launched in July 2008.
- Initial traction: [[10-years-of-instapaper]] reports more than 2,000 downloads on the first day and more than 15,000 before the end of that month.
- Pricing surface: [[10-years-of-instapaper]] records Instapaper Pro pricing, the 2011 removal of the free app, later iOS price changes, and the 2014 return to free App Store availability through freemium.
- Later visibility: [[10-years-of-instapaper]] says the iPhone X-specific update was featured at launch on Apple's new App Store.
- Revenue lead: [[app-annie-2015-google-play-saw-100-percent-more-downloads-than-ios-app-store-venturebeat]] reports Apple's App Store generated 75% more revenue than Google Play in 2015.
- Revenue geography: [[app-annie-2015-google-play-saw-100-percent-more-downloads-than-ios-app-store-venturebeat]] says App Store revenue growth was driven primarily by China, the U.S., and Japan.
- Games mix: [[app-annie-2015-google-play-saw-100-percent-more-downloads-than-ios-app-store-venturebeat]] says games contributed 75% of iOS App Store revenue.
- Chart volatility: [[74-of-no-1-iphone-apps-dont-last-a-month-in-the-top-25]] says 74% of first-time No. 1 U.S. iPhone download-chart apps in 2015-2016 left the top 25 within a month.
- Long-tail persistence: [[74-of-no-1-iphone-apps-dont-last-a-month-in-the-top-25]] says only 8% of those apps remained in the top 25 after three months.

## Qualifications
The Instapaper source is not an App Store history and covers only the aspects of Apple's marketplace that mattered to Instapaper's distribution, pricing, and launch visibility. The App Annie/VentureBeat source is a 2015 relative market snapshot and does not provide absolute revenue, methodology, later App Store policy changes, subscriptions, advertising, regulatory pressure, or current platform economics. The Sensor Tower source is limited to U.S. iPhone download-chart apps reaching No. 1 for the first time in 2015 and 2016; it does not cover paid charts, revenue charts, other countries, acquisition costs, or later ranking algorithms.
