## Overview
[[Lyft]] is used as a messaging-artifact example where a rider sharing an ETA link gives another person a useful tracking page and exposes them to the product.

## Current Profile
The source treats Lyft's "Send ETA" feature as viral because the user has a natural reason to message someone outside the app. The recipient gets a real-time ride-tracking experience, which can make Lyft salient for their own future time-sensitive trips.

## Key Characteristics
- Ride-hailing app used as a messaging-artifact growth example.
- Turns a practical coordination need into product exposure.
- Provides the recipient with a rich tracking experience rather than a bare promotion.

## Evidence
- Send ETA feature: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] describes Lyft prompting riders to send a tracking URL to another person.
- Recipient experience: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] says the tracking page reminds recipients to consider Lyft in future time-critical scenarios.
- Messaging artifact: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] places Lyft under products that generate URLs expected to be shared through messaging.

## Qualifications
The source does not provide Lyft conversion metrics from ETA sharing or compare the feature with other ride-hailing acquisition channels.

## What Changed
- Created Lyft as a messaging-artifact virality example.

## Relationships
- [[ViralLoops]] - shared tracking links expose non-users to product value.
- [[SocialProof]] - a link from someone taking a ride can make the service socially salient.
- [[GrowthHacking]] - the feature joins utility and acquisition in one product action.
