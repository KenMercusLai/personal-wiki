## Overview
[[HashiCorp]] is the software company used in [[MitchellHashimoto]]'s first-person case about startup banking, treasury controls, and the transition from founder-managed finances to a professional finance function.

## Current Profile
The source follows HashiCorp's original [[Chase]] account from a $20,000 founder loan through seed, Series A, and Series B funding that brought its balance to roughly $35 million. A newly hired VP of Finance then moved primary banking to Silicon Valley Bank and improved cash safeguarding, but the old Chase account was left open and continued receiving customer payments. A later internal audit found more than $100,000 in recurring fraudulent wires; Chase ultimately recovered the funds, and HashiCorp closed the account by withdrawing the remaining roughly $1 million through a cashier's check. The company had begun diversifying balances across several banks before the later Silicon Valley Bank crisis.

## Key Characteristics
- Progressed from founder-managed banking to a dedicated finance organization as capital and operating complexity grew.
- Raised approximately $1 million at seed, $10.2 million at Series A, and $24 million at Series B in the source's chronology.
- Held roughly $35 million in one bank account before its finance leader changed the treasury setup.
- Retained a forgotten legacy payment account that exposed weak account inventory, reconciliation, and monitoring.
- Recovered all reported fraudulent transfers and diversified bank balances before the source's 2023 publication context.

## Evidence
- Funding and concentration: [[my-startup-banking-story]] traces the original account from $20,000 to roughly $35 million after three funding rounds.
- Finance-function transition: [[my-startup-banking-story]] says the VP of Finance identified concentration and cash-use problems and led the move away from Chase.
- Legacy-account exposure: [[my-startup-banking-story]] says some customers continued paying the open account and recurring fraudulent wires exceeded $100,000 before discovery.
- Recovery and closure: [[my-startup-banking-story]] says Chase recovered all lost funds and that a roughly $1 million cashier's check closed the account.
- Diversification: [[my-startup-banking-story]] says the finance team had already spread balances across multiple banks before the 2023 Silicon Valley Bank events.

## Qualifications
The evidence is one cofounder's retrospective and does not include bank records, audit findings, fraud mechanics, finance-team testimony, or an independent company history. Approximate balances should not be read as audited figures, and the account does not establish whether the observed controls were typical of HashiCorp outside this episode.
