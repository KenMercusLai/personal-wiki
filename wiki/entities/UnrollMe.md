## Overview
[[UnrollMe]] is an email-management service represented here through a 2017 controversy over inbox permissions and the reported sale of anonymized purchase-receipt information.

## Current Profile
The source describes Unroll.me as a free service for decluttering email that requested unusually consequential Google-account capabilities: reading, sending, deleting, and managing email, viewing profile details, and managing contacts. It reports that the service examined email receipts and supplied anonymized Lyft receipt information to its parent company's analytics operation, Slice Intelligence, which sold the information to [[Uber]]. The case illustrates how feature utility, technical permission scope, and the provider's actual revenue model can diverge in users' understanding.

## Key Characteristics
- Offered inbox cleanup as a zero-price consumer service.
- Required broad email and contact permissions for the depicted Google-account integration.
- Used information extracted from email receipts for analytics beyond inbox organization, according to the 2017 report.
- Became a consumer trust case when secondary data use was publicized.

## Evidence
- Product role: [[heres-the-thing-with-free-apps-and-services]] describes Unroll.me as an email-management app promising to declutter the inbox.
- Permission scope: [[heres-the-thing-with-free-apps-and-services]] and its retained authorization screenshot show access to read, send, delete, and manage email, view profile information, and manage contacts.
- Secondary use: [[heres-the-thing-with-free-apps-and-services]] reports analysis of individual emails and transfer of anonymized Lyft receipt information through Slice Intelligence for sale to Uber.
- Trust response: [[heres-the-thing-with-free-apps-and-services]] records users pledging to remove inbox access and delete accounts after the practice became widely known.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one contemporary secondary report. It does not reproduce the relevant contracts, audit the anonymization method, measure reidentification risk, establish how representative the cited user reactions were, or document the company's later ownership, product, permission, policy, or enforcement history. The retained authorization screen demonstrates requested scope, not that every capability was exercised in every account.
