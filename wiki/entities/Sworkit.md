## Overview
[[Sworkit]] is a flexible-workout application created by [[RyanHanna]] as a learning side project and later acquired and merged with [[Nexercise]].

## Current Profile
The application began as Hanna's answer to a recurring personal problem: finding a workout that fit the time, space, equipment, and fitness capacity available. Its first implementation combined HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and jQuery in a website packaged for mobile through PhoneGap, with randomized workout selection adapted from a Code Year exercise.

The article traces a staged product path from personal utility to public release, Lifehacker-driven distribution, a paid version, full-time operation, and acquisition. Its reported scale is substantial—25 million downloads and more than 50 million completed workouts by 2017—but downloads are not equivalent to retained users, and all business and usage outcomes come from a Codecademy-published founder story.

## Key Characteristics
- Generates flexible workout routines for different time, location, equipment, and fitness constraints.
- Began as a personally useful project for its creator rather than as a formal startup plan.
- Used web technology and PhoneGap for its first mobile version.
- Adapted a beginner exercise's randomization pattern into product behavior.
- Gained early external distribution through direct outreach to Lifehacker.
- Progressed from free release to a paid version, full-time operation, and acquisition.
- Expanded its stated mission from busy adults to children, seniors, and rehabilitation users.

## Evidence
- Problem and audience: [[from-side-project-to-25-million-downloads-codecademy-medium]] describes workouts adjusted to the user's available time, place, equipment, and fitness level.
- Initial architecture: [[from-side-project-to-25-million-downloads-codecademy-medium]] says the first version was a website using HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and jQuery, converted into a native application with PhoneGap.
- Distribution: [[from-side-project-to-25-million-downloads-codecademy-medium]] attributes the first download surge to Hanna's direct tweet and next-day Lifehacker coverage.
- Business transition: [[from-side-project-to-25-million-downloads-codecademy-medium]] describes a paid release, full-time operation, and acquisition by Nexercise.
- Reported outcomes: [[from-side-project-to-25-million-downloads-codecademy-medium]] reports 25 million downloads, more than 50 million completed workouts, profitability, and an American College of Sports Medicine ranking.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one 2017 first-person success story published by the learning platform credited in the narrative. It does not provide active-user, retention, revenue, cost, acquisition-term, clinical-outcome, or independent award-verification data. The first-version technology and reported scale should not be assumed to describe the current product.
