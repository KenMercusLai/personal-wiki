## Overview
[[MikeLove]] is the founder and developer of [[Pleco]], represented through [[BenThompson]]'s account of building a Chinese dictionary product into a long-running independent software business.

## Current Profile
Love began Pleco as a Palm application in 2001 after studying in China. The account portrays him as combining product development with business work: licensing handwriting recognition and edited dictionaries, investing in a capable free product, selling modular add-ons, testing bundle prices, negotiating with publishers, and maintaining both iOS and Android versions. His objective was not maximum short-term efficiency on each platform, but durable ownership of the Chinese-dictionary niche.

## Key Characteristics
- Built around a specific Chinese-language-learning need rather than a broad app category.
- Used licensed recognition technology and dictionary content as differentiation that competitors could not reproduce through interface polish alone.
- Adopted free distribution with in-app purchases when the model became available on iOS.
- Treated pricing and bundle composition as testable business decisions.
- Accepted weaker Android economics to preserve cross-platform brand coverage.

## Evidence
- Origin and customer context: [[pleco-building-a-business-not-an-app]] says Love launched the Palm app in 2001 after being an exchange student in China.
- Licensed differentiation: [[pleco-building-a-business-not-an-app]] attributes Pleco's early advantage to Motorola handwriting recognition and exclusive Pocket Oxford Chinese dictionary rights.
- Business-model adaptation: [[pleco-building-a-business-not-an-app]] says Love changed the iOS plan to a free app with in-app purchases before its December 2009 launch.
- Pricing judgment: [[pleco-building-a-business-not-an-app]] reports that changing the basic bundle to a $40 feature-only offer increased net revenue.
- Category coverage: [[pleco-building-a-business-not-an-app]] says Love maintained Android despite its lower development return because recommendation lists could then name Pleco regardless of platform.

## Qualifications
The profile is bounded to Thompson's favorable 2014 article and does not independently verify Love's revenue, costs, licensing terms, customer base, product quality, or later decisions. The source offers no comparison group, and Pleco's early entry and accumulated publishing relationships may limit how readily the strategy transfers to new independent products.

## What Changed
- Established Love as a developer-businessperson whose durable advantage combined software, licensing, pricing, and platform coverage.
- Preserved the distinction between Android's weaker direct economics and its proposed brand value.
