## Overview
[[WindowsPhone]] appears in the wiki as Microsoft's modernized smartphone platform and Nokia's chosen replacement ecosystem, but also as a failed attempt to create a third major smartphone platform beside iOS and Android.

## Current Profile
The source presents Windows Phone as a strategically understandable but ultimately unsuccessful platform bet. Nokia chose it because the company wanted to join an ecosystem without surrendering to Android. Microsoft improved its mobile OS, but free [[Android]] undermined the licensing business and Windows Phone could not recruit large licensees or enough ecosystem momentum. Nokia's public commitment to Windows Phone also weakened demand for its existing devices before replacement handsets were ready.

## Key Characteristics
- Microsoft's post-Windows Mobile smartphone operating-system effort.
- Nokia's selected ecosystem after Stephen Elop's "burning platform" diagnosis.
- Failed to become a strong third ecosystem against iOS and Android.
- Was structurally hurt by Android's free licensing model.

## Evidence
- Nokia choice: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] says Elop chose to move Nokia to Microsoft's Windows Phone platform.
- Ecosystem logic: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] frames the choice as following Elop's own advice to join an ecosystem.
- Licensing obstacle: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] argues free Android killed Microsoft's hope for a smartphone OS licensing business.
- Business outcome: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] says Microsoft's handset business became effectively nonexistent.

## Qualifications
The page is limited to Windows Phone's role in a 2016 smartphone-transition essay. It does not independently assess product quality, developer tools, app availability, enterprise adoption, later Windows 10 Mobile choices, or Microsoft's broader mobile application strategy.

## What Changed
- Created Windows Phone as Nokia's chosen ecosystem and Microsoft's failed mobile-platform case.
