## Overview
[[AdamDAngelo]] is the author of the source defending 10-year startup stock-option exercise periods and presenting [[Quora]]'s rationale for adopting them.

## Current Profile
The source presents Adam D'Angelo as a founder/operator arguing from Quora's compensation practice. His position is not merely pro-employee sentiment; he frames extended exercise windows as compatible with market-based compensation, option-pool expansion, stakeholder interests, and transparent employer competition.

## Key Characteristics
- Defends 10-year stock-option exercise periods for startup employees.
- Treats equity-policy transparency as necessary for real labor-market competition.
- Rejects recovered-share accounting as the right lens for future employee grants.
- Frames vested equity as compensation employees already earned during their tenure.
- Acknowledges company freedom while arguing Quora's chosen policy is best for all stakeholders.

## Evidence
- Exercise-window defense: [[10-year-exercise-periods-make-sense-thoughts-quora]] states that Quora believes a 10-year exercise period is the right choice.
- Transparency norm: [[10-year-exercise-periods-make-sense-thoughts-quora]] argues that employees must understand exercise-period consequences before joining.
- Market-compensation frame: [[10-year-exercise-periods-make-sense-thoughts-quora]] says Quora grants compensation based on current market value for the role, not on shares recovered from former employees.
- Vested-equity view: [[10-year-exercise-periods-make-sense-thoughts-quora]] uses the employee A/B/C example to argue that earlier contributors should not lose vested equity solely because the IPO happens later.

## Qualifications
The source is self-authored advocacy for Quora's policy, not an empirical study of long-term startup outcomes. It reports investor resistance and employee appreciation from the author's perspective.
