## Overview
[[ZhangXuan]] is the programmer and creator also known as Viking who describes leaving regular employment in 2019 and sustaining three years of [[ProgrammerFreelancing]] through courses, product work, and audience building.

## Current Profile
The source presents Zhang Xuan as a pragmatic independent programmer rather than a pure lifestyle evangelist. He wanted to escape commuting, low-meaning work, coding interviews, and conventional job switching, but he also treats freelancing as a financially constrained experiment. His path combined [[Imooc]] course income, the [[EasyCV]] independent product, and [[PersonalBranding]] across multiple platforms.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses freelancing as a "B plan" and alternative career experiment rather than an abstract ideology.
- Prioritizes stable income from technical courses before relying on experimental product revenue.
- Builds software products to learn product development, DevOps, copywriting, and marketing in practice.
- Treats personal audience as a real distribution asset for products and opportunities.
- Manages solo-work risks through routines, milestones, exercise, social contact, and a time-boxed fallback.

## Evidence
- Career experiment: [[bu-shang-ban-de-1000-tian-cheng-xu-yuan-zi-you-zhi-ye-b-ji-hua]] says he began with a gap-year mindset and continued once freelancing proved sustainable.
- Income priority: [[bu-shang-ban-de-1000-tian-cheng-xu-yuan-zi-you-zhi-ye-b-ji-hua]] identifies course sales on [[Imooc]] as the main income source and warns that early product revenue was insufficient.
- Product learning: [[bu-shang-ban-de-1000-tian-cheng-xu-yuan-zi-you-zhi-ye-b-ji-hua]] describes [[EasyCV]] as a product experiment that improved full-stack development, CI/CD, copywriting, and promotion skills.
- Distribution asset: [[bu-shang-ban-de-1000-tian-cheng-xu-yuan-zi-you-zhi-ye-b-ji-hua]] compares platform audiences and their traffic value.
- Work discipline: [[bu-shang-ban-de-1000-tian-cheng-xu-yuan-zi-you-zhi-ye-b-ji-hua]] lists routines and safeguards for avoiding isolation, low focus, and confidence loss.

## Qualifications
The source is self-reported and centered on one programmer's experience. It does not prove that freelancing is generally safe for programmers under heavier financial obligations, weaker teaching fit, or smaller audiences.
