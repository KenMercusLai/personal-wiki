## Overview
[[YannLeCun]] is presented as a neural-network researcher whose work from the 1980s and 1990s helped establish trainable image-recognition architectures later used in the ImageNet era.

## Current Profile
The Fortune history describes LeCun as an early reader and collaborator in the multilayer-network revival, then as a Bell Labs researcher whose image-recognition work was commercialized for reading handwritten bank checks. It connects those foundations to the type of algorithm that won the 2012 ImageNet competition and to his later recruitment by Facebook during the industry's contest for deep-learning talent.

## Key Characteristics
- Pursued multilayer neural networks when explicit neural-network framing faced strong academic skepticism.
- Worked with Geoffrey Hinton during the 1980s revival of error-corrected multilayer learning.
- Conducted foundational image-recognition research at Bell Labs from 1988 through the following decade.
- Saw neural-network technology commercialized for handwritten check-digit recognition.
- Was recruited by Facebook after deep learning's 2012 computer-vision breakthrough.

## Evidence
- Early connectionist work: [[from-2016-why-deep-learning-is-suddenly-changing-your-life-fortune]] recounts LeCun finding Hinton's obliquely worded 1983 multilayer-network paper and meeting him in 1985.
- Applied computer vision: [[from-2016-why-deep-learning-is-suddenly-changing-your-life-fortune]] connects his Bell Labs work to bank systems that read handwritten digits on checks.
- Algorithmic continuity: [[from-2016-why-deep-learning-is-suddenly-changing-your-life-fortune]] says the model family he pioneered underpinned the 2012 ImageNet winner.
- Talent competition: [[from-2016-why-deep-learning-is-suddenly-changing-your-life-fortune]] names Facebook as the company that recruited him during the post-ImageNet hiring race.

## Qualifications
This is a source-bounded historical sketch, not a complete publication record. The article compresses multi-author lineages for backpropagation and convolutional networks and does not distinguish all architectural changes between early check-reading systems and the 2012 ImageNet model.
