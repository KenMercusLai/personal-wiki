## Overview
[[PriceIntelligently]] is the SaaS pricing publisher behind the discounting source.

## Current Profile
The sources present [[PriceIntelligently]] as a data-oriented pricing and SaaS economics voice. Its role in the wiki is not as a product case study but as the source of claims about [[SaaSDiscounting]], [[SaaSPricing]], [[CustomerAcquisitionCost]], [[CustomerLifetimeValue]], and the broader shift from acquisition-first growth toward retention quality.

## Key Characteristics
- Publishes practical SaaS pricing guidance.
- Uses company-level and unit-economics examples to argue about discounting.
- Treats pricing as a patient strategic process rather than a quick acquisition lever.
- Supplies SaaSFest claims about acquisition saturation, increased competition, declining feature value, willingness-to-pay pressure, and rising CAC.
- Frames SaaS growth through the tension between easy acquisition and harder retention.

## Evidence
- Pricing guidance: [[a-comprehensive-data-guide-to-why-you-shouldnt-discount]] frames discounting as part of a broader SaaS pricing series.
- Data-oriented argument: [[a-comprehensive-data-guide-to-why-you-shouldnt-discount]] compares minimal and aggressive discounting strategies across 88 SaaS companies.
- Unit-economics examples: [[a-comprehensive-data-guide-to-why-you-shouldnt-discount]] uses CAC recovery, churn, and LTV to evaluate discounting.
- SaaSFest framing: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] quotes and screenshots Hiten Shah's tweet summarizing Price Intelligently's SaaSFest points.
- Acquisition pressure: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] cites Price Intelligently research arguing that SaaS companies focus too much on acquisition instead of retention.
- Market-economics claims: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] reports claims that saturated markets, cheaper software, falling willingness to pay, and rising CAC make acquisition less defensible.

## Qualifications
The wiki uses Price Intelligently through two related SaaS strategy sources. It still does not attempt to describe the company's full history, ownership, product line, methodology, or current status.
