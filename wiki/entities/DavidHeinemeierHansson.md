## Overview
[[DavidHeinemeierHansson]], identified as DHH in the source file, is represented here as a technology executive and writer arguing that social forgiveness, legal exposure, and concentrated accountability can make AI adoption require much more than human-level accuracy.

## Current Profile
In this source, Hansson reasons from three domains with descending criticality: autonomous driving, medical diagnosis, and customer support. His central move is to separate comparative performance from acceptable machine performance. Even when an automated system produces fewer errors overall, its remaining failures may attract more blame than ordinary human mistakes because a product company appears to own and scale each failure. His account of experiments at [[37signals]] gives the customer-support example limited operational grounding, while the driving and medical examples remain hypothetical.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames AI adoption as a psychological and institutional problem, not only a technical benchmark.
- Uses cross-domain thought experiments to test how stakes and liability change acceptable error rates.
- Draws on 37signals' customer-support experiments as a practitioner example.
- Leaves open whether familiarity with AI will reduce asymmetric intolerance over time.

## Evidence
- Comparative-performance argument: [[dhh-its-easier-to-forgive-a-human-than-a-robot]] says a robot may need to be virtually perfect rather than merely as good as a human.
- High-stakes examples: [[dhh-its-easier-to-forgive-a-human-than-a-robot]] applies the claim to autonomous-vehicle deaths and diagnostic mistakes.
- Practitioner observation: [[dhh-its-easier-to-forgive-a-human-than-a-robot]] reports that 37signals' support experiments handled difficult cases well but also produced seriously wrong answers.
- Open conclusion: [[dhh-its-easier-to-forgive-a-human-than-a-robot]] considers both eventual normalization and a durable human advantage in forgivability.

## Qualifications
This profile is limited to one short 2024 essay. It does not establish Hansson's broader career, technical work, management views, or complete position on AI. The article supplies no controlled behavioral evidence, legal comparison, medical study, or measured customer-support error threshold, and its numerical examples are illustrative rather than forecasts.
