## Overview
[[BernieSanders]] is the 2016 presidential candidate whose insurgent Democratic primary campaign attracted a large volunteer technology ecosystem.

## Current Profile
In the POLITICO source, Sanders matters less as a policy figure than as the organizing symbol for [[VolunteerCampaignTechnology]]. His anti-establishment campaign drew politically inexperienced but technically capable supporters who built unofficial tools, amplified campaign messages, and sometimes fed software or labor back into the official operation. The available evidence is source-scoped to February 2016 and does not assess the full campaign outcome.

## Key Characteristics
- Serves as the motivating candidate for a large volunteer coder network.
- Benefits from unofficial software that helps voters, volunteers, and online supporters act.
- Operates through a campaign culture that is portrayed as receptive to bottom-up volunteer initiative.
- Faces accountability risks when supporter-built tools affect housing, rides, canvassing, or voter contact.

## Evidence
- Volunteer mobilization: [[2016-bernies-army-of-coders-politico-magazine]] reports more than 1,000 volunteer technologists building tools around the campaign.
- Tool adoption: [[2016-bernies-army-of-coders-politico-magazine]] says unofficial projects such as [[VoteForBernie]] and Rapi Castillo's event map influenced or became official campaign tools.
- Bottom-up culture: [[2016-bernies-army-of-coders-politico-magazine]] quotes campaign staff describing volunteers as central to the campaign's digital ethos.
- Risk exposure: [[2016-bernies-army-of-coders-politico-magazine]] cites concerns about overlapping apps, beta-quality tools, and safety or targeting problems in unofficial projects.
