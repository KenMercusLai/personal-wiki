## Overview
[[PhilippSchmid]] is represented in the wiki as an AI practitioner who publishes experience-based guidance for prompting Gemini 3 Pro.

## Current Profile
Schmid treats prompting as an empirical design practice: state the goal directly, separate instructions from data, position information deliberately, request planning or review when useful, and iterate against task-specific results. His article is a practical playbook rather than a controlled evaluation or official product specification.

## Key Characteristics
- Favors concise, literal instructions and explicitly defined parameters.
- Uses XML or Markdown structure to separate roles, constraints, context, and output requirements.
- Treats long-context placement and multimodal synthesis as deliberate prompt-design choices.
- Recommends decomposition, completeness checks, self-review, and persistent tool recovery for complex work.
- Presents templates as adaptable baselines rather than a universal standard.

## Evidence
- Directness and structure: [[philipp-schmid-gemini-3-prompting-best-practices-for-general-usage]] recommends concise instructions, consistent delimiters, and defined terms.
- Context design: [[philipp-schmid-gemini-3-prompting-best-practices-for-general-usage]] separates opening behavioral constraints from end-positioned questions over large contexts.
- Complex workflows: [[philipp-schmid-gemini-3-prompting-best-practices-for-general-usage]] supplies planning, critique, persistence, research, writing, problem-solving, and education patterns.
- Empirical boundary: [[philipp-schmid-gemini-3-prompting-best-practices-for-general-usage]] explicitly describes its patterns as starting points to refine.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one practitioner article about the author's experience with a named model generation. It supplies no comparative prompt evaluations, task set, failure rates, latency measurements, or evidence that the practices generalize unchanged to other models and domains.
