## Overview
[[Winnie]] is presented as a family information and community product co-founded by [[SaraMauskopf]] and [[AnneHalsall]] to centralize age-specific parenting advice, childcare discovery, and recommendations for family-friendly places.

## Current Profile
The Livingly profile describes Winnie through a “Yelp for parents” analogy, but its stated scope is broader than reviews. Parents could ask one another questions and locate childcare, playgrounds, museums, restaurants, shopping, and practical facilities, with the intended relevance shifting as children aged. Mauskopf reports more than 25,000 community members and users in more than 3,000 U.S. cities, and frames nationwide design as protection against solving only for Silicon Valley families.

The product also appears as a founder-use case. During her husband's cancer treatment, Mauskopf says she used Winnie to plan outings and meals while caring for her one-year-old daughter and trying to preserve household rest and routine. That example illustrates the proposed job, but it is not independent evidence of product quality or representative user outcomes.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines parenting information, peer questions, local discovery, and recommendations in one service.
- Organizes relevance around a child's changing age and stage rather than one short parenting episode.
- Seeks nationwide usefulness while drawing its founding team, capital, and talent from Silicon Valley.
- Uses local places and childcare as practical infrastructure for family routines.
- Was used by its co-founder during an acute period of solo parenting and family illness.

- Reported reach: [[inspiring-women-meet-the-co-founder-of-winnie-a-yelp-type-app-for-parents-inspiring-women-livingly]] reports more than 25,000 parents and presence in more than 3,000 U.S. cities.

## Qualifications
All product, reach, loyalty, and usage claims come from a promotional founder interview. The source does not define active users, geographic coverage depth, content quality, recommendation integrity, childcare-vetting standards, business model, retention, or outcomes, and it supplies no user comparison or later company history. “Yelp for parents” is an editorial analogy rather than a complete description of the product.
