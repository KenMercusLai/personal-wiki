## Overview
[[AmazonPrime]] is Amazon's membership program, framed as the connective system linking retail, recurring revenue, fulfillment, entertainment, physical stores, and third-party seller economics. Its delivery promise also turned logistics capacity into a core strategic dependency.

## Current Profile
Prime began as free two-day shipping for $79 a year, then grew into a 100 million-plus subscriber program that encouraged more buying while adding perks. The 2016 source describes an earlier stage with an estimated 63 million members, $99 annual pricing, and spending nearly three times that of non-Prime customers. Speed was the key attraction, but fulfilling it at growing scale required costly expedited carrier service and exposed peak-capacity limits. By 2018 Prime was estimated to generate about $10 billion in 2017 subscription revenue, support billions in Prime Day spending, justify entertainment investment, increase the value of Fulfillment by Amazon, and underwrite physical retail expansion. Bezos rejects treating Prime as standalone because it is tied into Amazon's consumer offering.

## Key Characteristics
- Started as a shipping membership and became a multi-benefit subscription platform.
- Encourages shopping frequency while generating recurring subscription revenue.
- Connects retail, entertainment, fulfillment, third-party sellers, and physical stores.
- Makes Prime Day a member-only shopping event with large transaction volume.
- Requires physical beachheads for same-day delivery and pickup, supporting stores and grocery expansion.
- Converts faster delivery from a benefit into a capacity, cost, and reliability obligation across the logistics network.

## Qualifications
The page is grounded in 2016 and 2018 profiles rather than audited membership or cohort data. Reported subscriber, spending, and revenue figures are historical estimates. The sources do not evaluate current pricing, retention, logistics profitability, entertainment returns, antitrust concerns, labor effects, or member satisfaction.

## What Changed
- Added delivery capacity and peak reliability as obligations created by Prime's growth loop.
