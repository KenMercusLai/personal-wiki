## Overview
[[RhondaMMerwin]] is a Duke University behavioral-science researcher represented here as coauthor of an integrative review of exercise in managing mental-health disorders.

## Current Profile
In the available source, Merwin helps frame exercise as both a biological stimulus and a setting for behavioral learning. The review links neuroplasticity, cognitive and affective control, exposure to bodily arousal, planning, feedback, and self-efficacy while preserving uncertainty about mechanisms and individual response.

## Key Characteristics
- Is affiliated in the source with Duke University's Department of Psychiatry and Behavioral Sciences.
- Integrates behavioral learning with health-neuroscience explanations of exercise benefit.
- Treats emotion regulation, experiential avoidance, goal practice, feedback, and self-efficacy as transdiagnostic mechanisms.
- Supports tailored intervention designs responsive to reward, cognition, affect, and adherence barriers.
