## Overview
[[IdSoftware]] is the game developer presented by the source as the central PC-era force that turned first-person shooters from experiments and arcade curiosities into a mainstream genre.

## Current Profile
GamesRadar+ depicts Id Software as a studio whose technical advances and design instincts repeatedly reset FPS expectations. Hovertank 3D and Catacomb 3-D were stepping stones; Wolfenstein 3D popularized fast run-and-gun play; Doom combined speed, level design, modding, co-op, and deathmatch; Quake moved the home FPS into polygonal 3D and community-driven competitive multiplayer.

## Key Characteristics
- Built the key early PC FPS lineage from experiments to Wolfenstein 3D, Doom, and Quake.
- Combined rendering progress with fast action-game pacing and tactile shooting.
- Popularized genre conventions such as health, ammo, multiple weapons, and free saving.
- Treated multiplayer, modding, clans, and community mastery as core to FPS longevity.
- Helped establish early eSports culture through Quake's competitive depth.

## Evidence
- Iterative technical base: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] says Id worked through Hovertank 3D and Catacomb 3-D before Wolfenstein 3D.
- Genre popularization: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] credits Wolfenstein 3D with smooth fast play and many now-standard FPS mechanics.
- Multiplayer and modding: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] says Doom offered WADs, co-op, and deathmatch over LAN or dial-in connections.
- Competitive depth: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] describes Quake's clans, modding, rocket-jumping, strafe-jumping, and annual QuakeCon culture.
