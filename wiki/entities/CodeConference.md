## Overview
[[CodeConference]] is presented as an exclusive technology executive conference descended from the live-journalism event [[KaraSwisher]] and [[WaltMossberg]] built at [[AllThingsD]], where senior founders, investors, reporters, and public figures meet for onstage interviews and informal access.

## Current Profile
The 2014 profile supplies the operating origin: Swisher and Mossberg rejected sponsor-controlled slots, prepared speeches, and conventional panels in favor of news-division control and live questioning of consequential technology figures. The first event in 2003 sold out and was profitable, later events developed a waiting list, and the stage hosted major interviews and product debuts before the partners left Dow Jones and continued the model through [[Recode]].

The 2016 report portrays the Recode-hosted event as unusually effective at convening technology's most powerful people. That density makes it valuable to insiders and journalists, but its high ticket price, luxury setting, curated attendance, personal relationships, and social hospitality also create a protected atmosphere in which visionary rhetoric can receive more attention than adversarial accountability. Editorial control can remove overt sponsor capture while leaving dependence on speakers, status, ticket revenue, relationships, and future participation.

## Key Characteristics
- Curates rare access to prominent technology executives and investors.
- Uses live editorial interviews rather than sponsor-reserved talks as its defining format.
- Combines public interviews with private social proximity in a luxury setting.
- Operates as an executive clubhouse as well as a reporting venue.
- Gives hosts significant agenda-setting power over questions and debate.
- Displayed limited onstage racial diversity in the reported 2016 event.

## Evidence
- Editorial origin: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] traces the conference to a 2003 AllThingsD event controlled by the news operation and designed around live interrogation rather than sponsored speeches.
- Business durability: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] reports immediate profitability, annual waiting lists, historic executive exchanges, and product debuts.
- Access concentration: [[ground-control-to-silicon-valley]] places Musk, Bezos, Gates, Pichai, Dorsey, venture capitalists, and reporters at the same three-day gathering.
- Protected setting: [[ground-control-to-silicon-valley]] describes a $6,500 ticket, coastal resort, catered hospitality, and informal investor access.
- Accountability limits: [[ground-control-to-silicon-valley]] contrasts enthusiastic response to speculative visions with limited questioning on antitrust, platform governance, diversity, and political power.
- Representation: [[ground-control-to-silicon-valley]] reports DeRay Mckesson as the only African-American speaker onstage that week and records his criticism of decision-making rooms that do not reflect the country.

## Qualifications
The profile combines one celebratory 2014 origin account with one critical report about the 2016 conference. It does not measure question quality, profitability, editorial independence, or representation across years; compare the event systematically with other conferences; or show that every session and attendee behaved uniformly. The source establishes continuity in editorial model, not that the event's branding, ownership, or incentives remained unchanged in every respect.

## What Changed
- Added the AllThingsD-era origin, editorial-control design, and live-journalism rationale.
- Added profitability and repeated demand as sources of durability and commercial dependence.
- Distinguished removal of sponsor-controlled talks from the remaining access and participation incentives.
