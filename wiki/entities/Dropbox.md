## Overview
[[Dropbox]] appears as both a disciplined early startup and a canonical referral-loop example.

## Current Profile
One source uses Dropbox as evidence that respected startups did not treat early headcount as proof of progress, placing it alongside [[Airbnb]] and [[Stripe]] in [[SamAltman]]'s warning about hiring before fit. The virality source adds a product-growth role: Dropbox's 500 MB bonus storage for both inviter and invitee made referral value symmetrical, visible, and economically attractive because storage costs were expected to fall.

## Key Characteristics
- Serves as an example of slow early hiring.
- Illustrates pre-product-market-fit discipline around headcount.
- Serves as a two-sided referral-reward example.
- Shows how a reward can feel valuable to users while becoming cheaper for the company over time.

## Evidence
- Hiring timing: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] says Dropbox took a long time to hire its first person.
- Pattern evidence: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] groups Dropbox with Airbnb and Stripe as slow early hiring examples.
- Two-sided referral: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] says Dropbox gave both the inviter and friend 500 MB of bonus storage when a friend joined.
- Declining cost logic: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] says the reward was especially strong because extra storage was valuable to users while becoming cheaper for Dropbox to provide.

## Qualifications
The sources do not provide a full Dropbox company history. The referral discussion is a product-growth case, while the scaling discussion is a hiring-discipline case; neither source should be treated as comprehensive evidence about Dropbox's later organization, pricing, or retention.
