## Overview
[[Feast]] was a cooking-education startup cofounded by [[DavidSpinks]] and Nadia Eghbal and later described by Spinks as the formative counterexample to [[CMX]]'s bootstrapped path.

## Current Profile
Feast's first product combined a cooking class with ingredients delivered to the customer's home. The company joined Batch 6 of [[500Startups]] and oriented months of work toward raising capital, but most of more than 70 investors declined and two reported commitments fell through. Spinks says the team created more value only after it stopped optimizing for fundraising. Feast was subsequently sold for enough to return investor money and preserve the product, which continued as Foodist Kitchen under Darya Rose.

## Key Characteristics
- Combined cooking instruction with delivered ingredients in its initial product.
- Participated in 500 Startups Batch 6 and prepared for venture fundraising.
- Failed to close a funding round despite extensive investor outreach.
- Became more product-directed after abandoning fundraising as the organizing goal.
- Reached a modest acquisition that returned capital and preserved the service.

## Evidence
- Product form: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] describes the first Feast offer as a cooking class with ingredients delivered to the home.
- Accelerator path: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] places Feast in 500 Startups Batch 6 and says the program focused the team on fundraising readiness.
- Fundraising outcome: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] reports more than 70 investor conversations, mostly rejections, and two commitments that later disappeared.
- Strategic reset: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] says the company produced something valuable only after deciding to build what the team wanted.
- Sale and continuity: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] says the acquisition returned everyone's money and allowed the product to continue as Foodist Kitchen.

## Qualifications
The source does not name Feast's buyer, sale date, price, investor amounts, revenue, customer count, or terms, and "everyone" getting money back does not establish a strong financial return. Its claim that Foodist Kitchen was teaching thousands is a 2016 founder report rather than current evidence.

## What Changed
- Created Feast as the failed-fundraising and modest-exit case behind Spinks's later bootstrapping strategy.
