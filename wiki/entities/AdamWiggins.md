## Overview
[[AdamWiggins]] is the author of the 2013 essay “End-user computing” and is represented here through his argument for task-oriented, low-friction programming environments.

## Current Profile
Wiggins says [[BonnieNardi]]'s *A Small Matter of Programming* influenced him while he was working on the initial prototype of [[Heroku]] in 2007. His essay translates that influence into a product-design thesis: broader programming access requires integrated defaults and environments organized around goals that matter to end users.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames programming as a potential form of broadly distributed literacy and agency.
- Treats setup complexity and technology-centered teaching as tool-design barriers.
- Uses spreadsheets as the primary model for successful end-user programming.
- Favors visible, inspectable logic over attempts to remove code entirely.
- Connects the argument to firsthand experience learning programming through games and prototyping Heroku.

## Evidence
- Intellectual influence: [[end-user-computing-the-truant-haruspex-medium]] says Nardi's book inspired and haunted Wiggins after he obtained it in 2007.

- Personal learning context: [[end-user-computing-the-truant-haruspex-medium]] says childhood interest in creating video games motivated Wiggins to learn programming.

## Qualifications
This profile is limited to one 2013 essay. It does not attempt a general biography, a complete account of Wiggins's role at Heroku, or an assessment of his later work. The article's product examples are proposals and historical observations rather than comparative adoption or education studies.
