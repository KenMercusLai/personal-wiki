## Overview
[[ChenHao]] is the author of the ingested article on the history and evolution of [[HTTP]].

## Current Profile
Within this wiki, Chen Hao is represented as a technical author who explains internet protocol evolution through an engineering and architecture lens. The article emphasizes how protocol features such as headers, status codes, persistent connections, multiplexing, and [[QUIC]] solve concrete maintainability and performance problems.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains protocol history as an engineering progression rather than a list of version changes.
- Connects protocol design choices to operational concerns such as monitoring, error handling, performance, and interoperability.
- Advocates using widely adopted standards instead of reinventing private application protocols.
- Treats architecture work as partly responsible for pushing standard protocols into organizational use.

## Evidence
- Engineering progression: [[chen-hao-http-de-qian-shi-jin-sheng]] describes HTTP/1.0's versioning, headers, status codes, and content types as signs of engineering maturity.
- Operational concerns: [[chen-hao-http-de-qian-shi-jin-sheng]] links status codes to shared error interpretation by clients, servers, and monitoring tools.
- Standards orientation: [[chen-hao-http-de-qian-shi-jin-sheng]] argues that post-2014 HTTP improvements reduced the reason to invent private RPC protocols.
- Architecture responsibility: [[chen-hao-http-de-qian-shi-jin-sheng]] says architects should push [[HTTP2]] because standardization improves internal architecture and access to industry tools.
