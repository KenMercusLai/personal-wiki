## Overview
[[FeiFeiLi]] is presented as a Stanford computer-vision researcher who founded [[ImageNet]] to test the thesis that large labeled datasets would change machine learning.

## Current Profile
The article places Li at the data side of deep learning's 2012 inflection point. Beginning in 2007, she organized a freely available collection of more than 14 million labeled images, launched it in 2009, and then created an annual competition. By standardizing a large training resource and public evaluation, ImageNet made it possible for the AlexNet result from Geoffrey Hinton's students to demonstrate a conspicuous performance jump to a skeptical field.

## Key Characteristics
- Founded ImageNet around the conviction that data scale would change machine learning.
- Organized a free labeled-image collection reported at more than 14 million images.
- Added an annual competition to incentivize and publicize computer-vision progress.
- Announced the 2012 result that helped shift opinion toward deep neural networks.

## Evidence
- Data thesis: [[from-2016-why-deep-learning-is-suddenly-changing-your-life-fortune]] quotes Li saying that data drives learning and describes the project as a bet on big data.
- Dataset creation: [[from-2016-why-deep-learning-is-suddenly-changing-your-life-fortune]] dates the effort to 2007 and reports a 2009 launch with more than 14 million labeled images.
- Evaluation institution: [[from-2016-why-deep-learning-is-suddenly-changing-your-life-fortune]] says she established the annual contest in 2010.
- Field inflection: [[from-2016-why-deep-learning-is-suddenly-changing-your-life-fortune]] describes her announcement of the 2012 deep-learning result that nearly doubled the nearest competitor's accuracy.

## Qualifications
The page reflects one secondary 2016 account and does not document ImageNet's complete construction, contributors, labeling process, category design, later governance, dataset biases, or the precise benchmark metric behind the article's simplified "accuracy" comparison.
