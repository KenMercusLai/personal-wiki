## Overview
[[CharlieWarzel]] is represented in this wiki as a technology journalist who tested an accessible voice-cloning service and reported how an imperfect synthetic voice fooled his mother.

## Current Profile
Warzel turns an abstract warning about falsified reality into a controlled personal demonstration. He records about sixty prompted sentences for [[Lyrebird]], evaluates the resulting vocal avatar with an editor, and then constructs a short call around shared dinner plans. His account emphasizes that the successful impersonation came from both technical recognizability and social staging: anticipated phrases, a familiar context, a brief interaction, and a poor-reception excuse that reframed artifacts as network trouble.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses a first-person experiment to make an emerging synthetic-media risk concrete.
- Separates imperfect model output from practical deception success.
- Documents the social-engineering choices that make a short cloned-voice interaction plausible.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one 2018 first-person article and describes only Warzel's reported experiment and argument there. The source does not provide the underlying audio for independent assessment, and its one successful call cannot establish population-wide susceptibility or the performance of other cloning systems and scenarios.
