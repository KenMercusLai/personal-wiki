## Overview
[[Flock]] was a photo-sharing product built by the [[Bump]] team after user calls revealed that Bump users were sharing photos, later used by [[DavidLieb]] as a negative case in cognitive product simplicity.

## Current Profile
The sources present Flock as an instructive pivot attempt rather than a growth success. It translated observed Bump behavior into semi-automated or predictive photo sharing among co-located friends, but Lieb says it did not satisfy Chris Dixon's "come for the tool, stay for the network" test. His later First Round account adds a cognitive-overhead diagnosis: Flock removed users from the sharing decision so thoroughly that many could not understand why the app selected particular photos or why they should trust the outcome. The team then redesigned the direction into an unreleased gallery-first product called Photoroll, whose ideas later reappeared in [[GooglePhotos]].

## Key Characteristics
- Emerged from real Bump user behavior around photo sharing.
- Focused on semi-automated sharing among friends who were physically together.
- Failed to grow because it lacked a strong initial utility and sharing reason.
- Led into Photoroll, a gallery-first redesign that was not released.
- Became a cautionary example of automation increasing cognitive overhead by hiding decision logic.
- Improved in a later version when it began from a familiar photo-gallery pattern before adding new sharing behavior.

## Evidence
- Discovery origin: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says Bump's top-user calls revealed that people were sharing photos.
- Product shape: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] describes Flock as semi-automated photo sharing among co-located friends.
- Growth failure: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says Flock did not pass the "come for the tool, stay for the network" test and did not grow.
- Redesign path: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says Flock was redesigned into Photoroll, a photo gallery app first and sharing app second.
- Cognitive-overhead diagnosis: [[cognitive-overhead-is-your-products-overlord-topple-it-with-these-tips-first-round-review]] says many users could not understand how Flock determined which photos they wanted to share.
- Familiarity fix: [[cognitive-overhead-is-your-products-overlord-topple-it-with-these-tips-first-round-review]] says a later stickier version started with a gallery resembling the native photo album before introducing unfamiliar delivery.

## Qualifications
The sources do not include Flock usage metrics, launch timing beyond the retrospective sequence, or independent user research. Their account of Flock's failure is Lieb's summary, with one source emphasizing weak utility/network spread and the other emphasizing cognitive overhead.
