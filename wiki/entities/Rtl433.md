## Overview
[[Rtl433]] is represented as an open-source decoder that turns radio transmissions from inexpensive household sensors into structured messages.

## Current Profile
The source uses `rtl_433` with an RTL-SDR USB receiver to capture and decode 433 MHz broadcasts from a WS2032 weather station. A Home Assistant add-on publishes the decoded readings to Mosquitto, while a companion auto-discovery add-on translates that stream into messages Home Assistant can use to create entities.

## Key Characteristics
- Works with commonly available software-defined radio receivers.
- Recognizes protocols used by many weather stations and other low-cost RF devices.
- Converts radio frames into structured observations suitable for MQTT transport.
- Extends smart-home integration to devices that do not natively use Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, ZigBee, Z-Wave, or Thread.

## Evidence
- Acquisition path: [[how-i-turned-a-cheap-weather-station-into-a-personal-devops-dashboard]] connects the WS2032's 433 MHz signal to an RTL-SDR receiver and `rtl_433` over USB.
- Message path: [[how-i-turned-a-cheap-weather-station-into-a-personal-devops-dashboard]] says the add-on publishes decoded RF data to Mosquitto for downstream consumers.
- Compatibility rationale: [[how-i-turned-a-cheap-weather-station-into-a-personal-devops-dashboard]] reports selecting the WS2032 because it appeared in the project's supported-device list.

## Qualifications
The source demonstrates one sensor and receiver, not protocol coverage, decoding accuracy, radio range, interference tolerance, privacy boundaries, or compatibility across current releases. Nearby unencrypted RF devices may also be observable, creating governance questions the article does not address.

## What Changed
- Established `rtl_433` as the protocol-decoding bridge between a legacy-style RF sensor and an MQTT-based automation stack.
