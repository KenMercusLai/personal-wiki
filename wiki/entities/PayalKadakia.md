## Overview
[[PayalKadakia]] is the [[ClassPass]] founder interviewed by [[FirstRoundReview]] about marketplace product-market fit, product reinvention, and behavior-changing startup metrics.

## Current Profile
The source presents Kadakia as a mission-driven founder whose dancer background shaped the original problem: making it easier for people to get to classes. Her key contribution in the wiki is not a full biography but an operating pattern: she learned to ignore external validation, return to the underlying behavior, do backend work manually, interpret customer workarounds as product evidence, and lead the team through the painful shutdown of earlier products.

## Key Characteristics
- Founded ClassPass from a personal frustration with discovering and booking fitness classes.
- Reframed early failure by asking whether the company was actually getting people to class.
- Used manual reservation handling and simple emails to test the transaction before polishing technology.
- Interpreted customer workarounds as motivation evidence rather than only fraud.
- Led with conviction while closing down loved but obsolete products such as search and Passport.
- Chose reservations per person as the metric that best represented mission and business health.

## Evidence
- Founder problem: [[classpass-founder-on-how-marketplace-startups-can-achieve-product-market-fit-first-round-review]] says Kadakia's dancer background led her to build a way to find and book classes.
- Mission reset: [[classpass-founder-on-how-marketplace-startups-can-achieve-product-market-fit-first-round-review]] says she returned to the question of what problem the company was trying to solve.
- Manual learning: [[classpass-founder-on-how-marketplace-startups-can-achieve-product-market-fit-first-round-review]] says she manually made reservations after customer emails and learned from cancellation timing.

- Metric focus: [[classpass-founder-on-how-marketplace-startups-can-achieve-product-market-fit-first-round-review]] says reservations per person became the heartbeat metric for the company.
