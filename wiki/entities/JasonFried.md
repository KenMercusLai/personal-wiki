## Overview
[[JasonFried]] is represented in this wiki as a 37signals co-founder and writer whose sources connect scarce attention with deliberate, asynchronous company communication.

## Current Profile
Fried's attention essay argues that a commitment needs preparation, follow-through, and mental presence rather than merely free minutes. His 37signals communication guide extends that individual constraint into organization design: routine messages should not demand immediate replies, consequential decisions should use complete written thoughts, meetings should be a last resort, and timing and placement should minimize side effects. Together, the sources frame communication quality as stewardship of both shared context and other people's attention.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses concrete workplace anecdotes to refine common productivity language.
- Treats attention as a more binding constraint than clock time.
- Frames refusal as a fairness issue when mental presence would be absent.
- Advocates asynchronous, durable writing for substantial internal communication.
- Treats urgency, meeting load, timing, and context placement as design choices with attention costs.

## Evidence
- Workplace anecdote: [[blog-jason-fried-signal-v-noise-the-difference-between-time-and-attention]] recounts a promising intern conversation that Fried later realized he lacked attention to support.
- Attention constraint: [[blog-jason-fried-signal-v-noise-the-difference-between-time-and-attention]] distinguishes nominal work hours from the smaller amount of useful attention.
- Fairness boundary: [[blog-jason-fried-signal-v-noise-the-difference-between-time-and-attention]] argues that physical presence without mental presence would not be fair to either participant.
- Communication default: [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] recommends asynchronous communication most of the time and immediate response only for true emergencies.
- Writing and meetings: [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] favors complete written thoughts for important decisions and treats meetings as a last resort.
- Context and timing: [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] argues that communication should stay beside the work it concerns and be sent with regard for recipients' schedules.

## Qualifications
Both sources are practitioner arguments rather than comparative evidence. The communication guide describes 37signals' stated norms and cannot establish that its methods outperform other operating systems or fit all roles, organizations, emergencies, and accessibility needs. The profile still covers only a narrow part of Fried's broader career and management philosophy.
