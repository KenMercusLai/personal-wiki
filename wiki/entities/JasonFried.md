## Overview
[[JasonFried]] is represented in this wiki as the author of the Signal v. Noise essay [[blog-jason-fried-signal-v-noise-the-difference-between-time-and-attention]], where he distinguishes available time from available attention.

## Current Profile
In the ingested source, Fried writes as a reflective operator rather than as a productivity-system designer. His example is concrete: an excellent intern candidate needed follow-through, preparation, and actual mental presence, not merely a few available minutes. The profile therefore centers on a humane boundary-setting claim: saying no can be more honest when the real constraint is attention.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses concrete workplace anecdotes to refine common productivity language.
- Treats attention as a more binding constraint than clock time.
- Frames refusal as a fairness issue when mental presence would be absent.

## Evidence
- Workplace anecdote: [[blog-jason-fried-signal-v-noise-the-difference-between-time-and-attention]] recounts a promising intern conversation that Fried later realized he lacked attention to support.
- Attention constraint: [[blog-jason-fried-signal-v-noise-the-difference-between-time-and-attention]] distinguishes nominal work hours from the smaller amount of useful attention.
- Fairness boundary: [[blog-jason-fried-signal-v-noise-the-difference-between-time-and-attention]] argues that physical presence without mental presence would not be fair to either participant.

## Qualifications
This profile is source-scoped to one short essay. It should not be treated as a full account of Fried's broader career, companies, or management philosophy without additional sources.
