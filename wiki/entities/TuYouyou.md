## Overview
[[TuYouyou]] is a Chinese scientist presented in the source as the Project 523 research leader whose team developed an effective artemisinin treatment for malaria.

## Current Profile
The essay uses Tu as its central case for disciplined effort under uncertainty. Beginning in 1969, her team surveyed traditional remedies, collected more than 600 plants, assembled nearly 2,000 possibilities, and tested 380 extracts in mice. When sweet wormwood worked inconsistently, Tu restarted the review and found an ancient instruction that suggested heat was destroying the active ingredient. A lower-boiling-point extraction produced a consistently effective preparation in the account. With no domestic trial center and secrecy preventing outside testing, Tu and colleagues became early human subjects; she continued the research despite publication restrictions, later identifying artemisinin's chemical structure and developing another antimalarial treatment.

## Key Characteristics
- Led the secret Project 523 research group described in the essay.
- Combined traditional textual research, field collection, screening, and laboratory experimentation.
- Responded to repeated failure by revisiting the evidence and changing the extraction method.
- Accepted personal risk by joining colleagues in early human testing, as the source recounts it.
- Continued research despite institutional limits on publication and recognition.
- Serves in the essay as an example of preparation and persistence interacting with contingent discovery.
