## Overview
[[Valve]] is a game developer represented through Half-Life's influence on first-person shooters, the Counter-Strike mod ecosystem, and a later strategic move toward Steam as an online platform for PC players.

## Current Profile
GamesRadar+ presents Valve as a late-1990s PC FPS milestone. Half-Life set new expectations for cinematic single-player design, environmental interactivity, writing, and enemy AI. The source also treats Counter-Strike, originally a Half-Life mod, as part of the period when PC FPS diversified into online, tactical, community-driven forms.

The value-hacking essay adds a strategic interpretation of Valve's later trajectory: as players placed more value on multiplayer and online communities, the company expanded beyond individual game development and built Steam. This makes Valve an example of following an adjacent customer value function, although the brief retrospective supplies no chronology, operating evidence, or causal comparison for that claim.

## Key Characteristics
- Used Half-Life to make single-player FPS campaigns more cinematic, interactive, and authored.
- Helped move the genre beyond "Doom clone" imitation into broader creative diversity.
- Provided the mod ecosystem from which Counter-Strike became a major tactical FPS.
- Represents the narrative-heavy branch of mature PC FPS design.
- Is presented as expanding from game development toward an online PC platform as player value shifted toward multiplayer and community.

## Evidence
- Single-player standard: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] says Half-Life did for single-player FPS craft what Quake did for multiplayer.
- Interactivity and AI: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] describes Half-Life as cinematic, well written, environmentally interactive, and driven by lifelike enemy AI.
- Mod-driven multiplayer: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] says Counter-Strike grew from a Half-Life mod into a widespread phenomenon.
- Platform expansion: [[growth-hacking-vs-value-hacking-by]] says Valve followed changing player demand for multiplayer and online community by building Steam.

## Qualifications
The FPS history focuses on genre impact and does not address Valve's broader corporate structure or non-FPS work. The value-hacking essay adds Steam only as a short retrospective example; it does not establish timing, investment, adoption, economics, alternatives, or whether the same strategic logic described the company's actual decisions.
