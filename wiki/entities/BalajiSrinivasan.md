## Overview
[[BalajiSrinivasan]] appears as the speaker of "Silicon Valley's Ultimate Exit," arguing that technology builders should reduce dependence on political voice by creating peaceful alternatives.

## Current Profile
Within this source, Srinivasan is a Silicon Valley political-technology thinker. He frames exit as a meta-concept covering emigration, founding companies, open-source forks, customer switching, and opt-in societies. His argument is not merely that Silicon Valley should lobby harder; he says it should build systems that make Washington, D.C. and other paper-era institutions less able to impose bad policies on those who can choose alternatives.

## Key Characteristics
- Presents exit as a peaceful complement to voice.
- Uses company competition, open-source forks, and emigration as parallel examples.
- Frames Silicon Valley as a rising institutional rival to older U.S. paper-era centers.
- Argues for technology that lowers switching, relocation, and jurisdictional barriers.
- Treats Bitcoin, 3D printing, telepresence, quantified self, drones, and smart property as exit-enabling infrastructure.

## Evidence
- Exit frame: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] defines exit as alternatives that reduce the influence of bad policies without direct political involvement.
- Silicon Valley contrast: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] contrasts Silicon Valley with the Paper Belt of Boston, New York, Los Angeles, and Washington, D.C.
- Technology stack: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] lists Bitcoin, 3D printing, quantified self, telepresence, drones, and smart property as tools for lowering exit barriers.
- Governance ambition: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] proposes an opt-in society outside the United States rather than reforming U.S. institutions from within.
