## Overview
[[StanleyWood]] is a designer represented by his 2016 first-person account of helping [[Spotify]] develop organizational practices for coherent design across distributed teams.

## Current Profile
Wood says that after joining Spotify's design team in 2012, he encountered fragmented typography, color, imagery, layout, information architecture, and interaction patterns. A visual redesign did not resolve the organizational source of that inconsistency, so he pursued a layered model combining shared principles, [[GLUE]], representative governance, and design quality assurance.

His account is most useful as a practitioner description of how design-system work moved from interface artifacts into recurring organizational coordination. It does not establish his sole authorship of the practices or provide independent measurements of their results.

## Key Characteristics
- Diagnosed product fragmentation as a reflection of distributed organizational work.
- Helped frame music-specific experience principles in language connected to business goals.
- Describes GLUE as both a shared design language and, from 2015, a dedicated designer-engineer team.
- Treats guild participation and global design QA as continuing governance rather than one-time rollout work.

## Evidence
- Initial diagnosis: [[design-doesnt-scale-stanley-wood-medium]] says Wood joined Spotify in 2012 and found inconsistency across visual, structural, and interaction decisions.
- Scaling response: [[design-doesnt-scale-stanley-wood-medium]] traces principles, GLUE, the Design Guild, and Global Design QA as successive coordination layers.
- Quality framing: [[design-doesnt-scale-stanley-wood-medium]] reports a checklist and the TUNE prompt for discussing product quality.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one participant-authored 2016 retrospective. The article uses collective language for much of the work, so it should not be read as evidence that Wood alone created the principles, GLUE, guild, or QA practices. It also supplies no comparative outcome measures or later assessment of which mechanisms persisted.
