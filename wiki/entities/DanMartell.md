## Overview
[[DanMartell]] is represented in the wiki as the entrepreneur to whom Hardy attributes a practical definition and vivid illustration of forcing functions.

## Current Profile
Hardy cites Martell's definition of a forcing function as a task, activity, or event that compels action and a result. The article then retells Martell's exchange with his brother about a three-month business goal, using an imagined lethal threat to dramatize how stakes can change perceived motivation. The operational definition is useful, but the violent hypothetical is not evidence and is inappropriate as a literal management technique.

## Key Characteristics
- Entrepreneur cited for designing external pressure around goals.
- Defines forcing functions through action and produced results.
- Uses a high-stakes hypothetical to distinguish ability from situational motivation.
- Appears only through Hardy's retelling rather than a complete primary account.

## Evidence
- Definition: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] attributes a concise forcing-function definition to Martell.
- Illustration: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] retells Martell's conversation about confidence in meeting a three-month business goal under radically changed imagined stakes.
- Motivational interpretation: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] concludes from the story that motivation changes with the situation.

## Qualifications
The profile is bounded to Hardy's secondary account. An extreme imagined threat may demonstrate a rhetorical point about urgency but does not establish what a person can safely or sustainably achieve. Real forcing functions need proportional stakes, consent, reversibility, anti-gaming safeguards, and room to revise a mistaken goal.
