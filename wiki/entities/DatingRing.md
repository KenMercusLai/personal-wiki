## Overview
[[DatingRing]] was a dating service represented in the wiki by a reported product relaunch that restored profile photos and preference controls after a rational no-photo premise attracted weak signups.

## Current Profile
The service initially argued that profile photos made lifelong-partner selection too superficial and reportedly used group blind dates to improve matching. Jackson presents the result as a need-versus-want failure: prospective users still wanted to see possible matches and control preferences. The relaunched homepage therefore emphasized real-person matches, dates rather than online browsing, and a free trial, with a visible member photograph.

## Key Characteristics
- Began with a deliberately photo-free dating premise.
- Used group blind dates as its proposed route to compatible matches.
- Relaunched with profile photos and user-controlled preferences after weak signup demand.
- Positioned the revised service around real matches and time spent on dates.

## Evidence
Original premise and response:
- [[people-need-my-product-but-do-they-want-it]] describes the rational case against photo-based swiping and reports that signups suffered because users wanted photos.

Relaunched product:
- [[people-need-my-product-but-do-they-want-it]] reports restored photo upload and preference controls, while the retained homepage screenshot foregrounds a member photograph, matches, dates, and “Try It Free.”

## Qualifications
The profile relies on one 2017 practitioner essay and a homepage screenshot. It supplies no signup counts, experiment design, match-quality results, retention, revenue, user interviews, or evidence that photos alone caused the reported weakness or that the relaunch succeeded. The source also does not establish the company’s complete history or current status.

## What Changed
- Created a source-scoped profile treating the relaunch as a qualified demand-alignment case rather than proof that photo-first dating is superior.
