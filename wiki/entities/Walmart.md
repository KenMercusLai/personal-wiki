## Overview
[[Walmart]] is presented as a discount retailer whose apparent 1962 beginning conceals [[SamWalton]]'s prior 15 years of retail experimentation and operating experience.

## Current Profile
The source uses Walmart less as a complete company history than as a lesson about historical starting points. Its first store was physically plain but competed through consistently lower prices, and even the short name was selected partly to reduce signage cost. A retained grand-opening advertisement reinforces that positioning by promising customer savings across 22 departments.

The company's later scale can make its beginning look inevitable, but the source stresses financing rejection and limited contemporary attention. Walton reportedly borrowed 95% of the first store's capital against his home and other stores, while newspaper clippings show a modest 1969 quotation and a brief 1970 market mention rather than immediate acclaim.

## Key Characteristics
- Emerged from Walton's long retail apprenticeship rather than from a sudden first attempt.
- Established its early position through low prices and operational economy rather than polished store design.
- Required concentrated founder financing risk after outside-investor rejection.
- Received limited national-press attention during its early years.

## Evidence
- Prehistory: [[why-you-should-ignore-every-founders-story-about-how-they-started-their-company-trevor-mckendrick]] says Walton had operated stores for 15 years before the first Walmart opened.
- Store model: [[why-you-should-ignore-every-founders-story-about-how-they-started-their-company-trevor-mckendrick]] describes eight-foot ceilings, concrete floors, wooden fixtures, and prices that beat competitors.
- Grand-opening positioning: [[why-you-should-ignore-every-founders-story-about-how-they-started-their-company-trevor-mckendrick]] retains an advertisement promising savings in a 22-department store.
- Financing: [[why-you-should-ignore-every-founders-story-about-how-they-started-their-company-trevor-mckendrick]] says Walton obtained only 5% of the capital from his brother and a manager and borrowed the remaining 95%.
- Press attention: [[why-you-should-ignore-every-founders-story-about-how-they-started-their-company-trevor-mckendrick]] retains 1969 and 1970 newspaper clippings that treat Walton and Wal-Mart briefly within broader stories.

## Qualifications
This is a narrow origin-history profile, not an assessment of Walmart's subsequent growth, financial performance, labor practices, supplier power, logistics, community effects, governance, or current operations. The source's retrospective focus can understate contributors other than Walton and cannot by itself establish causal weight among pricing, location, capital, execution, and market conditions.
