## Overview
[[Onavo]] appears as the Facebook-owned team behind Onavo Protect, a VPN-based security app whose aggregated app-usage telemetry reportedly informed Facebook's responses to competitors.

## Current Profile
The Engadget report describes Protect as collecting information about app-use frequency and duration and making aggregated trends available to [[Facebook]]. Those signals reportedly exposed slowing [[Snapchat]] use after Instagram Stories, [[WhatsApp]]'s strength in some markets, and behavior around Meerkat and Periscope. This made Onavo more than a user-facing security utility: it also functioned as a [[CompetitiveIntelligence]] channel for product, acquisition, and competitive decisions. Facebook said the collection and use were disclosed, while critics questioned meaningful notice, competitive fairness, and possible App Store rule conflicts.

## Key Characteristics
- Operated as a Facebook-owned mobile data and VPN team.
- Offered Onavo Protect as a VPN-based security product.
- Collected app-frequency and duration signals described as aggregated in the source.
- Supplied early visibility into rival adoption and engagement trends.
- Connected consumer telemetry to product imitation, acquisition, and feature-entry decisions.

## Evidence
- Rival trend detection: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] says Facebook used Onavo data to observe Snapchat slowing after Instagram Stories launched.
- Acquisition input: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] reports that WhatsApp's geographic strength influenced Facebook's purchase decision.
- Product input: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] links observations of Meerkat and Periscope use to Facebook's live-video move.
- Disclosure dispute: [[facebook-knew-about-snaps-struggles-months-before-the-public-engadget]] contrasts Facebook's notice defense with critics' consent, competition, and App Store concerns.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on a short 2017 Engadget report drawing heavily on Wall Street Journal reporting rather than primary datasets, internal decision records, or user research. It does not reveal sample coverage, aggregation thresholds, analytical methods, causal confidence, or how much weight executives gave Onavo relative to other evidence. Disclosed collection is not equivalent to demonstrated user comprehension, but the source does not establish that collection was secret or legally unlawful.

## What Changed
- Created Onavo as the link between a consumer VPN product and Facebook's rival-app intelligence.
- Preserved the distinction between formal disclosure and demonstrated meaningful consent.
- Marked the reported strategic influence as secondary reporting rather than proven causal attribution.
