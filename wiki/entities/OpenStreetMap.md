## Overview
[[OpenStreetMap]] is presented as the open-source and volunteer-maintained contrast to Google's proprietary mapping system.

## Current Profile
The source describes OpenStreetMap as a democratic mapping project maintained by volunteers using open data, with companies such as Mapbox and CartoDB building on top of it. Parsons praises it as a "fabulous project" while criticizing its tendency to reflect contributor enthusiasm by adding too much detail. In the article's broader argument, OpenStreetMap shows that openness does not remove cartographic judgment: maps still need abstraction, selection, and restraint.

## Key Characteristics
- Maintained by a volunteer community using open data.
- Offers a democratic alternative to Google's proprietary map stream.
- Supports commercial and technical ecosystems that build on open map data.
- Can overrepresent the interests and enthusiasm of contributors.
- Still requires cartographic abstraction because useful maps depend on what is omitted as well as what is added.

## Evidence
- Open-data contrast: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] contrasts Google's proprietary data stream with OpenStreetMap's volunteer-maintained open data.
- Ecosystem: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] names Mapbox and CartoDB as companies making products on top of OpenStreetMap.
- Abstraction critique: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] quotes Parsons saying cartography is about what is taken off, not only what is added.
- Contributor bias: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] says OpenStreetMap can reflect the enthusiasm of its makers so strongly that it risks parody.

## Qualifications
The source's assessment of OpenStreetMap comes largely through a Google employee's framing and does not independently evaluate the project's data quality, governance, licensing, community norms, or later ecosystem.

## What Changed
