## Overview
[[JohnSaddington]] is an indie developer and blogger represented here through his retrospective on buying two [[DaringFireball]] sponsorships for [[Desk]].

## Current Profile
Saddington presents himself as a long-running blogger who built Desk to solve his own dissatisfaction with desktop publishing tools. His account is most useful as a first-person marketing case: he committed US$9,000 to an audience-aligned publication, tracked immediate proceeds and App Store rank, then reflected on the press, testimonials, operational strain, award recognition, and weaker repeat campaign that followed.

## Key Characteristics
- Indie developer and blogger behind the Desk Mac application.
- Treated the first Daring Fireball sponsorship as a researched but concentrated financial risk.
- Evaluated return through direct sales and a broader awareness halo.
- Publicly documented both the strong first result and the weaker second campaign.
- Emphasized audience fit, product quality, timing, capacity, attitude, and experimentation.

## Evidence
- Product and role: [[does-sponsoring-daring-fireball-actually-work-john-saddington]] identifies Saddington as Desk's developer and a blogger since 2001.
- Campaign economics: [[does-sponsoring-daring-fireball-actually-work-john-saddington]] reports the US$9,000 first placement, nearly US$16,000 in first-week net proceeds, and more than US$32,000 in December net revenue.
- Operational learning: [[does-sponsoring-daring-fireball-actually-work-john-saddington]] says insufficient review codes forced more than 30 prominent blogs onto a waitlist.
- Repeatability: [[does-sponsoring-daring-fireball-actually-work-john-saddington]] says the second sponsorship helped but was notably less successful.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from Saddington's own marketing retrospective and contains no audited financial statement, conversion cohort, attribution model, or independent reconstruction of the campaign. It documents one product and two placements in a 2014-2015 Apple-focused market rather than a general law of sponsorship effectiveness.
