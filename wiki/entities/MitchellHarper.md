## Overview
[[MitchellHarper]] is represented in the wiki by two 2016 practitioner essays on CEO focus and selecting venture investors for a long-term financing and governance relationship.

## Current Profile
Harper presents CEO effectiveness as concentration on a few compounding responsibilities rather than personal ownership of every operating detail. His model asks the CEO to explain why the strategy matters until the whole company can repeat it, personally help close scarce talent, and preserve enough cash for adverse conditions. His investor-selection essay extends that operating frame to the cap table: drawing on about 150 pitches and a reported $125 million raised for [[BigCommerce]], he favors personally compatible investors with operating experience, follow-on reserves, geographic flexibility, candid but bounded advice, consistent conduct, and understandable terms. His desire for investors who always back the CEO is materially qualified by the wiki's company-centered governance evidence.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats prioritization as the central defense against an overextended CEO role.
- Connects strategy with repeated company-wide explanation and employee role clarity.
- Keeps the CEO personally involved in recruiting positions judged critical.
- Evaluates candidates through motivation, humility, values, behavior under pressure, and near-term stage fit.
- Frames runway around bad-case survival rather than forecasted best-case growth.
- Favors measured channel experiments before aggressive sales and marketing investment.
- Treats investor behavior, reserves, operating experience, location tolerance, and financing terms as long-lived company-design choices.

## Evidence
- CEO priorities: [[good-ceos-do-just-3-things-mitchell-harper-medium]] adopts Wilson's vision, talent, and cash framework and supplies operating guidance for each part.
- Communication: [[good-ceos-do-just-3-things-mitchell-harper-medium]] recommends frequent all-hands repetition of strategy, purpose, progress, and each employee's connection to the plan.
- Recruiting: [[good-ceos-do-just-3-things-mitchell-harper-medium]] advises advance planning and personal CEO involvement in critical candidate outreach, meetings, and closing.
- Candidate judgment: [[good-ceos-do-just-3-things-mitchell-harper-medium]] emphasizes character and an approximately 18-month company horizon over resume prestige or distant organizational needs.
- Cash discipline: [[good-ceos-do-just-3-things-mitchell-harper-medium]] recommends adverse-case runway, controlled burn, and small channel tests before scaling spend.
- Fundraising experience: [[not-all-vcs-are-assholes-mitchell-harper-medium]] reports about 150 VC pitches and $125 million raised across four BigCommerce rounds.
- Investor selection: [[not-all-vcs-are-assholes-mitchell-harper-medium]] favors trust, operating experience, reserves, directness, strategic flexibility, stable post-close conduct, and clear terms.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from two short first-person advice essays. Their numerical thresholds, pitch and financing figures, and claims about CEO or investor quality are self-reported heuristics rather than comparative findings. The sources provide no financial records, contract set, counterparty perspectives, hiring outcomes, or evidence that the frameworks are complete across industries, stages, and capital cycles. Harper's call for investors to back the CEO every time also leaves ordinary support insufficiently separated from warranted board accountability.
