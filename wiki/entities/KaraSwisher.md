## Overview
[[KaraSwisher]] is a technology journalist, [[AllThingsD]] and [[Recode]] co-founder, and [[CodeConference]] host represented through deep sourcing, blunt interviewing, industry longevity, mentorship, and recurring tensions between access and independence.

## Current Profile
The 2014 profile explains Swisher's unusual mix of fear, trust, and affection through mechanisms rather than personality alone. She had covered technology since the early commercial internet, knew founders before they became powerful, maintained relationships when she did not need a quote, and repeatedly broke deal, personnel, and governance stories. Her humor, insults, direct pressure, historical memory, and selective discretion could produce candor while allowing professional relationships to survive harsh coverage.

That influence was also institutional. With [[WaltMossberg]], she built [[AllThingsD]] and its live-interview conference, then launched [[Recode]] with media rather than venture backing. Recusal from Google coverage, extensive disclosure, and separated finances addressed parts of the conflict created by her then-wife's Google role, but conference revenue, friendships, board composition, investors, and source dependence still created genuine appearance and access risks. The 2016 [[NitashaTiku]] account carries the tension forward: Swisher remains an admired mentor and confrontational interviewer, while [[CodeConference]] is also a safe, relationship-rich environment for technology leaders.

## Key Characteristics
- Built authority through long industry memory, persistent sourcing, and repeated consequential scoops.
- Combines charm, humor, insults, and pressure in interviews and source relationships.
- Uses confirmation thresholds and business relevance to distinguish publishable reporting from private knowledge.
- Combines reporting with ownership and hosting of technology publications and conferences.
- Uses disclosure, recusal, financial separation, and funding choices to manage conflicts without claiming they disappear.
- Maintains professional relationships with people she criticizes and mentors other reporters.
- Exercises agenda-setting power while operating inside the elite network she scrutinizes.

## Evidence
- Reporting authority: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] attributes Swisher's influence to early internet coverage, deep sourcing, historical knowledge, persistent contact, and repeated scoops.
- Confrontational relationship style: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] supplies examples of blunt questioning, social play, pressure, generosity, and continued ties after severe criticism; [[ground-control-to-silicon-valley]] records later questioning of Musk and Denton.
- Editorial judgment: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] describes her withholding insufficiently confirmed or merely personal material while publishing private facts she judged commercially relevant.
- Institutional power: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] traces AllThingsD, its conference, and Re/code; [[ground-control-to-silicon-valley]] shows the later Code Conference convening senior executives, investors, and reporters.
- Conflict management: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] documents Google recusal, disclosure, separate finances, and avoidance of venture funding alongside unresolved board, backer, conference, and relationship concerns.
- Mentorship and representation: [[ground-control-to-silicon-valley]] describes Tiku's experience of Swisher's support and reports Swisher promising that the event's lack of Black speakers would not recur.

## Qualifications
The evidence consists of a 2014 magazine profile and one critical 2016 conference report, not a systematic review of Swisher's stories, unpublished decisions, sourcing accuracy, corrections, business interests, or later career. Both sources rely partly on reputation and anecdote; Tiku identifies Swisher as a mentor, while several 2014 critics had direct professional conflicts with her. The inability of quoted rivals to name a suppressed major story weakens any claim that access necessarily softened coverage, but it does not resolve story-selection, recusal, appearance, or institutional-incentive questions.

## What Changed
- Added the early-career, sourcing, historical-memory, and relationship mechanisms behind Swisher's influence.
- Added AllThingsD and Re/code as institutions that expanded both her reporting power and her conflicts.
- Added selective discretion, confirmation, disclosure, recusal, and funding structure as distinct editorial boundaries.
- Qualified the access critique with evidence of aggressive coverage and durable relationships after criticism.
