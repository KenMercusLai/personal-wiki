## Overview
[[CreativeCloud]] is [[Adobe]]'s subscription model for creative software, presented as both the replacement for packaged Creative Suite purchases and the product-leadership vehicle for Adobe's large business-model transition.

## Current Profile
Creative Cloud is a structural shift from owning a packaged version of an Adobe app toward paying for ongoing access. That shift changes who can start using Adobe tools, how Adobe earns from professionals, how updates and maintenance are funded, and how the product fits a multi-device world where per-device licenses are harder to sustain.

Creative Cloud was also the new foundation around which Adobe had to coordinate 15 major applications, many utilities, recurring revenue, direct customer relationships, service availability expectations, continuous development, and customers' emotional move from owning software to renting access.

## Key Characteristics
- Replaces perpetual packaged-software purchases with recurring subscription access.
- Lowers upfront cost for some casual or aspiring users.
- Lets Adobe earn recurring revenue from long-term professional use.
- Keeps users on current software versions in the source's argument.
- Fits a multi-device usage environment better than traditional per-device packaged licensing, though the article corrects that usage was not unlimited.
- Required Adobe to transform a full suite of applications, not just reprice one product.
- Increased organizational demands around continuous delivery, service availability, direct customer relationships, and stakeholder communication.

## Evidence
- Replacement role: [[adobes-subscription-model-why-platform-owners-should-care-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] says Adobe products would be solely available through Creative Cloud.
- Entry friction: [[adobes-subscription-model-why-platform-owners-should-care-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] says a subscription is more approachable for casual and student users than a large upfront purchase.
- Recurring capture: [[adobes-subscription-model-why-platform-owners-should-care-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] says Adobe can earn every month from professional users instead of only every few years.
- Current versions: [[adobes-subscription-model-why-platform-owners-should-care-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] says users always have the most recent version under the subscription model.
- Device qualification: [[adobes-subscription-model-why-platform-owners-should-care-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] updates the initial claim and says apps were limited to two PCs with separate tablet-app payments.
- Suite complexity: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says Creative Suite involved 15 major applications plus smaller utilities that all had to transform.
- Organizational transformation: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] lists finance, legal, marketing, sales, engineering, service availability, channels, and customer emotion as transition concerns.
- Leadership evidence: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says prototypes and continuous communication helped Adobe move the transition forward.

## Qualifications
This page reflects the sources' 2013 and 2016 framings of Creative Cloud, including Stratechery's correction about device limits and SVPG's retrospective product-leadership emphasis. It does not describe later Creative Cloud product bundles, pricing, licensing rules, customer reaction, or all execution details.

## What Changed
- Added Creative Cloud as a product-leadership case for transforming a complex product suite and revenue model.
- Created Creative Cloud as the subscription product model replacing Adobe's packaged creative software.
