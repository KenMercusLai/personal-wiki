## Overview
[[EvanSpiegel]] is presented as the [[Snapchat]] leader associated with acquisition-led capability building and with positioning Snap as a camera company whose products reshape visual communication.

## Current Profile
The sources attribute Snap's mapping, hardware, augmented-reality lens, avatar, search, computer-vision, and Snapcode capabilities partly to a sequence of acquisitions. Madrigal's earlier essay interprets Spiegel's “camera company” description as a product thesis: cameras are becoming processing and communication interfaces, with Lenses central to expressive self-presentation. The defensible evidence concerns public positioning and reported product associations; personal authorship, deal judgment, and causal impact remain secondary-source interpretations without decision records.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses acquisitions as a route to product capabilities rather than treating deals only as financial expansion.
- Is associated with integrating acquired technology into recognizable Snapchat experiences.
- Is framed as maintaining a broad product thesis that could include mapping, avatars, search, hardware, and computer vision alongside the camera.
- Positions Snap's camera as a communication and transformation interface rather than only a capture device.

## Evidence
- Acquisition portfolio: [[evan-spiegels-most-underrated-skill-product-hunt]] associates Zenly, Vergence Labs, Looksery, Bitstrips, Vurb, Seene, and an unnamed Snapcode acquisition with later Snap products.
- Product incorporation: [[evan-spiegels-most-underrated-skill-product-hunt]] links those purchases to Snap Map, Spectacles, lenses, Bitmoji, mobile search, computer vision, and Snapcodes.
- Strategic range: [[evan-spiegels-most-underrated-skill-product-hunt]] notes that location sharing did not directly match the stated “camera company” mission but could complement a friends-and-family network.
- Camera-company thesis: [[forget-drones-and-spaceships-the-snapchat-dogface-filter-is-the-future]] uses Spiegel's public positioning to interpret Lenses, image processing, and picture-based communication as one product direction.
- Lens acquisition: [[evan-spiegels-most-underrated-skill-product-hunt]] and [[forget-drones-and-spaceships-the-snapchat-dogface-filter-is-the-future]] associate Looksery's reported US$150 million purchase with Snapchat's real-time face effects, without establishing the technical or managerial contribution precisely.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on two short secondary essays from 2016-2017. They do not establish Spiegel's personal role in each deal, distinguish acquired technology from later internal work, cover failed acquisitions, or measure usage, retention, revenue, integration cost, or return on purchase price. The “camera company” interpretation is culturally and strategically suggestive but not evidence that face-centered AR became the category's lasting dominant form.

## What Changed
- Added the camera-company position as a product thesis linking capture, processing, visual communication, and Lenses.
- Preserved the separation between public positioning, reported product associations, and proof of Spiegel's personal causal role.
