## Overview
[[EvanSpiegel]] is presented by a 2017 Product Hunt essay as a Snapchat leader whose underappreciated product skill was selecting acquisitions that could become core user-facing capabilities.

## Current Profile
The source attributes Snap's mapping, hardware, augmented-reality lens, avatar, search, computer-vision, and Snapcode capabilities partly to a sequence of acquisitions. Its strongest defensible point is that Spiegel's product strategy used external companies as inputs to the roadmap; its stronger suggestion that this was personally his distinctive skill remains an authorial interpretation without decision records or comparisons to other Snap leaders.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses acquisitions as a route to product capabilities rather than treating deals only as financial expansion.
- Is associated with integrating acquired technology into recognizable Snapchat experiences.
- Is framed as maintaining a broad product thesis that could include mapping, avatars, search, hardware, and computer vision alongside the camera.

## Evidence
- Acquisition portfolio: [[evan-spiegels-most-underrated-skill-product-hunt]] associates Zenly, Vergence Labs, Looksery, Bitstrips, Vurb, Seene, and an unnamed Snapcode acquisition with later Snap products.
- Product incorporation: [[evan-spiegels-most-underrated-skill-product-hunt]] links those purchases to Snap Map, Spectacles, lenses, Bitmoji, mobile search, computer vision, and Snapcodes.
- Strategic range: [[evan-spiegels-most-underrated-skill-product-hunt]] notes that location sharing did not directly match the stated “camera company” mission but could complement a friends-and-family network.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one short secondary essay from 2017. It does not establish Spiegel's personal role in each deal, distinguish acquired technology from later internal work, cover failed acquisitions, or measure usage, retention, revenue, integration cost, or return on purchase price.

## What Changed
- Established an initial profile of acquisition-led capability building while separating observed product associations from personal causal attribution.
