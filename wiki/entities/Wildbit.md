## Overview
[[Wildbit]] is the software-company context for a practitioner account of team-centered [[ProductManagement]].

## Current Profile
The source presents Wildbit through its operating philosophy rather than its corporate history or performance. Its stated product-management approach treats a happy, effective team as a precondition for consistent customer value and asks product managers to combine direct customer learning, whole-team idea gathering, clear strategy, capacity-aware priorities, and detailed execution. The account is explicitly local: the author says the approach works for Wildbit but may not work everywhere.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames team fulfillment and sustainable effectiveness as product-quality concerns.
- Favors direct customer access while separating needs from requested features.
- Treats strategy and prioritization as facilitative, cross-functional work.
- Rejects overtime pressure and command-and-control product leadership.

## Evidence
- Team operating philosophy: [[good-bad-product-manager-wildbit-medium]] links retrospectives, informal feedback, sustainable pace, and internal motivation to consistent product work.
- Customer and strategy practice: [[good-bad-product-manager-wildbit-medium]] combines proactive research with team-derived strategy and customer, business, and technology trade-offs.
- Scope boundary: [[good-bad-product-manager-wildbit-medium]] explicitly describes the model as Wildbit's own take rather than a universal prescription.

## Qualifications
The single company-authored essay provides no organizational history, staffing data, team outcome measures, customer results, or independent comparison. It is evidence of an espoused operating philosophy, not proof that every Wildbit team consistently practiced it or that the model caused product success.
