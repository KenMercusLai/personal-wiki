## Overview
[[VincentVanGogh]] is cited in the source through his painting of shoes, which the essay uses as an example of art disclosing more than an object's ordinary tool function.

## Current Profile
Within this wiki, Van Gogh functions as an aesthetic example rather than a biographical subject. The article uses his painted shoes to show how art can make a familiar object appear as if it has a hidden life, while still preserving the object-oriented claim that the real object never becomes fully available.

## Key Characteristics
- Used as the source's central visual-art example.
- Associated with painted shoes that exceed ordinary tool-use perception.
- Illustrates art's power to reveal an object's hidden or deeper world.
- Supports the source's distinction between aesthetic disclosure and complete access to the real object.
