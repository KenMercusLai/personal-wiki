## Overview
[[RonConway]] is presented as a prolific Silicon Valley angel investor and early Google investor who advised portfolio-company CEOs through both the 2000 technology collapse and the 2008 financial crisis.

## Current Profile
Conway's emails depict an active portfolio adviser using experience from the 2000 downturn as pattern recognition for 2008. He warned that capital availability could deteriorate rapidly and translated that forecast into concrete founder actions: extend runway by three to six months or more, raise promptly when possible, broaden investor outreach, accept valuation pressure, seek corporate capital, and consider M&A before cash becomes too short to complete a transaction.

The posture is defensive about portfolio survival but not pessimistic about technology. [[MichaelArrington]] reports that Conway intended to continue investing and remained confident in innovation; Conway's concern was whether companies without large cash reserves could survive until financing conditions improved.

## Key Characteristics
- Used experience from the 2000 technology collapse to interpret the 2008 financial shock.
- Communicated urgent, portfolio-wide operating guidance directly to startup CEOs.
- Treated lower burn as an internal financing round that could buy at least three to six additional months.
- Favored broad, early fundraising outreach and realistic valuation expectations when capital tightened.
- Presented partnerships and M&A as survival and critical-mass options for cash-constrained companies.
- Continued investing despite warning founders about severe near-term financing conditions.

## Evidence
- Cross-cycle pattern recognition: [[michael-arrington-angel-investor-ron-conway-emails-his-portfolio-companies-over-financial-meltdown]] reproduces Conway's 2000 emails and his statement that their advice still applied in 2008.
- Runway discipline: [[michael-arrington-angel-investor-ron-conway-emails-his-portfolio-companies-over-financial-meltdown]] records his recommendation to add at least three to six months through cost reductions, even when cuts affect staffing, marketing, and administration.
- Financing and consolidation options: [[michael-arrington-angel-investor-ron-conway-emails-his-portfolio-companies-over-financial-meltdown]] advises earlier and broader investor meetings, realistic valuations, corporate capital, and timely M&A exploration.
- Continuing conviction: [[michael-arrington-angel-investor-ron-conway-emails-his-portfolio-companies-over-financial-meltdown]] reports that Conway remained optimistic about innovation and did not plan to stop investing.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on three crisis-period emails selected and published by Arrington plus Arrington's account of a conversation with Conway. It does not provide portfolio survival rates, company-specific constraints, founder responses, financing terms, or evidence that cuts and acquisitions outperformed continued investment. Reusing 2000 advice in 2008 is evidence of a consistent heuristic, not proof that the two crises had identical causes or effects.

## What Changed
- Created the entity as an investor whose downturn guidance joins capital availability, runway, fundraising, valuation, partnerships, and M&A.
- Preserved the distinction between short-term portfolio defense and long-term optimism about technology investing.
