## Overview
[[EvanWilliams]] is represented here as the CEO of [[Odeo]] giving a public retrospective on the podcasting startup's loss of focus in 2006.

## Current Profile
The source portrays Williams as unusually candid about the gap between his published startup rules and his own operating choices. He links Odeo's difficulties to excessive scope, weak founder-user fit, slow response to Apple, premature fundraising, and failure to act on his unease. His response was to reduce headcount, refocus on web podcast listeners whose behavior he shared, and protect a small [[Twitter]] team from the broader company's diffusion.

## Key Characteristics
- Publicly examined decisions that contradicted his own advice to keep startups narrow and small.
- Distinguished strategic hindsight from recovery by acknowledging that Odeo still lacked a business model.
- Connected capital and organizational commitments with reduced ability to act on founder judgment.
- Shifted Odeo toward a user behavior he personally understood after admitting he did not podcast.
- Used a small dedicated team to isolate Twitter as a side project.

## Evidence
- Self-critique: [[evan-williams-how-odeo-screwed-up]] reports Williams admitting that he ignored many of his own startup rules while building Odeo.
- Founder-user gap: [[evan-williams-how-odeo-screwed-up]] says he did not podcast and that Odeo's browser-based recording tools were too simplistic.
- Capital and judgment: [[evan-williams-how-odeo-screwed-up]] reports that funding, hiring, board discussion, and press attention drowned out his gut.
- Corrective action: [[evan-williams-how-odeo-screwed-up]] says Odeo downsized, targeted web listeners, and gave Twitter a full-time team of two.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to a brief conference report and Williams's retrospective framing. It does not supply customer research, employee or investor accounts, detailed product usage, financing terms, or later outcomes, and it cannot show which alternative decision would have succeeded.
