## Overview
[[ShanWeijian]] is presented as the author of *Out of the Gobi*, a finance executive and founder of [[PAG]], whose personal history grounds a three-part account of success: lifelong learning, good judgment, and luck.

## Current Profile
The source recounts that Shan spent six years in the Gobi during the Cultural Revolution and lost the chance to attend middle school, but continued learning and later seized the post-Cultural-Revolution opportunity to study in the United States. In a 2023 University of San Francisco speech, he reportedly ranked lifelong learning, judgment, and luck as the main contributors to success. Judgment matters because effort has value only when directed well, yet judgment itself is learned through accumulated knowledge rather than treated as an inborn gift.

His account also resists a clean adversity-to-triumph formula. Shan calls himself very lucky even though his adolescence included severe deprivation and a lost first opportunity to leave the Gobi. The source interprets his path as a mixture of misfortune, rare openings, and preparation that made an opening usable, not as proof that effort can reliably overcome every starting condition.

## Key Characteristics
- Continued learning despite prolonged exclusion from formal schooling.
- Treats lifelong learning as the foundation for later opportunity and judgment.
- Ranks judgment above undirected persistence because choosing the right direction determines whether effort is useful.
- Treats good judgment as developed through knowledge rather than fixed at birth.
- Explicitly credits luck and uses it as a reason for humility and continued learning.
- Embodies prepared responsiveness to opportunity without denying earlier misfortune.

## Evidence
- Adversity and learning: [[blog-1byte-io-yun-qi-yu-nu-li]] says Shan spent six years in the Gobi, missed middle school, and continued to study.
- Later opportunity: [[blog-1byte-io-yun-qi-yu-nu-li]] says he used the post-Cultural-Revolution opening to study in the United States and later built a finance career.
- Three-part framework: [[blog-1byte-io-yun-qi-yu-nu-li]] attributes to his 2023 University of San Francisco speech the combination of lifelong learning, good judgment, and luck.
- Judgment mechanism: [[blog-1byte-io-yun-qi-yu-nu-li]] says correct decisions rest on learning and accumulated knowledge, making judgment an extension of lifelong study.
- Mixed fortune: [[blog-1byte-io-yun-qi-yu-nu-li]] notes both the severe bad luck of his youth and a lost first chance to leave the Gobi before a later opportunity became usable.

## Qualifications

It does not independently verify the biographical details, compare his framework with outcome data, or cover his full investing record, institutional role, or the history of PAG. His self-description as extremely lucky is evidence of his stated interpretation, not a measured decomposition of luck, learning, judgment, and effort.
