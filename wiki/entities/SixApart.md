## Overview
[[SixApart]] was the blogging-software company that acquired Danga and [[LiveJournal]] in 2005 before selling LiveJournal to SUP Media in 2007.

## Current Profile
In this source, Six Apart represents an acquisition whose capital and professionalization initially raised staff optimism but whose business model and organizational experience did not fit LiveJournal's unusually vocal consumer community. It hired longtime volunteers and tried to introduce project management and advertising, yet inherited promises against ads, expectations of consultation and personalized responses, and policy conflicts such as “Nipplegate.” The account says distrust between decision-makers and power users remained unresolved before Six Apart sold the platform.

## Key Characteristics
- Blogging-software company best known in the source for TypePad.
- Acquired LiveJournal and funded the hiring of experienced community volunteers.
- Brought commercial and project-management pressure to a platform shaped by informal founder and volunteer governance.
- Struggled to reconcile advertising needs with inherited promises and power-user expectations.
- Sold LiveJournal to SUP Media in 2007 after a two-year ownership period.

## Evidence
- Acquisition and initial optimism: [[the-linux-of-social-media-how-livejournal-pioneered-then-lost-blogging-ars-technica]] says staff expected Six Apart to provide capital and a durable roadmap, and that volunteers became paid employees.
- Business-model conflict: [[the-linux-of-social-media-how-livejournal-pioneered-then-lost-blogging-ars-technica]] describes management pushing for ads after users had been promised the site would not include them.
- Governance mismatch: [[the-linux-of-social-media-how-livejournal-pioneered-then-lost-blogging-ars-technica]] says Six Apart was accustomed to selling to businesses rather than handling the policy intensity of a direct consumer community.
- Divestment: [[the-linux-of-social-media-how-livejournal-pioneered-then-lost-blogging-ars-technica]] records the 2007 sale to Russian company SUP Media.

## Qualifications
The account is told mainly through LiveJournal insiders and does not provide Six Apart executives' full rationale, financial data, alternative plans, or a controlled comparison with other acquisitions. The ownership period therefore supports a fit diagnosis more strongly than a simple claim of managerial incompetence.
