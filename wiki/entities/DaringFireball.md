## Overview
[[DaringFireball]] is [[JohnGruber]]'s Apple-focused independent publication, represented here as the direct sponsorship channel used to promote [[Desk]].

## Current Profile
The source describes Daring Fireball as a trusted, concentrated Apple and Mac audience whose commercial value comes from contextual fit as well as raw reach. Its sponsorship product paired a sponsor-written RSS placement with a later acknowledgment from Gruber. In Saddington's first campaign, immediate sales, App Store rank, review interest, and press followed; a second campaign was weaker, indicating that access to the audience did not guarantee a fixed return.

## Key Characteristics
- Independent Apple-focused publication written by John Gruber.
- Monetized through direct sponsorship rather than CPM-only inventory in the source's period.
- Offered sponsor control over the main paid message plus a later publisher thank-you.
- Charged roughly US$8,000-US$10,000 per placement in the historical account.
- Supplied a concentrated audience with strong relevance to a Mac blogging application.
- Produced variable results across Saddington's two campaigns.

## Evidence
- Audience and format: [[does-sponsoring-daring-fireball-actually-work-john-saddington]] describes the Apple focus, sponsor-authored RSS placement, and subsequent editorial acknowledgment.
- First-campaign outcome: [[does-sponsoring-daring-fireball-actually-work-john-saddington]] reports nearly US$16,000 in first-week net proceeds and substantial secondary attention.
- Variance: [[does-sponsoring-daring-fireball-actually-work-john-saddington]] says the later January 2015 campaign was less successful despite a similar format.

## Qualifications
The evidence is one advertiser's retrospective and does not provide Daring Fireball audience size, sponsorship inventory, standardized conversion data, or results from unsuccessful advertisers. Prices, platform mechanics, and audience composition are specific to the 2014-2015 period described.

- Created the publication entity from Saddington's two-campaign case study.
