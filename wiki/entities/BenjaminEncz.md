## Overview
[[BenjaminEncz]] is the author of the 2018 practitioner essay “Flavors of Engineering Management.”

## Current Profile
Encz is represented in the wiki through a taxonomy of tech-lead, product-team, and people-focused engineering managers. He writes from direct experience with the first two designs but explicitly treats the people-manager form as secondhand and more tentative. His comparison centers role design on technical depth, product ownership, team topology, management span, evaluation evidence, hiring, mentoring, and accountability rather than on one universal job description.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames engineering management as several overlapping role designs rather than one fixed job.
- Connects coding time to organizational scope and technical breadth.
- Compares functional and cross-functional reporting structures through diagrams and operating consequences.
- Highlights micromanagement, key-person dependency, specialist-evaluation gaps, and unclear accountability as distinct failure modes.
- Explicitly marks the boundary between firsthand and secondhand experience.

## Evidence
- Authorship and framework: [[flavors-of-engineering-management-thinking-inside-a-large-box]] presents Encz's three-part engineering-management taxonomy.
- Functional model: [[flavors-of-engineering-management-thinking-inside-a-large-box]] grounds the tech-lead form in deep stack expertise, coding, mentoring, and platform staffing.
- Product model: [[flavors-of-engineering-management-thinking-inside-a-large-box]] grounds the product-team form in cross-stack architecture and product-area coordination.
- Evidence boundary: [[flavors-of-engineering-management-thinking-inside-a-large-box]] says Encz had not personally worked in the people-manager model.

## Qualifications
The wiki has one Encz source: a historical practitioner essay without comparative outcome data. It does not establish that the three categories are exhaustive, that the stated team-size ranges are optimal, or that the described hiring and performance effects generalize across companies.
