## Overview
[[KristenKoster]] is an early Ultima Online designer whose account connects game-industry sexism with credibility denial, role displacement, caregiving, and extreme operational hours.

## Current Profile
Koster joined Ultima Online alongside her husband Raph at an equivalent level but reports that male colleagues with less multiplayer experience questioned or ignored her work and bypassed her data-driven designs. When management planned to make Raph lead designer, anti-nepotism reporting constraints displaced her from design; the offered live-support role required shifting hours that were poorly suited to a new mother. She left after two and a half years, continued volunteer design, programming, and community work on LegendMUD, and describes an industry where on-call expectations, 14-16-hour days, weekends, and all-nighters made usable time off scarce.

## Key Characteristics
- Brought multiplayer design experience to an early persistent online game.
- Reports gendered credibility denial despite equivalent responsibility and experience.
- Experienced a formal reporting rule as unequal role displacement after her husband's promotion.
- Connects caregiving constraints with the practical quality of alternative roles.
- Continued unpaid game-community work after leaving formal industry employment.

## Evidence
- Credibility denial: [[gamasutra-the-great-video-game-exodus]] says less-experienced male colleagues questioned or ignored Koster's designs and hardcoded around her system.
- Role displacement: [[gamasutra-the-great-video-game-exodus]] says her husband's planned promotion meant she could no longer remain on his design team.
- Caregiving mismatch: [[gamasutra-the-great-video-game-exodus]] describes an offered support job with irregular hours after she became a mother.
- Extreme availability: [[gamasutra-the-great-video-game-exodus]] reports pagers, continuous on-call expectations, 14-16-hour days, weekends, all-nighters, and repeatedly deferred leave.

## Qualifications
The page reflects Koster's retrospective account in one feature and does not include employer or colleague perspectives. The reporting-line rule may have had a legitimate conflict-of-interest purpose, but the source shows that its available remedy imposed the cost on her career.
