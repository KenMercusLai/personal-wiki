## Overview
[[MelonieFullick]] is represented in the wiki as the author of a [[UniversityAffairs]] essay diagnosing university website problems through user experience, institutional communication, and governance.

## Current Profile
In the available source, Fullick organizes her own experience and Twitter responses into three linked problem areas: design, navigation, and search; missing or unreliable information; and the dominance of promotional messaging over practical service. Her larger argument is institutional: a university website communicates whose needs matter, and it should help diverse audiences make sense of a complex organization rather than reproduce its silos.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames university website quality from the perspective of current users as well as prospective students.
- Connects navigation and content defects to institutional structure, ownership, and priorities.
- Treats the website as a public communication and service channel rather than only a marketing surface.
- Uses solicited user complaints to identify recurring patterns while acknowledging multiple audiences and causes.

## Evidence
- User-centered diagnosis: [[university-websites-the-so-so-the-bad-and-the-egregious-university-affairs]] groups reported frustrations around navigation, search, buried information, accessibility, mobile use, and content reliability.
- Institutional analysis: [[university-websites-the-so-so-the-bad-and-the-egregious-university-affairs]] argues that site structure reflects organizational silos and institutional judgments of importance.
- Communication standard: [[university-websites-the-so-so-the-bad-and-the-egregious-university-affairs]] presents the website as a way to help students, parents, journalists, faculty, and the public understand and navigate the university.
- Priority critique: [[university-websites-the-so-so-the-bad-and-the-egregious-university-affairs]] contrasts recruitment-oriented investment with the everyday needs of people already at the institution.

## Qualifications
This profile is source-scoped to one 2016 essay and does not attempt to summarize Fullick's wider scholarship, career, or later views. The source combines the author's analysis with informal Twitter responses and an appended comment thread, so not every staffing or governance claim is Fullick's own.
