Source: [4 Lessons From a Failed Startup - From and For First Time Founders]({{< relref "/wiki/sources/maderight-failed-startup-lessons.md" >}})

Maderight was a software-powered apparel manufacturer. The source says customers could submit designs and manage prototyping, production, and logistics through a web app. The company was accepted into Y Combinator and StartX, raised venture funding, operated for three years, and then shut down after failing to find product-market fit.

In the post-mortem, Maderight becomes a case study in startup learning. The author argues that the company should have used a hypothesis-testing framework from the beginning, evaluated runway by the number of tests it enabled, kept sales founder-led before product-market fit, and chosen between the service business and technology product instead of trying to operate both.
