## Overview
[[SpotLister]] was a marketplace that charged artists to submit tracks for review by independent Spotify playlist curators and used song metadata to match tracks with potentially relevant playlists.

## Current Profile
The 2018 Daily Dot account presents SpotLister as an attempt to productize the costly search for independent curators. Its Playlisting Indexing Algorithm reportedly used Spotify's API and Echo Nest song characteristics to route submissions by fit. The company said artists paid for review rather than guaranteed addition, while reporting access to 1,500 curators and 13,000 playlists with a combined 11.7 million followers after five months.

That formal distinction did not resolve the surrounding incentive problem. The article situates SpotLister among direct placement prices, retainers, fake followers, and artist efforts to generate enough activity to reach Spotify's algorithmic and editorial playlists. A March 17 update says the company, briefly renamed JamLister, shut down after Spotify deemed it non-compliant and disabled its API key.

## Key Characteristics
- Reduced artist search and outreach costs by aggregating independent playlist curators.
- Used platform metadata to match tracks with playlists by musical characteristics.
- Charged for curator review while disclaiming guaranteed placement.
- Reported rapid network scale in curators, playlists, and nominal followers.
- Depended on Spotify API access and ceased operating after that access was withdrawn.

## Evidence
- Matching model: [[inside-the-black-market-for-spotify-playlists]] says the service used Spotify and Echo Nest data to identify relevant playlists.
- Commercial model: [[inside-the-black-market-for-spotify-playlists]] reports a $2 submission price and quotes the company distinguishing paid review from paid placement.
- Claimed scale: [[inside-the-black-market-for-spotify-playlists]] attributes 1,500 curators, 13,000 playlists, and 11.7 million cumulative followers to SpotLister.
- Platform dependency: [[inside-the-black-market-for-spotify-playlists]] reports that Spotify disabled the API key after deeming the service non-compliant, prompting shutdown and refunds.

## Qualifications
The source is a journalistic account from early 2018, not an audit of submissions, curator behavior, follower authenticity, placement causality, or artist returns. Company scale figures and the review-versus-placement distinction are reported claims. The article's update establishes the stated shutdown reason but does not independently adjudicate every submission or curator relationship.

## What Changed
- Created SpotLister as a paid curator-review and playlist-matching marketplace.
- Preserved the distinction between a review fee and guaranteed placement while connecting both to broader playlist-market incentives.
- Recorded dependence on Spotify's API and the reported shutdown after access was disabled.
