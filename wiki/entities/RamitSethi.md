## Overview
[[RamitSethi]] is credited in the available source with naming and teaching the [[BriefcaseTechnique]] for job interviews.

## Current Profile
Holiday presents Sethi as the source of a candidate-side interview tactic: after establishing basic comfort and understanding, the applicant reveals prior research, explains the organization's needs, proposes improvements, and shows why their abilities fit the work. Within this wiki, that establishes Sethi's association with the named technique but not a broader profile of his career or teachings.

## Key Characteristics
- Credited with the name “Briefcase Technique.”
- Treats preparation as a visible work demonstration rather than private rehearsal alone.
- Recommends timing the presentation after initial rapport and role context are established.
- Connects research, organizational diagnosis, proposed improvement, and candidate fit.

## Evidence
- Attribution: [[heres-the-technique-that-ambitious-people-use-to-get-what-they-want-ryanholiday]] says Sethi calls the approach the Briefcase Technique.
- Interview sequence: [[heres-the-technique-that-ambitious-people-use-to-get-what-they-want-ryanholiday]] describes the reveal as following pleasantries, role explanation, and enough questions to establish trust.
- Demonstration content: [[heres-the-technique-that-ambitious-people-use-to-get-what-they-want-ryanholiday]] describes prior research, business learning, proposed improvements, and a fit argument.

## Qualifications
The evidence is Holiday's summary and a link to a Sethi video, not a locally ingested primary source from Sethi. The page therefore records attribution and the technique as represented by Holiday; it does not establish Sethi's complete formulation, outcome evidence, or wider work.
