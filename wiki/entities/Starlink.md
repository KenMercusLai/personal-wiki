## Overview
[[Starlink]] is [[SpaceX]]'s consumer-facing satellite-connectivity business and the article's proof point that launch scale can produce a large deployed low-Earth-orbit network and alter service expectations in adjacent industries.

## Current Profile
Thompson reports that Starlink generated $8.7 billion in revenue and $4.4 billion in profit in the prior year, while more than ten thousand active satellites delivered low-latency broadband. The airline case makes connectivity both a product benefit and a competitive threat: once rivals offer fast inflight Internet, carriers risk losing customers if they do not match it.

The network is also a technical precursor to the article's speculative compute thesis. Its satellite form factor, disposable deployment model, solar power, and laser interconnects suggest components of a distributed orbital system, although current communications satellites do not demonstrate that high-power AI racks can solve the much harder heat, radiation, reliability, and chip-supply problems.

## Key Characteristics
- Provides broadband through a large low-Earth-orbit satellite constellation.
- Benefits from SpaceX's integrated launch capacity.
- Expands into aviation, where service adoption can reset baseline customer expectations.
- Uses laser-linked, replaceable satellites that inform the proposed orbital-compute architecture.
- Supplies operating evidence for satellite scale, not proof of viable space-based AI compute.

## Evidence
- Business scale: [[spacex-ipo-data-centers-in-space]] reports prior-year revenue and profit attributed to Starlink.
- Network scale: [[spacex-ipo-data-centers-in-space]] reports more than ten thousand active low-Earth-orbit satellites.
- Airline adoption: [[spacex-ipo-data-centers-in-space]] cites American Airlines' plan for more than 500 narrowbody aircraft and compares earlier United adoption.
- Architectural precedent: [[spacex-ipo-data-centers-in-space]] points to satellite dimensions, power use, disposability, and laser links as ingredients for a future rack-satellite fleet.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one analyst article quoting announcements and estimates. It does not independently verify revenue recognition, profit allocation, launch costs, active-satellite counts, airline deployment outcomes, delivered speeds, service reliability, or contract terms. The comparison between a communications satellite and an AI rack is dimensional and conceptual; it does not establish equivalent power density, thermal behavior, radiation tolerance, compute performance, useful life, maintenance, or total cost.
