## Overview
[[DonNorman]] is a design thinker represented in this source through a quoted argument that an interface should recede behind the job a person is trying to accomplish.

## Current Profile
The 2018 article invokes Norman to support an outcome-centered view of interaction: people should direct their energy toward their task rather than toward understanding the interface. Within this wiki evidence boundary, the quote strengthens [[UtilityOrientedUX]] and the claim that familiar conventions can reduce unnecessary interface attention. It does not by itself establish which conventions work or remove the need for observation and testing.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats interfaces as means to user goals rather than ends in themselves.
- Supports minimizing interface attention that does not contribute to the intended job.
- Is used by the source as an authority for outcome-centered user-experience design.

## Evidence
Outcome-centered interaction:
- [[ever-wonder-why-the-most-popular-apps-are-starting-to-look-the-same-it-might-be-a-good-thing]] quotes Norman saying he wants to focus on the job rather than the interface.

Convention argument:
- [[ever-wonder-why-the-most-popular-apps-are-starting-to-look-the-same-it-might-be-a-good-thing]] places the quote inside its case that familiar interfaces can shift attention from navigation toward outcomes.

## Qualifications
The source provides one secondary quotation and a brief attribution, not a full account of Norman's work or the original context of the statement. The article's conclusion that visual convergence advances his goal is Akkawi's application, not evidence that Norman endorsed every standardized or sparse interface.
