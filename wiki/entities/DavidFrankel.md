## Overview
[[DavidFrankel]] is the managing-partner investor and author of a practitioner essay about volatility inside venture-capital decisions and the founder behaviors that may preserve fundraising momentum.

## Current Profile
Frankel describes an investment decision as a changing judgment rather than a clean progression from pitch to term sheet. Initial confidence from founder background, team quality, product, and traction can fall after references, market diligence, or the arrival of another opportunity. His advice focuses on the founder's limited levers: create observable commercial progress, run a concentrated process, avoid predictably weak seasonal periods when possible, and respond quickly and candidly to diligence.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes from the investor side of early-stage fundraising.
- Emphasizes changing confidence and attention between formal financing milestones.
- Treats customer, sales, and market progress as evidence that can restore investor conviction.
- Recommends process timing, competitive pressure, and diligence responsiveness as founder-controlled levers.

## Evidence
- Decision volatility: [[going-up-and-down-the-vc-roller-coaster-by]] compares an investor's changing excitement and concern with a roller coaster.
- Diligence factors: [[going-up-and-down-the-vc-roller-coaster-by]] names off-list references, market size, competition, capital intensity, and new opportunities as reasons confidence can decline.
- Founder guidance: [[going-up-and-down-the-vc-roller-coaster-by]] recommends visible commercial updates, a concentrated process, season-aware timing, and prompt responses to investor requests.
