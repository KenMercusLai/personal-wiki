## Overview
[[Delicious|Del.icio.us]] was [[JoshuaSchachter]]'s social-bookmarking service, built to let people save web links with lightweight tags and discover what others were collecting.

## Current Profile
The service grew from a personal workflow: Schachter saved URLs in a Unix text file and annotated them with a hash mark plus a word or two, exposed that collection through single-user Muxway, and then generalized it for multiple users. In the source's account, the important product move was reducing the cognitive overhead of hierarchical folders while making saved interests socially visible.

Growth outpaced the small company's operational capacity. The service experienced frequent downtime, expanded to dozens of machines, and raised US$1 million in early 2005 before a difficult second fundraising attempt. Yahoo acquired it later that year after presenting itself as a Web 2.0 buyer with search-indexing and infrastructure resources.

The acquisition improved some technical capacity but did not preserve momentum. Schachter reports slow internal approvals, weak strategic guidance, and a staffing shift toward Yahoo Answers that left Del.icio.us with about one engineer. The product was later sold through multiple owners and permanently shut down in 2017, according to the article's editorial introduction.

## Key Characteristics
- Turned terse user-defined tags into a low-friction bookmark organization method.
- Combined personal retrieval with visibility into other users' collections.
- Became an influential Web 2.0 product associated by the source with later services such as Reddit and Pinterest.
- Reached scale that strained a small team's availability and infrastructure capacity.
- Was acquired partly for access to Yahoo's technical resources and wider Web 2.0 push.
- Lost staff and product momentum inside the acquiring company before later ownership changes and shutdown.

## Evidence
- Interaction model: [[did-i-make-a-mistake-selling-del-icio-us-to-yahoo]] says users could save and tag with a word or two instead of carefully maintaining bookmark folders.
- Social layer: [[did-i-make-a-mistake-selling-del-icio-us-to-yahoo]] says users could see what others were working on and share or contribute to collections.
- Scale pressure: [[did-i-make-a-mistake-selling-del-icio-us-to-yahoo]] reports severe downtime and a fleet of roughly 30 or 40 machines before acquisition.
- Deal rationale: [[did-i-make-a-mistake-selling-del-icio-us-to-yahoo]] connects the Yahoo choice to a serious offer, Web 2.0 acquisitions, and promised indexing technology.
- Post-deal decline: [[did-i-make-a-mistake-selling-del-icio-us-to-yahoo]] reports limited technical follow-through and staffing reduced to roughly one engineer.

## Qualifications
The page is bounded to one retrospective interview and its editorial introduction. It lacks independent usage, uptime, market-share, acquisition, staffing, influence, ownership, and shutdown evidence. The claim that the service directly inspired Reddit and Pinterest comes from the introduction rather than from those companies or comparative product history.
