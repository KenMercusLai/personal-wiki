## Overview
[[VentureHacks]] is the publication credited as author of the essay "There Is No Finish Line for Entrepreneurs."

## Current Profile
In the available source, Venture Hacks presents entrepreneurship as a demanding product standard rather than merely company ownership or growth. It argues that entrepreneurs must continually improve both customer advancement and accessibility, using a non-scaling Italian restaurant and McDonald's as opposing examples of quality without reach and reach without sufficient quality.

## Key Characteristics
- Publishes concise, prescriptive startup and entrepreneurship commentary.
- Defines product quality through customer advancement.
- Treats broad product use as necessary but insufficient for entrepreneurship.
- Frames innovation as the response when quality and scale appear incompatible.

## Evidence
- Publication role: [[venture-hacks-there-is-no-finish-line-for-entrepreneurs]] identifies Venture Hacks as the source author.
- Customer standard: [[venture-hacks-there-is-no-finish-line-for-entrepreneurs]] defines quality by how far a product advances its customer.
- Joint objective: [[venture-hacks-there-is-no-finish-line-for-entrepreneurs]] rejects both quality without scale and scale without quality.
- Innovation stance: [[venture-hacks-there-is-no-finish-line-for-entrepreneurs]] says entrepreneurs must innovate when the two goals cannot both be met by the current approach.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one short essay and describes only the position expressed there. It does not establish the publication's broader editorial history, authorship structure, later views, or whether the restaurant examples accurately characterize the businesses named.
