## Overview
[[TheLegendOfZeldaLinksAwakening|The Legend of Zelda: Link's Awakening]] is a 1993 Game Boy game and later Nintendo Switch remake used by [[Piglei]] to illustrate how a durable conceptual core can survive extensive technical and visual modernization.

## Current Profile
The source presents the game as a compact but imaginative island adventure built around items, exploration, and interlocking dungeon puzzles. Its Switch remake replaces the original 160-by-144-pixel monochrome presentation with high-definition 3D graphics and more convenient controls while reportedly preserving the map and dungeon structure almost one to one. In the essay, that continuity makes the game an analogy for software whose implementation and surface can change while its valuable conceptual structure remains recognizable.

## Key Characteristics
- Originally released for [[GameBoy]] in 1993.
- Organizes play around items, exploration, overworld navigation, and dungeon mechanisms.
- Remade for Nintendo Switch with high-definition 3D presentation and modernized controls.
- Reported by the source to preserve most of the original map and dungeon design.
- Used as a case of long-lived product value surviving a major presentation-layer change.

## Evidence
- Original constraints: [[kai-fa-ruan-jian-huo-jian-zao-mi-gong-piglei]] describes a 160-by-144-pixel Game Boy release whose playability remained strong despite obsolete graphics, and retains a monochrome dungeon screenshot.
- Remake modernization: [[kai-fa-ruan-jian-huo-jian-zao-mi-gong-piglei]] describes new 3D graphics and easier item controls, supported by a retained Switch scene.
- Preserved core: [[kai-fa-ruan-jian-huo-jian-zao-mi-gong-piglei]] says the map, dungeons, item use, and exploration were retained nearly one to one.
- Commercial claim: [[kai-fa-ruan-jian-huo-jian-zao-mi-gong-piglei]] reports more than six million Switch sales and places the remake among the series' higher-selling entries.

## Qualifications
The supplied article is a reflective software-engineering essay rather than a game-history study. It does not provide primary design documents, a map comparison, a sales table, player research, or evidence that preservation of dungeon design caused the reported sales. The two retained screenshots show different locations, so they demonstrate presentation change but not one-to-one structural fidelity.
