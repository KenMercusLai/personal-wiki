## Overview
[[ByronSharp]] is a marketing researcher cited in the source for challenging the centrality of differentiation, especially in B2C markets where buyers often perceive competing brands as similar.

## Current Profile
The article uses Sharp and the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute as a counterweight to strong differentiation claims. His role is to shift attention from deep perceived difference toward distinctiveness, recognition, fame, and market share as drivers of consideration and recommendation.

## Key Characteristics
- Marketing researcher associated with critiques of differentiation-first brand strategy.
- Emphasizes distinctiveness and recognizability over fine-grained perceived difference in some consumer markets.
- Used in the source to qualify, not eliminate, the need for differentiation.

## Evidence
- Critique: [[cha-yi-hua-zhan-lue-zhi-nan-what-why-yu-how-36kr]] cites Sharp and the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute as questioning differentiation's importance in B2C contexts.
- Consumer perception: [[cha-yi-hua-zhan-lue-zhi-nan-what-why-yu-how-36kr]] says consumers often rate competing companies similarly and confuse brand attributes.
- Distinctiveness: [[cha-yi-hua-zhan-lue-zhi-nan-what-why-yu-how-36kr]] summarizes Sharp's alternative as building fame and recognition in competitive settings.

## Qualifications
The source does not present Sharp's full argument or data, and it still argues that smaller or newer firms often need meaningful differentiation when they lack market share.
