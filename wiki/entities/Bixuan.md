## Overview
[[Bixuan]] is a software-engineering practitioner-author who writes about reliability from the perspective of someone who has caused and handled serious production incidents.

## Current Profile
In this source, Bixuan presents reliability as a practical systems-engineering problem that spans code robustness, design choices, operational change, incident response, and organizational investment. His central stance is that the principles are widely known, but making them real requires capability, staffing, process discipline, and sustained attention.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames reliability as an implementation and investment problem rather than a mystery of technical knowledge.
- Connects code-level robustness with design-level dependency handling and operational change controls.
- Treats incident response as a restoration-first practice during outages.
- Emphasizes that reliability work is often undervalued because success is invisible.
- Argues that reliability should sometimes be funded like business feature delivery.

## Evidence
- Reliability framing: [[wen-ding-xing-nan-de-bu-shi-ji-shu-er-shi]] says principles and solutions are common in postmortems, but execution depends on capability and investment.
- Technical layering: [[wen-ding-xing-nan-de-bu-shi-ji-shu-er-shi]] organizes reliability into code, design, and change practices.
- Incident posture: [[wen-ding-xing-nan-de-bu-shi-ji-shu-er-shi]] says outage handling should restore service quickly before fully solving the underlying problem.
- Organizational critique: [[wen-ding-xing-nan-de-bu-shi-ji-shu-er-shi]] argues that many companies do reliability work in bursts because good reliability is hard to recognize when nothing breaks.
- Business alignment: [[wen-ding-xing-nan-de-bu-shi-ji-shu-er-shi]] recommends treating reliability investment as necessary feature-like work when business conditions justify it.

## Qualifications
The source is a practitioner essay rather than a formal study. It does not quantify the ideal reliability budget or prove one universal investment ratio across industries.
