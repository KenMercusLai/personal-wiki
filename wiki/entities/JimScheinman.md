## Overview
[[JimScheinman]] is represented as an early Friendster employee and Bebo's first employee, using those operating experiences to compare the product, growth, technical, hiring, leadership, and monetization choices of early social networks.

## Current Profile
Scheinman's retrospective is grounded in his stated responsibilities for marketing, business development, corporate development, and sales at [[Bebo]], together with his earlier work at [[Friendster]]. He presents Bebo as a deliberate attempt to apply lessons from Friendster: focus on a bounded student audience, protect performance, use proven technology, test small features quickly, hire slowly, align product and engineering, and wait for scale before emphasizing advertising.

His comparison with [[Facebook]] is interpretive rather than independent company history. It is most useful as an operator's account of how product, infrastructure, growth, organization, and business-model timing interact, not as proof that one checklist caused success or failure.

## Key Characteristics
- Draws comparative startup lessons from operating roles at Friendster and Bebo.
- Treats demographic focus, product focus, and technical restraint as connected allocation decisions.
- Frames site performance as a prerequisite for product iteration and growth.
- Advocates explicit ownership and repeated experimentation for viral growth.
- Favors patient hiring, stable leadership, and close product-engineering cooperation.
- Qualifies delayed monetization as appropriate for some fast-growing social networks rather than every startup.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one retrospective written by Scheinman. The supplied document does not independently verify his staffing figures, the internal reasoning of Friendster, Facebook, or Bebo, or the counterfactual effect of different product and management decisions. Successful-outcome hindsight may also make Bebo and Facebook's choices appear more intentional or decisive than they were at the time.
