## Overview
[[LucaDellanna]] is the author of the aggregator-trust essay represented in this wiki as a strategy writer connecting aggregation, market centralization, trust, and technology-enabled transaction design.

## Current Profile
The source presents Dellanna as extending [[AggregationTheory]] beyond demand control and matchmaking. His contribution here is to argue that aggregators become profitable because users trust them enough to transact with unfamiliar suppliers, which shifts both the trust burden and profit concentration upward to the platform layer. He also treats technology that reduces trust requirements as a possible source of future aggregator disruption.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes about aggregators through trust and transaction-cost dynamics.
- Uses [[Airbnb]] and [[Uber]] as examples of platforms that let strangers transact.
- Treats popularity as a proxy for trust in markets without adequate trust infrastructure.
- Connects trust transfer to profit redistribution between suppliers and aggregators.
- Frames smart contracts, smart locks, and self-driving cars as possible trust-minimization technologies.

## Evidence
- Trust extension: [[aggregators-and-trust-luca-dellanna]] argues that aggregators profit from demand popularity and trust minimization.
- Market examples: [[aggregators-and-trust-luca-dellanna]] uses hospitality and ride-hailing to show why customers once relied on known suppliers or taxis.
- Disruption thesis: [[aggregators-and-trust-luca-dellanna]] says technology that makes stranger transactions safer could take part of aggregator trust profits.
