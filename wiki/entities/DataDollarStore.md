## Overview
[[DataDollarStore]] was a one-week 2017 London pop-up where merchandise could be obtained only by surrendering personal phone data.

## Current Profile
The store turned an implicit digital exchange into a visible retail transaction. A mug cost selected photos or conversation screenshots; a shirt required recent photos or messages; and an original print required handing over the phone so staff could select material. This graded loss of choice made privacy cost salient, but the prices were campaign mechanics rather than evidence of a functioning data market.

## Key Characteristics
- Accepted personal data rather than money for all merchandise.
- Increased disclosure and reduced customer selection control for more exclusive goods.
- Used a physical storefront, queue, staff interaction, and artist merchandise to make an abstract online exchange tangible.
- Operated as a privacy-awareness marketing campaign rather than a proposed retail model.

## Qualifications
The report supplies no participant count, consent protocol, data-retention policy, valuation method, outcome measurement, or representative behavioral comparison. Merchandise scarcity, publicity, staff presence, and the novelty of a one-week event distinguish it from routine platform collection.

## What Changed
- Created the profile and separated its awareness mechanism from claims about actual data prices or future commerce.
