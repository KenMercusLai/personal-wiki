## Overview
[[KenNorton]] is represented as a product-management writer and speaker advocating order-of-magnitude ambition, evidence-led experimentation, and impact-oriented goals.

## Current Profile
The 2015 essay adapts Norton's Mind the Product keynote into an organizational argument. He uses Kodak, quartz watches, Gmail storage, Amazon operations, DHL's origin story, a ceramics-class anecdote, and the Apollo program to argue that extreme outcome targets can expose assumptions hidden by local optimization. His proposed operating conditions include failure tolerance, employee mobility and voice, open information, empirical tests, trend awareness, and measurement of impact rather than effort.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames product strategy through the contrast between 10x outcomes and 10% optimization.
- Uses historical and business anecdotes to translate strategic ideas into memorable operating prompts.
- Connects ambitious goals with experimentation, data, organizational openness, and failure tolerance.
- Treats incumbent success as a source of defensive incentives as well as capability.

## Evidence
- Authorship and setting: [[ken-norton-10x-not-10-percent]] identifies Norton as the author and says the essay derives from his September 2015 Mind the Product keynote.
- Strategic framework: [[ken-norton-10x-not-10-percent]] defines 10x as an order-of-magnitude improvement requiring a changed mentality and altered assumptions.
- Operating guidance: [[ken-norton-10x-not-10-percent]] recommends mobility, voice, openness, data, impact measures, trend awareness, and permission to fail.

## Qualifications
The profile is bounded to one motivational practitioner essay. Its cases are selected illustrations rather than controlled comparisons, and it does not establish that extreme goals outperform incremental strategies across organizations, risk levels, or domains.
