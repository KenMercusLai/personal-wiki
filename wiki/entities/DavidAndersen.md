## Overview
[[DavidAndersen]] is the computer-science professor and adviser who authored the 2016 guide "Finances for CS Ph.D. students."

## Current Profile
In the bounded source, Andersen translates conversations with his own doctoral students and his experience of starting work at CMU into a practical U.S. personal-finance sequence. His central objective is optionality: controlled spending, early saving, suitable tax treatment, simple investing, and transition liquidity should let students make career and life choices with less financial coercion.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes for a narrowly defined audience of U.S.-based computer-science Ph.D. students who are citizens, permanent residents, or expect to remain and retire in the United States.
- Combines adviser experience with a personal example of substantial moving, housing, and car expenses at the start of his CMU job.
- Favors simple low-cost funds and explicit cash-flow priorities over trading, timing, concentrated stock selection, and expensive management.

## Evidence
- Audience and role: [[finances-for-cs-ph-d-students]] says the post grew from conversations with Andersen's own Ph.D. students and states its U.S. residency scope.
- Transition experience: [[finances-for-cs-ph-d-students]] uses Andersen's first month at CMU to illustrate why graduates may need a liquid cash cushion.
- Investing stance: [[finances-for-cs-ph-d-students]] recommends low-cost diversified funds and warns against day trading, market timing, stock tips, and high expense ratios.

## Qualifications
This profile is limited to one 2016 advice article. It does not establish Andersen's complete academic biography, current institutional role, or present financial views, and the article's legal and numerical guidance should not be treated as current individualized advice.
