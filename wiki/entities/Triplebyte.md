## Overview
[[Triplebyte]] is represented here as a recruiting platform that helped software engineers obtain jobs at Bay Area technology companies and published a 2016 analysis of relocation economics.

## Current Profile
The article uses Triplebyte's candidate placements to challenge lower BLS salary figures for San Francisco: it reports an average of about $139,000 and slightly higher offers in San Francisco than on the peninsula. The company presents its access to candidates and employers as a source of practical evidence about relocation decisions. That same business model creates an incentive to emphasize the Bay Area's advantages, so its internal figures and recommendation require separation from neutral labor-market research.

## Key Characteristics
- Recruiting intermediary between software engineers and Bay Area technology employers.
- Source of internal offer data used to supplement public salary datasets.
- Publisher of career and location analysis aimed at prospective engineering candidates.
- Commercial beneficiary when candidates pursue jobs in the market it serves.

## Evidence
- Recruiting role: [[does-it-make-sense-for-programmers-to-move-to-the-bay-area-triplebyte-blog]] says Triplebyte helped engineers around the country and world get jobs at top Bay Area companies.
- Internal data: [[does-it-make-sense-for-programmers-to-move-to-the-bay-area-triplebyte-blog]] reports an average candidate salary near $139,000 and a small San Francisco advantage over peninsula placements.
- Publishing role: [[does-it-make-sense-for-programmers-to-move-to-the-bay-area-triplebyte-blog]] combines those observations with public salary, housing, and compensation sources.
- Incentive: [[does-it-make-sense-for-programmers-to-move-to-the-bay-area-triplebyte-blog]] ends by inviting readers interested in moving to the Bay Area to use Triplebyte's hiring process.

## Qualifications
This is a source-scoped 2016 profile, not a current company history. The article does not disclose candidate selection, sample size, role mix, experience distribution, offer dates, or employer composition, so the internal average cannot be generalized to all engineers. Triplebyte's commercial incentive does not invalidate the data, but it raises the evidentiary burden for its relocation recommendation.
