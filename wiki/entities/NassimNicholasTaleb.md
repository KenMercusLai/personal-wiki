## Overview
[[NassimNicholasTaleb]] is represented in the wiki as the author Hardy cites for skin in the game, risk-bearing conviction, bureaucracy's separation from consequence, and fragility created by protecting a position.

## Current Profile
Hardy's essay uses quotations attributed to Taleb to argue that belief becomes credible through willingness to bear risk and that systems perform poorly when decision-makers escape consequences. It further interprets fear of losing an existing position as a source of fragility and avoidance. The wiki's evidence here is secondary and selective: it records Hardy's use of Taleb, not a complete synthesis of Taleb's books or views.

## Key Characteristics
- Author associated with risk, consequence, and asymmetric exposure.
- Treats personal downside as a test of conviction and accountability.
- Critiques bureaucracy for separating decisions from their consequences.
- Connects excessive protection of existing position with fragility and avoidance.

## Evidence
- Consequence separation: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] attributes to Taleb a definition of bureaucracy centered on insulation from consequences.
- Risk-bearing belief: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] quotes Taleb on taking risks for one's opinions and showing belief through what one will risk.
- Fragility interpretation: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] uses another attributed quotation about feared losses to connect position protection with avoidance.

## Qualifications
This profile derives from one author's excerpting and interpretation and does not independently verify wording, context, or the larger argument of *Skin in the Game*. Personal exposure can improve alignment, but it does not guarantee expertise, truth, ethical conduct, or fair risk distribution; some public and organizational roles deliberately pool risk for legitimate reasons.
