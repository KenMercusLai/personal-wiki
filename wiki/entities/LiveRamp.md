## Overview
[[LiveRamp]] is a data company that [[AurenHoffman]] identifies as one of the businesses he previously ran in his 2019 account of [[DataAsAService]]. The source uses it as an example of middleware for customer data and identifiers about people.

## Current Profile
In Hoffman's account, LiveRamp sits between raw external or first-party data and the tools that use it. He groups it with middleware that helps companies make use of first-party data and describes its model as organized around people and identifiers. He also contrasts the Hadoop-era stack used at LiveRamp with the tools he later used at [[SafeGraph]] and cites LiveRamp when arguing that a data provider can dominate a particular niche.

These are historical observations from an operator's perspective, not a current profile of LiveRamp. They connect his earlier operating experience to the article's broader arguments about data joining, delivery, and market concentration without establishing how the company's products or ownership subsequently changed.

## Key Characteristics
- Appears as a data-middleware company in Hoffman's account of the first-party-data market.
- Uses people and identity-related keys as an example of how datasets can be joined.
- Serves as part of Hoffman's personal operating background before SafeGraph.
- Is cited by Hoffman as a niche-market-share example, a claim that requires independent verification before wider use.

## Qualifications
The only source in this page is a self-authored article from 2019. Its market-share estimate is not independently checked here, and the article does not establish LiveRamp's present-day ownership, products, market share, data practices, privacy protections, or outcomes. Person-linked data raises questions about consent, rights, and misuse that this historical company example does not resolve.
