## Overview
[[HubSpot]] is an inbound-marketing software company used in the wiki as an example of free tools, educational content, and narrow early product scope expanding toward a broader marketing platform.

## Current Profile
The growth-hacking source presents HubSpot as both seller and proof of inbound marketing: blogs and ebooks attract marketers, while the free Website Grader diagnoses site problems and routes users toward HubSpot's paid services. Yin's essay adds a product-evolution frame by saying early HubSpot focused on helping people write content before it added email, CRM, and fuller marketing-automation capabilities.

## Key Characteristics
- Provides tools for marketers managing inbound marketing.
- Uses its own content marketing as proof of its product philosophy.
- Offers Website Grader as a free diagnostic and acquisition tool.
- Used as a narrow-to-broad product-evolution example.

## Evidence
- Content engine: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says HubSpot publishes regular blog posts and added-value ebooks.
- Free diagnostic: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] describes Website Grader as a tool that shows site strengths and weaknesses while directing traffic to HubSpot services.
- Product evolution: [[build-a-product-that-fits-your-runway-elizabeth-yin]] says HubSpot initially focused on helping people write content and only later expanded into email, CRM, and traditional marketing automation.
