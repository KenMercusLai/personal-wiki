## Overview
[[JenniferTang]] is the author of the 2016 ZURB article "You're Thinking Too Big," represented in this wiki through her account of [[Microinteractions]].

## Current Profile
In the available source, Tang presents product design at the scale of individual moments of acknowledgment. She uses physical and digital examples to explain how triggers, rules, feedback, and loops or modes make system behavior legible, then argues that teams should design these details early and refine them through live product use.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains user experience through small, concrete interactions rather than only major features.
- Treats feedback as assurance, instruction, delight, and a possible source of brand memory.
- Connects human acknowledgment needs to visible product response.
- Advocates early design attention followed by critical iteration in the live product.

## Evidence
Design focus:
- [[youre-thinking-too-big]] argues that otherwise similar products can feel radically different because of their small interaction details.

Feedback model:
- [[youre-thinking-too-big]] centers feedback within a four-component model and illustrates it with key fobs, upload indicators, gesture animation, errors, and reminders.

Process guidance:
- [[youre-thinking-too-big]] says microinteractions are not after-the-fact decoration and that live use reveals opportunities paper artifacts miss.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one practitioner article and does not establish Tang's broader biography, career, full design practice, or later views. The article uses illustrative examples rather than controlled evidence and gives limited attention to accessibility, habituation, annoyance, or the costs of excessive feedback.
