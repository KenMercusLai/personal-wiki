## Overview
[[SimonSpati]] is a writer and practitioner whose source note argues for deliberate AI use while preserving manual writing, coding, reading, and thinking practice.

## Current Profile
In this wiki, Simon Späti appears as an AI-skeptical but not anti-AI author. His article accepts everyday AI use for bounded tasks such as discovery, diagrams, and defined code helpers, but warns that indiscriminate use can weaken the human capacities that make work meaningful and maintainable. His distinctive contribution is to connect AI dependency with craft enjoyment, attention, learning, and long-term competitiveness rather than only with output quality.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses AI personally but argues for selective, task-fit adoption.
- Treats writing and coding as thinking practices, not only production channels.
- Emphasizes skill exercise, competence, and the fun of manual craft.
- Connects AI overuse to distraction, shallow summaries, maintainability problems, and loss of learning.
- Curates outside opinions and charts to support a broad cautionary argument about AI dependence.

## Evidence
- Selective adoption: [[blog-simon-spati-will-ai-replace-human-thinking]] says AI can help with discovery, diagrams, autocomplete, and well-defined functions, but should not replace writing or major coding decisions.
- Writing and coding as thinking: [[blog-simon-spati-will-ai-replace-human-thinking]] links manual writing and coding to learning, thought, voice, and competence.
- Skill exercise: [[blog-simon-spati-will-ai-replace-human-thinking]] compares AI overuse to losing a trained memory muscle through non-use.
- Attention and maintainability: [[blog-simon-spati-will-ai-replace-human-thinking]] says constant suggestions can remove thinking space and that code not made or understood by the maintainer becomes less enjoyable and harder to own.
- Evidence curation: [[blog-simon-spati-will-ai-replace-human-thinking]] includes charts on AI productivity/error tradeoffs, reasoning-skill declines, AI-generated web content, and seniority-biased employment changes.

## Qualifications
The article is a reflective synthesis and cautionary argument rather than a controlled study of AI use. Its strongest claims apply to domains where personal insight, craft learning, maintainability, or source-grounded judgment matter. It is less hostile to AI for bounded support tasks, unfamiliar side tasks, images, diagrams, and other areas where the author does not seek deep skill formation.
