## Overview
The [[ElectronicFrontierFoundation]] (EFF) is presented as a digital-rights advocacy organization opposing identity-heavy age gates and broader surveillance while campaigning for privacy, anonymous access, and free expression.

## Current Profile
In the source, EFF supplies advocacy reporting about age-gating legislation, amplifies a legislator's warning about effects on LGBTQ+ youth, reports young people's objections to online-safety bills, and publishes broader anti-surveillance material. The author also names EFF as an organization worth supporting and includes evidence of a personal donation. This establishes EFF's role in the essay's argument, not the accuracy or representativeness of every cited claim.

## Key Characteristics
- Advocates for privacy, free expression, anonymity, and online civil liberties.
- Critiques age-assurance policies that require identity disclosure or restrict lawful access to information.
- Uses policy analysis, affected-person perspectives, public campaigning, and supporter funding in its advocacy.
- Connects age-gating debates with a wider concern about state and commercial surveillance infrastructure.

## Evidence
Age-gating advocacy:
- [[the-lies-about-online-age-verification]] cites EFF reporting that presents age gates as tools of control and highlights risks to LGBTQ+ youth seeking information and community.

Youth perspectives:
- [[the-lies-about-online-age-verification]] cites an EFF survey in which many young respondents opposed proposed online-safety legislation as harmful to minors.

Privacy campaigning and support:
- [[the-lies-about-online-age-verification]] uses EFF campaign illustrations, recommends donating, and shows the author's US$250 contribution as evidence of personal support.

## Qualifications
This profile is derived from one sympathetic secondary source and selected EFF materials, not a comprehensive organizational history or independent evaluation of EFF's research and policy influence. The local article does not provide the survey instrument, sampling method, response distribution, or causal evidence that EFF's advocacy changed legislation. Its illustrations communicate campaign themes but do not independently establish policy effects.
