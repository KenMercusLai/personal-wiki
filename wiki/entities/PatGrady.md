## Overview
[[PatGrady]] is a [[SequoiaCapital]] partner presented in the source as a venture investor whose startup advice centers on first-principles problem solving, category-defining ambition, careful scaling, and capital discipline.

## Current Profile
The source profiles Grady through a [[Drift]] interview after he participated in Drift's Series B round. His investment lens favors companies that can become independent, durable, category-defining businesses, while his founder advice warns against conventional playbooks, weak value propositions, excess fundraising, and communication breakdowns as companies grow.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses an investor lens focused on long-lived, category-defining companies.
- Treats companies as problem-solving institutions rather than wealth-production vehicles.
- Emphasizes narrow entry into large markets before expanding.
- Connects the 150-person threshold with rising coordination costs.
- Advises founders to avoid hype, excessive fundraising, and weak hiring signals.

## Evidence
- Category-defining lens: [[8-lessons-from-one-of-silicon-valleys-top-vcs-drift]] says Grady looks for fundamental companies that can define and dominate categories over decades.
- Problem-first framing: [[8-lessons-from-one-of-silicon-valleys-top-vcs-drift]] says Grady grounds company purpose in solving a durable customer problem.
- Market-entry discipline: [[8-lessons-from-one-of-silicon-valleys-top-vcs-drift]] says Grady advises targeting a narrow slice of a large market first.
- Scaling warning: [[8-lessons-from-one-of-silicon-valleys-top-vcs-drift]] uses Grady's discussion of Dunbar's number to explain why systems and culture matter around 150 employees.
- Capital and hiring discipline: [[8-lessons-from-one-of-silicon-valleys-top-vcs-drift]] says Grady recommends raising less money in many cases and hiring for problem commitment rather than growth hype.
