## Overview
[[DanielWessel]] is the author of a 2011 practitioner account of using [[DEVONthink]] for a large scientific and personal reference archive.

## Current Profile
Within this wiki, Wessel contributes an experience-based model for organizing heterogeneous files at personal scale. He places active and reference material in one database, separates work from private presentation contexts through groups, and uses replicants, smart groups, selective tags, and duplicate detection to create reusable views without losing a primary structure.

## Key Characteristics
- Reports operating a roughly 29 GB DEVONthink database containing about 50,000 files.
- Organizes scientific projects, teaching, topic notes, community references, literature, web captures, presentations, images, videos, and private material in one database.
- Uses replicants for synchronized active-work views and smart groups for criteria-based retrieval.
- Prefers selective, discriminating tags over broad labels applied to whole collections.

## Evidence
- Archive scale: [[daniel-wessel-devonthink-second-impression-and-some-tips]] reports a roughly 29 GB database and about 50,000 files that still worked adequately for the author.
- Organization model: [[daniel-wessel-devonthink-second-impression-and-some-tips]] describes stable groups for projects and material types plus smart groups, tags, and replicants for overlapping access.
- Curation practice: [[daniel-wessel-devonthink-second-impression-and-some-tips]] recommends content-based duplicate cleanup, original-quality image export, and selective tagging.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one self-reported 2011 article rather than independent performance testing or a longitudinal comparison. Its product capabilities, capacity figures, interface advice, and Aperture workflow are historically scoped, and the author's one-database preference may not fit archives requiring collaboration, portability, strict access separation, or smaller failure domains.
