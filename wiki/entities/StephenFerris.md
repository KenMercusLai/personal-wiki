## Overview
[[StephenFerris]] is a finance professor and Rogers Chair of Money, Credit and Banking at the [[UniversityOfMissouriTrulaskeCollegeOfBusiness]] whose research on shared governance anchors the co-CEO article.

## Current Profile
In the source, Ferris is the main research authority arguing that [[CoCEOLeadership]] can be effective. He uses evidence from more than 100 shared-governance examples and an event study of 30 companies moving to co-CEOs to support claims about complementarity, compensation, tenure, and market reaction.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies shared-governance and co-CEO structures.
- Treats complementarity and mutual oversight as core success conditions.
- Interprets lower option compensation as evidence that peer monitoring can reduce incentive-pay needs.
- Presents positive market reaction data for dual-CEO appointments.

## Evidence
- Research role: [[co-ceos-are-two-better-than-one]] identifies Ferris as a finance professor and Rogers Chair at Missouri's Trulaske College of Business.
- Complementarity: [[co-ceos-are-two-better-than-one]] attributes to Ferris the finding that 90% of the examined sample had either job or educational complementarity.
- Market evidence: [[co-ceos-are-two-better-than-one]] says Ferris analyzed 30 companies announcing co-CEO transitions and found a 2.58% average abnormal announcement return.

## Qualifications
The source presents Ferris's interpretation favorably and does not include detailed methods, sample construction, failed cases, or long-term performance outcomes.
