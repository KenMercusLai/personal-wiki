## Overview
[[Bhutan]] is a small Himalayan Buddhist kingdom between India and China, represented here through [[TimUrban]]'s 2025 travelogue [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-the-sights-and-sounds-of-bhutan]].

## Current Profile
The source portrays Bhutan as a country that has deliberately limited outside influence to preserve religious, architectural, and social traditions. Its forests, guided-tourism rules, monarchy, and [[GrossNationalHappiness]] policy distinguish that model, while youth migration and the proposed [[GelephuMindfulnessCity]] expose the pressure to create more domestic opportunity without dissolving cultural continuity.

## Key Characteristics
- Maintains a strong Buddhist-monarchical identity and a visibly consistent traditional architectural style.
- Uses restricted tourism, difficult immigration, and historically delayed mass-media access as cultural-preservation mechanisms.
- Is reported as carbon-negative because extensive forests absorb more carbon dioxide than the population emits.
- Frames public policy through [[GrossNationalHappiness]] as well as conventional economic output.
- Faces youth outmigration and a resulting tension between economic development and preservation.

## Evidence
- Cultural and architectural continuity: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-the-sights-and-sounds-of-bhutan]] describes ubiquitous temples and uniform Bhutanese design and visually documents a riverside dzong and the cliffside Tiger's Nest monastery.
- Managed openness: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-the-sights-and-sounds-of-bhutan]] reports guided-tour requirements, near-impossible immigration, and the ban on television and internet until 1999.
- Environmental and wellbeing priorities: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-the-sights-and-sounds-of-bhutan]] reports forest-driven carbon negativity and the national preference for Gross National Happiness over GDP.
- Development pressure: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-the-sights-and-sounds-of-bhutan]] identifies youth emigration and presents Gelephu Mindfulness City as the king's proposed response.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from a one-week guided visit and relies heavily on what a tour guide and other local narrators told the author. The source supplies no primary policy documents, emissions inventory, migration statistics, happiness data, or independent verification of royal COVID-19 support stories. Its affectionate tone and selected photographs illuminate a visitor experience rather than establishing a complete social, political, or economic account.
