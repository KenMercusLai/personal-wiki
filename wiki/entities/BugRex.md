## Overview
[[BugRex]] was presented as a two-sided marketplace where developers could obtain on-demand coding help from other developers over chat.

## Current Profile
The source describes BugRex as an experiment built to answer whether developers wanted chat-based help, what customers would pay, and what experts would demand. The initial version, branded ReactHelp.com for a narrow React.js audience, joined five hosted services rather than a custom application. Its visible offer gave customers fifteen free minutes and then quoted US$25 per hour, while the payment flowed directly from customer to expert.

## Key Characteristics
- Connected developers seeking help with experts available over chat.
- Began with a narrow, disposable ReactHelp.com brand.
- Combined hosted landing-page, chat, notification, payment, and application services.
- Used direct expert payment, leaving marketplace fee capture outside the initial test.
- Treated the prototype as an assumption-testing instrument rather than finished infrastructure.

## Evidence
- Marketplace purpose: [[building-products-without-coding-learning-new-stuff-medium]] describes a two-sided on-demand coding-help service.
- Customer offer: [[building-products-without-coding-learning-new-stuff-medium]] and its retained interface show fifteen free minutes followed by US$25 per hour.
- Composed implementation: [[building-products-without-coding-learning-new-stuff-medium]] assigns the five required functions to Unbounce, Olark, Trillian, PayPal.me, and Typeform.
- Economic boundary: [[building-products-without-coding-learning-new-stuff-medium]] says payments went directly to experts because platform take rate was not an assumption under test.

## Qualifications
The source provides no launch results, transaction volume, conversion, retention, expert response time, marketplace liquidity, fraud, dispute, or later-company outcome. Its description establishes prototype design, not durable product-market fit or a scalable marketplace business.
