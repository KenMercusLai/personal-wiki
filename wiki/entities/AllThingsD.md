## Overview
[[AllThingsD]] was the Dow Jones technology publication and conference operation built by [[KaraSwisher]] and [[WaltMossberg]] before they launched [[Recode]].

## Current Profile
The 2014 profile traces AllThingsD from a 2003 conference designed as live journalism into a blog able to support more voice, speed, experimentation, and disclosure than the partners found possible in *The Wall Street Journal*. The conference became profitable immediately and developed a waiting list, while the combined business reportedly reached roughly $12.5 million. Its editorial and commercial success did not remove internal conflict over staffing, links, credit, resources, profit sharing, or the fit between an entrepreneurial unit and a large corporation.

## Key Characteristics
- Combined a technology-news blog with a high-priced executive conference.
- Treated onstage interviews as live reporting rather than sponsored presentation.
- Hosted consequential executive exchanges and product debuts.
- Gave Swisher and Mossberg more editorial voice and disclosure flexibility than the parent newspaper.
- Became profitable while remaining a relatively small and contentious operation inside Dow Jones.

## Evidence
- Conference origin: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] reports a sold-out, profitable first event in 2003 and annual waiting lists thereafter.
- Editorial design: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] describes live interrogation, news-division control, and rejection of sponsor-reserved speaking slots.
- Publication role: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] says the blog supported opinionated reporting and longer disclosures.
- Corporate friction: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] reports disputes over staffing, resources, credit, links, and conference economics before the partners left.

## Qualifications
The financial figures and internal-conflict account come from a contemporaneous magazine profile, named participants, and an anonymous former colleague. They do not provide audited results, ownership terms, audience metrics, or Dow Jones's full institutional perspective.
