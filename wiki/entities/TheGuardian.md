## Overview
[[TheGuardian]] is a news organization represented here through its development and newsroom-wide use of the internal [[Ophan]] analytics system.

## Current Profile
By January 2015, more than 900 staff across Guardian newsrooms in the United States, United Kingdom, and Australia could access Ophan. The organization used the tool to distribute audience evidence directly to reporters and editors, connect editorial actions with live results, and maintain a regular feedback loop between editorial, audience-engagement, and development teams.

The case presents the Guardian as willing to begin with a small internal prototype and expand it through use: Ophan progressed from three minutes of traffic retention to mobile, article, section, site, alert, and referral-query views. That accessibility also created responsibility, because rapid re-promotion of an older article confused some readers when its age was not clear.

## Key Characteristics
- Distributed real-time audience analytics broadly rather than restricting it to specialist analysts.
- Connected editorial staff, audience engagement, and developers through recurring product feedback.
- Favored minimum viable internal tooling followed by continuous feature iteration.
- Used traffic sources, attention time, and page views to guide promotion, headlines, linking, and contextual additions.
- Faced an editorial-framing risk when analytics-driven re-promotion made old reporting appear current.

## Evidence
- Access and operating model: [[constantly-tweaking-how-the-guardian-continues-to-develop-its-in-house-analytics-system-nieman-journalism-lab]] reports more than 900 users across three countries and routine editorial use of Ophan.
- Product collaboration: [[constantly-tweaking-how-the-guardian-continues-to-develop-its-in-house-analytics-system-nieman-journalism-lab]] describes ongoing discussion between audience-engagement and development teams, with editorial requests producing features such as mobile access.
- Iterative scope: [[constantly-tweaking-how-the-guardian-continues-to-develop-its-in-house-analytics-system-nieman-journalism-lab]] traces Ophan from a hack-day prototype with three minutes of retained traffic to multiple aggregation levels, alerts, and experimental query visibility.
- Editorial application and risk: [[constantly-tweaking-how-the-guardian-continues-to-develop-its-in-house-analytics-system-nieman-journalism-lab]] gives examples of headline and link changes, renewed-story promotion, and reader confusion about an older Chris Kyle article.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one 2015 secondary account, not current organizational documentation or measured adoption research. Staff access does not establish frequency, decision quality, causal audience gains, or effects on journalistic independence, and the organization, product, metrics, and editorial safeguards may have changed substantially since publication.

## What Changed
- Established the Guardian's profile around broadly accessible analytics, cross-functional feedback, and iterative internal product development.
- Added the risk that traffic-responsive promotion can weaken temporal clarity for readers.
