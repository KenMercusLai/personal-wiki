## Overview
[[Halide]] is a paid iPhone camera app created by [[BenSandofsky]] and [[SebastiaanDeWith]] for deliberate, advanced photography with tactile controls and access to professional camera capabilities.

## Current Profile
In its first-year retrospective, Halide developed from a launch product missing volume-button capture into a broader but still focused camera through versions 1.1 to 1.8. The team reported more than 350 million launch-week App Store impressions, a number-three Top Paid Apps peak, more than 100,000 monthly active users, recouped development cost, and slow-day revenue roughly four times its August 2017 level. Its evolution combined user-requested fixes, device adaptation, depth and RAW tools, accessibility work, an editing partnership with Darkroom, and an explicit refusal to become a general-purpose photo suite.

## Key Characteristics
- Targets advanced photographers seeking deliberate control rather than the mass market.
- Uses a tactile interface intended to resemble a high-end physical camera.
- Evolved through frequent feature, compatibility, stability, speed, and accessibility releases.
- Preserved shooting focus by integrating with Darkroom instead of building an editor.
- Sold through an upfront price while promising future updates to existing buyers.
- Relied on App Store discovery, press, product releases, and useful photography content more than paid social advertising.

## Evidence
- Product position: [[halide-one-year-later-halide]] describes deliberate photography, professional controls, and a DSLR-like tactile experience as the original product test.
- Product evolution: [[halide-one-year-later-halide]] traces stability, speed, RAW, iPhone X, depth, Apple Watch, timer, review, and accessibility work across versions 1.1 through 1.8.
- Focus boundary: [[halide-one-year-later-halide]] says the team rejected built-in editing, partnered with Darkroom, and filtered portrait mode through the advanced-photography promise.
- Business model and distribution: [[halide-one-year-later-halide]] reports upfront pricing, launch and update spikes, higher slow-day revenue, App Store effects, unsuccessful social advertising, and successful educational content.

## Qualifications
The profile is based on one celebratory company-authored retrospective covering Halide's first year. Its commercial and usage figures are self-reported, and the source does not provide audited revenue, retention cohorts, acquisition costs, refunds, current product state, or controlled causal evidence for the effects attributed to releases and distribution events.
