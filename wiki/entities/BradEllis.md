## Overview
[[BradEllis]] is the designer and founder of [[TallWest]] who argues that iOS navigation should adapt to larger phones by moving important controls into thumb reach.

## Current Profile
The source presents Ellis as a practitioner making a platform-design argument from ergonomic observation, Apple precedent, and app examples. His central claim is that the iOS navbar solved clarity and implementation problems on earlier phones, but larger iPhones changed the physical cost of top-corner controls enough to require a new pattern: [[ReachNavigation]].

## Key Characteristics
- Frames mobile interface design through one-handed physical use.
- Critiques the traditional iOS top navbar without dismissing its historical benefits.
- Uses Apple system features and app redesigns as evidence for a broader design direction.
- Offers practical implementation guidance for tab-bar, non-tab-bar, and legacy app designs.

## Evidence
- Ergonomic argument: [[all-thumbs-why-reach-navigation-should-replace-the-navbar-in-ios-design]] says larger iPhones put navbar buttons outside comfortable thumb reach.
- Balanced critique: [[all-thumbs-why-reach-navigation-should-replace-the-navbar-in-ios-design]] lists the navbar's recognizability, hierarchy, titles, and tab-bar complement role before naming its drawbacks.
- Apple evidence: [[all-thumbs-why-reach-navigation-should-replace-the-navbar-in-ios-design]] uses swipe-back, Reachability, Maps sheets, and Apple Music sheets to show Apple moving toward reach-based navigation.
- Practical guidance: [[all-thumbs-why-reach-navigation-should-replace-the-navbar-in-ios-design]] recommends bottom placement, sheets, exposed drawers, edge-swipe back support, and moving important actions away from the top right.

## Qualifications
The article is a 2017 practitioner essay and does not provide usability-test data, platform adoption metrics, accessibility studies, or later iOS design outcomes.
