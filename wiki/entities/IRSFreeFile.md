## Overview
[[IRSFreeFile]] is represented here as a historical partnership between the [[InternalRevenueService]] and private tax-software companies that offered qualifying taxpayers free preparation and filing in exchange for restrictions on an IRS-created service.

## Current Profile
For the filing season described by ProPublica, the umbrella program covered taxpayers below a $66,000 adjusted-gross-income threshold, but access was fragmented across participating providers with different age, income, location, military, credit, and state-return rules. TurboTax’s offer used a narrower threshold and a separate “Free File” or “Freedom Edition” route. The resulting program joined a broad public eligibility promise to provider-specific discovery and qualification work, while critics cited declining participation and inadequate IRS oversight.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses private providers to deliver a publicly negotiated free-filing benefit.
- Establishes an umbrella income ceiling while allowing provider-specific eligibility rules.
- Requires taxpayers to discover and compare separate offers rather than enter one universal filing product.
- Historically constrained the IRS from creating a competing online preparation service under the industry agreement described by the source.
- Drew criticism for low use, falling participation, and weak oversight.
