## Overview
[[NickKolenda]] is represented in the wiki as the author of research-heavy practitioner guides connecting visual-attention and consumer-pricing findings to marketing and interface design.

## Current Profile
Kolenda synthesizes large academic reading lists into memorable taxonomies and concrete tactics. His strength is translation: research about attention, magnitude, fluency, reference points, anchoring, and assortment becomes examples involving thumbnails, calls to action, advertisements, menus, ecommerce, plan labels, bundles, and discounts. Across both guides, he marks many caveats and speculative extensions, but the work should still be read as practitioner synthesis rather than systematic review because it mixes heterogeneous published evidence with personal inference, pilots, A/B-test reports, humor, and untested recommendations.

## Key Characteristics
- Marketing-psychology author translating attention and pricing research into design tactics.
- Uses diagrams and visual examples to explain perceptual mechanisms.
- Organizes a wide literature into nine overlapping stimulus families.
- Distinguishes bottom-up capture from top-down goal-directed selection.
- Frequently labels speculative explanations and tactics as hunches or tests rather than established outcomes.
- Uses context-specific caveats to show how apparently conflicting tactics can both be plausible.

## Evidence
- Scope and method: [[visual-attention-an-enormous-guide]] says Kolenda read more than 300 articles on vision and neuroscience to identify nine attention-triggering stimulus families.
- Practical translation: [[visual-attention-an-enormous-guide]] applies research claims to thumbnails, menus, buttons, icons, banners, profiles, product imagery, and advertising placement.
- Contextual boundary: [[visual-attention-an-enormous-guide]] warns that tactics for passive browsing can reverse during goal-directed search.
- Visual pedagogy: [[visual-attention-an-enormous-guide]] contains 83 instructional image references, including comparisons, diagrams, screenshots, and a long summary infographic.
- Pricing synthesis: [[nick-kolenda-pricing-a-list-of-tactics]] organizes visible tactics around price design, digits, bundles, promotions, anchors, and decoy effects.
- Evidence calibration: [[nick-kolenda-pricing-a-list-of-tactics]] distinguishes established findings from author pilots, failed replications, and applications needing more research.
- Visual pricing pedagogy: [[nick-kolenda-pricing-a-list-of-tactics]] uses experiments, charts, interface mockups, and retail examples to demonstrate how presentation changes comparison.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to two saved guides and does not establish Kolenda's complete biography, later views, or the quality of his wider work. Both are informal secondary syntheses whose citations range across different tasks and evidence types; several mechanistic explanations and direct marketing recommendations go beyond what the summarized findings alone demonstrate. The saved pricing capture exposes only 22 of 102 advertised tactics.

## What Changed
- Expanded the profile from visual attention to pricing psychology and promotion design.
- Added his consistent practice of separating stronger findings from pilots, failed replications, and conjecture.
