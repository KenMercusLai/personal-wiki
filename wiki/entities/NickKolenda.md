## Overview
[[NickKolenda]] is represented in the wiki as the author of a research-heavy practitioner guide connecting visual-attention findings to marketing and interface design.

## Current Profile
Kolenda synthesizes a large academic reading list into a memorable nine-part taxonomy and practical tactics. His strength is translation: laboratory ideas about feature contrast, motion, biological forms, spatial cues, arousal, novelty, self-relevance, and goal-directed search become concrete examples involving thumbnails, calls to action, advertisements, menus, icons, and ecommerce. His account should be read as a practitioner synthesis rather than a systematic review because it mixes cited evidence with evolutionary explanation, personal inference, humor, and untested recommendations.

## Key Characteristics
- Marketing-psychology author translating attention research into design tactics.
- Uses diagrams and visual examples to explain perceptual mechanisms.
- Organizes a wide literature into nine overlapping stimulus families.
- Distinguishes bottom-up capture from top-down goal-directed selection.
- Frequently labels speculative explanations and tactics as hunches or tests rather than established outcomes.

## Evidence
- Scope and method: [[visual-attention-an-enormous-guide]] says Kolenda read more than 300 articles on vision and neuroscience to identify nine attention-triggering stimulus families.
- Practical translation: [[visual-attention-an-enormous-guide]] applies research claims to thumbnails, menus, buttons, icons, banners, profiles, product imagery, and advertising placement.
- Contextual boundary: [[visual-attention-an-enormous-guide]] warns that tactics for passive browsing can reverse during goal-directed search.
- Visual pedagogy: [[visual-attention-an-enormous-guide]] contains 83 instructional image references, including comparisons, diagrams, screenshots, and a long summary infographic.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one saved guide and does not establish Kolenda's complete biography, later views, or the quality of his wider work. The article is an informal secondary synthesis whose citations range across different tasks and evidence types; several evolutionary stories and direct marketing recommendations go beyond what the summarized findings alone demonstrate.
