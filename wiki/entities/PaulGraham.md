## Overview
[[PaulGraham]] appears in this wiki as a [[YCombinator]] partner associated with the startup-life diagram that frames [[AndrewChen]]'s [[TroughOfSorrow]] essay and with advice, investment, and a determination thesis in [[ArramSabeti]]'s [[ZeroCater]] account.

## Current Profile
Chen uses Graham as part of the YC context for a visual model of startup life: the diagram supplies the article's central metaphor for a new product's emotional and operational path from launch excitement through the trough and into possible recovery. Sabeti's account adds a hands-on accelerator role. Graham reportedly recognized office catering as a recruiting problem, helped create ZeroCater's memorable “wave of food” Demo Day ending, later became an investor or adviser, and is cited for the proposition that determination predicts founder success better than intelligence.

## Key Characteristics
- Associated with Y Combinator in the source.
- Connected to a diagram of the early startup journey.
- Used as contextual authority for the Trough of Sorrow frame.
- Evaluated ZeroCater's customer value partly through employee recruiting.
- Helped shape ZeroCater's Demo Day presentation and later supported the company.
- Cited by Sabeti for a strong determination-over-intelligence founder thesis.

## Evidence
- YC dinner context: [[after-the-techcrunch-bump-life-in-the-trough-of-sorrow-at-andrewchen]] says Paul Graham and other YC partners drew a diagram depicting the life of a new product.
- Diagram role: [[after-the-techcrunch-bump-life-in-the-trough-of-sorrow-at-andrewchen]] uses that diagram to frame launch excitement, novelty decay, the trough, false hope, and possible later recovery.
- Concept link: [[after-the-techcrunch-bump-life-in-the-trough-of-sorrow-at-andrewchen]] turns the diagram into practical advice for founders before product-market fit.
- Product framing: [[from-selling-scoops-of-ice-cream-to-founding-zerocater-techcrunch]] says Graham connected ZeroCater's office-meal service to company recruiting.
- Pitch advice: [[from-selling-scoops-of-ice-cream-to-founding-zerocater-techcrunch]] credits Graham with the “wave of food” presentation ending used at Demo Day.
- Determination claim: [[from-selling-scoops-of-ice-cream-to-founding-zerocater-techcrunch]] attributes to Graham the view that determination predicts success more strongly than intelligence.

## Qualifications
This remains a narrow source-scoped page rather than a biography or complete account of Graham's writing, investing, or startup philosophy. Chen and Sabeti both use him as authority within founder-advice narratives. The ZeroCater source does not independently establish the quoted predictive ranking of determination and intelligence, and a selected success case cannot validate that causal claim.
