## Overview
[[JakobNielsen]] is a usability author and practitioner represented in this wiki by his introductory framework for interface quality and iterative user testing.

## Current Profile
Nielsen presents usability as an operational discipline rather than a taste judgment. He decomposes ease of use into five dimensions, distinguishes usability from utility, ties interface difficulty to business and employee outcomes, and recommends direct observation of representative people completing realistic tasks. His practical stance favors early and repeated low-cost studies over a single large evaluation at the end of implementation.

## Key Characteristics
- Defines usability through learnability, efficiency, memorability, errors and recovery, and satisfaction.
- Treats utility and usability as jointly necessary for a useful product.
- Prioritizes observed behavior over focus-group discussion or unverified self-report when evaluating interaction design.
- Advocates small iterative studies throughout the design lifecycle.

## Evidence
- Conceptual framework: [[usability-101-introduction-to-usability]] supplies Nielsen's five usability components and usability-plus-utility model.
- Research method: [[usability-101-introduction-to-usability]] presents representative-user task observation as the basic usability method.
- Iterative practice: [[usability-101-introduction-to-usability]] recommends testing old designs, alternatives, prototypes, refinements, and implementations.
- Organizational case: [[usability-101-introduction-to-usability]] connects usable design to web retention, conversion, and internal productivity.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one introductory article rather than Nielsen's full research record. Its budget, improvement, and sample-size figures are broad practitioner recommendations and should be adapted to audience diversity, task coverage, risk, and the kind of inference a study needs to support.
