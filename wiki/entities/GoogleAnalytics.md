## Overview
[[GoogleAnalytics]] is Google's website and campaign analytics product, represented in the sources as both an incumbent measurement baseline and part of a wider advertising-tag ecosystem.

## Current Profile
One source presents Google Analytics as a widely used free incumbent that still left room for [[CrazyEgg]] to differentiate through heatmaps. The digital-marketing guide presents a broader 2018 role: a JavaScript tracking ID records site activity, Google Tag Manager and a data layer distribute website information to analytics and marketing tags, and Analytics can integrate with Google's advertising tools for reporting and remarketing. Its product and price comparison with Adobe Analytics is time-bound rather than current procurement guidance.

## Key Characteristics
- Serves as the incumbent analytics reference point in the Crazy Egg case.
- Is described as free and useful to many people.
- Lacked the heatmap-specific behavior visualization that Crazy Egg emphasized.
- Receives website behavior through tracking code or tag-management workflows.
- Connects campaign measurement to Google's advertising ecosystem.
- Was positioned in 2018 as easier to implement and more accessible to small sites than Adobe Analytics.

## Evidence
- Incumbent role: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] says Crazy Egg entered a market where Google Analytics already existed.
- Free and useful baseline: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] says Google Analytics was free and helped many users.
- Missing feature: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] says dissatisfied users wanted heatmapping to track every click on the page.
- Tracking mechanism: [[a-comprehensive-guide-to-digital-marketing-and-analytics]] retains a JavaScript snippet highlighting the Analytics tracking ID.
- Tag ecosystem: [[a-comprehensive-guide-to-digital-marketing-and-analytics]] retains diagrams of Tag Manager exchanging website data through a data layer with Google Analytics and marketing tags.
- Product comparison: [[a-comprehensive-guide-to-digital-marketing-and-analytics]] retains a 2018 table comparing Google Analytics and Adobe Analytics on audience, implementation, price, integrations, remarketing, and support.

## Qualifications
The sources describe Google Analytics in 2017-2018 contexts and do not cover later product generations, privacy controls, consent requirements, attribution limits, pricing, or integration changes. The Adobe comparison is source-authored and should not be treated as an independent or current feature audit.

## What Changed

- Added an explicit time boundary around the 2018 Adobe Analytics comparison.
