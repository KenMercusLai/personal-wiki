## Overview
[[JeanBaptisteSchwilgue]] is presented as the French inventor credited with the first working key-driven calculator prototype in the keypad-history source.

## Current Profile
The source places Schwilgue near the beginning of numerical keyboard history. His 1844 prototype used a single row of keys increasing from 1 to 9, making it an early working example of number entry by direct keys rather than by drums, clocks, or levers.

## Key Characteristics
- French inventor in the source's early calculator lineage.
- Credited with a working 1844 key-driven calculator prototype.
- Used a single-row 1-to-9 numerical keyboard.
- Serves as evidence that numeric keyboards predate modern telephones and electronic calculators.

## Evidence
- Working prototype: [[a-brief-history-of-the-numeric-keypad]] says Schwilgue produced the first working key-driven calculator prototype in 1844.
- Key order: [[a-brief-history-of-the-numeric-keypad]] says the machine used a single row of keys increasing from 1 to 9.
- Historical role: [[a-brief-history-of-the-numeric-keypad]] places Schwilgue after earlier speculative or poorly documented concepts by James White and Luigi Torchi.

## Qualifications
The source treats earlier key-based calculator concepts as possible predecessors, so Schwilgue's status is framed as the first working prototype rather than the first imagined key-based numerical interface.
