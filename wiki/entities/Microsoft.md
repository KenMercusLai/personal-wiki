## Overview
[[Microsoft]] appears in the source as the legacy PC-platform owner that missed the mobile platform shift.

## Current Profile
The article presents Microsoft as still powerful through Windows and Office cash flows, but no longer able to pursue the older "Windows Everywhere" strategy as the universal computing platform. In Evans' framing, Windows 10 can slow the PC-to-mobile transition but not prevent it, Windows Mobile is marginal, Xbox is non-core, and Microsoft's strategic problem becomes how to use legacy position to build new businesses and reinvent enterprise connective tissue.

## Key Characteristics
- Missed the shift to the new mobile platform.
- Retains legacy market power through Windows and Office rather than mobile leadership.
- Is described as abandoning the old universal Windows platform strategy.
- Needs to rebuild enterprise connective tissue around new cloud and mobile-era workflows.

## Evidence
- Missed platform shift: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] says Microsoft missed the move to the new platform.
- Legacy prop: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] says Windows 10 can support the legacy business but cannot prevent the broader change.
- Strategy change: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] says Satya Nadella had ended the decades-old Windows Everywhere strategy.
- Enterprise question: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] argues Microsoft has to work out how to build new businesses from its legacy cash and market position.

## Qualifications
The source is a 2015 view and does not cover Microsoft's later cloud, AI, developer-tooling, gaming, or enterprise-platform trajectory.
