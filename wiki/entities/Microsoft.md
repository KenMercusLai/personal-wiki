## Overview
[[Microsoft]] appears in the sources as the legacy PC-platform owner that missed the mobile platform shift and as a Windows 8 landing-page example where missing student-relevant testimonials become negative social proof.

## Current Profile
The mobile source presents Microsoft as still powerful through Windows and Office cash flows, but no longer able to pursue the older "Windows Everywhere" strategy as the universal computing platform. In Evans' framing, Windows 10 can slow the PC-to-mobile transition but not prevent it, Windows Mobile is marginal, Xbox is non-core, and Microsoft's strategic problem becomes how to use legacy position to build new businesses and reinvent enterprise connective tissue. The landing-page source adds a marketing example: a Windows 8 student page full of product and price information is criticized for lacking relatable student testimonials, so the absence of proof reinforces the perception that the product is uncool.

## Key Characteristics
- Missed the shift to the new mobile platform.
- Retains legacy market power through Windows and Office rather than mobile leadership.
- Is described as abandoning the old universal Windows platform strategy.
- Needs to rebuild enterprise connective tissue around new cloud and mobile-era workflows.
- Can suffer marketing credibility gaps when proof does not match the target audience.

## Evidence
- Missed platform shift: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] says Microsoft missed the move to the new platform.
- Legacy prop: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] says Windows 10 can support the legacy business but cannot prevent the broader change.
- Strategy change: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] says Satya Nadella had ended the decades-old Windows Everywhere strategy.
- Enterprise question: [[16-mobile-theses-benedict-evans]] argues Microsoft has to work out how to build new businesses from its legacy cash and market position.
- Landing-page proof gap: [[3-ways-to-make-your-landing-page-social-proof-prove-something]] uses a Windows 8 student-store screenshot to argue that product shots and discounts did not substitute for testimonials from relatable students.

## Qualifications
The mobile source is a 2015 view and does not cover Microsoft's later cloud, AI, developer-tooling, gaming, or enterprise-platform trajectory. The landing-page source is a 2013 copywriting critique of one Windows 8 page, not a full assessment of Microsoft's marketing.
