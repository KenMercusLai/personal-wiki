## Overview
[[AndreasKlinger]] is the author of a practitioner guide to managing action, follow-up, delegation, and scheduled commitments inside [[Gmail]].

## Current Profile
The available source presents Klinger through one personal workflow he says he had used since 2010. He treats email as a small task-state system: process new mail, classify unfinished obligations with special stars, archive the thread, and review search-backed panels rather than using the inbox itself as the complete work list.

## Key Characteristics
- Advocates built-in Gmail features for a lightweight email task system.
- Separates messages into action, awaiting-reply, delegated, and scheduled states.
- Uses aggressive archiving, automation, and shortcuts to reduce inbox clutter and handling effort.

## Evidence
- State model: [[dont-drown-in-email-how-to-use-gmail-more-efficiently-startup-lessons-learned]] documents Klinger’s special-star categories and matching Multiple Inboxes searches.
- Daily workflow: [[dont-drown-in-email-how-to-use-gmail-more-efficiently-startup-lessons-learned]] describes immediate handling, deferred-action marking, follow-up marking, and archiving.
- Efficiency tactics: [[dont-drown-in-email-how-to-use-gmail-more-efficiently-startup-lessons-learned]] recommends filters, keyboard shortcuts, auto-advance, undo send, and account consolidation.

## Qualifications
The source is Klinger’s personal advice, not a comparative evaluation. Its interface screenshots and some feature names are historical, mobile support is explicitly weaker, and the workflow assumes enough autonomy to batch, archive, delegate, and configure mail behavior.
