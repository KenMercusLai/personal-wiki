## Overview
[[GuyShachar]] is an entrepreneur and the author of a 2016 HackerNoon essay warning founders not to confuse growth, convenience, or fundraising with viable [[UnitEconomics]].

## Current Profile
Shachar presents founder optimism as both necessary and dangerous: conviction can sustain difficult company building, but it can also support unrealistic retention, virality, and scale assumptions. His essay uses on-demand services to argue that a real customer pain and a convenient smartphone interface do not make a company viable when acquisition and service-delivery costs exceed customer value. The contribution is a forceful early-stage diagnostic rather than a detailed financial model.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames LTV exceeding CAC as a basic startup-viability condition.
- Critiques unsupported assumptions about indefinite retention, virality, and volume-led profitability.
- Uses 2016-era on-demand services as examples of convenience that may not translate into sustainable economics.
- Treats commercial awareness as a necessary complement to founder passion and tenacity.

## Evidence
- Viability test: [[dear-unit-economics-i-hate-you-by]] reduces the central test to expected customer value exceeding acquisition cost.
- Optimism risk: [[dear-unit-economics-i-hate-you-by]] links founder confidence to weakly tested retention and organic-growth assumptions.
- On-demand critique: [[dear-unit-economics-i-hate-you-by]] argues that per-order losses, premium pricing, fees, and labor exposure can survive beneath growth narratives.
- Opportunity judgment: [[dear-unit-economics-i-hate-you-by]] says identifying customer pain is only half of building a viable business.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one short, opinionated 2016 essay. It supplies no company-level cohort economics, does not distinguish temporary market-building subsidy from structurally negative contribution margins, and names several on-demand companies without testing them individually. The original HackerNoon page identifies Shachar as the author and describes him generically as a co-founder and CEO, but the supplied article export does not identify the company or provide a substantive biography.
