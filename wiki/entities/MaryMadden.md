## Overview
[[MaryMadden]] is presented as a [[DataSociety]] researcher studying privacy and security concerns among lower-income communities.

## Current Profile
The source uses Madden's survey research to show that low-income Americans are acutely aware of digital privacy harms but often have fewer resources for self-protection. Her work connects online privacy concerns with offline insecurity, including neighborhood safety and fear of unfair law-enforcement targeting. Madden also broadens the article's policy claim: individual privacy self-defense is becoming unrealistic even for experts, so accountability needs to move toward companies and policy structures.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies privacy and security attitudes across income groups, with attention to low-income respondents.
- Finds that low-income people report higher concern about data collection, financial-information loss, harassment, scams, and law-enforcement targeting.
- Links online privacy concern with offline vulnerability and safety concerns.
- Emphasizes resource constraints in learning and applying privacy-protection strategies.
- Argues that corporate and policy accountability should carry more of the burden than individual users alone.

## Evidence
- Survey role: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] describes Madden's nationally representative privacy survey with a low-income oversample.
- Income differences: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] reports higher concern among lowest-income households about unknown data collection, financial-data theft, online harassment, and scams.
- Offline-online overlap: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] quotes Madden connecting offline safety and law-enforcement fears to online privacy harms.
- Resource tradeoff: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] quotes Madden on the time and resource tradeoff involved in protecting information.
- Policy burden: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] quotes Madden arguing that experts cannot fully navigate the personal-data ecosystem individually.

## Qualifications
The source summarizes Madden's 2015 survey and 2019 comments; it does not cover later Data & Society research or subsequent changes in privacy law.
