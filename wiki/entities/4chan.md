## Overview
[[4chan]] is represented as an image-board community whose ephemeral content model helped make reposting, variation, meme circulation, trolling, and coordinated attention campaigns central participation practices.

## Current Profile
In Boyd's account, Chris Poole created 4chan in 2003 to share anime and pornography. Limited storage led older material to disappear, prompting users to repost favored images with modifications. The site then became a training ground for learning how content spreads, how rankings and media incentives can be gamed, and how decentralized participants can coordinate without conventional institutions.

The same repertoire had divergent outcomes. Some campaigns were jokes aimed at confusing mainstream media; Anonymous developed more explicit ideological agendas; Gamergate used related coordination and exposure tactics for harassment and harm. The source therefore makes 4chan historically important without treating every participant, campaign, or later movement as identical.

## Key Characteristics
- Ephemeral image-board architecture that encouraged reposting and mutation of disappearing material.
- Community associated with early meme culture and coordinated “lulz”-oriented campaigns.
- Environment in which participants learned practical attention-distribution and media-gaming tactics.
- Origin point in the source's account for Anonymous, while containing divergent norms and political agendas.
- Historically linked to abusive tactics and Gamergate, although the essay notes that Gamergate itself became taboo on 4chan.

## Evidence
- Product mechanism: [[hacking-the-attention-economy-data-society-points]] connects limited content storage with reposting and modification.
- Attention campaigns: [[hacking-the-attention-economy-data-society-points]] describes coordinated manipulation of Oprah's forum, the TIME 100 poll, and wider media attention.
- Political divergence: [[hacking-the-attention-economy-data-society-points]] traces Anonymous from 4chan while distinguishing playful, ideological, vigilante, and harmful motives.
- Norm boundary: [[hacking-the-attention-economy-data-society-points]] says Gamergate crossed even 4chan's permissive internal lines, complicating any claim that the community had one stable agenda.

## Qualifications
This profile depends on one retrospective essay and selected campaigns. It does not establish that 4chan invented memes, represent all boards or users, quantify participation or campaign effects, or prove a simple causal chain from site architecture to later political manipulation. Reusable tactics and overlapping participants do not make 4chan, Anonymous, Gamergate, marketers, or activists one organization.

## What Changed
- Created a bounded profile linking ephemeral design, meme mutation, attention gaming, and political divergence.
- Kept 4chan's internal heterogeneity and the source's Gamergate qualification explicit.
