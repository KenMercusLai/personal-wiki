## Overview
[[QNX]] appears in the wiki as the Unix-like software platform [[BlackBerry]] acquired in 2010 after belatedly accepting that its old smartphone operating system could not compete with iOS and Android.

## Current Profile
The source treats QNX as technically capable but strategically late. By the time BlackBerry bought QNX, [[Apple]] and [[Google]] had already established app-centered smartphone ecosystems, so replacing the operating-system base still left BlackBerry with the harder work of building an application framework, developer momentum, and handset demand.

## Key Characteristics
- Unix-like operating-system platform acquired by BlackBerry.
- Represented a more modern software foundation than BlackBerry's old platform.
- Arrived too late to reverse BlackBerry's ecosystem decline.

## Evidence
- Acquisition timing: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] says RIM acquired QNX in April 2010.
- Technical role: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] calls QNX a well-respected Unix-like platform capable of supporting modern smartphone development.
- Ecosystem gap: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] argues that OS replacement still required building an application framework after iOS and Android had pulled ahead.

## Qualifications
This page only captures QNX's role in the BlackBerry smartphone transition. It does not evaluate QNX's broader embedded, automotive, real-time operating-system, or later BlackBerry business roles.

## What Changed
- Created QNX as BlackBerry's late software-platform response.
