## Overview
[[MarthaStout]] is cited in the article as the author associated with The Sociopath Next Door and with a cautionary statistic about people who may exploit trust.

## Current Profile
Within this wiki, Stout appears only as a cautionary reference inside Rick Manelius's trust-first argument. Manelius uses her work to acknowledge that assuming positive intent carries real risk because some people may act without conscience or exploit trust.

## Key Characteristics
- Cited as an author on sociopathy and conscience.
- Used to qualify, not reject, the practice of assuming positive intent.
- Represents the article's warning that interpersonal trust must account for bad-faith actors.

## Evidence
- Citation role: [[assume-positive-intent-rick-manelius]] links to Stout's The Sociopath Next Door.
- Risk frame: [[assume-positive-intent-rick-manelius]] uses Stout's claimed 4% figure to argue that some people can exploit positive intent.
- Qualification role: [[assume-positive-intent-rick-manelius]] still concludes that the benefits of trust-first relationships can outweigh the risk in many ordinary contexts.

## Qualifications
The wiki has not ingested Stout's original book or independent research on the cited statistic. This page records how Manelius uses Stout as a cautionary reference, not a verified psychological prevalence claim.
