## Overview
[[GergelyOrosz]] is represented in the wiki as a software-engineering writer who combines reported operational cases with practitioner guidance about product-engaged engineering.

## Current Profile
Across the available sources, Orosz connects implementation practice to product and organizational consequences. His Atlassian analysis links a destructive change and missing selective restore tooling to customer communication and trust; his Khan Academy case links architecture, migration mechanics, staffing, product opportunity cost, and retrospective tradeoffs across a 3.5-year rewrite. His product-minded-engineering essay generalizes the same boundary-spanning orientation into guidance: engineers should understand why work matters, reason across product value and implementation effort, validate early, and follow outcomes after release.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes about software-engineering and technology-company operations.
- Uses public technical material and stakeholder interviews to reconstruct cases.
- Connects implementation choices to operational, organizational, and commercial consequences.
- Presents engineering as a product-learning role that can extend from problem framing through post-release outcomes.
- Preserves practitioner uncertainty and hindsight rather than presenting every successful outcome as an unqualified prescription.
