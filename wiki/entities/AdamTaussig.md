## Overview
[[AdamTaussig]] appears as the investor-author of [[LightspeedVenturePartners]] essays about early product demand and brand formation.

## Current Profile
One source presents Taussig as an investor who learned to value early consumer products that are visibly flawed but still used intensely. His lens is diagnostic: rather than treating polish as the main signal, he looks for cases where users tolerate friction because the product's core value is unusually strong. His brand essay similarly looks beneath visual polish, defining a brand through promises about product, price, channel, and marketing and treating [[BrandEquity]] as the result of fulfilling them consistently.

## Key Characteristics
- Investor-author evaluating early consumer products.
- Treats user tolerance of flaws as possible evidence of real demand.
- Uses historical consumer-product examples to explain how rough beginnings can precede stronger mature products.
- Treats brand as an operational result of repeated customer-promise delivery rather than visual identity alone.

## Evidence
- Author role: [[broken-is-beautiful-lightspeed-venture-partners-medium]] is written from Taussig's investor perspective.
- Product-evaluation lens: [[broken-is-beautiful-lightspeed-venture-partners-medium]] says he developed a taste for broken products that are clearly working anyway.
- Example method: [[broken-is-beautiful-lightspeed-venture-partners-medium]] compares Uber, Stitch Fix, Twitter, HQ Trivia, and Android as imperfect products with strong user pull.
- Brand framework: [[four-promises-a-brand-makes-to-its-customers-lightspeed-venture-partners-medium]] defines brand through product, price, channel, and marketing promises.
- Brand measurement: [[four-promises-a-brand-makes-to-its-customers-lightspeed-venture-partners-medium]] proposes NPS as a referability-based brand-equity measure but does not substantiate the claimed link with sustained market leadership.

## Qualifications
The page only reflects Taussig as represented in two short essays. They do not provide a full biography, investment history, role history, or independent evaluation of his product and brand judgments. The brand essay's four-part model and market-leadership association are practitioner claims without comparative evidence.
