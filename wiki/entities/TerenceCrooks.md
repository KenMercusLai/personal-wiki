## Overview
[[TerenceCrooks]] is represented in this wiki as a University of Otago researcher and coauthor of a study relating lecture note-taking to university examination performance.

## Current Profile
Crooks and three colleagues analyzed notes from a full introductory-psychology course rather than assigning students an unfamiliar short-term strategy. They compared attendance and several note measures with a broad multiple-choice paper and a choice-based essay paper, finding that note quantity had the strongest and most assessment-dependent association with results.

## Key Characteristics
- Coauthored a naturalistic investigation of study behavior across a complete university course.
- Helped connect note-taking measures with two examination formats that sampled course content differently.
- Preserved causal and generalizability limits despite large observed correlations.

## Evidence
Research design:
- [[nye-et-al-student-note-taking-related-to-university-examination-performance]] credits Crooks jointly with Nye, Powley, and Tripp for the 38-student note and examination study.

Assessment interpretation:
- [[nye-et-al-student-note-taking-related-to-university-examination-performance]] relates stronger multiple-choice correlations to broader content coverage, recall orientation, and likely reliability while treating these as explanations rather than experimentally isolated causes.

## Qualifications
The publication does not allocate individual author contributions, so the profile describes the team's work rather than claiming that Crooks alone designed the assessment comparison or analysis. The source establishes only this 1984 study context, not a comprehensive professional biography.
