## Overview
[[MarcoArment]] is the developer and founder who turned a personal read-later problem into [[Instapaper]], first as an after-hours side project and then as a profitable one-person business.

## Current Profile
The sources now show more of the transition behind Instapaper's origin. While working as lead developer at [[Tumblr]], Arment says he spent about five hours building a simple link-saving application after losing articles he wanted to revisit. Friends' interest, the iPhone App Store announcement, and his own need to read offline on the subway led him to develop the mobile product during evenings. By September 2010, reported revenue from the paid app, advertising, and an optional subscription was sufficient for him to leave Tumblr.

The 2011 profile also clarifies that remaining small was a preference, not merely an early stage. Arment associated outside investment with growth and return expectations that would create a different job, while the later retrospective shows that the product subsequently acquired teams and owners. His profile is therefore best understood as the founder of Instapaper's initial independent era, not as proof that the product permanently remained a one-person company.

## Key Characteristics
- Built the initial application rapidly around a personal problem with losing articles.
- Incubated Instapaper alongside employment before moving to it full time.
- Expanded the product around offline mobile reading and early App Store distribution.
- Preferred a low-overhead, investor-free company over growth obligations he did not want.
- Represents Instapaper's earliest independent-development era before later teams and acquisitions.

## Evidence
- Rapid origin and employment context: [[entrepreneurs-who-go-it-alone-by-choice-ideas-for-small-business-time]] says Arment built a simple first version in about five hours while he was Tumblr's lead developer.
- Mobile development: [[entrepreneurs-who-go-it-alone-by-choice-ideas-for-small-business-time]] says friends' interest, App Store timing, and offline subway reading led him to develop the iPhone app during evenings.
- Full-time transition: [[entrepreneurs-who-go-it-alone-by-choice-ideas-for-small-business-time]] reports that he left Tumblr in September 2010 after Instapaper produced enough income to support him.
- Deliberate company shape: [[entrepreneurs-who-go-it-alone-by-choice-ideas-for-small-business-time]] quotes Arment rejecting investor growth expectations because they would create a job he did not want.
- Launch and historical continuity: [[10-years-of-instapaper]] dates the public announcement to January 28, 2008 and later refers to the early "Marco days."

## Qualifications
Both sources are narrow accounts centered on Instapaper rather than full biographies. The 2011 profile relies heavily on Arment's retrospective statements and gives user totals and profitability without costs, workload accounting, or independent financial detail. Its one-person-company description is a historical snapshot that must not be projected across Instapaper's later ownership and team changes.

## What Changed
- Added the five-hour prototype, Tumblr employment bridge, evening mobile development, and September 2010 full-time transition.
- Reframed the one-person operation as a deliberate 2011 company-design choice rather than a permanent product state.
