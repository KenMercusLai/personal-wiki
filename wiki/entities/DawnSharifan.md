## Overview
[[DawnSharifan]] is represented here as Slack's people-operations director explaining both the workplace value of outside interests and the bias risk of using them in interviews.

## Current Profile
Sharifan argues that hobbies and side projects may signal curiosity, continued improvement, broader experience, teamwork, and recovery from work. She draws a firm boundary around selection, however: an impressive band or shared hobby can distract a hiring team from the role and favor candidates who resemble the interviewers, so evaluation should stay focused on ability to do the job.

## Key Characteristics
- Connects outside interests with curiosity, improvement, wellbeing, and possible teamwork experience.
- Separates the value of employee self-expression from the validity of candidate-selection evidence.
- Warns that shared hobbies and impressive projects can introduce affinity bias and distract from job ability.

## Evidence
- Employee value: [[elevate-yourself-with-side-projects-the-official-slack-blog]] quotes Sharifan linking outside interests to diverse ideas, continued improvement, curiosity, and broader experience.
- Wellbeing and teamwork: [[elevate-yourself-with-side-projects-the-official-slack-blog]] attributes to her the view that side projects can support burnout prevention and team-oriented experience.
- Interview boundary: [[elevate-yourself-with-side-projects-the-official-slack-blog]] says Slack discourages side-project questions because they can distract from role fit and favor similar backgrounds.

## Qualifications
The source reports Sharifan's practitioner judgment but supplies no candidate-outcome, burnout, teamwork, or retention data. Access to side projects also varies with working hours, caregiving, health, income, and other constraints, reinforcing her warning against using them as a hiring proxy.
