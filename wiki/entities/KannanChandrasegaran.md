## Overview
[[KannanChandrasegaran]] is a practitioner-author represented in the wiki by an essay about hidden software-feature complexity and early specification discovery.

## Current Profile
Chandrasegaran presents software scope as a product-design problem before it is a coding problem. His article uses a hypothetical local book marketplace to show how a short feature statement expands when translated into actual user steps, then recommends fast outlines, mockups, or flowcharts plus systematic questioning before implementation. The profile is source-bounded: it establishes this argument but not his broader career, projects, or later views.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains software-development risk through concrete product and user-flow examples.
- Distinguishes necessary elaboration of an original objective from unrelated feature creep.
- Advocates low-fidelity specification work before expensive implementation cycles.
- Organizes product questioning around inputs, outputs, interactions, and operator functionality.

## Evidence
- Feature-depth argument: [[kannan-chandrasegaran-your-app-is-an-onion-why-software-projects-spiral-out-of-control]] compares software features to fractals whose details emerge at smaller scales.
- Marketplace example: [[kannan-chandrasegaran-your-app-is-an-onion-why-software-projects-spiral-out-of-control]] expands a two-line book-request pitch into identity, catalog, matching, communication, payment, verification, and location needs.
- Workflow recommendation: [[kannan-chandrasegaran-your-app-is-an-onion-why-software-projects-spiral-out-of-control]] recommends mapping every user step and repeatedly questioning the representation before coding.
- Cost claim: [[kannan-chandrasegaran-your-app-is-an-onion-why-software-projects-spiral-out-of-control]] contrasts day-scale specification revisions with month-scale app revisions in its illustrative diagrams.

## Qualifications
The wiki has one 2017 essay by Chandrasegaran. Its examples and cycle times are illustrative, not comparative project data, and the source does not establish whether the method improved delivery outcomes in his own or others' projects.
