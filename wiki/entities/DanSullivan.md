## Overview
[[DanSullivan]] is represented in the wiki as the Strategic Coach founder to whom the source attributes the distinction between a time-and-effort economy and a results economy.

## Current Profile
In Hardy's account, Sullivan argues that entrepreneurs cross a “risk line”: their revenue depends on creating customer value rather than receiving guaranteed pay for elapsed time. The cited passage also extends this logic to employees of entrepreneurial firms, even when their salaries partially shield them from direct business outcomes. This is a secondhand presentation in one essay, not a complete or independently verified profile of Sullivan's work.

## Key Characteristics
- Entrepreneurial coach associated with Strategic Coach.
- Frames entrepreneurship around value creation, revenue, and outcome exposure.
- Separates productive results from time and effort, which may vary independently.
- Treats efficiency as maximizing results while minimizing unnecessary effort.

## Evidence
- Role and framework: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] identifies Sullivan as Strategic Coach's founder and attributes the two-economy distinction to him.
- Risk exposure: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] quotes Sullivan describing entrepreneurs as living without guaranteed income.
- Employee boundary: [[one-decision-separates-the-wealthy-from-the-non-wealthy]] quotes him saying salaried employees inside entrepreneurial firms remain connected to the results economy despite partial shelter.

## Qualifications
The wiki currently has only Hardy's excerpt and interpretation, not Sullivan's complete original context or outcome evidence. The framework can obscure delayed, collective, preventive, care, research, and infrastructure work whose value is real but difficult to assign immediately to one person. Guaranteed compensation can also support prudent long-term work rather than mere busyness.
