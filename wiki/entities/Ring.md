## Overview
[[Ring]] is the home-security hardware company formerly called DoorBot, represented in the source through its Wi-Fi video doorbell, cash-constrained early development, television-driven sales, product expansion, and venture financing.

## Current Profile
The source says [[JamieSiminoff]] created DoorBot in 2012 after being unable to hear the doorbell from his garage. The product let a person use a smartphone to see and speak with someone at the front door. When the eight-person company was running out of money in 2013, Siminoff asked [[SharkTank]] for $700,000 in exchange for 10% and received no offer. He nevertheless attributes at least $5 million in subsequent sales to the broadcast, saying that cash supported more engineers and another product without an immediate new financing round.

By the 2017 interview, DoorBot had become Ring and expanded from a doorbell into a home-security product line. The article reports about one million customers, estimated prior-year sales of $155 million, and more than $200 million in outside investment. These figures establish only the source's historical growth account; they do not show profitability, retention, product reliability, security outcomes, financing terms, or the broadcast's counterfactual effect.

## Key Characteristics
- Began as a smartphone-connected video doorbell addressing a problem experienced by its founder.
- Operated as an eight-person hardware startup during the 2013 Shark Tank pitch.
- Used post-broadcast customer sales as a reported substitute for an immediately needed financing round.
- Expanded from video doorbells into a broader home-security product line.
- Combined product revenue with substantial external investment in the source's 2017 account.
