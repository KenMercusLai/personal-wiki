## Overview
[[SequoiaCapital]] is a venture-capital firm presented through [[PatGrady]]'s account of selective investing, category-defining company selection, and institutional reinvention, and through David Lee's recommendation of Sequoia's business-plan and pitch-deck checklist.

## Current Profile
The sources describe Sequoia as a firm that looks for fundamental companies with a chance to remain independent, define categories, and dominate those categories over decades. It is also presented as an organization trying to preserve founder [[DonValentine]]'s wisdom while reinventing its own operating methods and technology use as its deal funnel grows. Lee's checklist article adds a founder-facing role: Sequoia's business-plan and pitch-deck checklist is recommended as a preparation aid for getting money.

TechCrunch adds a market-signal role that does not depend on Sequoia's investment process. Once a startup has traction enough to choose among investors, the reputation of the firm becomes part of what the founder is buying: an association with a credible investor such as Sequoia can lend credibility that helps the company form business partnerships and recruit employees. In that telling Sequoia is not only a supplier of capital and advice but a brand that transfers some of its standing to the portfolio company.

## Key Characteristics
- Selects for companies that can define durable categories rather than merely follow best practices.
- Maintains a highly selective investment funnel in the period described.
- Grounds its founding story in connecting technical builders to human problems.
- Treats institutional reinvention as necessary even when founding principles remain useful.
- Provides a business-plan and pitch-deck checklist used as fundraising preparation.
- Functions as a credibility signal whose association can help a portfolio company win partnerships and hire employees.

## Evidence
- Category-defining selection: [[8-lessons-from-one-of-silicon-valleys-top-vcs-drift]] says Sequoia prefers companies that can show the world something it did not know before.
- Investment funnel: [[8-lessons-from-one-of-silicon-valleys-top-vcs-drift]] reports roughly 500 first meetings for each investment in Grady's account.
- Founding premise: [[8-lessons-from-one-of-silicon-valleys-top-vcs-drift]] says Valentine believed brilliant engineers needed help connecting great things to human problems.
- Reinvention: [[8-lessons-from-one-of-silicon-valleys-top-vcs-drift]] says Sequoia seeks to keep its founders' wisdom while changing how it operates and uses technology.
- Fundraising checklist: [[checklists-for-startups-david-lee-medium]] recommends Sequoia's business-plan and pitch-deck checklist under "How to Get Money."
- Credibility transfer: [[wtf-is-a-funding-round-techcrunch]] says having a credible investor like Sequoia attached to a startup can assist with credibility, which helps in forming business partnerships and hiring new employees.

## Qualifications
The Drift source speaks from Sequoia's own and Drift's perspective and focuses on investment philosophy rather than independent performance analysis, fund economics, or failed investments. The Lee source references Sequoia's checklist but does not reproduce its full contents or show how it affects fundraising outcomes. TechCrunch uses Sequoia as a single named example of investor credibility rather than as a studied case, and it gives no evidence on how much of the reputational benefit is causal, how long it lasts, or whether later-stage firms now provide the same signal.
