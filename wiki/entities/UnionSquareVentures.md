## Overview
[[UnionSquareVentures]] is an early-stage venture-capital firm represented through Del.icio.us and Indeed financing, a later 55-company valuation sample, evolving investment theses, Wilson's description of a partnership largely formed outside the operator-first career path, and a reported eight-to-ten-investment annual pace designed around sustained portfolio service.

## Current Profile
The sources present USV from six historical angles. [[JoshuaSchachter]] says he approached the firm in early 2005 because Del.icio.us was failing under its traffic; the company raised US$1 million “on $3 million” and worked from a closet-sized office at USV, although the interview does not define the valuation convention or document the round. Wilson's Indeed retrospective moves further upstream: he and [[BradBurnham]] discussed a search-style job-listing model while raising the first fund, decided they would act if a matching company appeared, and then persistently courted [[Indeed]] after recognizing it through a blog post. Wilson later uses 55 new portfolio companies associated with three funds to estimate directional changes in seed and Series A pricing across North America and Europe. Wenger describes a later evolution of the firm's thesis toward trusted brands with customer-aligned incentives and consumer subscriptions. Wilson also says most of his USV partners did not first work as startup operators, using the firm as one example of an investor-developed partnership rather than evidence that one career background produces better performance. His pacing essay reports eight to ten new firm investments per year, or one to two per partner, and presents that cadence as necessary to preserve partner bandwidth and team-backed service across seven-to-ten-year relationships.

## Key Characteristics
- Led or co-led seed, Series A, and some Series B rounds in the source's six-year sample.
- Invested across North America and Europe, with particular density in New York City and the Bay Area.
- Supplied a 55-company internal sample for comparing 2012- and 2017-timeframe valuations and round sizes.
- Used customer-aligned incentives as the meaning of “trusted brands” in its 2018 thesis.
- Held a growing 2018 portfolio of consumer-subscription companies, including hybrid ad-supported and paid tiers.
- Financed and housed Del.icio.us during its early 2005 effort to stabilize and grow before its Yahoo sale.
- Uses explicit theses, collaborative decisions, and a controlled annual pace to prepare recognition while preserving capacity for company-specific pursuit and support.

## Evidence
- Portfolio composition: [[valuation-inflation-avc]] reports 16 seed, 31 Series A, and 8 Series B rounds led or co-led across 55 new USV portfolio companies.
- Geographic scope: [[valuation-inflation-avc]] says USV invests in North America and Europe and identifies the Bay Area as its most expensive market and Europe as its least expensive.
- Analytical limit: [[valuation-inflation-avc]] says the firm's recent Series B data was insufficient for a conclusion.
- Trusted-brand thesis: [[continuations-by-albert-wenger-from-advertising-to-subscriptions-and-the]] says trusted brands align company incentives with customers and end users over the long term.
- Subscription portfolio: [[continuations-by-albert-wenger-from-advertising-to-subscriptions-and-the]] names USV investments across music, health, education, finance, and publishing as evidence of the firm's interest in consumer subscriptions.
- Del.icio.us financing: [[did-i-make-a-mistake-selling-del-icio-us-to-yahoo]] reports a US$1 million early-2005 raise “on $3 million” and a small office inside USV.
- Partner-background claim: [[investor-vcs-and-operator-vcs-avc]] says most of Wilson's USV partners did not follow the operator-to-investor path.
- Prepared opportunity model: [[knowing-what-you-are-looking-for-avc]] says Wilson and Burnham committed to pursue a search-style job-listing company before encountering Indeed.
- Founder courtship: [[knowing-what-you-are-looking-for-avc]] says USV persisted until it invested in fall 2005 even though Indeed's self-funded founders initially did not need its capital.
- Investment cadence: [[investment-pace-avc]] reports eight to ten new USV investments per year, equivalent to roughly one or two per partner.
- Capacity rationale: [[investment-pace-avc]] says a seven-year average hold can produce seven to fourteen concurrent companies per partner and connects the firm's pace to partner bandwidth, team support, thesis evolution, collaborative rigor, and post-investment value-add.

## Qualifications
This remains a narrow historical profile from five firm-affiliated essays and one founder interview. The Del.icio.us account does not define whether “on $3 million” is pre-money or post-money, name all investors, or provide financing documents. The Indeed account is a hindsight-selected success without a denominator of failed thesis matches or rejected outliers, and its company-quality and hypothetical-valuation claims lack return or counterfactual evidence. The 55-company sample is not a market census, and the subscription list is a point-in-time portfolio illustration rather than evidence of company outcomes. Wilson's general statement about partner backgrounds supplies no names, biographies, denominator, or performance comparison. The pacing article does not name partners or covered companies, measure workload, explain shared board coverage or team leverage, or document its claimed annual billion-dollar-plus exits. The sources do not cover USV's ownership, full fund history, complete thesis, team, or performance.

## What Changed
- Added the firm's reported eight-to-ten-deal annual cadence and its one-to-two-deal partner equivalent.
- Connected pace to long-duration service, partner bandwidth, supporting teams, and thesis-driven collaborative selection.
- Preserved the absence of workload, company-level attribution, comparative strategy, and verified return evidence.
