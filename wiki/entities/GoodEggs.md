## Overview
[[GoodEggs]] is used as a two-sided referral-reward example where store credit for the inviter and a free item for the invitee create an economically bounded viral loop.

## Current Profile
The source presents Good Eggs as a Bay Area grocery-delivery service whose referral program must be evaluated against customer lifetime value. Its example also illustrates the "gifting effect": letting a user choose an item for a friend can make the invitation feel thoughtful rather than spammy.

## Key Characteristics
- Grocery-delivery service example.
- Uses two-sided referral incentives.
- Connects referral design to unit economics and gifting psychology.

## Evidence
- Two-sided reward: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] says Good Eggs offers store credit to the inviter and a free item to the referred friend.
- Economics: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] says the program works only if invited-customer lifetime value exceeds the combined reward cost and purchase requirement.
- Gifting effect: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] argues that choosing a gift redirects attention from spamming toward selecting something a friend might like.

## Qualifications
The source gives a referral-design example but does not analyze Good Eggs' broader business performance or retention.

## What Changed
- Created Good Eggs as a two-sided referral and gifting-effect example.
