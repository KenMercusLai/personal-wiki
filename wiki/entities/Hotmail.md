## Overview
[[Hotmail]] is an email service used in the source as a classic example of a product message carrying its own acquisition prompt.

## Current Profile
The sources say Hotmail appended a promotional sign-off link to outgoing emails. Because every user sent messages to non-users, ordinary product use created repeated exposure and a direct signup path; Cselle presents this as the signature-based viral-loop pattern for products that send messages.

## Key Characteristics
- Web email service.
- Used outgoing email footers as an acquisition channel.
- Represents a simple product-integrated viral loop.
- Shows how sent messages can carry a promotional signature at scale.

## Evidence
- Email footer: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] describes Hotmail's signup strapline in user-sent email.
- Conversion path: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says a percentage of recipients clicked through and became users.
- Signature mechanism: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] says Hotmail grew from 20,000 users to 1.5 million users in four months with an email signature line.
