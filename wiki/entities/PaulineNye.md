## Overview
[[PaulineNye]] is represented in this wiki as a University of Otago researcher and coauthor of a naturalistic study of student note-taking and examination performance.

## Current Profile
Nye and three colleagues collected complete course notes from 38 introductory-psychology students, sampled one lecture from each of ten instructors, quantified attendance and note features, and related those measures to essay and multiple-choice final examinations. The paper's central contribution is a strong association between the quantity of lecture notes and exam scores, paired with an explicit warning that the observational design cannot establish causality.

## Key Characteristics
- Coauthored a course-scale observational study rather than a short artificial note-taking experiment.
- Helped operationalize note-taking through quantity, additions, abbreviation, layout, emphasis, and attendance measures.
- Interpreted the findings conditionally through assessment coverage, course design, and possible confounding.

## Evidence
Research design:
- [[nye-et-al-student-note-taking-related-to-university-examination-performance]] attributes the study, sampling, note analysis, and examination comparison jointly to Nye, Crooks, Powley, and Tripp.

Interpretive stance:
- [[nye-et-al-student-note-taking-related-to-university-examination-performance]] reports a strong quantity-performance relationship while rejecting a direct causal interpretation and limiting generalization beyond the course.

## Qualifications
The source does not separate the four authors' individual responsibilities, and this profile should not attribute a particular design choice, analysis, or conclusion to Nye alone. It documents one 1984 publication and does not establish her broader biography or research program.
