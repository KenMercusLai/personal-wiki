## Overview
[[DeepMind]] is represented as the AI research organization behind [[AlphaGo]] and the prospective subject of Ben Weber's 2016 StarCraft challenge.

## Current Profile
The source presents DeepMind through its use of neural representations, search, expert examples, and reinforcement learning in game-playing systems. Weber does not report a DeepMind StarCraft implementation; he uses the organization's AlphaGo success to ask what additional capabilities would be needed in a partially observable, real-time strategy environment.

## Key Characteristics
- Developed [[AlphaGo]], the immediate context for Weber's proposed next challenge.
- Combined learning and search methods in the source's high-level account.
- Was publicly associated with interest in StarCraft as a research testbed in 2016.
- Would need uncertainty handling, action abstraction, continual adaptation, and real-time control for Weber's proposed task.
- Is discussed prospectively rather than through an evaluated StarCraft system.

## Evidence
- Game-system context: [[gamasutra-ben-webers-blog-deepmind-challenges-for-starcraft]] begins from AlphaGo's win over Lee Sedol.
- Proposed research direction: [[gamasutra-ben-webers-blog-deepmind-challenges-for-starcraft]] attributes public interest in StarCraft to co-founder Demis Hassabis.
- Capability gap: [[gamasutra-ben-webers-blog-deepmind-challenges-for-starcraft]] enumerates the properties a future system would need to address.

## Qualifications
This single 2016 commentary is neither a corporate history nor a primary technical description of DeepMind's systems. Its StarCraft discussion is hypothetical, and its compact explanation of AlphaGo should not be treated as an exact architecture specification.
