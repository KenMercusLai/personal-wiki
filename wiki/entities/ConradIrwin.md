## Overview
[[ConradIrwin]] is represented in this wiki as the author of a 2025 [[Zed]] essay arguing that current LLMs can generate and revise code but cannot reliably own the full software-engineering loop.

## Current Profile
Irwin's source centers engineering expertise on maintaining and comparing models of intended and actual behavior. His practical position is collaborative: models are useful for bounded implementation, documentation, requirements synthesis, testing, logging, and debugging, while the human stays responsible for context, diagnosis, and acceptance.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats clear, maintained [[MentalModels]] as a distinguishing engineering capability.
- Separates code-generation competence from end-to-end [[SoftwareEngineering]].
- Attributes current LLM unreliability partly to context omission, recency bias, and hallucination.
- Advocates human-directed collaboration rather than autonomous replacement or blanket rejection of AI tools.

## Evidence
- Engineering-loop account: [[why-llms-cant-really-build-software]] describes repeated comparison of requirement and implementation models.
- Capability boundary: [[why-llms-cant-really-build-software]] credits LLMs with coding and tool use while denying that those abilities establish stable project understanding.
- Practical stance: [[why-llms-cant-really-build-software]] places the engineer in control and the LLM in a supporting role.

## Qualifications
The profile is based on one short practitioner essay. Its account of model cognition is an explanatory hypothesis without comparative evaluation data, and it explicitly leaves open whether memory or architectural changes will alter the boundary.
