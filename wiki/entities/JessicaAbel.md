## Overview
[[JessicaAbel]] is an author and comics creator represented here through her 2016 essay naming the creative burden of fantasized but unmade projects as [[IdeaDebt]].

## Current Profile
Abel combines practitioner reflection, student observation, and attributed ideas from [[KazuKibuishi]] and [[IraGlass]]. Her central intervention is a choice rule: make an imperfect project before expectation compounds, or deliberately abandon an obsolete idea so attention can return to current work. She applies the rule autobiographically by releasing projects associated with earlier versions of herself.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames creative avoidance through the concept of idea debt.
- Connects project fantasy with perfectionism and creative sunk costs.
- Treats repeated finished work as a route for developing skill.
- Legitimatizes deliberate abandonment alongside persistence.

## Evidence
- Conceptual framing: [[imagining-your-future-projects-is-holding-you-back]] defines idea debt as extensive imagined reward and insufficient actual making.
- Synthesis role: [[imagining-your-future-projects-is-holding-you-back]] combines Kibuishi's action analogy with Glass's volume-of-work argument.
- Personal application: [[imagining-your-future-projects-is-holding-you-back]] says abandoning several long-carried projects restored attention for present priorities.

## Qualifications
The available profile is derived from one reflective essay, not a comprehensive biography or assessment of Abel's wider work. Its causal claims about attention, output, and creative development are experiential rather than measured.
