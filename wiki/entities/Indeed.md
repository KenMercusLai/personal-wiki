## Overview
[[Indeed]] is a job-search company represented as the close match between a pre-existing [[UnionSquareVentures]] investment model and an opportunity the firm discovered and pursued in 2004-2005.

## Current Profile
In [[FredWilson]]'s retrospective, Indeed implemented a search-style alternative to hosted job boards: jobs could remain on employers' own websites while employers bought traffic to them. Wilson encountered the company through a John Battelle blog post shortly after USV's first fund closing and connected it to a model he and [[BradBurnham]] had already discussed. The founders were self-funding after selling a prior company and initially did not need USV's capital, but the firm persisted and invested in fall 2005. Wilson later called Indeed possibly USV's best company investment; the company sold to Recruit rather than remaining independent and going public.

## Key Characteristics
- Applied search mechanics and paid traffic to online job discovery rather than relying only on hosted paid listings.
- Matched an applications-layer business model USV had articulated before discovering the company.
- Was self-funded by founders who had sold a previous company and were initially reluctant to accept USV capital.
- Received a USV investment in fall 2005 after sustained pursuit.
- Was later sold to Recruit rather than remaining independent and pursuing a public listing.

## Evidence
- Thesis fit: [[knowing-what-you-are-looking-for-avc]] says Wilson immediately recognized Indeed as the job-search model Burnham had proposed.
- Financing context: [[knowing-what-you-are-looking-for-avc]] says the founders were self-funding and did not need USV's money, requiring persistent courtship before the 2005 investment.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from a short investor retrospective rather than operating records, founder testimony, acquisition documents, or return data. It does not establish Indeed's complete product, business model, performance, acquisition terms, present ownership, or current operations. Wilson's claim that an independent public company might have been worth more than $10 billion is explicitly counterfactual.
