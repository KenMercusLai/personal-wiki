## Overview
[[Inoki]] is a software practitioner and writer represented in the wiki through a retrospective on ten years of participation in open-source projects.

## Current Profile
The source presents Inoki as a contributor who prefers investigating bugs, developing features, and building things over the relationship maintenance and status work required to shepherd changes through mature communities. The account draws on an AX88179/178a Linux driver investigation, a vLLM issue and follow-on pull request, GitHub Accelerator training, and experience as both a Google Summer of Code student and mentor.

From those cases, Inoki argues that open-source participation has become intertwined with company strategy, professional advancement, quantitative contribution metrics, and AI-assisted development. The author is not opposed to commercialization or AI, but favors collaboration systems that recognize upstream reasoning, preserve learning paths, support maintainers, and let participants find value beyond public scoreboards.

## Key Characteristics
- Draws conclusions from ten years of first-person open-source participation.
- Prefers exploratory technical creation over prolonged coordination and organizational status work.
- Proposes a “street-stall” model alongside the cathedral and bazaar metaphors.
- Treats maintenance capacity, credit allocation, learning paths, and commercial incentives as connected governance problems.
- Supports continued open-source participation while warning against exclusion, metric gaming, and AI-enabled centralization.
