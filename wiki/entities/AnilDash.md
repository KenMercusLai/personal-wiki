## Overview
[[AnilDash]] appears as a writer, product operator, longtime Twitter user, and early blogging-software builder who uses firsthand platform history to examine how technical choices become governance choices.

## Current Profile
The sources position Dash as both a builder inside early social publishing and a skeptical observer of dominant platforms. His Twitter essay draws authority from long use, product judgment, a failed startup exposed to Twitter's API decisions, and concern for Twitter's cultural role. His later retrospective adds direct work on Movable Type and TypePad: a dispute over underscores and hyphens taught him how user dependence on Google ranking could force a CMS team to adopt a platform's preferred convention despite philosophical resistance.

Across the two essays, Dash treats small product decisions as expressions of institutional power. API stability, abuse response, metrics, URL defaults, and algorithmic opacity determine what developers and publishers build, while platform users often absorb the cost of adapting.
