## Overview
[[LloydTabb]] is the Looker founder and CTO interviewed by [[FirstRoundReview]] about operational metrics, event-stream analysis, customer outliers, and distributed data fluency.

## Current Profile
The source presents Tabb as a data practitioner drawing on work at [[LiveOps]], Netscape, ReadyForce, and [[Looker]]. His central distinction is between externally comparative [[VanityMetrics]] and [[ClarityMetrics]] that help operators predict individual behavior and improve a business. He favors unified chronological activity data, early service proxies, department-level failure measures, and direct conversations with unusual users when numbers cannot reveal the experience behind them.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames metric selection around actionability and prediction rather than impressive aggregate size.
- Uses behavioral proxies when subjective qualities such as service or accountability lack direct measures.
- Treats event streams as a way to follow customers across acquisition, use, transaction, and departure.
- Pairs quantitative outlier detection with direct customer calls and qualitative interpretation.
- Advocates data fluency across functions rather than dependence on a central analytics queue.

## Evidence
- Metric framework: [[im-sorry-but-those-are-vanity-metrics-first-round-review]] attributes the vanity-versus-clarity distinction to Tabb.
- LiveOps case: [[im-sorry-but-those-are-vanity-metrics-first-round-review]] says he found contractor attendance more predictive of agent performance than call length or extra sales.
- Looker practice: [[im-sorry-but-those-are-vanity-metrics-first-round-review]] says he tracked active engagement and personally called unusual active and inactive customers.
- Operational breadth: [[im-sorry-but-those-are-vanity-metrics-first-round-review]] applies his framework to service, advertising, ecommerce, software, and marketplace businesses.
- Data culture: [[im-sorry-but-those-are-vanity-metrics-first-round-review]] argues that customer support, design, business development, and other teams should be able to explore data and investigate proxies.

## Qualifications
This profile is limited to one 2017 edited interview and does not establish Tabb's complete career, Looker's measured outcomes, or whether the reported practices remained in use. The examples are self-reported and lack datasets, definitions, comparison groups, and causal tests. The source's broad skepticism toward A/B testing should be read as a warning against shallow immediate outcomes and siloed data, not evidence that experimentation is generally inferior to interviews or observational analysis.
