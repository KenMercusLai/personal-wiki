## Overview
[[Metaculus]] appears in this wiki as a prediction-market setting used to test metaknowledge questions about political forecasts.

## Current Profile
The source uses Metaculus as the experimental platform where [[GeorgeMusser]] and Anthony Aguirre asked respondents both for their own political forecast and for their prediction of the crowd's forecast. The resulting gaps between personal estimates and expected crowd estimates are used as evidence that [[SharedInformationBias]] can appear in public forecasting.

## Key Characteristics
- Hosts prediction questions where respondents can state probabilistic beliefs.
- Supports metaknowledge experiments by letting researchers ask about both individual and crowd expectations.
- Provides examples involving Bernie Sanders's nomination chances and Brexit.

## Evidence
- Prediction setting: [[a-mathematical-bs-detector-can-boost-the-wisdom-of-crowds-aeon-essays]] describes a Metaculus experiment asking about Bernie Sanders's chance of becoming the Democratic nominee.
- Metaknowledge design: [[a-mathematical-bs-detector-can-boost-the-wisdom-of-crowds-aeon-essays]] says respondents also predicted what fellow respondents would say.
- Shared-information signal: [[a-mathematical-bs-detector-can-boost-the-wisdom-of-crowds-aeon-essays]] reports similar crowd-prediction mismatch patterns in a Brexit poll.

## Qualifications
The source uses Metaculus as an example platform and does not assess the platform's broader accuracy, governance, or forecasting community.
