## Overview
[[Disney]] is a global entertainment company represented here through its 2016 withdrawal from internally publishing major console and PC games.

## Current Profile
The Business Insider account presents Disney as exceptionally rich in recognizable intellectual property but poorly matched to the uncertainty and long payback periods of large-game publishing. It reportedly acquired and later closed at least six studios, repeatedly changed mandates, and applied layered corporate and franchise oversight without developing stable game-making judgment. Management ultimately kept mobile activity but shifted major games to external licensees, choosing lower operational exposure over internal publishing margin and control.

## Key Characteristics
- Owns major entertainment properties including Star Wars, Marvel, Mickey Mouse, ESPN, and theme-park brands.
- Allocates capital across films, television, parks, and games with much greater confidence in its established businesses.
- Built an internal game operation through studio acquisitions but did not sustain stable mandates or long-horizon portfolio commitment.
- Uses licensing to retain intellectual-property leverage while transferring much of game development and publishing risk.
- Ended Disney Infinity and closed Avalanche Software when toys-to-life growth and development economics appeared unfavorable.

## Evidence
- Intellectual-property position: [[inside-disneys-messy-video-game-business-business-insider]] describes Disney's portfolio as a major advantage for both internal games and licensing.
- Internal capability limits: [[inside-disneys-messy-video-game-business-business-insider]] reports weak institutional game knowledge, shifting studio direction, repeated cancellations, and at least six acquired-studio closures.
- Governance friction: [[inside-disneys-messy-video-game-business-business-insider]] says one Marvel mobile project faced separate Disney and Marvel approval layers.
- Licensing pivot: [[inside-disneys-messy-video-game-business-business-insider]] quotes Bob Iger framing licensing as better risk management than publishing.
- Portfolio exit: [[inside-disneys-messy-video-game-business-business-insider]] says Disney ended Disney Infinity after forecasting category decline despite the franchise's reported billion-dollar milestone.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one 2016 retrospective assembled mainly from two former employees, with Disney declining to comment. It does not provide audited project economics, a complete studio chronology, the internal forecasts behind cancellations, licensee outcomes, or Disney's later game strategy. Strong intellectual property and capital do not by themselves show that internal publishing was the best use of Disney resources, while exiting publishing does not prove that every canceled project lacked merit.
