## Overview
[[GV]] is represented in this wiki as the institutional home of a rapid [[DesignSprint]] process and of practitioner guidance that treats clear [[InterfaceCopywriting]], prototyping, and user observation as connected design work.

## Current Profile
One source credits GV with a four-to-five-day product-design process that favors rapid high-fidelity prototyping and user testing over intensive up-front research. The interface-copy essay adds a compatible view from work with startups: writing determines what a product says, belongs inside early prototypes, and should be improved through user studies or experiments alongside interaction and visual design. Together they describe a test-oriented design practice rather than GV's investing activity or organizational history.

## Key Characteristics
- Credited with a rapid Design Sprint process.
- Associated with moving teams toward a tangible test within one working week.
- Reached AJ&Smart through Jake Knapp's writing.
- Presented as a setting where design approaches are tried repeatedly with different startups.
- Connects clear product language with prototype quality and user research.

## Evidence
- Process origin: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] names GV's Design Sprint process.
- Process emphasis: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] describes its focus on a realistic prototype and test in four to five days.
- Adoption path: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] connects Knapp's writing about the method to AJ&Smart's trial.
- Copy as design material: [[five-principles-for-great-interface-copywriting-gv-library]] presents writing, interaction, visuals, prototyping, and user research as interdependent design skills.
- Repeated startup practice: [[five-principles-for-great-interface-copywriting-gv-library]] says GV's design partners try approaches with different startups and use research to evaluate prototypes.

## Qualifications
The sources are practitioner essays, not an institutional history or comparative evaluation. They do not document GV's investing work, the formal evolution of its design practice, representative startup outcomes, or evidence that copy-led or sprint-led prototypes outperform alternatives generally. The copywriting essay mentions recurring prototype performance but supplies no study design, sample, or metrics.

## What Changed
- Broadened the profile from Design Sprint attribution to a test-oriented practice that includes interface writing.
- Added the boundary that reported prototype performance lacks comparative study details.
