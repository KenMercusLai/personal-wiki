## Overview
[[GV]], formerly Google Ventures, is represented as [[Alphabet]]'s most prolific venture fund in a 2017 deal-volume analysis and as the institutional home of a rapid [[DesignSprint]] process and practitioner guidance connecting clear [[InterfaceCopywriting]], prototyping, and user observation.

## Current Profile
Two design-practice sources credit GV with a four-to-five-day process that favors rapid high-fidelity prototyping and user testing over intensive up-front research, and with treating interface language as testable design material. [[JasonRowley]] adds the investment context missing from those accounts: GV was the most active of Alphabet's funds in the 2017 Crunchbase snapshot and had little portfolio overlap with late-stage [[CapitalG]]. Together the sources show a broad venture investor with a prototype-and-test design practice, but they do not connect that practice causally to investment selection or portfolio outcomes.

## Key Characteristics
- Credited with a rapid Design Sprint process.
- Associated with moving teams toward a tangible test within one working week.
- Presented as a setting where interface and prototype approaches are tried repeatedly with different startups.
- Described as Alphabet's most prolific venture fund in the source's 2017 deal data.
- Shared only Gusto and Pindrop with CapitalG in the analyzed portfolio.

## Evidence
- Process origin: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] names GV's Design Sprint process.
- Process emphasis: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] describes its focus on a realistic prototype and test in four to five days.
- Adoption path: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] connects Knapp's writing about the method to AJ&Smart's trial.
- Copy as design material: [[five-principles-for-great-interface-copywriting-gv-library]] presents writing, interaction, visuals, prototyping, and user research as interdependent design skills.
- Repeated startup practice: [[five-principles-for-great-interface-copywriting-gv-library]] says GV's design partners try approaches with different startups and use research to evaluate prototypes.
- Fund role and activity: [[jason-rowley-a-peek-inside-alphabets-investing-universe]] identifies GV as Alphabet's most prolific venture fund.
- Limited CapitalG overlap: [[jason-rowley-a-peek-inside-alphabets-investing-universe]] reports shared investments only in Gusto and Pindrop.
- Multi-stage Gusto exposure: [[jason-rowley-a-peek-inside-alphabets-investing-universe]] says GV invested at seed and joined CapitalG in Series B.

## Qualifications
The design sources are practitioner essays, not comparative evaluations; they do not show that copy-led or sprint-led prototypes outperform alternatives generally. The investment source is a February 2018 interpretation of 2017 Crunchbase records, not audited fund disclosure. Deal volume and limited overlap do not reveal check size, ownership, returns, selection quality, reserves, governance, or whether the design practice affected investment outcomes.
