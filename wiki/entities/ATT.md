## Overview
[[ATT]] appears in the wiki as a source-licensing constraint in Unix editor history, a telecommunications research context for push-button telephone interfaces, and the corporate acquirer behind HBO's 2018 ownership transition.

## Current Profile
The Vim source mentions AT&T indirectly through source-code licensing: restrictions around original `vi` source helped motivate open-source clones that later supported Vim's emergence. The keypad source adds a direct interface-design role: AT&T and Bell-system researchers studied push-button telephone sets in the 1950s and 1960s, testing many digit arrangements before the American touch-tone keypad stabilized around a 1-2-3 top row.

The HBO excerpt adds a separate corporate-strategy role. It reports that AT&T acquired HBO parent Time Warner for $85 billion and places AT&T executive and Warner Media CEO [[JohnStankey]] at HBO's 2018 post-Emmys party. This establishes ownership and oversight context, but the short excerpt does not explain AT&T's operating plan or its later results.

## Key Characteristics
- Represents telecommunications infrastructure and research in the keypad-history source.
- Tested multiple push-button telephone layouts before the modern phone keypad became conventional.
- Prioritized human factors, speed, accuracy, compactness, and expected user behavior in telephone-keypad design.
- Appears as a licensing constraint in Unix editor history.
- Expanded into media ownership through the reported $85 billion acquisition of Time Warner.

## Evidence
- Telephone studies: [[a-brief-history-of-the-numeric-keypad]] says AT&T engineers used 1955 and 1960 studies to test expected digit and letter locations on push-button sets.
- Layout options: [[a-brief-history-of-the-numeric-keypad]] says the company tested 15 layouts, including diagonal, pyramidal, circular, horizontal, calculator-like, and punch-card-machine-like arrangements.
- Selection logic: [[a-brief-history-of-the-numeric-keypad]] says the 5-by-2 horizontal layout performed similarly, but AT&T chose the compact 3x3-plus-1 format.
- Licensing pressure: [[a-look-at-vim-a-text-editor-for-the-ages-the-new-stack]] says AT&T source licensing around `vi` motivated open-source reimplementations.
- Media ownership: [[inside-hbos-plan-to-win-the-streaming-wars-vanity-fair]] reports AT&T's $85 billion Time Warner acquisition and identifies John Stankey as the executive overseeing HBO under the new structure.

## Qualifications
The keypad source attributes several decisions cautiously and does not claim AT&T published one definitive reason for choosing the final telephone layout. The Vim source only touches AT&T through licensing context, not through a full corporate history. The HBO evidence is a two-paragraph historical excerpt that establishes the acquisition and executive relationship without evaluating integration, strategy, or outcomes.
