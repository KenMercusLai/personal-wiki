## Overview
[[RandallKoutnik]] is a software-industry writer represented in this wiki through a practitioner guide to evaluating employers during technical job interviews.

## Current Profile
Koutnik frames the candidate as an active investigator rather than only the subject of evaluation. His method replaces broad culture questions with specific prompts, recent examples, and follow-up probes across role scope, engineering operations, communication, authority, feedback, and development. The available source establishes this interview framework, not his broader biography or the predictive validity of the questions.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats job interviews as two-way evaluation of role and employer fit.
- Prefers concrete examples and follow-up questions over abstract assurances.
- Connects technical operating practices with workplace culture and candidate risk.
- Uses pointed and sometimes humorous prompts to expose hidden expectations and decision norms.

## Evidence
- Two-way diligence and specificity: [[questions-to-ask-your-interviewer]] says attentive interviewers may value candidate questions and recommends asking for concrete recent examples when general answers remain polished or vague.
- Operating-practice coverage: [[questions-to-ask-your-interviewer]] tests task interruption, development, deployment, communication, disagreement, feedback, and professional growth.
- Warning-signal interpretation: [[questions-to-ask-your-interviewer]] treats unsupported expectations that a new hire will repair culture or process as wishful thinking rather than a credible mandate.

## Qualifications
The source is a practitioner essay rather than a systematic study of hiring or employee outcomes. Its examples may help candidates request decision-relevant evidence, but polished answers, selective anecdotes, team variation, and unequal candidate leverage limit what can be inferred from a single interview.
