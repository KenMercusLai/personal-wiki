## Overview
[[YCombinator]] appears as a startup equity-policy signal, the accelerator context for early Airbnb advice in the scaling source, and part of [[Maderight]]'s early validation context before failure.

## Current Profile
The equity source presents Y Combinator as a signal that extended exercise periods had moved from an unusual Quora policy into broader startup-norm discussion. The scaling source adds a product-development role: during YC, Airbnb's founders acted on advice associated with doing things that do not scale by spending time with New York hosts and helping them manually. The Maderight source adds a qualification: YC acceptance can be a strong ecosystem signal, but it does not replace the hard work of finding product-market fit.

## Key Characteristics
- Serves as a startup-ecosystem reference point for equity-policy norms.
- Is cited as recommending 10-year exercise periods for new companies.
- Helps show the spread of extended exercise windows beyond Quora.
- Functions as a counterweight to investor resistance described in the source.
- Serves as the accelerator context for Airbnb's early high-touch customer work.
- Is associated with advice that early customer love matters more than weak mass adoption.
- Accepted Maderight before that company shut down after failing to reach product-market fit.

## Evidence
- Recommendation signal: [[10-year-exercise-periods-make-sense-thoughts-quora]] says Y Combinator recently began recommending 10-year exercise periods as standard for new companies.
- Diffusion evidence: [[10-year-exercise-periods-make-sense-thoughts-quora]] places the recommendation after Quora's switch and after other companies followed.
- Norm-setting role: [[10-year-exercise-periods-make-sense-thoughts-quora]] uses Y Combinator's recommendation to show that exercise-window policy was becoming a public startup debate.
- Airbnb advice context: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] says Airbnb's founders acted during YC on advice to pursue 100 people who loved the product.
- Manual host work: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] describes Airbnb commuting from Mountain View to New York to meet hosts and improve listings.
- Maderight context: [[4-lessons-from-a-failed-startup-from-and-for-first-time-founders]] says [[Maderight]] was accepted into Y Combinator and [[StartX]] but still failed to find product-market fit.

## Qualifications
The equity source cites Y Combinator's recommendation but does not reproduce the full policy text, legal mechanics, or evidence from YC companies. The scaling and Maderight sources use YC as narrative context and do not analyze the accelerator independently.

## What Changed
- Added Maderight as a reminder that accelerator acceptance does not guarantee product-market fit.
