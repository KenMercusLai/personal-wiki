## Overview
[[YCombinator]] appears as a startup equity-policy signal, the accelerator context for early Airbnb advice, part of [[Maderight]]'s early validation context before failure, and the source context for the startup-life diagram behind the [[TroughOfSorrow]].

## Current Profile
The equity source presents Y Combinator as a signal that extended exercise periods had moved from an unusual Quora policy into broader startup-norm discussion. The scaling source adds a product-development role: during YC, Airbnb's founders acted on advice associated with doing things that do not scale by spending time with New York hosts and helping them manually. The Maderight source adds a qualification: YC acceptance can be a strong ecosystem signal, but it does not replace the hard work of finding product-market fit. Chen's source adds YC as a shared conceptual language for startup stages: a dinner diagram associated with [[PaulGraham]] and other partners visualizes the path from launch spike to trough to possible recovery.

## Key Characteristics
- Serves as a startup-ecosystem reference point for equity-policy norms.
- Is cited as recommending 10-year exercise periods for new companies.
- Helps show the spread of extended exercise windows beyond Quora.
- Functions as a counterweight to investor resistance described in the source.
- Serves as the accelerator context for Airbnb's early high-touch customer work.
- Is associated with advice that early customer love matters more than weak mass adoption.
- Accepted Maderight before that company shut down after failing to reach product-market fit, and supplies the context for a startup-life diagram that names the emotional and operational trough before fit.

## Evidence
- Recommendation signal: [[10-year-exercise-periods-make-sense-thoughts-quora]] says Y Combinator recently began recommending 10-year exercise periods as standard for new companies.
- Diffusion evidence: [[10-year-exercise-periods-make-sense-thoughts-quora]] places the recommendation after Quora's switch and after other companies followed.
- Norm-setting role: [[10-year-exercise-periods-make-sense-thoughts-quora]] uses Y Combinator's recommendation to show that exercise-window policy was becoming a public startup debate.
- Airbnb advice context: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] says Airbnb's founders acted during YC on advice to pursue 100 people who loved the product.
- Manual host work: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] describes Airbnb commuting from Mountain View to New York to meet hosts and improve listings.
- Maderight context: [[4-lessons-from-a-failed-startup-from-and-for-first-time-founders]] says [[Maderight]] was accepted into Y Combinator and [[StartX]] but still failed to find product-market fit.
- Trough diagram: [[after-the-techcrunch-bump-life-in-the-trough-of-sorrow-at-andrewchen]] says [[PaulGraham]] and other YC partners drew a diagram of the life of a new product.
- Startup-stage language: [[after-the-techcrunch-bump-life-in-the-trough-of-sorrow-at-andrewchen]] uses the diagram to explain launch excitement, novelty decay, the trough, false hope, and possible later recovery.

## Qualifications
The equity source cites Y Combinator's recommendation but does not reproduce the full policy text, legal mechanics, or evidence from YC companies. The scaling, Maderight, and Chen sources use YC as narrative or conceptual context and do not analyze the accelerator independently.
