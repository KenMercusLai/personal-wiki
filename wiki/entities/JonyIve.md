## Overview
[[JonyIve]] is presented as an [[Apple]] designer who used familiar physical forms to make new digital interactions approachable and removed literal visual cues after users learned the behavior.

## Current Profile
In the source, Ive’s design goal is “the strangely familiar.” The Apple Watch Digital Crown borrows the recognizable crown of a traditional watch as an entry point to a new device category, while his account of touch-screen design says physical-button imagery became unnecessary after people grew comfortable touching glass. This makes familiarity a temporary learning scaffold rather than a requirement for permanent skeuomorphism.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses recognizable physical forms as gateways into unfamiliar device behavior.
- Treats user familiarity as something that changes over time.
- Supports removing literal interface representations when the underlying interaction has become understood.

## Evidence
- Watch gateway: [[people-dont-want-something-truly-new-they-want-the-familiar-done-differently]] associates Ive’s “strangely familiar” goal with the Apple Watch Digital Crown.
- Learned interaction: [[people-dont-want-something-truly-new-they-want-the-familiar-done-differently]] quotes Ive saying users no longer needed physical buttons after becoming comfortable touching glass.
- Continued scaffolding: [[people-dont-want-something-truly-new-they-want-the-familiar-done-differently]] contrasts simplified touch interaction with Apple’s card-like Wallet representation for a newer payment behavior.
