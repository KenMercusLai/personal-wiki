## Overview
[[ZakSlayback]] is an author represented in this wiki by an essay about converting aspirations into goals, repeatable habits, and stronger self-efficacy.

## Current Profile
In the available source, Slayback writes in a compact personal-development style built around a revision of the familiar claim that success comes from showing up. His distinctive addition is continuity: useful goals require repeated action, habits should fit personality, and increasingly demanding successes can strengthen a person's belief that they are capable of effective action.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames personal improvement as a progression from aspiration to goal, habit, and efficacy.
- Emphasizes systems and repetition over one-time motivation.
- Recommends fitting habits to individual dispositions rather than prescribing one universal routine.

## Evidence
- Progression model: [[zak-slayback-you-have-to-show-up-every-day]] distinguishes fuzzy aspiration, actionable goals, habits, and self-efficacy.
- Consistency: [[zak-slayback-you-have-to-show-up-every-day]] argues that achievement requires repeatedly showing up rather than making one effort.
- Personality fit: [[zak-slayback-you-have-to-show-up-every-day]] contrasts habits that support conscientiousness with habits that preserve creativity and self-expression for highly open people.

## Qualifications
The wiki currently has one short advice essay by Slayback. Its claims are practitioner arguments illustrated by examples rather than controlled evidence, and the source does not establish how broadly its personality distinctions or daily-continuity prescription generalize.
