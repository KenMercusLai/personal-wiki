## Overview
[[AboveAvalon]] is an independent publication focused on [[Apple]] analysis, supported by paid subscriptions, and represented in the wiki by publishing-economics, product-strategy, Apple financial-strategy, growth-driver, services-economics, content-distribution, product-theory, and pricing-strategy sources.

## Current Profile
The subscription source presents Above Avalon as a one-company analysis publication built around reader payments rather than advertising, sponsorships, or broad platform scale. [[NeilCybart]] describes a product mix of public weekly articles and podcast episodes, daily subscriber-only email analysis, archive access, and a subscriber forum in [[Slack]]. The smart-glasses source shows one side of the publication's analytical mode: it reads Apple announcements, sales trends, existing wearables, and missing form factors together to argue that Apple was preparing for [[SmartGlasses]]. The pricing source shows another product-strategy mode: it reads AirPods and Apple Watch launch prices as evidence that Apple can use accessible entry pricing, supply-chain scale, premium SKUs, and vertical integration to redefine luxury. The buyback source shows the Wall Street side: it reads cash generation, tax reform, debt, share count, and investor skepticism as an Apple financial-strategy problem. The growth-story source adds an operating-performance mode by decomposing Apple's revenue rebound into iPhone ASP, Services, and wearables, then asking which levers are durable. The Services-machine source adds a segment-economics mode by decomposing Apple's Services business into digital content, iCloud, AppleCare, Apple Pay, and licensing while rejecting a simple services-company narrative. The content-distribution source adds a services and media strategy mode: it argues that Apple can put [[AppleMusic]], iTunes content, and [[AirPlay2]] on selected non-Apple hardware without abandoning premium hardware such as [[AppleTV]] and [[HomePod]]. The connecting-the-dots source adds a framework-building mode: it uses the 2018 iPhone and Apple Watch event to restate [[AppleProductTheory]], a ladder connecting Mac, iPad, iPhone, Watch, and possible glasses through increasingly personal computing. Above Avalon's distinction is not just Apple coverage, but a point-of-view product: readers pay for analysis and perspective on Apple, its competitors, investors, and adjacent industries through an Apple-centered lens.

## Key Characteristics
- Operates as an independent Apple analysis publication funded by subscriptions rather than ads, sponsors, or other revenue streams in the source's framing.
- Offers public weekly articles and podcasts plus daily subscriber-only email analysis.
- Uses archive access and a subscriber forum as additional membership value.
- Treats Apple as a broad enough analysis lens to cover competitors, industries, investors, supply chains, and media narratives.
- Positions quality, trust, and perspective above page-view growth.
- Produces strategy analysis across Apple product direction, product pricing, capital allocation, services, content distribution, installed base, premium hardware positioning, and product theory.
- Produces financial/product analysis that separates near-term revenue drivers from longer-run strategic growth engines.

- Subscription basis: [[above-avalon-above-avalon-subscriptions-turn-three]] says Above Avalon is 100% supported by subscriptions and does not depend on ads or sponsors.
- Product bundle: [[above-avalon-above-avalon-subscriptions-turn-three]] lists weekly public articles and podcast episodes, daily subscriber emails, an archive of roughly 600 emails, and Slack forum access.
- Pricing: [[above-avalon-above-avalon-subscriptions-turn-three]] names $20 monthly and $200 annual subscription options.
- Community: [[above-avalon-above-avalon-subscriptions-turn-three]] reports subscribers in 55 countries and increasing forum interaction.
- Coverage lens: [[above-avalon-above-avalon-subscriptions-turn-three]] argues Apple analysis still requires monitoring Apple's competitors and related industries, but from Apple's perspective.
- Incentive alignment: [[above-avalon-above-avalon-subscriptions-turn-three]] says paid subscriptions remove the incentive to publish sensational page-view articles.
- Strategy analysis: [[above-avalon-apple-is-figuring-out-whats-next]] interprets WWDC 2018, ARKit, Siri Shortcuts, Apple Watch, AirPods, and Magic Leap through an Apple-centered smart-glasses thesis.
- Financial analysis: [[above-avalon-apple-on-track-to-buy-50-of-itself-in-three-years]] interprets Apple's buyback path through cash flow, foreign cash, debt, tax reform, and Wall Street expectations.
- Content strategy analysis: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] interprets Apple Music on Amazon Echo, iTunes on Samsung TVs, and AirPlay 2 expansion as selective content distribution rather than a retreat from hardware.
- Growth analysis: [[above-avalon-apples-growth-story]] interprets Apple's 2018 revenue rebound through iPhone ASP, Services, and wearables, while warning that iPhone ASP growth was less durable than Services and wearables growth.
- Services analysis: [[above-avalon-the-apple-services-machine]] interprets Apple's Services segment as a hardware-dependent apparatus led by digital content distribution and licensing rather than a services-company pivot.
- Product-theory analysis: [[above-avalon-connecting-the-apple-dots]] uses iPhone XS/XR and Apple Watch Series 4 to argue that Apple's product line is organized around making technology more personal.
- Pricing analysis: [[above-avalon-the-curious-state-of-apple-product-pricing]] interprets AirPods at $159 and Apple Watch Series 1 at $269 as evidence that Apple was pairing mass-market access with luxury positioning.

## Qualifications
The subscription source is a self-assessment by Above Avalon's operator, so it does not independently verify subscriber counts, renewal rates, profitability, or reader outcomes. The strategic and financial sources are analyst interpretations and snapshots rather than outcome audits, and the pricing, growth-story, and Services-machine sources depend partly on Cybart's estimates. The current wiki evidence is still a small sample of Above Avalon articles and does not provide a full history of later product, pricing, forum, audience, or analytical-track-record changes.
