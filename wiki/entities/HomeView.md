## Overview
[[HomeView]] is Apple's 2026 shared household display and smart-home controller, presented by [[BenThompson]] as a potentially useful interface whose value depends on integration reliability, privacy, and [[Siri]].

## Current Profile
The source identifies five credible roles for HomeView: an always-visible household information board, touch control for thermostats, a doorbell screen, camera access, and hands-free information while cooking. Its multi-user design uses on-device facial recognition and recent unlocked-iPhone presence to show personal information. The device is intended to work with Apple's Home Collection accessories and broader Apple Home or Matter ecosystem, but the source says it was delayed for nearly two years while revised Siri software lagged. Thompson therefore treats HomeView as a strong hardware-and-integration idea exposed to an unusually weak primary interface: home fixtures last for years, and choosing HomeView can mean choosing Siri over faster-moving agent alternatives.

## Key Characteristics
- Persistent shared screen for household information, entertainment, and home control.
- Supports multi-user personal context through facial recognition and nearby recent iPhone authentication.
- Designed around voice-first Siri interaction while retaining a touchscreen.
- Integrates with cameras, thermostats, doorbells, locks, and other Apple Home accessories.
- Creates a long-lived ecosystem commitment because mounted displays and home infrastructure are replaced infrequently.

## Evidence
- Household utility: [[do-you-trust-apple]] derives five use cases from the author's long-running dashboard, thermostat, doorbell, camera, and kitchen experiences.
- Multi-user behavior: [[do-you-trust-apple]] says facial recognition and recent unlocked-iPhone presence support personal household information.
- Voice dependency: [[do-you-trust-apple]] reports that HomeView was conceived around revised Siri and repeatedly delayed while that assistant was unfinished.
- Commitment cost: [[do-you-trust-apple]] argues that wall mounting and integration with long-lived home fixtures increase both switching cost and AI opportunity cost.

## Qualifications
The page is based on one launch-period strategy article rather than hands-on reliability tests, adoption data, teardown evidence, accessibility assessment, or long-term support history. The author finds the product compelling but does not consider himself a normal target customer, and the source's description of delays and warehoused hardware relies on quoted reporting.
