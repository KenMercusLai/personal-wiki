## Overview
[[MarcBenioff]] is represented as the [[Salesforce]] founder-author explaining how the company won its first customers by treating them as product partners.

## Current Profile
Benioff's account links early selling with product learning. He describes using an existing relationship to recruit [[BlueMartiniSoftware]], targeting pioneers who could tolerate the trust risk of hosted CRM, giving pilot customers free access, and delaying a dedicated sales hire until the service showed measurable value. His recommended customer loop combines direct contact, request tracking, rapid response, satisfaction checking, and observation of actual use.

## Key Characteristics
- Founder-practitioner narrating Salesforce's early customer acquisition.
- Used personal trust and a prospect's urgent operating need to secure the first customer.
- Framed pilot users as design partners whose experience informed engineering.
- Connected free product experience and flexible monthly payment to paid conversion.

## Evidence
- First customer: [[marc-benioff-win-customers-by-treating-them-like-partners]] says Benioff approached Blue Martini founder Monte Zweben, a company founder in whom he had previously invested, with hosted CRM that addressed the sales team's spreadsheet-based workflow.
- Pioneer targeting: [[marc-benioff-win-customers-by-treating-them-like-partners]] says Salesforce focused on prospects willing to accept the novelty and perceived data risk of the service.
- Partner loop: [[marc-benioff-win-customers-by-treating-them-like-partners]] describes frequent customer contact, tracked requests, rapid delivery, follow-up, and attention to use.
- Commercial transition: [[marc-benioff-win-customers-by-treating-them-like-partners]] says the company added its first dedicated salesperson after measurable value emerged and then converted free pilots to monthly payment.
