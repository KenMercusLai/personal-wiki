## Overview
[[BlueMartiniSoftware]] is represented as [[Salesforce]]'s first customer and an early design partner in [[MarcBenioff]]'s account.

## Current Profile
Blue Martini's sales team was using spreadsheets and wanted CRM without the cost and implementation complexity of traditional enterprise software. After founder Monte Zweben discussed Salesforce with the end users, the company adopted the hosted service in August 1999; Benioff reports that it was running within two weeks and later became a paying customer.

## Key Characteristics
- Early software company with a spreadsheet-based sales workflow.
- Needed CRM without a large upfront enterprise-software investment.
- Salesforce's first customer and an early source of product feedback.
- Free pilot that later converted to payment.

## Evidence
- Customer problem: [[marc-benioff-win-customers-by-treating-them-like-partners]] says Blue Martini needed a CRM system but could not afford traditional enterprise software or divert its sales team into a complex implementation.
- Adoption: [[marc-benioff-win-customers-by-treating-them-like-partners]] reports that end users embraced the proposal and the service went live within two weeks in August 1999.
- Conversion: [[marc-benioff-win-customers-by-treating-them-like-partners]] says Blue Martini began paying after the free pilot demonstrated value.

## Qualifications
All details come from Benioff's retrospective. The source does not include Blue Martini's own account, adoption measures, retention data, security assessment, contract terms, or quantified business outcome, and its note that the company later became Escalate Retail is not developed further.
