## Overview
[[AspenInsurance]] is a diversified insurance and reinsurance provider used in the source as a case where [[CoCEOLeadership]] helped manage global and product complexity.

## Current Profile
The article says Aspen had $9.3 billion in assets and more than 670 employees in eight countries. It had already been led by co-CEOs for about a year and a half when it chose to continue the model with [[RupertVillers]] and [[MarioVitale]].

## Key Characteristics
- Diversified across insurance and reinsurance products.
- Operated across multiple countries and time zones.
- Chose co-CEOs because one person struggled to maintain sufficient coverage and control.
- Relied on complementary skills, overlapping trust, plain speech, and divided responsibility.

## Evidence
- Scale and spread: [[co-ceos-are-two-better-than-one]] describes Aspen as having $9.3 billion in assets, 670+ employees, and operations in eight countries.
- Complexity rationale: [[co-ceos-are-two-better-than-one]] quotes Villers explaining that geography, products, and time zones made single-person coverage difficult.
- Leadership fit: [[co-ceos-are-two-better-than-one]] presents Villers and Vitale as complementary leaders who divide responsibility.

## Qualifications
The source presents Aspen as a positive case and does not give independent performance data, employee views, board details, or later outcomes.
