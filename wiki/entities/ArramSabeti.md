## Overview
[[ArramSabeti]] is the founder and then-CEO of [[ZeroCater]], represented through his first-person account of using [[JustinTV]] as a startup apprenticeship, validating managed office meals manually, and continuing after becoming a solo, non-technical founder.

## Current Profile
Sabeti describes moving to the Bay Area at 21 without a degree, marketable skills, or a company idea. After briefly working at Ben & Jerry's, he joined Justin.tv in a deliberately broad role, where ordering team lunches exposed an acute office-management problem and relationships later supplied advice and investment. He built ZeroCater first through direct selling and spreadsheet-based operations, sought software only after those processes broke under growth, completed [[YCombinator]], and reportedly raised $1.5 million after his technical cofounder left.

## Key Characteristics
- Pursued startup work as an apprenticeship before having a specific company idea.
- Found a business problem through direct responsibility for an unpleasant recurring workflow.
- Sold continuously and opportunistically rather than waiting for a polished product or formal channel.
- Used manual fulfillment to learn the service before investing in custom software.
- Continued through cofounder loss and investor concerns about a solo, non-technical founder.
- Interprets his outcome primarily through determination and willingness to endure pain.

## Evidence
- Apprenticeship: [[from-selling-scoops-of-ice-cream-to-founding-zerocater-techcrunch]] says Sabeti tested releases, handled applicants, ordered lunch, learned beside Justin.tv's founders, and built relationships that later helped ZeroCater.
- Problem discovery: [[from-selling-scoops-of-ice-cream-to-founding-zerocater-techcrunch]] traces the company idea to another office manager asking for his restaurant list and showing relief when he offered to take over meals.
- Founder sales: [[from-selling-scoops-of-ice-cream-to-founding-zerocater-techcrunch]] describes referrals, social lead capture, reversed inbound pitches, and a chance Verizon conversation that became an order.
- Manual operation: [[from-selling-scoops-of-ice-cream-to-founding-zerocater-techcrunch]] says the company initially consisted of an inbox and spreadsheet, later reaching 500 columns and 20 weekly billing hours.
- Resilience: [[from-selling-scoops-of-ice-cream-to-founding-zerocater-techcrunch]] reports that Sabeti presented alone at Demo Day after his technical cofounder quit, then raised $1.5 million.
- Determination thesis: [[from-selling-scoops-of-ice-cream-to-founding-zerocater-techcrunch]] concludes that sufficient grit can overcome effectively any upper bound on achievement.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one celebratory first-person 2013 essay. Its customer, financing, ranking, and scale claims are not independently verified in the source, and its causal account gives determination more attention than relationships, timing, market conditions, capital, accelerator selection, prior skill accumulation, or luck. The examples of other successful people are selected after the fact and do not reveal equally persistent failures. Sabeti's experience therefore supports specific operating practices more strongly than the universal claim that grit is sufficient for global-scale success.
