## Overview
[[TheSpeedOfTrust]] is a trust-and-leadership book cited by Rick Manelius as support for the claim that high-trust relationships improve efficiency and effectiveness.

## Current Profile
Within this wiki, The Speed of Trust functions as a secondary support reference inside the [[AssumePositiveIntent]] source. Manelius invokes it to argue that trust is not merely a moral preference but a practical accelerator: when people trust a recommendation, partner, or counterpart, they spend less time checking, second-guessing, and contractually guarding every action.

## Key Characteristics
- Presented as a book about qualitative and quantitative gains from high-trust situations.
- Used to support the efficiency argument for extending trust early.
- Represented indirectly through Manelius's article rather than through a direct book ingest.

## Evidence
- Trust gains: [[assume-positive-intent-rick-manelius]] says the book provides evidence that high-trust situations produce efficiency and effectiveness gains.
- Recommendation example: [[assume-positive-intent-rick-manelius]] contrasts hours of research under low trust with quick decisions based on a trusted recommendation.
- Relationship path: [[assume-positive-intent-rick-manelius]] argues that extending trust first is one way to reach the gains described by the book.

## Qualifications
Because the book itself has not been ingested, this page should not be treated as a full synthesis of Covey's argument. It records the role the book plays in Manelius's article.
