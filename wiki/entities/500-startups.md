Source: [99 VC Problems But A Batch Ain't One: Why Portfolio Size Matters For Returns]({{< relref "/wiki/sources/99-vc-problems-but-a-batch-aint-one.md" >}})

500 Startups is the venture investment firm used as the source's case context. The article says that, over the prior five years, 500 Startups had invested in more than 1,000 companies and had seen only a small number produce exceptional outcomes.

The source uses 500 Startups' portfolio experience to argue for larger VC portfolios. It estimates that most investments fail to exit or return less than invested capital, while a small tail of centaur and unicorn companies drives fund returns. The essay's closing joke about portfolios possibly needing up to 500 startups links the firm's name to its proposed early-stage portfolio size.

Twilio and Credit Karma appear in the source as examples of companies from 500 Startups portfolios that performed extremely well, illustrating the kind of rare outliers the article says VC funds need to capture.
