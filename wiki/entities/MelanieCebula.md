## Overview
[[MelanieCebula]] is an Airbnb software engineer represented through a 2017 conference talk on the company's monolith-to-microservices transition.

## Current Profile
Cebula's reported guidance treats microservices as a scale-dependent organizational and platform investment, not a default starting architecture. Her ten lessons connect monolith-first development, engineer production ownership, SysOps training, configuration and alerts as code, standardized monitoring, automated delivery, tolerance for poor first services, and product-team participation in later extraction.

## Key Characteristics
- Advocates starting with a monolith rather than pre-optimizing for distributed services.
- Connects DevOps culture to supported production ownership and open operational training.
- Treats standardized automation and service creation as prerequisites for service scale.

## Evidence
- Architecture timing: [[tc-currie-airbnbs-10-takeaways-from-moving-to-microservices]] reports Cebula advising teams to begin with a monolith and invest in service infrastructure only as growth creates a credible need.
- Operational ownership: [[tc-currie-airbnbs-10-takeaways-from-moving-to-microservices]] attributes Airbnb's SysOps training and “Democratic Deploys” model to her account.
- Platform standardization: [[tc-currie-airbnbs-10-takeaways-from-moving-to-microservices]] reports her emphasis on configuration, metrics, alerts, testing, delivery, and service creation through code and defaults.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from one secondary report of one 2017 conference talk. It does not establish Cebula's broader work, quantify the claimed outcomes, or independently verify Airbnb's scale and practice descriptions.
