## Overview
[[ChristianTietze]] is a writer whose 2013 practitioner essays explain Zettelkasten as durable external memory, an interconnected communication partner, and a workflow for processing paper reading notes into digital Zettel.

## Current Profile
Across the sources, Tietze first distinguishes the permanent-storage role of notes from the discovery role of their connections, then develops Zettelkasten as a linked communication partner and demonstrates a personal reading workflow organized as collect, process, and write. His essays translate surprise, information, and relational complexity into practical advice about broad topic coverage, loose filing, stable timestamp IDs, direct links, keywords, and search. The worked example uses paper slips to avoid switching mental modes while reading, then restores order by clustering the slips around reading intent before writing linked overview and detail notes. His formulation of atomicity emphasizes separating concerns so ideas can be reused and reconnected rather than reducing every note to the smallest possible fragment. In a comment on Fast's 2015 essay, he adds a repair loop for unclear older notes: rephrase them, add a short introduction, reconsider titles and tags, and connect them to newer material.

## Key Characteristics
- Separates low-friction paper capture from later digital processing.
- Organizes reading material by purpose and topic rather than preserving source order.
- Uses overview notes and linked detail branches to represent both synthesis and prerequisites.
- Frames note atomicity as concern separation in service of reuse.
- Treats connections between new and existing notes as a source of reinterpretation and creativity.
- Frames a sufficiently interconnected note system as a queryable partner that can return unexpected associations.
- Separates durable storage from interconnection as a discovery mechanism, while repairing resurfacing legacy notes through better prose, orientation, retrieval cues, and newer connections.

## Qualifications
The profile is bounded to three 2013 practitioner essays and one comment on a 2015 essay. They describe Tietze's framing and workflow at that time, not a comparative study, a complete biography, or evidence that permanent storage, paper capture, dense linking, loose filing, legacy-note repair, or this specific processing sequence improves cognition or works best for other readers.
