## Overview
[[ChristianTietze]] is the author of the source's practitioner account of processing paper reading notes into digital Zettel.

## Current Profile
In the source, Tietze presents a personal reading workflow organized as collect, process, and write. He uses paper slips to avoid switching mental modes while reading, then restores order by clustering the slips around reading intent before writing linked overview and detail notes. His formulation of atomicity emphasizes separating concerns so ideas can be reused and reconnected rather than reducing every note to the smallest possible fragment.

## Key Characteristics
- Separates low-friction paper capture from later digital processing.
- Organizes reading material by purpose and topic rather than preserving source order.
- Uses overview notes and linked detail branches to represent both synthesis and prerequisites.
- Frames note atomicity as concern separation in service of reuse.
- Treats connections between new and existing notes as a source of reinterpretation and creativity.

## Qualifications
The profile is bounded to one 2013 practitioner essay. It describes Tietze's workflow at that time, not a comparative study, a complete biography, or evidence that paper capture and this specific processing sequence work best for other readers.
