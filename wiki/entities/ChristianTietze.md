## Overview
[[ChristianTietze]] is the author of the source's practitioner account of processing paper reading notes into digital Zettel.

## Current Profile
Across the sources, Tietze introduces Zettelkasten as a linked communication partner and then demonstrates a personal reading workflow organized as collect, process, and write. The introductory essay translates Luhmann's surprise, information, and complexity criteria into practical advice about loose filing, stable IDs, direct links, keywords, and search. The worked example uses paper slips to avoid switching mental modes while reading, then restores order by clustering the slips around reading intent before writing linked overview and detail notes. His later formulation of atomicity emphasizes separating concerns so ideas can be reused and reconnected rather than reducing every note to the smallest possible fragment.

## Key Characteristics
- Separates low-friction paper capture from later digital processing.
- Organizes reading material by purpose and topic rather than preserving source order.
- Uses overview notes and linked detail branches to represent both synthesis and prerequisites.
- Frames note atomicity as concern separation in service of reuse.
- Treats connections between new and existing notes as a source of reinterpretation and creativity.
- Frames a sufficiently interconnected note system as a queryable partner that can return unexpected associations.

## Qualifications
The profile is bounded to two 2013 practitioner essays. They describe Tietze's framing and workflow at that time, not a comparative study, a complete biography, or evidence that paper capture, dense linking, loose filing, or this specific processing sequence works best for other readers.
