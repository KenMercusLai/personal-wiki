## Overview
[[NirEyal]] is presented as the author of *Hooked* and a product-design writer who systematizes habit-forming products while arguing that unfamiliar innovations need recognizable gateways into user behavior.

## Current Profile
Eyal openly teaches designers to build repeated behavior but distinguishes persuasion, which works with an existing want, from coercion. He points to beneficial health applications, says he refuses pornography and gambling clients, and offers an ethical rubric, while conceding that nothing prevents harmful application. He argues that dissatisfied users can create a self-regulatory incentive and supports warnings for patterns resembling severe compulsion. [[NatashaSchull]] and [[MichaelSchulson]] qualify his defense by arguing that design can accelerate and amplify what users initially wanted far beyond their intended duration.

His [[CaliforniaRollRule]] adds an adoption-oriented complement: a new product should introduce its unfamiliar value through a form, ingredient, or routine users already understand. This lowers an initial learning barrier but does not answer the ethical question of whether the resulting behavior serves the user.

## Key Characteristics
- Defines a four-stage habit loop of trigger, action, variable reward, and escalating investment.
- Treats repeated product use as a designable cycle influenced by reinforcement principles.
- Distinguishes persuasion from coercion on the basis of whether users already want the outcome.
- Presents beneficial health behavior as an ethical application and refuses some harmful domains.
- Concedes that an ethical rubric cannot prevent designers from applying the model harmfully.
- Supports platform detection and warning for unusually compulsive usage patterns.
- Proposes familiar mental models as gateways into genuinely new products and behaviors.

## Qualifications
The ethical profile comes from Schulson's 2015 interview-based representation, while the adoption rule comes from Eyal’s own practitioner essay; neither is a complete reading of *Hooked* or later work. The sources do not test the habit model, prove that familiarity caused adoption in the selected examples, or determine when persuasion becomes coercive. Existing desire does not settle whether amplified use is informed, welfare-enhancing, or within the user's intended bounds.

## What Changed
- Added the California Roll Rule as an adoption heuristic alongside the habit-design model.
- Distinguished familiar entry scaffolding from evidence that a product creates value or benefits its user.
