## Overview
[[NirEyal]] is presented as the author of *Hooked* and a Silicon Valley consultant who systematizes habit-forming products through a trigger, action, variable-reward, and investment cycle.

## Current Profile
In the source, Eyal openly teaches designers to build repeated behavior but distinguishes persuasion, which works with an existing want, from coercion. He points to beneficial health applications, says he refuses pornography and gambling clients, and offers an ethical rubric, while conceding that nothing prevents harmful application. He argues that dissatisfied users can create a self-regulatory incentive and supports warnings for patterns resembling severe compulsion. [[NatashaSchull]] and [[MichaelSchulson]] qualify his defense by arguing that design can accelerate and amplify what users initially wanted far beyond their intended duration.

## Key Characteristics
- Defines a four-stage habit loop of trigger, action, variable reward, and escalating investment.
- Treats repeated product use as a designable cycle influenced by reinforcement principles.
- Distinguishes persuasion from coercion on the basis of whether users already want the outcome.
- Presents beneficial health behavior as an ethical application and refuses some harmful domains.
- Concedes that an ethical rubric cannot prevent designers from applying the model harmfully.
- Supports platform detection and warning for unusually compulsive usage patterns.

## Qualifications
This is Schulson's 2015 interview-based representation of Eyal, not a complete reading of *Hooked* or later work. The source does not test the model, establish that every stage is necessary, or determine when persuasion becomes coercive. Existing desire does not settle whether amplified use is informed, welfare-enhancing, or within the user's intended bounds.
