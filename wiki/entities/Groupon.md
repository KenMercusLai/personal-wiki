## Overview
[[Groupon]] is an online deals company represented in the sources through two sides of the same mechanism: consumer sharing, referrals, and daily email can distribute bargains rapidly, while the discount and platform share can impose severe acquisition and fulfillment costs on participating merchants.

## Current Profile
One source frames Groupon as a company that made bargain discovery socially shareable: post-purchase sharing, friend referrals, multiple purchases, and daily emails reinforced distribution. A restaurant consultant's 2013 critique examines the merchant side. In its illustrative $40-face-value voucher, the customer pays $20 and the restaurant receives $9.30 after Groupon's stated share and processing fees; estimated food cost alone is $10–$16. The model can therefore buy reach by making the initial redemption loss-making, and it works for the merchant only if unused vouchers, incremental capacity, add-on purchases, or profitable repeat visits recover that cost.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines deeply discounted merchant offers with a platform revenue share.
- Turns post-purchase sharing, friend referrals, multiple purchases, and daily deal emails into consumer distribution.
- Can make a redeemed restaurant promotion loss-making before labor and opportunity cost when merchant proceeds fall below direct food cost.
- Depends on merchant-side incrementality and customer quality, not merely voucher sales or consumer reach.
- Was reported by the growth-tactics source as growing 228% in one year, without evidence that this translated into healthy merchant economics.

## Evidence
- Consumer distribution: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says customers can share offers after a purchase and lists referrals, multiple purchases, and daily emails as growth tactics.
- Merchant transaction economics: [[groupon-a-bad-deal-for-restaurants-and-everyone-else-including-groupon-odell-restaurant-consultings-blog]] calculates that a restaurant receives $9.30 for a $40-face-value redemption whose food cost is estimated at $10–$16.
- Acquisition quality: [[groupon-a-bad-deal-for-restaurants-and-everyone-else-including-groupon-odell-restaurant-consultings-blog]] argues that deal customers may follow the next discount rather than return to the restaurant at full price.

## Qualifications
The positive growth source is a retrospective tactic catalog and does not analyze Groupon's unit economics, merchant outcomes, retention, or later trajectory. The critical source is a restaurant consultant's opinion supported by one simplified example and several reader anecdotes, not Groupon contract data or representative merchant cohorts. It omits voucher breakage, add-on purchases, negotiated terms, tips, unused capacity, repeat visits, and customer incrementality. Its 2013 prediction that Groupon would not recover in the US should be treated as a dated forecast, not established by the article itself.

## What Changed
- Added the merchant side of the platform: discount depth, revenue share, fulfillment cost, and customer quality can make reach economically destructive.
- Recast consumer growth and merchant viability as coupled rather than treating distribution volume alone as success.
