## Overview
[[Groupon]] is an online deals company represented through consumer distribution loops, contested merchant economics, and founder [[AndrewMason]]'s later claim that an exceptional startup outcome concentrated employee wealth sharply.

## Current Profile
One source frames Groupon as a company that made bargain discovery socially shareable: post-purchase sharing, friend referrals, multiple purchases, and daily emails reinforced distribution. A restaurant consultant's 2013 critique examines the merchant side. In its illustrative $40-face-value voucher, the customer pays $20 and the restaurant receives $9.30 after Groupon's stated share and processing fees; estimated food cost alone is $10–$16. The model can therefore buy reach by making the initial redemption loss-making, and it works for the merchant only if unused vouchers, incremental capacity, add-on purchases, or profitable repeat visits recover that cost.

Mason's 2015 retrospective adds an internal distributional concern. He says Groupon's success gave founders wealth beyond ordinary consumption, made a small group of early employees financially independent, and left most employees with a useful but not life-changing bonus. By the time this pattern was visible, he judged a large corrective employee grant unlikely to win board support, while personal equity transfers created adverse tax consequences. The account motivates his later Progressive Equity proposal but does not supply Groupon payout data or independently establish the distribution.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines deeply discounted merchant offers with a platform revenue share.
- Turns post-purchase sharing, friend referrals, multiple purchases, and daily deal emails into consumer distribution.
- Can make a redeemed restaurant promotion loss-making before labor and opportunity cost when merchant proceeds fall below direct food cost.
- Depends on merchant-side incrementality and customer quality, not merely voucher sales or consumer reach.
- Was reported by the growth-tactics source as growing 228% in one year, without evidence that this translated into healthy merchant economics.
- Is cited by its former founder as a case where exceptional company value produced sharply unequal employee wealth and where retrospective redistribution was difficult.

## Evidence
- Consumer distribution: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says customers can share offers after a purchase and lists referrals, multiple purchases, and daily emails as growth tactics.
- Merchant transaction economics: [[groupon-a-bad-deal-for-restaurants-and-everyone-else-including-groupon-odell-restaurant-consultings-blog]] calculates that a restaurant receives $9.30 for a $40-face-value redemption whose food cost is estimated at $10–$16.
- Acquisition quality: [[groupon-a-bad-deal-for-restaurants-and-everyone-else-including-groupon-odell-restaurant-consultings-blog]] argues that deal customers may follow the next discount rather than return to the restaurant at full price.
- Employee outcomes: [[introducing-progressive-equity-detour-blog-medium]] says founders, a few early employees, and the wider workforce received qualitatively different levels of wealth, and that later grant or personal-transfer remedies were impractical.

## Qualifications
The positive growth source is a retrospective tactic catalog and does not analyze Groupon's unit economics, merchant outcomes, retention, or later trajectory. The critical source is a restaurant consultant's opinion supported by one simplified example and several reader anecdotes, not Groupon contract data or representative merchant cohorts. It omits voucher breakage, add-on purchases, negotiated terms, tips, unused capacity, repeat visits, and customer incrementality. Its 2013 prediction that Groupon would not recover in the US should be treated as a dated forecast, not established by the article itself. Mason's employee-outcome account is a qualitative founder recollection with no cap table, payout distribution, tax calculation, employee testimony, or board record; it explains his later policy motivation but cannot measure Groupon's workforce-wide outcome.
