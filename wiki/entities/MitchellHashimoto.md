## Overview
[[MitchellHashimoto]] is a cofounder of [[HashiCorp]] represented here through his first-person retrospective on the company's early business-banking mistakes.

## Current Profile
Hashimoto describes opening HashiCorp's first [[Chase]] business account at age 22 with a $20,000 personal loan, then continuing to treat banking as a passive utility as venture funding lifted the balance to roughly $35 million. He later supported a finance executive's move to Silicon Valley Bank but did not close the old Chase account, allowing legacy customer payments and recurring fraudulent transfers to persist until an internal audit. His account is unusually self-critical: he treats the episode as evidence of founder naivety, weak engagement, and delayed delegation rather than proof that Chase could not have served the company well.

## Key Characteristics
- Founded and initially handled banking for HashiCorp as a young, first-time founder.
- Treated banks as interchangeable utilities and repeatedly declined relationship-manager engagement.
- Delegated financial operations after hiring a vice president of finance as the company scaled.
- Accepts responsibility for leaving an obsolete business account open and poorly monitored.
- Distinguishes his direct experience from second-hand branch claims and avoids overgeneralizing from the episode.

## Evidence
- Early account ownership: [[my-startup-banking-story]] says Hashimoto opened the account with a $20,000 personal loan when he was 22.
- Relationship disengagement: [[my-startup-banking-story]] recounts repeated calls after large funding deposits that he quickly dismissed.
- Delegation: [[my-startup-banking-story]] says a newly hired VP of Finance identified the concentrated cash position and proposed a safer banking setup.
- Account-lifecycle failure: [[my-startup-banking-story]] says Hashimoto postponed the in-person closure after moving the balance, and the account remained open for years.
- Reflective limits: [[my-startup-banking-story]] labels the teller's account as unverified second-hand information and says Chase may have been an excellent partner had he engaged with it.

## Qualifications

It does not establish Hashimoto's complete role at HashiCorp, the company's broader financial controls, or the exact cause of the fraud. Dollar amounts and conversations are recalled rather than supported by statements, audit records, or independent testimony.
