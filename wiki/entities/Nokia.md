## Overview
[[Nokia]] appears in the wiki as a once-dominant handset maker that understood the smartphone ecosystem threat but damaged its transition by publicly abandoning existing platforms before replacement Windows Phone devices were ready.

## Current Profile
The source treats Nokia as more complicated than BlackBerry or Palm. Nokia still had design, supply-chain, and carrier strengths, and Stephen Elop's memo correctly described the shift from device competition to ecosystem competition. Gassee argues the error was not ignorance but execution: choosing [[WindowsPhone]] over [[Android]] and announcing the move too early created an [[OsborneEffect]], reducing demand for existing Symbian and related devices before the new product line could replace them.

## Key Characteristics
- Former high-volume handset incumbent, described as selling about 100 million handsets per quarter near its peak.
- Had multiple strained platform paths, including Symbian and Linux derivatives.
- Understood the smartphone competition as a war of ecosystems.
- Chose [[WindowsPhone]] rather than Android as its replacement ecosystem.
- Suffered an [[OsborneEffect]] when the transition was announced before replacement devices were available at scale.

## Evidence
- Peak scale: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] says Nokia peaked around 2010 at about 100 million handsets per quarter.
- Platform fragmentation: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] cites multiple Symbian versions and Linux derivatives as unsustainable.
- Ecosystem recognition: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] quotes Elop's memo saying device competition had become a war of ecosystems.
- Osborne effect: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] argues the public plunge away from existing platforms caused carriers, distributors, and customers to stop buying no-future Nokia phones.
- Acquisition and write-off: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] says Microsoft bought Nokia's devices business in 2013 and wrote off more than the purchase price in 2015.

## Qualifications
The source is an opinionated postmortem and does not fully analyze Nokia's internal platform politics, MeeGo/Maemo technical state, carrier incentives, regional pricing, patents, mapping assets, or Microsoft negotiations. Its counterfactual that Nokia could have secretly built Android phones is plausible within the source but not proven.

## What Changed
- Created Nokia as the ecosystem-aware but badly communicated transition case.

## Relationships
- [[WindowsPhone]] - Nokia's selected replacement smartphone ecosystem.
- [[Android]] - Gassee's preferred counterfactual platform for Nokia.
- [[OsborneEffect]] - transition-communication failure mode in the Nokia case.
- [[SmartphonePlatformDisruption]] - Nokia shows that understanding the threat does not guarantee good execution.
- [[MobileEcosystem]] - Nokia's handset scale was overtaken by app-centered ecosystems.
- [[Microsoft]] - buyer of Nokia's devices business and provider of Windows Phone.
