## Overview
[[Photoshop]] is the [[Adobe]] image-editing product used in the source to explain why packaged productivity software can misprice users and why subscriptions can better match value over time.

## Current Profile
The article uses Photoshop as a deliberately extreme productivity-app example. A casual photo editor may find a large one-time purchase too expensive, a professional designer may receive career-scale value from the same purchase, and a student may become much more valuable to Adobe only after learning the tool. Subscription pricing is presented as a better fit because it lowers entry friction while letting Adobe participate in long-term value creation.

## Key Characteristics
- Has widely varying value across casual, professional, and aspiring users.
- Carries a learning curve that can make early value lower than later value.
- Can become indispensable to professionals whose income depends on it.
- Illustrates the packaged-software mismatch between upfront price and long-term user value.

## Evidence
- Casual-user example: [[adobes-subscription-model-why-platform-owners-should-care-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] describes a user for whom a photo-editing app may be worth much less than Photoshop's packaged price.
- Professional-user example: [[adobes-subscription-model-why-platform-owners-should-care-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] describes a graphic designer who uses Photoshop daily and receives career-scale value.
- Student-user example: [[adobes-subscription-model-why-platform-owners-should-care-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] describes an aspiring photographer whose value grows as proficiency develops.
- Subscription fit: [[adobes-subscription-model-why-platform-owners-should-care-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] says subscriptions allow users to start more easily while Adobe earns over time.

## Qualifications
The source uses Photoshop as an economic illustration, not as a full product review. It does not evaluate Photoshop's feature set, competitors, customer satisfaction, or later subscription terms.
