## Overview
[[MyTabata]] is a mobile interval timer used in a 2016 practitioner essay as a case of strong task usability paired with visually weak interface design.

## Current Profile
The source presents My Tabata as a narrowly focused aid for workouts that alternate 20 seconds of high-intensity activity with 10 seconds of rest. Its session screen makes the first action immediate, accepts a tap anywhere to start or pause, uses audio near interval boundaries, and combines remaining time with an eight-step progress indicator. A settings menu sits outside the primary session and preserves preferences for later use.

The captured Google Play panel reports a 4.4 average across 4,243 reviews, with a heavily five-star distribution and selected comments praising its single-purpose simplicity. That snapshot supports historical user approval but does not isolate which interface choices caused it or establish the app’s current quality, availability, or accessibility.

## Key Characteristics
- Focuses on timing alternating work and rest intervals for Tabata exercise.
- Uses a one-screen tap-to-start flow with almost no navigation before the primary task.
- Treats the active screen as a large start-and-pause target suited to movement during exercise.
- Uses countdown audio when users may not be looking at the device.
- Shows remaining time, activity state, and position across eight intervals.
- Keeps persistent settings behind a menu and outside the active workout flow.

## Qualifications
The profile is bounded to one favorable 2016 practitioner article and its selected screenshots. It supplies no independent testing, comparison with competing timers, accessibility assessment, retention data, or evidence about later releases. A large tap target can reduce motor demands, but no visible dedicated control or alternative interaction is assessed; sparse navigation can likewise improve focus while making settings or recovery paths harder to discover.
