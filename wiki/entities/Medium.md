## Overview
[[Medium]] is an online publishing platform represented through a 2017 business-model reset and co-founder [[EvWilliams]]'s later reader-funded publishing strategy.

## Current Profile
The earlier source uses Medium's reported 50-person layoff and acknowledged failure to make its business model work as the prompt for a search for value-based creator compensation. Later in 2017, Williams described the chosen direction as a reader-funded, ad-free product: an open paywall combining independent contributors and established publishers, with a Partner Program intended to reward depth and member value rather than advertiser-facing attention. Together the sources show a strategic transition from an unresolved monetization problem to a stated subscription model, not evidence that the model later succeeded.

## Key Characteristics
- A publishing platform built around written ideas and perspectives from many contributors.
- A platform whose early-2017 business-model failure prompted a public search for new creator compensation.
- A subscription product intended to align platform service with paying readers rather than advertisers.
- An open-paywall model that combines independent expertise with stories from established publishers.
- A partner program intended to compensate contributors according to member value and depth.

## Evidence
- Mission and platform role: [[words-still-matter-3-min-read]] says Medium was created to connect minds, deepen understanding, and give ideas from anywhere a welcoming space.
- Business-model reset: [[expert-revenue-models-for-new-media]] reports 50 layoffs and quotes Williams saying Medium was redirecting resources toward a new model based on creator value.
- Reader funding: [[words-still-matter-3-min-read]] presents subscriptions and an ad-free experience as the mechanism for reducing conflict between readers and advertisers.
- Contributor economics: [[words-still-matter-3-min-read]] announces open enrollment in a Partner Program and says earnings should reflect value to members rather than fleeting attention.

## Qualifications
The evidence combines a panel article's brief account of layoffs with a first-party 2017 mission and strategy essay. Neither supplies operating costs, subscriber outcomes, partner-income distribution, content-quality measures, or an independent assessment of whether the reset achieved its stated incentive alignment.
