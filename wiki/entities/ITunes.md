## Overview
[[ITunes]] is Apple's earlier digital media distribution platform, used as the historical music-download model, the content library Apple brought to Samsung televisions, and a product-management case for integrating digital music into mass-market entertainment behavior.

## Current Profile
iTunes is presented as Apple's earlier content distribution breakthrough: the Mac's smaller market share helped Apple persuade the music industry to test digital music sales, and iTunes helped shift consumption from albums to singles. By 2019, however, Cybart says the iTunes paid-download era was over because streaming had made access more important than ownership. iTunes also appears as part of Apple's newer cross-hardware strategy when Apple brought iTunes content to Samsung TVs.

[[CamilleHearst]]'s iTunes case shows that mass-market digital music adoption involved not only catalog and payments but also careful media partnership design: iTunes could become part of fan behavior, but it had to avoid surfacing popularity signals that would spoil or influence American Idol voting.

## Key Characteristics
- Historical Apple digital media store and distribution platform.
- Helped move recorded music from album purchases toward single-track digital sales.
- Represents the paid-download era displaced by music streaming.
- Reappears in the article as Apple content made available on Samsung televisions.
- Became a mass-market music behavior through integrations and partnerships, including American Idol in the SVPG source.
- Required product decisions that balanced engagement, discovery, and partner fairness constraints.

## Evidence
- Download-era role: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] says iTunes helped move the music industry from albums to singles.
- Strategic contrast: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] says the iTunes era of paid downloads was over as music shifted from ownership to streaming access.
- Non-Apple TV support: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] names Apple's move to bring iTunes content to Samsung TVs as part of its content distribution strategy.
- Mass-market push: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says Apple's American Idol relationship was one effort to expose an ideal target market to iTunes and digital music.
- Voting constraint: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says iTunes normally showed trending music, but the Idol integration had to avoid influencing or revealing voting results.
- Product integration: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says Hearst worked on integrations to make iTunes a component of fan life while complementing the show experience.

## Qualifications
The sources use iTunes as historical, strategic, and product-management context, not as a complete history of the product, store, rights negotiations, device syncing, Windows support, podcasts, later app transitions, or the full American Idol business deal.
