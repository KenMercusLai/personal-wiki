## Overview
[[ITunes]] is Apple's earlier digital media distribution platform in the source, used as both the historical music-download model and the content library Apple brought to Samsung televisions.

## Current Profile
The article presents iTunes as Apple's earlier content distribution breakthrough: the Mac's smaller market share helped Apple persuade the music industry to test digital music sales, and iTunes helped shift consumption from albums to singles. By 2019, however, Cybart says the iTunes paid-download era was over because streaming had made access more important than ownership. iTunes also appears as part of Apple's newer cross-hardware strategy when Apple brought iTunes content to Samsung TVs.

## Key Characteristics
- Historical Apple digital media store and distribution platform.
- Helped move recorded music from album purchases toward single-track digital sales.
- Represents the paid-download era displaced by music streaming.
- Reappears in the article as Apple content made available on Samsung televisions.

## Evidence
- Download-era role: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] says iTunes helped move the music industry from albums to singles.
- Strategic contrast: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] says the iTunes era of paid downloads was over as music shifted from ownership to streaming access.
- Non-Apple TV support: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] names Apple's move to bring iTunes content to Samsung TVs as part of its content distribution strategy.

## Qualifications
The source uses iTunes as historical and strategic context, not as a complete history of the product, store, rights negotiations, device syncing, Windows support, podcasts, or later app transitions.
