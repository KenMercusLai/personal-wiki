## Overview
[[SaraMauskopf]] is presented in a Livingly profile as a mother and technology executive who left employment to co-found [[Winnie]], a family information and local-recommendation product, with [[AnneHalsall]].

## Current Profile
Mauskopf describes motherhood as both problem discovery and founder motivation: fragmented answers about childcare, infant behavior, and family-friendly places convinced her that parents needed a centralized, locally relevant resource. She credits previous work at Postmates, [[Twitter]], [[Google]], and [[YouTube]], as well as [[MIT]], family support, and [[SiliconValley]] relationships, with helping her recruit a team and raise capital.

Her husband's cancer diagnosis five months after she began the company tested that arrangement. She initially expected to step away indefinitely, but two weeks of strong team execution changed her view of the company's dependence on her. Family help and Winnie itself supported her parenting routines, while time at work gave her valued separation from caregiving. She explicitly rejects a self-made interpretation by naming privilege, upbringing, education, her spouse, professional relationships, and persistence as interacting contributors.

## Key Characteristics
- Turned first-hand parenting needs into the founding rationale for a family information product.
- Frames parenthood as a source of entrepreneurial confidence and determination rather than only a competing demand.
- Credits team, co-founder, investors, engineers, family, education, and professional relationships alongside her own effort.
- Treats a team's ability to operate during founder absence as evidence of organizational strength.
- Values meaningful work and family health over a precisely specified long-term title or career path.

- Work and caregiving: [[inspiring-women-meet-the-co-founder-of-winnie-a-yelp-type-app-for-parents-inspiring-women-livingly]] describes using Winnie for outings and restaurants while solo parenting and says time at work helped her return to parenting with greater focus.

## Qualifications
The current profile rests on one celebratory, edited interview and primarily on Mauskopf's own retrospective. It does not independently verify Winnie's scale, financing, team performance, user outcomes, or later trajectory, and it provides no employee, co-founder, user, investor, or family perspective. The source's company and family circumstances are historical, and its missing publication date limits precise chronology. Mauskopf's acknowledgment of privilege and support is material: her account should not be generalized into a claim that every parent can combine company formation and caregiving under comparable conditions.
