## Overview
[[VisionPro]] is [[Apple]]'s head-mounted spatial-computing device, represented here through its ability to create a strong sense of presence and through criticism that Apple's immersive-video production choices undermine that advantage.

## Current Profile
The source treats Vision Pro's strongest live-event use case as a remote seat rather than a television screen worn on the head. Stable scorer's-table and baseline views of an NBA game reportedly conveyed player scale, speed, and physicality in a way ordinary video could not. That advantage depended on remaining spatially situated: producer-directed camera changes required the viewer to regain orientation and repeatedly broke the feeling of being courtside.

This makes content supply and production design part of the product's value. A $3,499 headset can feel inexpensive relative to broad on-demand access to premium live experiences, but expensive and uncomfortable when it merely reproduces a conventional broadcast. The source therefore ties recommendation less to weight, battery, or display capability than to whether Apple supplies abundant, presence-first media.

## Key Characteristics
- Creates a convincing sense of scale and physical presence from well-placed immersive cameras.
- Makes abrupt viewpoint changes more disruptive than they are on a two-dimensional screen.
- Depends on scarce purpose-made immersive content to demonstrate differentiated value.
- Can expose venue sound, visible scoreboards, warmups, and continuous action without conventional broadcast layers.
- Remains difficult to recommend in this account because limited coverage and television-style production suppress its strongest use case.

## Evidence
- Presence and scale: [[apple-you-still-dont-understand-the-vision-pro]] says the scorer's-table view reproduced the height, speed, and power of NBA players, while the baseline view conveyed physicality.
- Spatial disruption: [[apple-you-still-dont-understand-the-vision-pro]] reports that cuts among sideline and baseline positions forced repeated reorientation and felt physically uncomfortable.
- Content constraint: [[apple-you-still-dont-understand-the-vision-pro]] traces sparse releases after launch and notes only six announced immersive Lakers games for the 2025–26 season.
- Value contingency: [[apple-you-still-dont-understand-the-vision-pro]] contrasts a compelling remote ticket to live events with a $3,499 device used as an uncomfortable television.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one enthusiast's first-person viewing of one geographically restricted NBA game plus retrospective comments on several Apple-produced videos. It does not measure representative comfort, accessibility, image quality, willingness to pay, content demand, production cost, rights constraints, device sales, or later improvements. The source's praise of fixed viewpoints does not establish that every sport, event, or viewer should use one unchanging camera.

## What Changed
- Established presence—not screen replacement—as the device's distinctive live-media value in the wiki.
- Added content availability and production grammar as central constraints on product recommendation.
