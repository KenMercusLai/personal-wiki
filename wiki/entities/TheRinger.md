## Overview
[[TheRinger]] is [[BillSimmons]]'s independently owned sports-and-culture publication, used as the principal case for separating editorial ownership from a publisher's scalable backend functions.

## Current Profile
In the 2017 source, The Ringer combines a website with a podcast network and moves its hosting from [[Medium]] to [[VoxMedia]]. Vox Media supplies technology and sells advertising on a revenue-share basis, while The Ringer keeps its brand, ownership, and editorial independence. Thompson interprets the arrangement as a way for a creator-led publication to avoid duplicating sales and technical infrastructure.

## Key Characteristics
- Creator-led sports-and-culture publication with a multimedia ambition.
- Combines audience-building writing with revenue-generating podcasts.
- Retains a distinct brand, ownership, and editorial independence.
- Uses an external partner for technology and advertising sales.

## Evidence
- Ownership and identity: [[faceless-publishers-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] reports that Simmons retained ownership and The Ringer remained editorially independent.
- Multimedia economics: [[faceless-publishers-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] presents podcasts as covering the cost of the podcast network and website.
- Backend partnership: [[faceless-publishers-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] says Vox Media would host the site, sell its advertising, and share revenue.
- Strategic fit: [[faceless-publishers-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] argues that joining a large seller makes premium advertising easier to transact than a small direct deal.

## Qualifications
The page reflects the partnership as announced and interpreted in May 2017. It does not verify revenue, costs, later ownership changes, or the partnership's subsequent performance.
