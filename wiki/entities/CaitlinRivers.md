## Overview
[[CaitlinRivers]] is the computational epidemiologist whose public Ebola repository is the article's central example of collaborative scientific-data curation on [[GitHub]].

## Current Profile
During the 2014 West African Ebola outbreak, Rivers downloaded daily health-ministry PDF updates and converted their figures into machine-readable tables for outbreak modeling. Publishing those tables on GitHub let other researchers use the data, perform conversions before she did, contribute updates, and add simple scripts that checked whether daily patient counts were internally plausible.

The case presents Rivers less as the sole owner of a finished dataset than as the initiator and maintainer of a shared working resource. At the time of the project she was a PhD student in computational epidemiology at Virginia Tech; the 2016 article identifies her as an epidemiologist at the US Army Public Health Center.

## Key Characteristics
- Converted recurring public-health reports from PDF into machine-readable tables.
- Published the working Ebola dataset openly for reuse and contribution.
- Accepted distributed updates from researchers following the same outbreak.
- Benefited from contributor-written scripts that checked daily counts for simple inconsistencies.
- Used a software-collaboration platform to support time-sensitive epidemiological work.

## Qualifications
The profile relies on one 2016 journalistic account and describes Rivers' role during a specific outbreak. It does not measure the repository's completeness, error rate, contributor governance, downstream research impact, or later maintenance, and it should not be read as a current employment profile.
