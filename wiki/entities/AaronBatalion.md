## Overview
[[AaronBatalion]] is the author of a 2016 argument that Messenger bots should be understood as platform-hosted "micro apps" rather than as text-only automated agents.

## Current Profile
In the available source, Batalion analyzes early Facebook Messenger services through startup development and distribution economics. He uses [[Shyp]] with its CEO's permission and discloses that he was an early angel investor in the company, making the essay useful as a period platform thesis while also giving it a direct investment context.

## Key Characteristics
- Advocated the "micro app" frame for services built inside large messaging platforms.
- Emphasized development leverage, lower acquisition friction, and platform reach for startups.
- Treated rich UI, multimodal input, SaaS components, and human operators as complements to AI.
- Disclosed an early angel investment in Shyp, the article's main worked example.

## Evidence
- Category framing: [[aaron-batalion-bot-is-the-wrong-name]] argues that "bot" obscures an application-like service assembled inside Messenger.
- Startup thesis: [[aaron-batalion-bot-is-the-wrong-name]] links shared platform capabilities to lower development effort and a shorter advertising-to-use funnel.
- Product scope: [[aaron-batalion-bot-is-the-wrong-name]] says successful experiences could combine UI components, NLP, computer vision, audio transcription, SaaS tools, and human labor.
- Investment context: [[aaron-batalion-bot-is-the-wrong-name]] identifies Batalion as an early Shyp angel investor.
