## Overview
[[Xiaoyong|小泳]] is the authorial identity of the X account `@zcwzhy`, represented here by concise Chinese-language explainers on respiratory terminology and cardiovascular risk management.

## Current Profile
Across the available sources, 小泳 uses categorical distinctions to make medical topics legible to a general audience. One post separates influenza-virus disease from a multi-virus upper-respiratory syndrome by pathogen, anatomy, symptoms, course, and severity. A second separates long-term modification of atherosclerotic plaque from stenting or thrombolysis and organizes management by goals, risk factors, treatment horizon, and imaging follow-up. This is strongest as terminology and risk communication, but neither archived post supplies clinical references, and the plaque post includes a conflicting reader theory without adjudicating it. The sources provide no biography or professional credentials and cannot support diagnosis or individualized treatment by themselves.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes concise public-facing health education in Chinese.
- Organizes medical explanations through explicit goals, categories, time horizons, and treatment boundaries.
- Challenges misleading equivalences, including influenza with the common cold and procedural rescue with modification of underlying plaque disease.
- Emphasizes long-term risk management and follow-up alongside near-term clinical distinctions.
- Presents clinically consequential claims without references in the archived posts.

## Evidence
- Terminology: [[twitter-xiao-yong-liu-gan-yu-pu-tong-gan-mao-de-qu-bie]] separates influenza from the common cold and argues that the translation “流行性感冒” encourages conflation.
- Clinical explanation: [[twitter-xiao-yong-liu-gan-yu-pu-tong-gan-mao-de-qu-bie]] describes typical cold symptoms, examination findings, a five-to-seven-day uncomplicated course, and progressively more specific names for lower-respiratory involvement.
- Risk communication: [[twitter-xiao-yong-liu-gan-yu-pu-tong-gan-mao-de-qu-bie]] names pneumonia, worsening underlying disease, ARDS, acute necrotizing encephalopathy, multiorgan dysfunction, and death as possible severe influenza outcomes.
- Treatment taxonomy: [[dong-mai-zhou-yang-ying-hua-ban-kuai-gan-yu-yu-zhi-liao]] distinguishes plaque stabilization and regression from stenting and thrombolysis and groups intervention around lifestyle, lipids, blood pressure, glucose, inflammation, and thrombosis.
- Time and follow-up: [[dong-mai-zhou-yang-ying-hua-ban-kuai-gan-yu-yu-zhi-liao]] contrasts an asserted 30-day stabilization window with one-to-two-year regression and recommends repeat imaging to reassess treatment.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on two archived social-media posts. They do not state the author's credentials or cite clinical guidelines, epidemiological data, trials, or primary research. The influenza post lacks a validated diagnostic rule, and the plaque post omits patient selection, treatment targets, drug details, imaging protocols, and adverse effects. Its appended reader reply makes an unsupported rapid-response claim that should not be attributed to 小泳 as an endorsed conclusion. The general distinctions may aid orientation, but neither post is a diagnostic or treatment guide.
