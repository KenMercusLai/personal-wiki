## Overview
[[Xiaoyong|小泳]] is the authorial identity of the X account `@zcwzhy`, represented here by concise Chinese-language explainers on respiratory terminology, cardiovascular risk management, and speculative sleep mechanisms.

## Current Profile
Across the available sources, 小泳 uses categorical distinctions to make health topics legible to a general audience. One post separates influenza-virus disease from a multi-virus upper-respiratory syndrome by pathogen, anatomy, symptoms, course, and severity. A second separates long-term modification of atherosclerotic plaque from stenting or thrombolysis and organizes management by goals, risk factors, treatment horizon, and imaging follow-up. A third distinguishes an actually interrupted dream from fragmentary recall, then groups possible disruption under sleep-stage transition, external or bodily awakening, physiological arousal, and psychological conflict. The first two posts present clinical claims without references; the dream post goes further by explicitly labeling its account personal speculation. The sources provide no biography or professional credentials and cannot support diagnosis, treatment, or confident causal inference by themselves.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes concise public-facing health education in Chinese.
- Organizes medical explanations through explicit goals, categories, time horizons, and treatment boundaries.
- Challenges misleading equivalences, including influenza with the common cold and procedural rescue with modification of underlying plaque disease.
- Emphasizes long-term risk management and follow-up alongside near-term clinical distinctions.
- Separates an underlying event from how it is described or remembered, as in dream interruption versus fragmentary recall.
- Presents consequential physiological and clinical claims without references, while explicitly disclaiming scientific support in the dream post.

## Evidence
- Terminology: [[twitter-xiao-yong-liu-gan-yu-pu-tong-gan-mao-de-qu-bie]] separates influenza from the common cold and argues that the translation “流行性感冒” encourages conflation.
- Clinical explanation: [[twitter-xiao-yong-liu-gan-yu-pu-tong-gan-mao-de-qu-bie]] describes typical cold symptoms, examination findings, a five-to-seven-day uncomplicated course, and progressively more specific names for lower-respiratory involvement.
- Risk communication: [[twitter-xiao-yong-liu-gan-yu-pu-tong-gan-mao-de-qu-bie]] names pneumonia, worsening underlying disease, ARDS, acute necrotizing encephalopathy, multiorgan dysfunction, and death as possible severe influenza outcomes.
- Treatment taxonomy: [[dong-mai-zhou-yang-ying-hua-ban-kuai-gan-yu-yu-zhi-liao]] distinguishes plaque stabilization and regression from stenting and thrombolysis and groups intervention around lifestyle, lipids, blood pressure, glucose, inflammation, and thrombosis.
- Time and follow-up: [[dong-mai-zhou-yang-ying-hua-ban-kuai-gan-yu-yu-zhi-liao]] contrasts an asserted 30-day stabilization window with one-to-two-year regression and recommends repeat imaging to reassess treatment.
- Experience versus recall: [[chun-meng-zhong-duan-de-sheng-li-ji-zhi]] distinguishes an interrupted dream from an incomplete remembered narrative.
- Mechanism grouping: [[chun-meng-zhong-duan-de-sheng-li-ji-zhi]] proposes sleep-stage transition, sensory or bodily awakening, physiological arousal, and psychological conflict while expressly calling these explanations personal speculation.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on three archived social-media posts. They do not state the author's credentials or cite clinical guidelines, epidemiological data, sleep studies, trials, or primary research. The influenza post lacks a validated diagnostic rule, and the plaque post omits patient selection, treatment targets, drug details, imaging protocols, and adverse effects. Its appended reader reply makes an unsupported rapid-response claim that should not be attributed to 小泳 as an endorsed conclusion. The dream post provides no measurement or method for distinguishing sleep-stage transition, awakening, arousal, psychological disruption, and failed recall, and its disclaimer explicitly limits the account to personal conjecture. The general distinctions may aid orientation, but the sources do not establish diagnosis, treatment, or causal mechanisms.
