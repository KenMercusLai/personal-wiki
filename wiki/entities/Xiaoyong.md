## Overview
[[Xiaoyong|小泳]] is the authorial identity of the X account `@zcwzhy`, represented here by a short Chinese-language explainer distinguishing influenza from the common cold.

## Current Profile
In the available source, 小泳 uses definitions, symptom descriptions, anatomical progression, and severe-outcome examples to correct the everyday tendency to treat influenza as an ordinary cold. The post is strongest as terminology and risk communication: it separates influenza-virus disease from a multi-virus upper-respiratory syndrome and explains why both “感冒” and its English translation can obscure important differences. The source supplies no biography, professional credentials, citations, or diagnostic protocol, so it does not establish the author's wider expertise or support clinical decisions by itself.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes concise public-facing health education in Chinese.
- Organizes respiratory illness by pathogen, anatomical site, symptom pattern, course, and severity.
- Challenges misleading everyday terminology that can minimize influenza risk.
- Presents clinically consequential claims without references in the archived post.

## Evidence
- Terminology: [[twitter-xiao-yong-liu-gan-yu-pu-tong-gan-mao-de-qu-bie]] separates influenza from the common cold and argues that the translation “流行性感冒” encourages conflation.
- Clinical explanation: [[twitter-xiao-yong-liu-gan-yu-pu-tong-gan-mao-de-qu-bie]] describes typical cold symptoms, examination findings, a five-to-seven-day uncomplicated course, and progressively more specific names for lower-respiratory involvement.
- Risk communication: [[twitter-xiao-yong-liu-gan-yu-pu-tong-gan-mao-de-qu-bie]] names pneumonia, worsening underlying disease, ARDS, acute necrotizing encephalopathy, multiorgan dysfunction, and death as possible severe influenza outcomes.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one archived social-media post. The source does not state the author's credentials or cite clinical guidelines, epidemiological data, or primary research. Its general educational distinctions may be useful, but symptom overlap and individual risk mean the post should not be treated as a diagnostic or treatment guide.
