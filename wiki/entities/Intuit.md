## Overview
[[Intuit]] is a financial-software company represented here as the maker of [[TurboTax]] and a participant in the historical [[IRSFreeFile]] partnership.

## Current Profile
In ProPublica’s 2019 account, Intuit operated two materially different free offers: a widely promoted commercial Free Edition limited to simple returns and an IRS-backed Free File product for qualifying lower-income taxpayers. The report argues that naming, search visibility, website routing, delayed upgrade disclosure, and competing controls favored the commercial funnel. It also places those product choices beside industry lobbying against an IRS-run filing service. Intuit’s response emphasized that simple returns could use its commercial edition at no charge and that it donated 2.3 million federal and state returns through Free File programs in the prior year.

## Key Characteristics
- Makes TurboTax and participates, in the source’s historical period, in the IRS-industry Free File arrangement.
- Separates a commercial free product from an IRS-backed free product with different eligibility and entry routes.
- Uses search marketing and online guided filing as major acquisition and conversion channels.
- Appears in the source as both a product designer and a policy actor through industry lobbying.
- Defends its contribution through simple-return pricing and the claimed value and volume of donated returns.

## Qualifications

It does not establish current Intuit products, prices, Free File participation, policy positions, or later regulatory outcomes. Company contribution figures are quoted from Intuit rather than independently audited in the source, and the report does not reveal the internal rationale for every screen.
