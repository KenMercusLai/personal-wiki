## Overview
[[BradFitzpatrick]] is the founder and original developer of [[LiveJournal]], which he grew from a personal CGI experiment into a major early social platform.

## Current Profile
Fitzpatrick's early product practice was unusually direct: he built features in response to his own impulses and visible user behavior, then opened the service to friends and fellow students. As growth accelerated, he became responsible for programming, support, hosting, income, policy, and hiring despite preferring technical work and describing himself as poor at delegation. He hired LiveJournal's first paid systems administrator in 2001, created memcached for the platform in 2003, and sold Danga to [[SixApart]] in 2005 as burnout, infrastructure cost, and competition increased.

## Key Characteristics
- Built products experimentally from immediate personal and community use rather than a fixed roadmap.
- Added many early social features in response to observed behavior and informal feedback.
- Scaled a hobby project into a service with millions of users and significant operational demands.
- Struggled with delegation and the shift from programmer to manager and business operator.
- Contributed infrastructure work, including memcached, beyond LiveJournal's visible product surface.

- Company formation: [[the-linux-of-social-media-how-livejournal-pioneered-then-lost-blogging-ars-technica]] describes the transition around 2000 from experimentation to the mission of keeping a growing service alive.
- Management strain: [[the-linux-of-social-media-how-livejournal-pioneered-then-lost-blogging-ars-technica]] quotes Fitzpatrick describing weak delegation, constant firefighting, and near burnout.
- Infrastructure legacy: [[the-linux-of-social-media-how-livejournal-pioneered-then-lost-blogging-ars-technica]] credits him with writing memcached for LiveJournal in 2003.
- Exit: [[the-linux-of-social-media-how-livejournal-pioneered-then-lost-blogging-ars-technica]] says he sold Danga to Six Apart in early 2005 amid management burden and a more competitive blogging landscape.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from one retrospective article and concentrates on LiveJournal. It does not assess Fitzpatrick's later work, independently compare his management choices, or establish whether remaining independent would have produced a better outcome.
