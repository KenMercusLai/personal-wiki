## Overview
[[Trello]] is a visual collaboration product organized around boards, lists, and cards; the wiki currently represents it through a 2016 case about localization and international marketing.

## Current Profile
The source presents Trello as an already international, freemium product that used existing user behavior to choose language markets and experiments to reduce rollout risk. After professional translations in Brazil, Spain, and Germany, the company tested volunteer translation in France and then coordinated a four-month launch of 16 additional languages. Its own boards provided one coordination space per language, while a translation management system moved strings between the product and translators.

The case also shows Trello treating localization as product, engineering, community, and marketing work. Volunteers supplied product-aware language and peer review; local writers, journalists, events, ambassadors, PR, and content made launches visible; developers handled string extraction and pluralization; professional translators covered deadline-sensitive or continuous work.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses boards, lists, and cards as a visual collaboration model.
- Selected language investment from observed user demand and device, browser, country, and signup data.
- Combined professional translation with a large volunteer community rather than using either model exclusively.
- Used Trello itself to coordinate per-language instructions, context, testing, questions, glossaries, and consistency work.
- Paired translated product availability with local and global marketing.
- Required engineering internationalization across web, mobile, stores, commerce, and notifications.

## Evidence
- Product and market selection: [[heres-how-trello-nailed-localization-and-global-marketing]] describes Trello's collaboration model and the use of existing user data to choose experiments and languages.
- Community production: [[heres-how-trello-nailed-localization-and-global-marketing]] reports 520 volunteers, 30–50 per language, and roughly 800,000 translated words during the four-month program.
- Operating workflow: [[heres-how-trello-nailed-localization-and-global-marketing]] describes the translation management system, per-language Trello boards, glossaries, contextual material, reviewers, peer discussion, deadlines, and Trello Gold rewards.
- Hybrid delivery: [[heres-how-trello-nailed-localization-and-global-marketing]] says professional services completed deadline-risk languages and handled weekly user updates.
- Growth activation: [[heres-how-trello-nailed-localization-and-global-marketing]] contrasts translated-only Spain with marketing-intensive Brazil and France.

## Qualifications
This profile relies on one 2016 article written by OneSky, a localization vendor, from an interview with Trello's international marketing lead. Its word counts, volunteer totals, translation-quality judgments, and signup changes are self-reported; the country experiments did not isolate translation, marketing, seasonality, or other launch effects. The source also uses inconsistent totals for localized languages, and its product, ownership, pricing, and localization practices may no longer describe Trello.

## What Changed
- Created the initial Trello profile around its 2016 localization operating model.
- Distinguished volunteer translation from the tooling, review, engineering, and marketing systems that made it usable.
- Recorded professional fallback and unresolved internationalization limits alongside the launch success claims.
