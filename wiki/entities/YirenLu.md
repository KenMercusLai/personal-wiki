## Overview
[[YirenLu]] is represented as the journalist who reported how Houseparty and Musical.ly involved teenage users in product design and early distribution.

## Current Profile
In the available source, Lu connects interviews with founders, an investor, and users to a broader argument about demographic distance in consumer technology. The report contrasts earlier social-network founders who resembled their initial audiences with adult teams designing for teenagers, then examines school visits and persistent group chats as ways to replace assumed intuition with direct observation and feedback. The article also resists a permanently age-bounded interpretation by documenting how the same social product can acquire uses across generations.

## Key Characteristics
- Reports on consumer social products and their design practices.
- Uses Houseparty and Musical.ly as paired cases of builder-audience distance.
- Connects product research with school-based network distribution.
- Preserves both teenage early-adopter specificity and broader product reinterpretation.
