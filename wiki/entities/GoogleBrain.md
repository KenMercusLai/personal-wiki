## Overview
[[GoogleBrain]] is presented as the Google project begun in 2011 to advance large neural networks and move them into production systems.

## Current Profile
The source's distinctive claim is organizational as much as technical. Google Brain worked directly with Android, Gmail, Photos, speech, search, translation, and other product groups, allowing research methods to encounter deployment constraints and useful datasets quickly. Its leverage came from combining that collaboration with large-scale compute, reusable model components, and short training-feedback cycles.

## Key Characteristics
- Began in 2011 with the goal of advancing practical neural-network capability.
- Worked with operating product teams rather than remaining an isolated research group.
- Applied shared deep-learning building blocks across speech, text, images, video, labels, entities, and audio.
- Used large-scale distributed training to shorten model experimentation.
- Helped translate research results into deployed Google features.

## Evidence
- Origin and purpose: [[jeff-dean-on-large-scale-deep-learning-at-google-high-scalability]] dates the project to 2011 and describes its goal as pushing what neural networks could do.
- Product collaboration: [[jeff-dean-on-large-scale-deep-learning-at-google-high-scalability]] says the team began with a few product groups and expanded as successful applications became visible internally.
- Technical scope: [[jeff-dean-on-large-scale-deep-learning-at-google-high-scalability]] lists Android, Gmail, Photos, speech, search, translation, image understanding, robotics, and other areas.
- Training infrastructure: [[jeff-dean-on-large-scale-deep-learning-at-google-high-scalability]] describes parallel training with many model replicas and centralized parameter servers.

## Qualifications
The evidence is a secondary 2016 summary of a talk, not an independent history of the organization. It does not measure which product improvements were caused by Google Brain, compare its structure with isolated research groups, or establish the project's later organizational status.
