## Overview
[[RichardLi]] is represented here as the cofounder and CEO of [[Datawire]] and as an author explaining the organizational and infrastructure consequences of microservice adoption to engineering executives.

## Current Profile
Li defines microservices through independently shippable team boundaries rather than code size alone. His account joins claimed delivery benefits to organizational delegation, automated deployment, elastic scaling, network protocols, distributed tracing, failure isolation, and recovery, while recommending a monolith-first approach for teams too small to exploit parallel service development.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames software architecture in terms of executive, organizational, and delivery consequences.
- Treats independent team shipping as the core microservice capability.
- Connects service autonomy to automation, network infrastructure, observability, and resilience.
- Makes engineering-team capacity an explicit adoption constraint.

## Evidence
- Executive framing: [[richard-li-microservices-essentials-for-executives]] connects architecture to agility, organizational scale, efficiency, onboarding, recruiting, and engineering leadership metrics.
- Boundary definition: [[richard-li-microservices-essentials-for-executives]] describes a microservice as an independently shippable unit and microservices as distributed development of cloud applications.
- Operating prerequisites: [[richard-li-microservices-essentials-for-executives]] specifies automated deployment, elastic scaling, common communication protocols, tracing, isolation, and recovery.
- Adoption restraint: [[richard-li-microservices-essentials-for-executives]] advises small engineering organizations to begin with a monolith.
