## Overview
[[Kik]] appears as a mobile messaging app in the wiki, first through promoted brand chats, later as a company-token precedent, and now as the platform context for [[TedLivingston]]'s 2016 chatbot thesis.

## Current Profile
The Mobile Dev Memo source uses Kik as a primary example of opt-in messaging advertising. Brands can pay to appear in the Promoted Chats tab, and once users start a brand chat, the account can send ad-like content or answer questions through keyword-sensitive bots and links opened in Kik's native browser. Livingston's source adds Kik's own platform ambition: its Bot Shop launched alongside Facebook's bot platform, and Kik Codes, web bubbles, invites, mentions, and million-user bots are presented as evidence that chat could become a service runtime and distribution layer.

Livingston's August 2016 checkpoint reports more than 20,000 bots built since April and almost two billion user-bot messages, while conceding that no killer bot had appeared. He reframes Kik's Bot Shop, Codes, Mentions, and Invites as discovery and instant-interaction infrastructure rather than evidence that open conversation worked. Peterson's March 2017 report turns those features into a comparison with Messenger. Kik foregrounded more than 200 of roughly 20,000 bots in its in-app store, promoted partner bots, used suggested responses more often than open text, and let bots join friend conversations through Mentions. That merchandising and inline invocation could improve discovery and sharing, while the smaller, younger audience constrained scale and commerce. The source also preserves a disputed adoption claim: Kik rejected an interviewee's characterization and said 10,000 bots had more than 30 subscribers. Coin and Crypto adds a separate crypto-era reference: Kik is cited as proof that a large established company had already launched a token, raising $75 million in a 2017 ICO.

## Key Characteristics
- Messaging app example of promoted brand chat placement.
- Uses user-initiated brand engagement as the permission boundary.
- Connects chat advertising to lightweight bots and in-app web content.
- Serves as Livingston's main example of chatbot-platform tooling, sharing mechanics, and early bot audience scale.
- Foregrounded bot discovery through a store, platform promotion, and inline Mentions, while relying heavily on suggested responses.
- Serves as a company-token precedent in a speculative article about whether Amazon could create its own cryptocurrency.
- Supplies an early platform-learning case where bot utility was separated from humanlike conversation.

## Evidence
- Messaging advertising: [[advertising-models-in-mobile-messaging-apps-mobile-dev-memo]] says advertisers can pay for visibility in Kik's Promoted Chats tab, then use bot replies and Kik's native browser after users engage.
- Bot platform tooling: [[chat-is-the-new-browser-ted-livingston-medium]] cites Kik's Bot Shop, Kik Codes, web bubbles, invites, and mentions as bot ecosystem advances.
- Bot audience scale: [[chat-is-the-new-browser-ted-livingston-medium]] says Roll, DisOrDat, and Tickers the Bot each reached a million users on Kik.
- Bot merchandising: [[inside-chatbots-year-of-growing-pains-were-at-an-inflection-point-marketing-land]] reports roughly 20,000 bots, more than 200 Bot Shop listings, and active platform promotion for selected brand bots.
- Structured interaction: [[inside-chatbots-year-of-growing-pains-were-at-an-inflection-point-marketing-land]] says more Kik bots used suggested responses than open text, while noting that the platform could not say which produced greater engagement.
- Inline discovery: [[inside-chatbots-year-of-growing-pains-were-at-an-inflection-point-marketing-land]] says Mentions let users invoke a bot inside friend conversations and that enabled bots reportedly achieved higher viral growth.
- Campaign example: [[inside-chatbots-year-of-growing-pains-were-at-an-inflection-point-marketing-land]] reports more than 1.2 million messages for the American Music Awards bot on Kik, including 11 to 13 per user around the show.
- Company-token precedent: [[amazon-is-the-biggest-threat-to-bitcoin-right-now-by-coin-and-crypto]] says Kik launched an ICO in September 2017 and raised $75 million.
- Four-month checkpoint: [[ted-livingston-bots-are-better-without-conversation]] reports more than 20,000 bots and almost two billion user-bot messages while acknowledging no killer bot and incomplete payments.

## Qualifications
The sources use Kik as a 2016 ad-format example, two insider bot-platform snapshots, a March 2017 discovery and campaign comparison, and a brief 2017 ICO precedent. Livingston's bot counts and message volume are platform-reported and do not establish retention, usefulness, bot quality, or business outcomes. Peterson's participant figures are attributed rather than audited, compare unlike campaigns and denominators, and include a subscriber claim that Kik directly disputed; its five unique screenshots could not be opened during ingest. The sources do not assess Kik's broader product trajectory, later demographics, token design, regulatory history, or later business performance.

## What Changed
- Added the August 2016 operating checkpoint and its explicit concession that no killer bot had emerged.
- Reframed Kik's Codes, store, Mentions, and Invites as non-conversational distribution infrastructure.
