## Overview
[[Kik]] appears as a mobile messaging app in the wiki, first through promoted brand chats and later as an example of a company launching a cryptocurrency token through an ICO.

## Current Profile
The Mobile Dev Memo source uses Kik as a primary example of opt-in messaging advertising. Brands can pay to appear in the Promoted Chats tab, and once users start a brand chat, the account can send ad-like content or answer questions through keyword-sensitive bots and links opened in Kik's native browser. Coin and Crypto adds a separate crypto-era reference: Kik is cited as proof that a large established company had already launched a token, raising $75 million in a 2017 ICO.

## Key Characteristics
- Messaging app example of promoted brand chat placement.
- Uses user-initiated brand engagement as the permission boundary.
- Connects chat advertising to lightweight bots and in-app web content.
- Serves as a company-token precedent in a speculative article about whether Amazon could create its own cryptocurrency.

## Evidence
- Messaging advertising: [[advertising-models-in-mobile-messaging-apps-mobile-dev-memo]] says advertisers can pay for visibility in Kik's Promoted Chats tab, then use bot replies and Kik's native browser after users engage.
- Company-token precedent: [[amazon-is-the-biggest-threat-to-bitcoin-right-now-by-coin-and-crypto]] says Kik launched an ICO in September 2017 and raised $75 million.

## Qualifications
The sources use Kik only as a 2016 ad-format example and a brief 2017 ICO precedent. They do not assess Kik's broader product trajectory, demographics, token design, regulatory history, or later business performance.
