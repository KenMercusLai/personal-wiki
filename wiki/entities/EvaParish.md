## Overview
[[EvaParish]] is a writer and technical-documentation practitioner represented in the wiki through a 2019 essay on cross-genre editing.

## Current Profile
Parish draws one editorial philosophy from academic papers, poetry, short fiction, email, and workplace documentation. Her approach starts with a written statement of purpose and audience, then combines precision, concision, explicit agency, reader context, consistent tone, and scan-friendly structure. She presents these techniques as choices in service of meaning rather than rules to follow without judgment.

## Key Characteristics
- Applies lessons across creative, academic, interpersonal, and technical writing.
- Treats editing as a way to test both communicative intent and underlying understanding.
- Prioritizes exact meaning and removal of unnecessary words.
- Designs prose and page structure around readers who may lack context and skim on screens.
- Qualifies categorical craft advice with a final appeal to deliberate, message-specific judgment.

## Evidence
- Cross-genre practice: [[eva-parish-what-i-think-about-when-i-edit]] says the same basic techniques informed the author's academic, poetic, fictional, and technical work.
- Editorial method: [[eva-parish-what-i-think-about-when-i-edit]] joins a purpose-and-audience preamble with sentence simplification, explicit actors, defined terminology, tone control, and visual hierarchy.
- Governing philosophy: [[eva-parish-what-i-think-about-when-i-edit]] reduces the method to exact meaning and removal of unnecessary words, then states that choices should suit the message.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one first-person craft essay. It does not establish Parish's broader career history, the full range of her editorial work, or measured reader outcomes from the recommended techniques.
