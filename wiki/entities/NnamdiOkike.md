## Overview
[[NnamdiOkike]] is a venture-capital author represented by a [[645Ventures]] essay on why investors miss rare breakout companies.

## Current Profile
Okike coauthors a framework that shifts attention from ordinary failed investments to costly omissions under power-law returns. His represented investment approach combines prepared product-market theses with openness to unconventional founders, direct customer evidence, independent judgment, and sensitivity to category or behavior change.

## Key Characteristics
- Coauthor of the seven-part venture-capital blind-spot framework.
- Treats power-law outcomes as the reason omission errors deserve special attention.
- Connects founder evaluation and deal access to pattern matching, homophily, and group composition.
- Recommends pairing market preparation with openness about who can build the winning company.

## Evidence
- Authorship: [[venture-capital-blind-spots-645-ventures-medium]] credits Okike and Aaron Holiday as coauthors.
- Omission focus: [[venture-capital-blind-spots-645-ventures-medium]] argues that missed exceptional investments matter more than optimizing the failure rate.
- Founder judgment: [[venture-capital-blind-spots-645-ventures-medium]] links investor blind spots to pattern matching, similar networks, and homogeneous decision groups.
- Countermeasure: [[venture-capital-blind-spots-645-ventures-medium]] recommends a prepared mind about the market and an open or beginner's mind about founder qualities.
