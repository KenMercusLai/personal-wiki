## Overview
[[GoogleDocs]] appears as both a collaboration product whose value grows through sharing and a 2016 example in the debate between uniform [[MaterialDesign]] and [[PlatformNativeDesign]].

## Current Profile
The virality source presents Google Docs as a product with a useful single-player mode but a stronger multi-player mode. The "Share" action is treated as the viral mechanism because collaboration both helps the original user and brings another person into the product experience.

The Macworld essay examines a different surface: Google Docs on iOS in 2016. It criticizes the application for carrying Android-associated Material Design controls into Apple's environment, including a large floating action button, vertical overflow dots, and card-style menus. This is evidence of a contemporary practitioner objection, not proof that the interface harmed usability or adoption.

## Key Characteristics
- Collaboration software with both solo and multi-user use.
- Uses sharing as a natural invitation surface.
- Demonstrates virality through increased utility rather than external promotion.
- Served as a concrete case in a 2016 critique of using Google's cross-platform design language unchanged on iOS.
- Illustrates the tension between recognizable product-system consistency and host-platform familiarity.

## Evidence
- Collaboration loop: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] says Google Docs becomes more powerful once a user clicks "Share" and starts collaborating with coworkers.
- Single-player qualification: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] notes that editing alone is possible, but additional value appears with others.
- Viral control surface: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] treats "Share" and "Invite people" buttons as viral mechanisms for collaboration products.
- Cross-platform controls: [[google-is-making-the-same-mistake-now-that-microsoft-did-in-the-90s-macworld]] identifies the iOS app's floating action button, vertical overflow icon, and card menus as Material Design choices.
- Visual context: the retained lead photograph in [[google-is-making-the-same-mistake-now-that-microsoft-did-in-the-90s-macworld]] shows Google Docs' Material Design recent-documents surface on Android; the archived iOS screenshot itself was an uninterpretable 1x1 placeholder.

## Qualifications
The sources use Google Docs as a brief virality example and a 2016 interface-design case; they do not analyze its broader product history, monetization, enterprise adoption, accessibility, or measured usability. The Macworld source supplies one author's judgment, and its archived iOS screenshot cannot be independently inspected.

## What Changed
- Added Google Docs as a qualified cross-platform design case, distinguishing visible Android evidence from the unavailable iOS screenshot.
