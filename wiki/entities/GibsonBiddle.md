## Overview
[[GibsonBiddle]] is a former [[Netflix]] VP of Product who presents brand as a cross-functional product-and-marketing system and teaches the [[BrandPositioning]] and [[BrandPyramid]] frameworks.

## Current Profile
Biddle's account draws on children's educational software and his 2005-2010 Netflix role. He argues that marketing should define the intended brand while product makes it credible through customer experience, with both functions using research and experiments to revise how the offer is presented. His Netflix retrospective treats a coherent brand as a potentially hard-to-copy, margin-enhancing capability, but it does not isolate brand from Netflix's catalog, pricing, distribution, technology, or market timing.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines a marketing background with product-leadership practice.
- Defines brand through positioning, customer benefit, personality, emotion, and aspiration rather than visual identity alone.
- Uses simple, memorable frameworks intended to align product and marketing teams.
- Advocates consumer research and A/B testing to refine brand presentation.
- Treats stable customer meaning as a guide for changing product attributes.

## Evidence
- Career context: [[gibson-biddle-branding-for-builders]] describes Biddle's work with Sesame Street, Schoolhouse Rock, and Madeline software before his Netflix product role.
- Cross-functional model: [[gibson-biddle-branding-for-builders]] assigns brand definition to marketing and brand delivery to product while emphasizing collaboration.
- Frameworks: [[gibson-biddle-branding-for-builders]] presents the positioning model and brand pyramid as practical team exercises.
- Experimentation: [[gibson-biddle-branding-for-builders]] reports frequent Netflix homepage tests using trial starts and free-to-paid conversion.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one first-person 2017 retrospective. Its frameworks are practitioner tools rather than validated causal models, and the selected Netflix and Apple examples may reflect survivorship and hindsight. Reported historical metrics lack experiment details and should not be treated as current benchmarks.
