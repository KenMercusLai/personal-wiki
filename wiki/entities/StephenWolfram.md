## Overview
[[StephenWolfram]] appears in this wiki as the author of the ChatGPT explanation piece, writing as a scientist-entrepreneur who has spent decades building a computational language and who reads large language models through that project's concepts.

## Current Profile
The source presents Wolfram as an explainer with a distinctive method: rather than describing ChatGPT's engineering, he rebuilds the ideas from first principles inside one toolchain. Every example in the essay is runnable [[WolframLanguage]] code, and he deliberately works with the smaller [[GPT2]] model so that readers can reproduce each step on a desktop machine. His framing draws on his own prior work - cellular automata and rule 30, the notion of computation as a general phenomenon, [[ComputationalIrreducibility]], the Principle of Computational Equivalence, the physics project, and the idea of the ruliad - and he uses that background to argue that the interesting question is not only how ChatGPT works but what its success reveals about language and thought. He is also an interested party: the essay proposes his own [[WolframAlpha]] and Wolfram Language as the computational tool layer that language models need, and its closing recommendations about [[SemanticGrammar]] and [[ComputationalLanguage]] extend his long-running symbolic-representation programme.

## Key Characteristics
- Explains ChatGPT by building up from probability tables, n-gram baselines, and small trainable networks rather than starting from the full system.
- Insists on reproducibility: he uses [[GPT2]] and Wolfram Language code cells so that each figure in the essay can be regenerated by a reader.
- Frames machine learning as engineering lore - trial-and-error practice with little underlying theory - while still treating its success as scientifically informative.
- Distinguishes what a network can learn from what requires explicit computation, using [[ComputationalIrreducibility]] as the boundary.
- Reads ChatGPT's success as evidence that human language is more law-like and computationally shallower than expected.
- Argues from his own decade-scale project that precise [[ComputationalLanguage]] could make those implicit "laws of language" explicit.
- Is transparently an advocate: the essay's tool, semantic-grammar, and language recommendations align with products and research he has built.

## Evidence
- First-principles method and reproducibility: [[what-is-chatgpt-doing-and-why-does-it-work]] says he mostly avoids the full ChatGPT system and uses GPT-2 because it runs on a standard desktop, and every figure is a runnable Wolfram Language cell.
- Inside-view of a model: [[what-is-chatgpt-doing-and-why-does-it-work]] opens the network's token and position embeddings, attention-head recombination weights, and fully connected weight matrices, and reports that the inner features remain uninterpretable.
- Engineering-lore framing: [[what-is-chatgpt-doing-and-why-does-it-work]] says most training practice was found by trial and error, and that there is no theory for why the architecture works as well as it does.
- Computation boundary: [[what-is-chatgpt-doing-and-why-does-it-work]] uses rule 30-style irreducible processes and a parenthesis-matching experiment to argue that a feed-forward network learns shallow patterns well and deep computation poorly.
- Language-law thesis: [[what-is-chatgpt-doing-and-why-does-it-work]] concludes that ChatGPT's success suggests major undiscovered "laws of language" and "laws of thought" that could be made explicit.
- Advocacy: [[what-is-chatgpt-doing-and-why-does-it-work]] proposes Wolfram|Alpha and Wolfram Language as uniquely suitable tools for ChatGPT and links repeatedly to his own writings and product repositories.
- Personal framing: [[what-is-chatgpt-doing-and-why-does-it-work]] opens by describing 43 years of following neural networks and closes with a long acknowledgment list of researchers he has discussed them with.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on a single source: one long explanatory essay by the subject himself. It does not cover his biography, Mathematica's history, his physics work, or any critical assessment of those projects, and the essay's self-referential tool and language recommendations are advocacy as much as explanation. The technical details are also a snapshot of ChatGPT's family as of early 2023.
