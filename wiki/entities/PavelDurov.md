## Overview
[[PavelDurov]] is the interview subject whose personal discipline practices supply the source essay's examples about phone use, focus, alcohol avoidance, and leading by example.

## Current Profile
In this wiki, Durov appears less as a company biography and more as a model of disciplined agency. The source presents his practices as attempts to protect attention and cognitive control from external feeds, addictive substances, and inconsistent personal example.

## Key Characteristics
- Presents morning phone avoidance as a way to keep the day's attention self-directed.
- Frames alcohol avoidance as protection of the brain as a core life and work tool.
- Treats personal example as more persuasive than verbal instruction, especially around children and smartphone use.
- Connects freedom to discipline: refusal of low-value impulses creates room for chosen action.

## Evidence
- Attention boundary: [[zhi-you-zi-lv-cai-neng-kan-dao-zhen-zheng-de-zi-you-durov-podcast-sui-bi]] quotes the interview claim that opening a phone first thing in the morning lets outside inputs choose one's thoughts.
- Cognitive protection: [[zhi-you-zi-lv-cai-neng-kan-dao-zhen-zheng-de-zi-you-durov-podcast-sui-bi]] summarizes Durov's refusal of alcohol as a way to avoid damaging or impairing the brain for short-term pleasure.
- Example-setting: [[zhi-you-zi-lv-cai-neng-kan-dao-zhen-zheng-de-zi-you-durov-podcast-sui-bi]] uses Durov's comments to argue that children copy visible smartphone behavior more than parental explanations.
