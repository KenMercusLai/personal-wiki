## Overview
[[AndreaGoulet]] is the author of the 2016 DEV Community article that introduces [[MakerMenderDeveloperStyles]] as a way to discuss developer preferences across software-product phases.

## Current Profile
In the captured article, Goulet argues that software teams need both rapid greenfield creation and sustained improvement of existing systems. She connects those orientations to different tasks and management conditions, then invites readers to locate themselves on or between the two styles. Her replies in the appended discussion emphasize that the distinction is subjective, that people may evolve, and that pleasure in the work can influence performance.

## Key Characteristics
- Introduces the labels "maker" and "mender" for contrasting but complementary developer orientations.
- Connects developer interest to product lifecycle, work design, autonomy, and deadline conditions.
- Treats the categories as preferences that can coexist or change rather than as a ranking of engineers.
- Uses practitioner analogy and community discussion rather than formal personality or performance research.

## Evidence
- Terminology: [[developer-differences-makers-vs-menders-dev-community]] defines makers through greenfield and MVP work and menders through refactoring, testing, bug fixing, and stable-system improvement.
- Management advice: [[developer-differences-makers-vs-menders-dev-community]] recommends experimentation and deadlines for makers, and predictable backlogs, advance notice, and autonomy for menders.
- Flexible identity: [[developer-differences-makers-vs-menders-dev-community]] includes Goulet's comment that she sits between the styles and can crave the other after too much time on one side.

## Qualifications
The wiki currently represents Goulet through one 2016 practitioner article and its appended discussion. The source does not establish her broader career history, validate the categories psychometrically, or measure whether style-based staffing improves software or employee outcomes.
