## Overview
[[VenkateshRao]] is a writer represented in the wiki through [[BenThompson]]'s summary and quotation of the essay “Cognitive Mercantilism,” which proposes a demand-expansion interpretation of abundant intelligence.

## Current Profile
In the available source, Rao contrasts scarcity-and-control views of intelligence with [[CognitiveCapitalism]]. The analogy is historical: just as release from subsistence agriculture enabled new economic and cultural activity, release from “subsistence cognition” might allow human and artificial minds to discover and satisfy demands that could not previously be served.

This profile is intentionally narrow. The underlying essay has not been independently ingested, so the wiki records only the argument Thompson selected and quoted, not Rao's complete reasoning, evidence, definitions, or broader work.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames AI abundance through demand formation rather than only labor substitution or intelligence oversupply.
- Uses the agricultural-to-industrial transition as an analogy for release from subsistence cognition.
- Contrasts cognitive mercantilist, Malthusian, and capitalist interpretations of abundant intelligence.

## Evidence
- Attribution: [[the-end-of-the-internet]] identifies Rao as the author of “Cognitive Mercantilism” and calls the essay central to its final market argument.
- Demand thesis: [[the-end-of-the-internet]] quotes Rao arguing that new cognitive demands may expand after minds are freed from intelligence production required for survival.
- Competing frames: [[the-end-of-the-internet]] reproduces Rao's contrast among oversupply, redundancy, and demand-expansion interpretations.

## Qualifications
The evidence is one secondary treatment with a selected excerpt, not the complete Rao essay or an empirical economic study. The historical analogy does not establish AI's labor, demand, income, market-structure, or distributional outcomes. No claim here should be treated as a full profile of Rao's career or views.
