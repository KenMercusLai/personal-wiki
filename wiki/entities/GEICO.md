## Overview
[[GEICO]] is the insurance company used in the source to show how a durable cost and convenience advantage can persist through major channel and technology changes.

## Current Profile
The essay presents GEICO as Buffett's long-running example of timeless business advantage. The company changed from selling auto insurance to government employees in cafeterias toward selling multiple insurance products broadly through modern digital channels. Yet the source says the core advantage remained: direct sales can be cheaper and more convenient than broker-mediated sales.

## Key Characteristics
- Serves as Warren Buffett's long-term investment example in the essay.
- Changed sales channels and product scope over decades.
- Retained the same underlying direct-sales cost and convenience logic.

## Evidence
- Long holding case: [[betting-on-things-that-never-change-collaborative-fund]] says Buffett has owned GEICO stock since 1951.
- Channel change: [[betting-on-things-that-never-change-collaborative-fund]] contrasts old cafeteria sales to government employees with modern broad insurance sales on phones.
- Durable advantage: [[betting-on-things-that-never-change-collaborative-fund]] says selling directly preserves a cost and convenience advantage over broker-based distribution.

## Qualifications
The source uses GEICO as an illustrative strategic example and does not evaluate its complete history, underwriting performance, ownership structure, competition, or later operating results.
