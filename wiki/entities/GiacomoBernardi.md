## Overview
[[GiacomoBernardi]] is identified by Amazon as an AWS principal applied scientist who co-led the effort to build a flat data-center network inspired by random-graph theory.

## Current Profile
Bernardi began from a practical dissatisfaction with hierarchical router topologies and explored a Penrose-tiling-inspired alternative before turning with [[RatulMahajan]] to random graphs. He led implementation work spanning large simulations, the deterministic ShuffleBox wiring design, the Spraypoint protocol, and a hand-wired production proof in Ireland, according to Amazon's account.

## Key Characteristics
- Approached data-center topology as a router-design and implementation problem.
- Connected academic graph ideas to physical cabling, routing, simulation, and deployment constraints.
- Co-led the AWS random-graph network effort with Mahajan and [[SeshadhriComandur]].

## Evidence
- Design origin: [[aws-random-graph]] attributes the Penrose-inspired sketch and pursuit of a flat router topology to Bernardi.
- Implementation: [[aws-random-graph]] describes Bernardi running a large search for the ShuffleBox wiring parameters and helping develop Spraypoint.
- Validation: [[aws-random-graph]] quotes Bernardi on simulations preceding proof and on the hand-wired Ireland prototype.
