## Overview
[[Gmail]] is [[Google]]'s email service, represented in the available sources both as an invite-scarcity growth case and as a configurable platform for personal email task management.

## Current Profile
One source says Gmail launched with limited server capacity and converted that constraint into scarcity: invite-only access began with opinion formers who could refer friends, making the service feel exclusive. A later practitioner guide shows a different layer of the product's value: users could combine special stars, search queries, filters, Multiple Inboxes, shortcuts, auto-advance, undo send, and multiple account identities into a personal workflow for action and follow-up. That guide documents a historical interface rather than current product capability.

## Key Characteristics
- Email service from [[Google]].
- Used invite scarcity and referral access to create curiosity and demand at launch.
- Supports search, message markers, filters, and account configuration that can be composed into user-defined workflows.
- Historically exposed Multiple Inboxes, special stars, auto-advance, and undo send through settings or Labs.

## Evidence
- Invite scarcity: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says Gmail launched by invitation only with about 1,000 initial opinion formers.
- Exclusivity effect: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says the strategy made signing up feel like joining an exclusive club.
- Workflow composition: [[dont-drown-in-email-how-to-use-gmail-more-efficiently-startup-lessons-learned]] shows special-star searches driving right-side action, waiting, scheduled, and delegated panels.
- Processing controls: [[dont-drown-in-email-how-to-use-gmail-more-efficiently-startup-lessons-learned]] documents filters, keyboard shortcuts, auto-advance, undo send, SMTP identities, and reply-from-address behavior.

## Qualifications
The growth source does not isolate Gmail's invite mechanism from storage, brand, product quality, search integration, or broader competitive context. The workflow source is one person's advice and uses an older Gmail interface; named Labs, settings paths, mobile limitations, and third-party extensions should not be assumed to describe the current product.
