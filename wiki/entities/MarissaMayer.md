## Overview
[[MarissaMayer]] is presented as a Google and Yahoo operator whose scaling lessons cover process breakage, acquisitions, culture, communication cadence, and CEO path clearing, while a second source disputes the effectiveness of her Yahoo acquisition and culture interventions.

## Current Profile
The scaling source uses Mayer to connect [[Google]] operating lessons with [[Yahoo]] scaling problems. She relays [[EricSchmidt]]'s order-of-magnitude process warning, describes using acquisitions to build mobile capability, cites Google and Facebook as strong-culture examples, and explains Google's executive cadence of staff meetings, strategy reviews, one-on-ones, and full-company meetings.

[[XavierAmatriain]] supplies a conflicting external assessment. He accepts that Mayer sought a mobile-ad direction and interprets acquisitions as an attempt to repair Yahoo's talent gap, but argues that too many simultaneous initiatives, acquisition-driven fragmentation, cultural dilution, and an unsuccessful work-from-home intervention prevented the strategy from changing the company. This does not negate the capability gap; it separates intervention rationale from net organizational outcome.

## Key Characteristics
- Emphasizes that processes break at each order of magnitude.
- Uses talent, building-block, and strategic acquisition categories.
- Treats strong culture as recognizable even without explicit branding.
- Values structured executive communication cadence.
- Frames the CEO as setting direction, defending teams, and removing obstacles.
- Has her Yahoo acquisition and culture interventions evaluated differently by operator and outside-observer sources.

## Qualifications
This profile is limited to two short secondary accounts and does not assess Mayer's wider tenure, outcomes, or controversies at Google or Yahoo. The scaling note presents her own rationale, while Amatriain did not work at Yahoo and supplies no acquisition performance or policy outcome data. The disagreement is retained as a difference between intended intervention and alleged company-wide effect, not resolved as a factual verdict.
