## Overview
[[Nolla]] is a software practitioner represented in this wiki through a short essay about AI coding leverage, production accountability, and the threatened apprenticeship path from junior to senior engineer.

## Current Profile
The available source presents Nolla as the originator of a conversation about AI-assisted software work and the provider of earlier writing samples used to shape the resulting prose. The position attributed to the essay is neither simple automation enthusiasm nor rejection: experienced engineers can multiply implementation output with AI, but the enduring scarce work is making systems correct, consistent, operable, reversible, and organizationally accountable. Nolla's proposed response is to preserve the engineering career ladder by designing new forms of supervised, verifiable practice.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes about AI's effects on software-engineering work and careers.
- Distinguishes cheap implementation from costly convergence and production acceptance.
- Treats senior experience as leverage in setting contracts, tests, rollout, rollback, and observability controls.
- Warns that removing junior work can weaken the future supply of experienced engineers.
- Advocates deliberate training environments, review, and SRE practice as replacement learning infrastructure.
