## Overview
[[BillJoy]] is the Berkeley graduate student and later Sun Microsystems co-founder presented in the source as the principal creator of `vi`, the Unix editor from which [[Vim]] descends.

## Current Profile
The article profiles Joy through the development of `vi` around 1976-1979. Working in a constrained Unix environment at U.C. Berkeley, Joy moved from Ken Thompson's `ed` through George Coulouris's `em` and then, with Chuck Haley, toward `en`, `ex`, and `vi`. His recollections explain why `vi` emphasizes terse commands, visual mode over slow links, and keyboard navigation that fits old terminal hardware.

## Key Characteristics
- Created `vi` while still a graduate student at U.C. Berkeley, before co-founding Sun Microsystems.
- Built on and modified earlier Unix editor work rather than starting from a blank slate.
- Worked with Chuck Haley as `em` evolved through `en` and `ex` toward `vi`.
- Optimized the editor for usability over 300-baud modem connections.
- Interpreted `vi` retrospectively as both constrained by its era and less programmable or modeless than Emacs.

## Evidence
- Berkeley origin: [[a-look-at-vim-a-text-editor-for-the-ages-the-new-stack]] says Joy wrote `vi` around 1976 as a U.C. Berkeley graduate student.
- Editor lineage: [[a-look-at-vim-a-text-editor-for-the-ages-the-new-stack]] describes Joy receiving Coulouris's `em` and modifying it into `en`, `ex`, and then `vi`.
- Collaboration: [[a-look-at-vim-a-text-editor-for-the-ages-the-new-stack]] reports Joy and Chuck Haley trading work, breakage, and fixes.
- Constraint-aware design: [[a-look-at-vim-a-text-editor-for-the-ages-the-new-stack]] quotes Joy explaining that `vi` had to feel productive over a 300-baud modem.
- Retrospective qualification: [[a-look-at-vim-a-text-editor-for-the-ages-the-new-stack]] quotes Joy wishing he had considered Emacs-like programmability and modelessness.
