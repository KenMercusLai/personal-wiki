## Overview
[[TonyHsieh]] is presented in the source as the [[Zappos]] CEO whose public schedule, email practices, meeting load, and transparency experiments offered a case study in executive work design.

## Current Profile
Daniel Rodic's analysis portrays Hsieh as a high-hours but not unusually early-starting executive. His workflow combines heavy email processing, meeting time, immediate handling of short replies, delegation through a chief-of-staff team, and public sharing of schedule and priorities. At Zappos, the same transparency preference is linked to customer-service participation and a company-wide Holacracy rollout intended to distribute authority, although the source does not establish the rollout's effects causally.

## Key Characteristics
- Led Zappos during the 2015 period analyzed by the source.
- Shared schedule and priorities publicly as part of a radical-transparency orientation.
- Used [[Yesterbox]] to bound daily email processing around the previous day's inbox.
- Relied on a chief-of-staff team to triage email volume beyond his individual capacity.
- Spent substantial reported time on email and in-person meetings.
- Promoted distributed authority through Zappos's company-wide Holacracy implementation.

## Evidence
- Schedule allocation: [[i-studied-the-zappos-ceos-schedule-for-a-year-heres-what-i-learned]] estimates a 75-hour total week, with about 35% of time on email and 24% in meetings.
- Email workflow: [[i-studied-the-zappos-ceos-schedule-for-a-year-heres-what-i-learned]] describes Yesterbox, quick replies, delegation, and chief-of-staff triage.
- Transparency: [[i-studied-the-zappos-ceos-schedule-for-a-year-heres-what-i-learned]] says Hsieh publicly shared schedule and priorities and advocated a transparent-first mindset.
- Organizational design: [[i-studied-the-zappos-ceos-schedule-for-a-year-heres-what-i-learned]] describes the 2015 company-wide Holacracy mandate and severance option for employees who declined it.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from one secondary practitioner article analyzing a public schedule rather than Hsieh's own full account. The saved source does not include the underlying dataset, its coding rules, missing-calendar treatment, or independent verification. Its schedule observations cover January through November 2015 and should not be treated as a timeless profile or a universal executive model.
