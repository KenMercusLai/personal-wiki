## Overview
[[AJSmart]] is a Berlin product-design consultancy represented here by its move from research-heavy engagements toward rapid, prototype-led Design Sprints and by its remote redesign of [[Oak]].

## Current Profile
In [[JonathanCourtney]]'s accounts, AJ&Smart moved away from six-to-eight-week research-heavy packages after finding that realistic prototype tests drove more useful learning. The Oak case shows how the consultancy operationalized that preference across continents: an expert interview, shared mapping, cross-industry inspiration, hand-drawn concepts, voting, narrated feedback, animated prototype variants, and repeated check-ins produced client alignment in the first week before two weeks of screen design and developer handoff. It also exposes a boundary between sprint rhetoric and delivery reality because the engagement lasted four weeks, expanded from one screen to a full redesign, and does not document end-user testing.

## Key Characteristics
- Previously used a conventional research-first UX process.
- Found that early research artifacts often lost influence once product testing began.
- Replaced a long client package with a one-week prototype-and-test sprint in the reported case.
- Serves as the experiential basis for Courtney's prototype-first argument.
- Adapted sprint exercises for distributed client collaboration through explicit artifacts and repeated alignment.
- Used staged commitment—from sketches and votes to prototypes and full production—to reduce design-direction uncertainty.

## Evidence
- Former process: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] describes two to four weeks of interviews, personas, empathy maps, and documentation inside a six-to-eight-week package.
- Learning source: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] says first user tests produced the actionable data while earlier artifacts gathered dust.
- Sprint trial: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] reports starting cold and reaching a realistic test in five days.
- Remote coordination: [[from-idea-to-app-store-a-design-sprint-case-study-ux-planet]] documents shared notes, client inspiration, concept votes, narrated feedback, prototype variants, and additional check-ins across Berlin and San Francisco.
- Delivery boundary: [[from-idea-to-app-store-a-design-sprint-case-study-ux-planet]] reports first-week alignment, two further weeks of screen design, four weeks to handoff, and no representative-user test.

## Qualifications
Both sources are favorable first-person essays from a founding partner. They do not provide matched baselines, independent outcome measures, unsuccessful sprint cases, user-test evidence for Oak, or proof that the design process caused the app's launch reception.

## What Changed
- Added the Oak case as a concrete remote-delivery workflow while separating client alignment from user validation.
- Qualified the sprint label with the project's four-week duration and exceptional scope expansion.
