## Overview
[[AJSmart]] is a Berlin product-design consultancy represented here by its move from a research-heavy design package to a Design Sprint model.

## Current Profile
In [[JonathanCourtney]]'s account, AJ&Smart formerly sold six-to-eight-week UX and product-design engagements with two to four weeks of interviews, personas, empathy maps, and documentation. The firm changed course after finding that realistic user tests drove the useful design learning and then trialed a five-day [[DesignSprint]] with a new client. The case is presented as an internal process correction, not a controlled comparison of consulting methods.

## Key Characteristics
- Previously used a conventional research-first UX process.
- Found that early research artifacts often lost influence once product testing began.
- Replaced a long client package with a one-week prototype-and-test sprint in the reported case.
- Serves as the experiential basis for Courtney's prototype-first argument.

## Evidence
- Former process: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] describes two to four weeks of interviews, personas, empathy maps, and documentation inside a six-to-eight-week package.
- Learning source: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] says first user tests produced the actionable data while earlier artifacts gathered dust.
- Sprint trial: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] reports starting cold and reaching a realistic test in five days.

## Qualifications
The source is a founding partner's retrospective marketing-era essay about one named process shift. It does not report comparable project baselines, client outcomes, the sprint's later product performance, or unsuccessful sprint cases.
