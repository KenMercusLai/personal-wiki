## Overview
[[KurtGessler]] is represented as a Chicago Tribune digital editor who analyzed the newspaper's organic Facebook reach in 2017.

## Current Profile
Gessler combined 30 Facebook Insights exports covering 15 months, compared averages with the median, and grouped posts into reach bands to show that the Tribune's distribution problem was concentrated in a rapidly growing number of severe misses. He considered several competing explanations and kept Facebook's ranking change as a hypothesis because the observational data could not identify a cause.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses distribution-sensitive measures rather than relying only on averages.
- Connects newsroom publishing practice with platform analytics.
- Separates observed deterioration from causal speculation.

## Evidence
- Data assembly: [[facebooks-algorithm-isnt-surfacing-one-third-of-our-posts-and-its-getting-worse]] says Gessler merged 30 exports covering 15 months of Facebook posts.
- Diagnostic method: [[facebooks-algorithm-isnt-surfacing-one-third-of-our-posts-and-its-getting-worse]] compares daily average, average per post, median, and four reach buckets.
- Causal restraint: [[facebooks-algorithm-isnt-surfacing-one-third-of-our-posts-and-its-getting-worse]] evaluates frequency, format mix, Instant Articles, and ranking updates without claiming to isolate the cause.
