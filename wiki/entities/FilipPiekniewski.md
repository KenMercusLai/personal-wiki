## Overview
[[FilipPiekniewski]] appears in the wiki as the author of a 2018 skeptical essay predicting that deep-learning hype was likely to turn into an [[AIWinter]].

## Current Profile
In this source, Piekniewski is an AI critic focused on the mismatch between public deep-learning narratives and real-world progress. He uses autonomous-driving failures, compute-scaling limits, spurious visual correlations, and skeptical voices such as Gary Marcus to argue that deep learning had not delivered the generality implied by corporate and media rhetoric.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes from a skeptical position toward deep-learning hype.
- Treats autonomous driving as the clearest real-world test of AI capability claims.
- Distinguishes compute growth from broad intelligence or robust perception.
- Favors predictive world-model-style approaches over simple classification or sparse image-to-action mappings.

## Evidence
- Skeptical forecast: [[ai-winter-is-well-on-its-way-piekniewskis-blog]] predicts that an AI winter is likely because deep-learning promises had outrun visible progress.
- Autonomous-driving focus: [[ai-winter-is-well-on-its-way-piekniewskis-blog]] repeatedly uses self-driving failures and the Uber crash as evidence of deep-learning brittleness.
- Scaling critique: [[ai-winter-is-well-on-its-way-piekniewskis-blog]] reads the compute-growth chart as showing that more compute did not create proportional real-world capability.
- Alternative direction: [[ai-winter-is-well-on-its-way-piekniewskis-blog]] argues for predicting the whole perceptual input along with action as a path toward semantic understanding.
