## Overview
[[BlackBerry]] appears in the wiki as a pre-iPhone smartphone incumbent whose hardware and software platform collapsed after [[Apple]] and [[Google]] moved the market into the modern app-centered [[MobileEcosystem]].

## Current Profile
The source presents BlackBerry as the clearest case of "seeing without seeing." Its leaders recognized that the [[IPhone]] represented a deeper software-platform leap, but BlackBerry stayed with its old platform for years before acquiring [[QNX]] in 2010. The article says unit sales peaked at 14.6 million in Q4 2010 and fell to 1.7 million three years later, while 2016 results showed shrinking revenue, large losses, and a formal end to internal hardware development. The inspected device image also underscores the legacy design center: a physical keyboard handset with BlackBerry services and a traditional app grid rather than a modern full-touch app ecosystem.

## Key Characteristics
- Legacy smartphone maker built around messaging, physical keyboards, and enterprise reputation.
- Recognized the iPhone as a serious software-platform threat but delayed replacing its own operating-system base.
- Acquired [[QNX]] too late to catch up with iOS and Android application ecosystems.
- Exited internal hardware development in 2016 and moved toward outsourced hardware plus software, security, and applications.
- Suffered a steep unit-sales collapse from a 2010 peak to low single-digit millions by 2013.

## Evidence
- Strategic pivot: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] cites John Chen saying BlackBerry would end internal hardware development and outsource hardware to partners.
- Financial decline: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] reports a swing from profit on $1.2B revenue in early 2015 to a $1B loss on $734M revenue in the comparable 2016 period.
- Unit collapse: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] says BlackBerry units fell from 14.6M in Q4 2010 to 1.7M three years later.
- Platform delay: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] says BlackBerry waited until April 2010 to buy [[QNX]] despite recognizing the iPhone's software depth in 2007.
- Android move: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] says a later Android-only move was framed as "best of both worlds" but failed to revive demand.

## Qualifications
The source is a 2016 interpretive essay and does not cover BlackBerry's later software, cybersecurity, licensing, automotive, patent, or enterprise-service history. Its failure diagnosis emphasizes platform timing and software architecture more than carrier relationships, enterprise buying, pricing, industrial design, or internal organizational detail.
