## Overview
[[HackReactor]] is represented here as a premium JavaScript coding bootcamp through one graduate's critical account of its 2016 curriculum, teaching model, tuition, and employment claims.

## Current Profile
The captured source describes a roughly twelve-week program split between six weeks of compressed technical sprints and six weeks centered on team projects and job-search preparation. Students reportedly learned JavaScript-based browser and server development through short lectures, paired exercises, solution videos, projects, and help queues staffed partly by recent graduates. The author argues that this structure, limited language breadth, aging equipment, and disputed outcome framing did not justify tuition then reported at US$19,780.

This is a historical profile of how one graduate experienced the program, not a current institutional description. The source does not independently establish cohort economics, teaching quality across instructors, audited placement outcomes, or how the curriculum and support model later changed.

## Key Characteristics
- Presented in the source as an intensive, selective programming bootcamp charging premium tuition.
- Used short lectures, paired two-day technical sprints, solution videos, assessments, and project work in the described 2016 curriculum.
- Concentrated technical instruction in the first half and team projects plus career preparation in the second half.
- Taught a JavaScript-centered full-stack curriculum spanning browser applications, Node servers, databases, authentication, deployment, and multiple frontend frameworks.
- Used some recent graduates as temporary instructional support, a model the author considered inadequate for best-practice guidance.
- Marketed graduate job outcomes that the author believed were weakened by a longer placement window and an already-qualified student population.

## Evidence
- Program structure: [[confessions-of-a-20k-bootcamp-graduate-hack-reactor]] lists about six weeks of orientation, data structures, algorithms, frontend and server work, databases, deployment, projects, and assessment, followed by about six weeks of thesis and hiring sprints.
- Teaching model: [[confessions-of-a-20k-bootcamp-graduate-hack-reactor]] describes hour-long lectures, paired two-day sprints, solution videos, help queues, and recent-graduate support staff.
- Curriculum scope: [[confessions-of-a-20k-bootcamp-graduate-hack-reactor]] records JavaScript-centered browser and server technologies and criticizes the absence of a second programming language.
- Price and resources: [[confessions-of-a-20k-bootcamp-graduate-hack-reactor]] reports US$19,780 tuition, older Mac minis, unreliable peripherals, and slow internet in the technical phase.
- Outcome framing: [[confessions-of-a-20k-bootcamp-graduate-hack-reactor]] disputes a reported shift from 99% placement within three months to 98% within six months and questions selection effects.

## Qualifications
Every characteristic comes from one pseudonymous graduate's 2016 article. The source supplies no audited outcome methodology, representative student survey, instructor response, cohort comparison, or counterfactual employment data. Its negative value judgment may accurately describe the author's experience without representing every cohort, and none of its operational details should be treated as current.
