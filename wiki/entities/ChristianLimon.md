## Overview
[[ChristianLimon]] is the author of a 2016 product-strategy essay proposing that vanity is the strongest motivation available to consumer social networks.

## Current Profile
In the available source, Limon defines vanity as concern for others' perception of oneself rather than simply excessive self-admiration. He turns that definition into [[SocialDriverHierarchy]], placing utility, content, community, and vanity in ascending order of claimed network strength and defensibility. His examples span entertainment, intellectual sharing, wellness, and incumbent-platform shifts, but the essay provides a practitioner thesis rather than comparative research.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames visible recognition and local celebrity as product-design inputs.
- Distinguishes four social motivations and claims higher layers are harder to copy.
- Uses 2016 platform cases to connect cultural norms with product opportunity.
- Treats vanity loops as capable of supporting both entertainment and prosocial communities.

## Evidence
- Hierarchy: [[vanity-is-good-a-hierarchy-of-social-drivers-christian-limon-medium]] orders utility, content, community, and vanity as progressively stronger social drivers.
- Product test: [[vanity-is-good-a-hierarchy-of-social-drivers-christian-limon-medium]] asks whether a loop increases an ordinary user's feeling of personal celebrity.
- Case method: [[vanity-is-good-a-hierarchy-of-social-drivers-christian-limon-medium]] applies the thesis to lip-sync media, Twitter idea sharing, PumpUp wellness support, Facebook's publishing shift, and Snapchat self-recording.

## Qualifications
The profile is grounded in one 2016 essay and does not establish Limon's wider career, later views, or empirical validation of the hierarchy. The named platform conditions are historical snapshots.
