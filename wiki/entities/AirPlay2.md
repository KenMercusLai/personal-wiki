## Overview
[[AirPlay2]] is Apple's media streaming protocol in the source, presented as a way to expand playback to non-Apple speakers and televisions while keeping Apple devices central to the user experience.

## Current Profile
Cybart treats expanded AirPlay 2 support as one of the most important mechanisms in Apple's content distribution strategy. AirPlay 2 lets content controlled from an iPhone, iPad, or Mac appear on compatible third-party speakers or TV sets. In the source's interpretation, that makes third-party hardware a distribution surface without surrendering the Apple device's role as the user's control point.

## Key Characteristics
- Extends Apple media playback to compatible non-Apple speakers and televisions.
- Keeps iPhone, iPad, or Mac involved in the content experience.
- Supports selective openness without requiring Apple to build every hardware category.
- Helps reduce the risk that Apple users turn to rival content services.

## Evidence
- Expanded support: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] says Apple widened AirPlay 2 support across high-end television sets.
- Control path: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] says users can send content from an iPhone, iPad, or Mac to non-Apple speakers or televisions through AirPlay 2.
- Strategic role: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] calls AirPlay a way to keep an Apple product at the center of people's lives.

## Qualifications
The source discusses AirPlay 2 strategically and does not evaluate implementation details, compatibility, latency, audio/video quality, or later ecosystem adoption.

## What Changed
