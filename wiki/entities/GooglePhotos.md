## Overview
[[GooglePhotos]] is presented as the later Google product that benefited from people and ideas carried forward from [[Bump]], [[Flock]], and Photoroll, and as Lieb's context for product decisions around user trust and control.

## Current Profile
The sources do not describe Google Photos broadly; they position the product as the eventual destination of Bump team's photo-sharing learning and as a later setting for Lieb's cognitive-simplicity advice. After [[Google]] acquired Bump, the team moved quickly into Google work, retained nearly all members for years, and Lieb says they were fortunate to play a large role in creating Google Photos. The First Round article adds the Free Up Space example, where a user-pressed button can be more reassuring than fully automatic device-storage cleanup because people understand and control what is happening to their photos.

## Key Characteristics
- Receives lineage from Bump team's photo-sharing exploration.
- Is connected to unreleased Photoroll ideas rather than only the original Bump app.
- Serves as evidence that an exploratory startup path can lead to a later major product.
- Is tied in the source to unusually high post-acquisition team retention.
- Illustrates the trust value of visible user control for sensitive actions such as freeing device storage.

## Evidence
- Product lineage: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says Photoroll's unreleased ideas later came back in the form of Google Photos.
- Team continuity: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says the Bump team began work at [[Google]] after acquisition and that 15 of 17 members remained after five years.
- Creation role: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says the team played a large role in creating Google Photos.
- Free Up Space example: [[cognitive-overhead-is-your-products-overlord-topple-it-with-these-tips-first-round-review]] says Google Photos' user-triggered cleanup became popular because it let users understand and control photo removal from the device after backup.

## Qualifications
This page reflects Lieb-linked sources about Bump's lineage and one Google Photos feature example. It does not cover Google Photos' full product history, other teams, launch details, feature-by-feature influence, or current storage behavior.
