## Overview
[[MichaelSimmons]] is an author represented in the wiki by an essay about finding high-leverage knowledge amid information abundance and attention-capturing media.

## Current Profile
Simmons presents deliberate learning as a competitive and personal advantage. He divides online information conditions into content shock, echo chambers, constant distraction, and fear of missing out, then recommends proactive source selection, disconfirming evidence, [[MentalModels]], and [[LearningHowToLearn]]. The essay also promotes his courses and mental-model membership, so its conceptual framework and commercial funnel are intertwined.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames information selection as a scarce-attention problem.
- Distinguishes life-changing knowledge from merely confirming or entertaining material.
- Advocates mental models as compressed, durable, cross-domain tools.
- Connects media discipline with scientific reasoning and awareness of cognitive bias.

## Evidence
- Attention framing: [[while-everyone-is-distracted-by-social-media-successful-people-double-down-on-an-underrated-skill]] organizes the problem around content growth, echo chambers, distraction, and fear of missing out.
- Learning filter: [[while-everyone-is-distracted-by-social-media-successful-people-double-down-on-an-underrated-skill]] asks whether a source could fundamentally change the learner's life.
- Knowledge format: [[while-everyone-is-distracted-by-social-media-successful-people-double-down-on-an-underrated-skill]] ranks mental models above posts, books, book summaries, and field summaries for density and transfer.
- Commercial context: [[while-everyone-is-distracted-by-social-media-successful-people-double-down-on-an-underrated-skill]] repeatedly directs readers to Simmons's learning products.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one practitioner essay rather than independent evidence about Simmons's methods or their outcomes. Its examples are illustrative, its strongest comparative claims are not experimentally tested in the source, and its product promotion gives the author an interest in elevating mental-model study.
