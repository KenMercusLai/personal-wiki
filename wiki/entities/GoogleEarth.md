## Overview
[[GoogleEarth]] is presented as Google's planetary visualization product, built from Keyhole's EarthViewer and recast as a future canvas for place-based storytelling.

## Current Profile
The source connects Google Earth's history to Keyhole, military and intelligence-adjacent satellite imagery, CNN war coverage, and Google's 2004 acquisition of the EarthViewer technology. By 2016, much of its imagery had been incorporated into [[GoogleMaps]] satellite view, while Google planned to bring Earth and Maps closer together. Parsons and Rebecca Moore describe the future Google Earth as a web and mobile platform for authoring, sharing, feeds, and planetary stories.

## Key Characteristics
- Originated in Keyhole's EarthViewer, acquired by Google after a 2004 demo to Larry Page and Sergey Brin.
- Began with uneven data rights and U.S.-centered coverage, reflecting the priorities and available data of its makers.
- Functions as a visual canvas for user-authored place content rather than only a static globe viewer.
- Is planned in the source as a browser-like way to visit places and tell geographic stories.
- Supports Google Earth Outreach work with groups such as Indigenous communities mapping territory in their own symbols.

## Evidence
- Keyhole origin: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] traces EarthViewer from Keyhole through Google's acquisition and launch of Google Earth.
- Coverage bias: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] notes that early Google Earth had missing oceans and started from data most important to its makers.
- Product convergence: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] says Earth imagery was largely incorporated into Maps satellite view and that Google wanted Maps and Earth closer together.
- Storytelling platform: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] quotes Parsons and Moore describing Earth as a browser for the planet, a canvas, and a dashboard-like place to discover events.
- Outreach mapping: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] describes Rebecca Moore's work with Amazon Indigenous groups using Google Earth to map territory with local symbols.

## Qualifications
The article anticipates a future Google Earth and therefore mixes existing 2016 product facts with Google leaders' plans. It does not evaluate whether those planned web, mobile, feed, or storytelling capabilities later succeeded.

## What Changed
- Created Google Earth as a planetary visualization and storytelling platform tied to Google's broader mapping strategy.

## Relationships
- [[Google]] - acquired Keyhole and operates Google Earth.
- [[GoogleMaps]] - related product whose satellite view absorbed much Earth imagery.
- [[EdParsons]] - describes Google Earth as a future browser for the planet.
- [[DigitalCartography]] - Earth embodies screen-based, 3-D, and user-authored mapping.
- [[UserGeneratedMapping]] - planned Earth authoring turns personal stories into place-based map content.
