## Overview
[[NBA]] is the professional basketball league represented in these sources as an early major-sports adopter of scheduled virtual-reality production and as the live-event setting for a later [[VisionPro]] test of presence-first broadcasting.

## Current Profile
In the 2017 account, the NBA had progressed from one-off experiments—including a 2015 live VR broadcast—to a multi-year [[NextVR]] partnership built around 25 productions, weekly League Pass games, all 30 teams, and postgame Finals highlights. [[JeffMarsilio]] framed live games as the primary product, supplemented by documentaries, talk-show storytelling, training, promotion, and exploratory esports work.

The league used VR to address a structural access gap between its reported global fan base and the small share able to attend arenas. Its operating posture combined long-term optimism with iterative production learning and explicit caution: engagement was modest compared with conventional video, the experience was difficult to market without a headset, and the article supplied no financial or adoption outcomes.

The 2026 account adds a later but narrower product judgment. A Bucks-Lakers immersive broadcast made scorer's-table and baseline positions feel convincingly courtside, yet producer-directed switching repeatedly broke spatial orientation. Only six Lakers games were announced for the 2025–26 immersive schedule. This supports the original presence thesis but qualifies the expectation that recurring production and additional camera work would necessarily improve the experience or scale availability.

## Key Characteristics
- Treated recurring live production as more valuable for learning than isolated demonstrations.
- Positioned virtual reality as an additive, highly immersive option alongside television and mobile.
- Connected media experimentation to global fan access, brand leadership, storytelling, training, and prospective interaction.
- Demonstrated that stable courtside viewpoints can convey player scale and physicality, while directed camera switching can undermine presence.
- Used engagement duration, enjoyment, social response, and game-by-game improvement as early signals while acknowledging limited reach and later event scarcity.

## Evidence
- Production commitment and access: [[inside-the-nbas-virtual-reality-strategy]] reports 25 planned NextVR broadcasts, weekly League Pass availability, representation of every team, and global rollout outside China.
- Portfolio strategy: [[inside-the-nbas-virtual-reality-strategy]] describes live games, Finals highlights, Oculus content, House of Legends, referee training, and exploratory NBA 2K work.
- Measurement and constraints: [[inside-the-nbas-virtual-reality-strategy]] reports modest numbers, a focus on engagement quality, offseason production iteration, headset-marketing difficulty, and internal caution about technological forecasts.
- Presence evidence: [[apple-you-still-dont-understand-the-vision-pro]] reports that scorer's-table and baseline cameras each reproduced distinct qualities of courtside attendance.
- Production qualification: [[apple-you-still-dont-understand-the-vision-pro]] says forced camera changes felt like repeated teleportation and made the headset experience worse than television.
- Later supply: [[apple-you-still-dont-understand-the-vision-pro]] reports six announced immersive Lakers games for the 2025–26 season.

## Qualifications
The profile combines a 2017 trade-press interview centered on an NBA executive with one enthusiast's 2026 account of one geographically restricted game. It does not establish current league-wide strategy, profitability, representative comfort, audience retention, production cost, rights economics, or whether immersive viewing became a durable third medium. Fan-base and arena-attendance figures lack underlying methodology, positive social response is unquantified, and the later six-game schedule does not by itself identify why coverage remained limited.

## What Changed
- Added a 2026 live-game account that validates the strength of stable courtside presence.
- Qualified the earlier production-learning thesis with evidence that directed multi-camera switching can degrade immersion.
- Added the six-game 2025–26 Lakers schedule as evidence of continued supply scarcity.
