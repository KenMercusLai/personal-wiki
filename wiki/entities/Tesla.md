## Overview
[[Tesla]] is represented as both one of Elon Musk's post-PayPal commitments and a 2017 autonomous-driving strategy built around a camera-equipped customer fleet, computer vision, and integrated control of vehicle data.

## Current Profile
The motivational source pairs Tesla with [[SpaceX]] to show how serious desire can pull a founder into a difficult, uncertain field. Evans' autonomy essay adds a specific historical strategy: Tesla planned to build the significant technology itself, retain its fleet data, and use eight cameras, forward radar, and ultrasonic sensors to gather mapping and driver-behavior observations. Because only the radar looked forward and practical lidar was absent, Evans describes this as a contrarian bet that computer vision would become capable enough to interpret the collected imagery before cheap lidar made the shortcut irrelevant.

## Key Characteristics
- Represents electric-vehicle ambition in the article's founder examples.
- Functions as evidence of desire expressed through concrete commitment.
- Is paired with SpaceX as part of Musk's all-in narrative.
- Uses vertical integration to keep vehicle data inside its own autonomy learning loop in the 2017 essay.
- Pursues a camera-led autonomy approach that trades early fleet reach against a harder computer-vision problem.

## Qualifications
The two sources do not provide a complete Tesla history, business assessment, current autonomy architecture, safety record, regulatory status, or later performance. Evans' sensor and capability claims are a 2017 snapshot and explicitly speculative: a large raw dataset has limited value if the system cannot interpret it, while cheaper lidar or slower progress elsewhere in the stack could erase the timing advantage he proposed.
