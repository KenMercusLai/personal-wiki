## Overview
[[Tesla]] is represented as one of Elon Musk's post-PayPal commitments, as a 2017 autonomous-driving strategy built around a camera-equipped customer fleet and integrated vehicle data, and through an incomplete 2018 memo about the Model 3 production ramp.

## Current Profile
The motivational source pairs Tesla with [[SpaceX]] to show how serious desire can pull a founder into a difficult, uncertain field. Evans' autonomy essay adds a specific historical strategy: Tesla planned to build the significant technology itself, retain its fleet data, and use eight cameras, forward radar, and ultrasonic sensors to gather mapping and driver-behavior observations. Because only the radar looked forward and practical lidar was absent, Evans describes this as a contrarian bet that computer vision would become capable enough to interpret the collected imagery before cheap lidar made the shortcut irrelevant.

The 2018 Mindset fragment adds a production-system view. Musk reports three Model 3 weeks above 2,000 vehicles, plans short factory shutdowns for upgrades, calls for around-the-clock Fremont production and rapid hiring, and requires internal teams and suppliers to demonstrate capacity above the desired near-term output. The stated rationale is [[ProductionCapacityBuffer]]: when thousands of components and logistics steps are coupled, nominal capacity without margin leaves whole-system output exposed to the weakest part. These are historical internal claims and targets, not independently verified outcomes.

## Key Characteristics
- Represents electric-vehicle ambition in the article's founder examples.
- Functions as evidence of desire expressed through concrete commitment.
- Is paired with SpaceX as part of Musk's all-in narrative.
- Uses vertical integration to keep vehicle data inside its own autonomy learning loop in the 2017 essay.
- Pursues a camera-led autonomy approach that trades early fleet reach against a harder computer-vision problem.
- Treated internal manufacturing, external suppliers, staffing, factory upgrades, and logistics as one coupled Model 3 production system in the 2018 memo.
- Required subsystem burst capacity above the near-term whole-system output target to create margin for variation.

## Qualifications
The sources do not provide a complete Tesla history, business assessment, current autonomy or manufacturing architecture, safety record, labor assessment, regulatory status, or current performance. Evans' sensor and capability claims are a 2017 snapshot and explicitly speculative: a large raw dataset has limited value if the system cannot interpret it, while cheaper lidar or slower progress elsewhere in the stack could erase the proposed timing advantage. The 2018 production source is an incomplete blog reproduction of an executive email; it reports internal numbers, plans, and aspirations without independently showing that the 6,000-per-week rate or tenfold precision goal was achieved at acceptable quality, cost, or worker impact.
