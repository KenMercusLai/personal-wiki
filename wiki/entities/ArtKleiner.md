## Overview
[[ArtKleiner]] is the author of the 2000 management essay "Corporate Culture in Internet Time," represented here through his account of early Internet-company culture, team stability, and organizational learning.

## Current Profile
Kleiner's contribution to the wiki is a distinction between two early-company subcultures: commercially oriented "hype" and production-oriented "craft." He argues that leaders cannot manufacture culture on demand, but can create persistent teams, learning infrastructure, boundaries, and experiments through which healthier norms develop.

## Key Characteristics
- Interprets corporate culture as an emergent result of behavior rather than a declared program.
- Uses the hype/craft distinction to diagnose conflict in venture-backed Internet companies.
- Treats stable teams as primary units of capability, mentoring, and cultural development.
- Recommends cross-team knowledge exchange and bounded team-level experimentation.

## Evidence
- Culture diagnosis: [[corporate-culture-in-internet-time]] contrasts deal-, capital-, and scale-oriented hype with reflective production craft.
- Team design: [[corporate-culture-in-internet-time]] recommends accountable persistent teams, postmortems, and leaders able to translate between subcultures.
- Organizational learning: [[corporate-culture-in-internet-time]] proposes rotations, shared standards, cross-team forums, and senior participation in knowledge exchange.

## Qualifications
The wiki profile is bounded to one 2000 essay. Its examples are anecdotal, its dot-com setting is historically specific, and it does not independently measure whether the proposed team structures improved delivery, retention, or organizational resilience.
