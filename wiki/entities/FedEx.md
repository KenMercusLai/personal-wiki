## Overview
[[FedEx]] is a parcel and logistics carrier evaluated in the 2016 source as a possible target of Amazon's emerging delivery network.

## Current Profile
The source presents FedEx as both an Amazon carrier and a possible future competitor. FedEx was among the networks strained during the 2013 holiday season, but CEO Fred Smith publicly called the idea of an Amazon challenge "fantastical" in 2016. An outside logistics scholar offered the counterview that industry concern rose when Amazon moved from same-day delivery into leased aircraft and warned that Amazon's characteristic low-price entry could destabilize shipping.

## Key Characteristics
- Carried Amazon packages and was strained alongside UPS during the 2013 holiday surge.
- Publicly dismissed Amazon as a direct competitive threat in 2016.
- Faced a stronger competitive signal once Amazon began leasing cargo aircraft.
- Could be pressured if Amazon applied low-price market entry to external logistics services.

## Evidence
- Holiday capacity: [[will-amazon-kill-fedex]] says FedEx, like UPS, struggled to meet deadlines amid the 2013 Amazon package surge.
- Public stance: [[will-amazon-kill-fedex]] reports CEO Fred Smith calling the competitive-threat thesis "fantastical."
- Strategic signal: [[will-amazon-kill-fedex]] says industry concern increased when Amazon began leasing aircraft rather than limiting itself to local same-day delivery.
- Price risk: [[will-amazon-kill-fedex]] quotes an outside scholar warning that Amazon's low-price entry pattern could destabilize shipping.

## Qualifications
This is a 2016 competitive snapshot, not a current assessment of FedEx's Amazon exposure, network performance, management view, or later competitive outcomes. The article reports public statements and outside interpretation but does not quantify FedEx's dependence on Amazon or prove that Amazon would sell delivery services externally.

## What Changed
- Created FedEx as an incumbent that publicly discounted Amazon's threat while outside observers treated aircraft leasing as a strategic escalation.
