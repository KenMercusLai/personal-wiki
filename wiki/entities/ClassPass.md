## Overview
[[ClassPass]] is the fitness and wellness marketplace presented in this source as [[PayalKadakia]]'s company and as a case in reaching [[ProductMarketFit]] through repeated product reinvention.

## Current Profile
The source traces ClassPass from its earlier Classtivity class-search concept through Passport, a paid 30-day package for trying different studio classes, into the subscription product that became the company's main model. Within this wiki, ClassPass is most important as a marketplace product-discovery case: the team moved from helping people find classes to motivating them to actually attend, discovered that variety was the stronger behavior, and chose reservations per person as the metric that joined customer habit, studio value, revenue, and mission impact.

## Key Characteristics
- Began as Classtivity, a search-and-booking product inspired by models such as OpenTable and Zocdoc.
- Failed to turn early attention into reservations, exposing a gap between market story and real user behavior.
- Used Passport to test whether prepaid class variety would motivate attendance.
- Reached a stronger model when customers worked around Passport limits, revealing demand for ongoing variety.
- Treated reservations per person as the clearest signal of product-market fit.
- Evolved through rebrands, product shutdowns, subscription pricing, and later credits-based wellness expansion.

## Evidence
- Product lineage: [[classpass-founder-on-how-marketplace-startups-can-achieve-product-market-fit-first-round-review]] says Classtivity moved from search to Passport and then to the ClassPass subscription.
- Failed initial fit: [[classpass-founder-on-how-marketplace-startups-can-achieve-product-market-fit-first-round-review]] says the company had press and funding but no meaningful reservations in the first version.
- Passport demand: [[classpass-founder-on-how-marketplace-startups-can-achieve-product-market-fit-first-round-review]] says Passport produced 20,000 reservations in its first six months.
- Variety insight: [[classpass-founder-on-how-marketplace-startups-can-achieve-product-market-fit-first-round-review]] says users created new accounts to keep using Passport, revealing that variety was the stronger value proposition.
- Fit metric: [[classpass-founder-on-how-marketplace-startups-can-achieve-product-market-fit-first-round-review]] says reservations per person became the company's true-north metric.

## Qualifications
The source is a founder interview and retrospective advice piece, not an independent audit of ClassPass' later marketplace economics, studio satisfaction, pricing effects, or competitive history. It is strongest for the company's self-understanding of its early product-market-fit journey.
