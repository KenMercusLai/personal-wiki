## Overview
[[KennethFriedman]] is represented in the wiki as the author of a 2018 tutorial for batching [[Gmail]] delivery with filters and a time-driven Apps Script.

## Current Profile
The available source presents Friedman as a practitioner translating attention advice into a concrete technical constraint. His design hides unread arrivals behind a label, periodically restores them to the inbox, and allows explicit exceptions for urgent senders or keywords.

## Key Characteristics
- Advocates scheduled email visibility as protection against repeated checking.
- Uses built-in Google filtering, labeling, scripting, and trigger capabilities rather than a paid third-party batching service.
- Presents a copy-and-paste workflow for readers without programming experience.

## Evidence
- Behavioral aim: [[kf-batch-gmail]] argues that a constant trickle of messages fragments focused work and that scheduled releases can reduce the incentive to check.
- Technical method: [[kf-batch-gmail]] combines a Gmail filter with an Apps Script that restores labeled threads on a timer.
- Accessibility of setup: [[kf-batch-gmail]] frames the process as a roughly 20-minute configuration requiring code copying but no prior programming.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one tutorial rather than a broader biography or body of work. The source offers no outcome measurement, its interface steps are historical, and its confidence about approving broad Gmail access should not substitute for a current security review.
