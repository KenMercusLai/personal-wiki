## Overview
[[NathanKontny]] is represented in the wiki as the author of a marketing essay that applies [[MurrayDavis]]'s theory of interesting propositions to viral video and a satirical journalist-outreach campaign.

## Current Profile
Kontny writes from practitioner experience running a CRM and bulk-newsletter business that needed more press. He frames [[CaseyNeistat]]'s work and his own [[TrickAJournalist]] campaign through a common creative move: expose or invert something the audience takes for granted. His account is strongest as a method for generating provocative ideas, not as measured proof that expectation-breaking reliably causes reach or business results.

## Key Characteristics
- Connects sociology, filmmaking examples, and first-person marketing practice.
- Uses satire to criticize normalized mass outreach to journalists.
- Treats expectation-breaking as an idea filter rather than a guarantee.
- Reports campaign virality without publishing comparative performance data.

## Evidence
- Theoretical framing: [[viral-marketing-the-set-list-medium]] uses Davis's negation pattern to explain why some ideas feel interesting.
- Practitioner case: [[viral-marketing-the-set-list-medium]] says Kontny built Trick a Journalist, rejected undesirable customers, and advertised the satire across several platforms.
- Method boundary: [[viral-marketing-the-set-list-medium]] calls viral content difficult and unpredictable even while recommending the expectation-breaking filter.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one first-person essay. It does not independently verify Kontny's career history, the campaign's reach, or the causal contribution of any individual stunt.
