## Overview
[[MomoDog|Momo]] is Caleb Leak's nine-pound cavapoo, trained to touch a keyboard that supplies arbitrary input to an AI-assisted game-development system.

## Current Profile
Momo is the participant and comic premise of the project, not its game designer in a literal sense. She learned through chimes and food rewards to tap or swipe the keyboard; [[ClaudeCode]] and the human-built harness assign meaning to the resulting characters. The case therefore separates physical input generation from intent, interpretation, implementation, and verification.

## Key Characteristics
- Supplies genuine, uncontrolled keyboard input to the game-building workflow.
- Learned keyboard interaction through progressively automated food reinforcement over roughly two weeks.
- Was selected over the larger dog Hana because she learned the interaction quickly and typed more gently.
- Adapted to a dispenser bug by continuing to mash for a possible second immediate serving.

## Evidence
- Role boundary: [[i-taught-my-dog-to-vibe-code-games-caleb-leak]] explicitly says Momo is not secretly a designer and presses the keyboard because it produces kibble.
- Training process: [[i-taught-my-dog-to-vibe-code-games-caleb-leak]] describes treat association, rewarding paw swipes, raising the requirement from one to three swipes, and later automating a 16-character threshold.
- Selection: [[i-taught-my-dog-to-vibe-code-games-caleb-leak]] says Momo learned slightly faster and typed more gently than Hana.
- Unintended learning: [[i-taught-my-dog-to-vibe-code-games-caleb-leak]] reports that accidental repeated servings taught Momo to keep trying for another immediate reward.

## Qualifications
The training account is informal and self-reported, with inconsistent sessions and no controlled animal-behavior study. Game authorship is metaphorical: the system interprets arbitrary input, while humans and the coding agent supply the objectives, software, tools, and quality criteria.
