## Overview
[[NeilHunt]] is a computer scientist and longtime Netflix product and technology executive represented through a first-person history of the company's DVD subscription, recommendation, streaming, device, cloud, and production-technology transitions.

## Current Profile
Hunt describes moving from computer-vision and image-processing research into product engineering, joining [[ReedHastings]] at Pure Software, and then joining [[Netflix]] in early 1999 as VP of Internet Engineering. His account emphasizes mechanisms connecting product experience to business viability: the queue made a continuous DVD subscription practical, recommendations spread demand across inventory, HTTP simplified early streaming delivery, device neutrality widened distribution, and streaming uptime requirements forced a re-architecture around [[AWS]].

As Chief Product Officer, Hunt presents product, engineering, and business design as interdependent. He argues that behavioral viewing data improved enjoyment prediction and project-audience estimation, while explicitly rejecting the idea that Netflix used data to author stories. He also treats the DVD-streaming split as necessary [[StrategicSelfCannibalization]] whose customer handling was too aggressive.

## Key Characteristics
- Connected consumer-facing product mechanisms with inventory, subscription, and retention economics.
- Helped lead Netflix's transition from DVD fulfillment to internet streaming and broad device distribution.
- Favored ordinary HTTP and partner-compatible software over specialized protocols or first-party hardware dependence.
- Describes cloud migration as re-architecture for failure tolerance rather than relocation of a legacy monolith.
- Treats observed behavior as useful for recommendations and investment decisions while preserving creative autonomy.
- Acknowledges uncertainty, luck, and execution mistakes within an otherwise successful retrospective.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one long retrospective interview conducted near the end of Hunt's Netflix tenure. Hunt sometimes corrects dates or expresses uncertainty about chronology, and he explicitly defers content-strategy authority to Ted Sarandos. The account offers valuable participant evidence but not a complete company history, independent causal evaluation, or audited technical and business record.
