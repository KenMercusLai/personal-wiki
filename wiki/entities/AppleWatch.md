## Overview
[[AppleWatch]] is presented as Apple's wearable product that began as an iPhone companion, became a large early watch business, supplied learning for Apple's broader post-iPhone wearable strategy, moved toward proactive body-centered assistance with Series 4, and used entry pricing plus premium SKUs to widen Apple wearable adoption.

## Current Profile
[[HoraceDediu]] frames Apple Watch as both financially significant and strategically unfinished. By his estimate, it had sold about 15 million units in the previous twelve months at around $330 average revenue, giving it a revenue run rate slightly above [[Rolex]]'s 2016 sales; the embedded chart also estimates about 33 million cumulative units and $12.3 billion cumulative revenue. Strategically, the Watch still depends on [[IPhone]] activation and plan extension, but Series 3's LTE, W2 processor, faster Wi-Fi, faster processor, and improved efficiency suggest a path toward more independent [[WearableComputing]]. The pricing source adds that Apple was not keeping Watch artificially scarce or luxury-only: Series 1 at $269 made Watch one of the lowest-priced smartwatches worth buying, while Hermès and Edition versions allowed a much higher-end experience. The earlier Above Avalon smart-glasses source adds another role: Apple Watch is not only a wrist product, but also a learning base for proactive assistants, body-worn miniaturization, and possible support technology for future [[SmartGlasses]]. The connecting-the-dots source sharpens that role with Apple Watch Series 4: heart rhythm monitoring, fall detection, cellular independence, and on-body presence make Watch a proactive digital assistant that can take tasks the iPhone cannot naturally handle.

## Key Characteristics
- Reached meaningful early scale in units and revenue shortly after launch.
- Began as an [[IPhone]] companion rather than an independent computing platform.
- Added connectivity and silicon capabilities that made more independent use cases plausible.
- Took some lightweight communication, media, navigation, and call usage away from the iPhone.
- Repeated the original iPhone's iPod, internet communicator, and phone roles in a wrist-worn form.
- Opened a distinct health and fitness direction rather than merely shrinking the iPhone.
- Serves in the Above Avalon sources as a proactive body-worn assistant, possible bridge to future smart glasses, and segmented pricing case with accessible entry models plus premium Hermès or Edition variants.

## Evidence
- Revenue scale: [[a-small-screen-ipod-an-internet-communicator-and-a-phone-asymco]] estimates roughly 15 million annual units at around $330 average revenue, or about $4.9 billion.
- Cumulative scale: [[a-small-screen-ipod-an-internet-communicator-and-a-phone-asymco]] includes a chart estimating about 33 million units and $12.3 billion in cumulative Watch revenue.
- Companion status: [[a-small-screen-ipod-an-internet-communicator-and-a-phone-asymco]] says the Watch still needed iPhone activation and plan extension in 2017.
- Independence path: [[a-small-screen-ipod-an-internet-communicator-and-a-phone-asymco]] compares Watch dependence to the iPhone's former need for a PC or Mac.
- Capability gains: [[a-small-screen-ipod-an-internet-communicator-and-a-phone-asymco]] cites LTE, W2 silicon, faster Wi-Fi, faster processing, and better power efficiency.
- Health direction: [[a-small-screen-ipod-an-internet-communicator-and-a-phone-asymco]] treats fitness, resting rate, arrhythmia, and atrial fibrillation as evidence of a new product language around health.
- Wearables momentum: [[above-avalon-apple-is-figuring-out-whats-next]] pairs Apple Watch with AirPods as evidence that Apple was winning wrists and ears before the proposed battle for eyes.
- Glasses support: [[above-avalon-apple-is-figuring-out-whats-next]] argues Apple Watch could house technology and assistant learning useful to future smart glasses.
- Proactive assistant: [[above-avalon-connecting-the-apple-dots]] argues Series 4's heart and fall features show Watch handling body-centered tasks that will not be given to the iPhone.
- User-base trajectory: [[above-avalon-connecting-the-apple-dots]] estimates Apple Watch had reached about 40M users in three years and projects eventual iPhone decoupling as the key to a larger user base.
- Entry pricing: [[above-avalon-the-curious-state-of-apple-product-pricing]] says Apple cut the first-generation Watch into Apple Watch Series 1 at $269 after adding the dual-core processor.
- Market comparison: [[above-avalon-the-curious-state-of-apple-product-pricing]] argues Series 1 at $269 and Series 2 at $349 were competitive against other smartwatches and narrowed the gap with fitness trackers.
- Premium segmentation: [[above-avalon-the-curious-state-of-apple-product-pricing]] contrasts low entry prices with Apple Watch Hermès and Edition models starting above $1,100.

## Qualifications
The Asymco source is an analyst estimate from 2017, not audited Apple Watch unit reporting. It also projects future independence and category direction from Series 3 capabilities. The Above Avalon sources add pricing, speculative glasses-enabler, iPhone-alternative, and eventual decoupling roles, so later product, pricing, and adoption outcomes need separate sources before being treated as confirmed.

## What Changed
