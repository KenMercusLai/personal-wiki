## Overview
[[IBM]] is presented in a 2018 [[Stratechery]] essay as an enterprise-technology company that recovered in the 1990s by selling integrated outcomes across fragmented Internet technologies, later missed the hyperscale public-cloud shift, and sought a new position through its announced acquisition of [[RedHat]].

## Current Profile
The source's historical model separates IBM's products from its differentiated asset: global enterprise presence and enough breadth to accept responsibility for an entire problem. Under [[LouGerstner]], IBM integrated open technologies, services, operations, and proprietary middleware into one accountable solution. Thompson argues that this advantage weakened when standardized cloud services became good enough and IBM underinvested in infrastructure. The proposed response was [[HybridCloudStrategy]] built around Red Hat and [[OpenShift]], but the essay leaves customer demand and organizational readiness unresolved.

## Key Characteristics
- Used scale, breadth, and global enterprise relationships to sell solutions rather than isolated products.
- Captured value by integrating commoditized technologies with services and proprietary middleware.
- Accumulated customer lock-in and organizational complacency alongside the model's success.
- Underinvested in hyperscale public cloud according to the source's historical argument.
- Turned to Red Hat and portable container infrastructure as a proposed hybrid-cloud layer above other providers.

## Evidence
- Integration advantage: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] says enterprises valued one globally present supplier able to integrate many immature Internet technologies.
- Value capture: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] connects services and systems integration to profitable IBM middleware and increasing customer dependence.
- Cloud miss: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] argues the 2015 earnings roadmap and low infrastructure investment left IBM unable to catch hyperscale providers.
- Strategic response: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] identifies Red Hat and OpenShift as the basis for bridging private systems with several public clouds.

## Qualifications
This profile reflects one analyst's 2018 historical interpretation and forecast. It is not a current IBM profile, does not establish the later outcome of the Red Hat acquisition, and relies partly on IBM-supplied workload claims and historical financial snapshots.

## What Changed
- Created IBM's profile around the conditional advantage of enterprise integration and the proposed shift from public-cloud competition to hybrid-cloud orchestration.

- [[EnterpriseIntegrationBusinessModel]] - explains how IBM monetized breadth, accountability, and integration.
- [[HybridCloudStrategy]] - proposed post-hyperscale-cloud position described in the source.
- [[Microsoft]] - hybrid-cloud competitor with its own infrastructure investment.
