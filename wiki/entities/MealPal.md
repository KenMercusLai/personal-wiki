## Overview
[[MealPal]] is represented as a lunch subscription whose customer and restaurant constraints illustrate how deliberately added friction can support a different service model.

## Current Profile
The source describes customers paying and selecting meals in advance, choosing only one offering from each participating restaurant, and collecting the food themselves. Those restrictions are presented as a trade: customers receive discounted meals without delivery fees and spend less time waiting or choosing, while restaurants gain advance demand visibility, bulk preparation, and potentially higher labor utilization and throughput. The model time-shifts demand rather than removing work altogether, and group pickup can create a shared office ritual.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses subscription and advance meal selection to move demand decisions earlier.
- Limits each restaurant to one available option in the described flow.
- Requires customer pickup rather than delivery.
- Trades variety and spontaneity for lower price and less point-of-sale delay.
- Gives restaurants earlier demand information that may support bulk preparation and throughput.

## Evidence
- Customer constraints: [[in-products-as-in-life-not-all-friction-is-bad-better-everyday]] lists advance payment and selection, one option per restaurant, and self-pickup.
- Customer exchange: [[in-products-as-in-life-not-all-friction-is-bad-better-everyday]] says those constraints support discounted prices, no delivery fee, shorter waits, and less deliberation.
- Restaurant exchange: [[in-products-as-in-life-not-all-friction-is-bad-better-everyday]] links advance orders with bulk preparation, worker utilization, and throughput.
- Social effect: [[in-products-as-in-life-not-all-friction-is-bad-better-everyday]] describes team lunch pickup as a communal and shareable moment.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one favorable 2017 portfolio-company example and supplies no prices, subscription terms, restaurant margins, order accuracy, waste, retention, geographic coverage, accessibility analysis, or before-and-after operational measures. Advance choice and pickup may shift cost and coordination onto customers, and the article does not establish that every customer or restaurant realizes the claimed benefits.

## What Changed
- Established MealPal as a bounded example of time-shifting demand through deliberate customer constraints.
- Preserved the distinction between lower point-of-sale friction and effort moved into advance planning and pickup.
