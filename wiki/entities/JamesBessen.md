## Overview
[[JamesBessen]] is an economist cited for explaining how ATM adoption changed bank-teller work toward marketing, interpersonal service, and customer relationships.

## Current Profile
The represented source uses Bessen’s bank-teller account as a concrete case of partial automation. ATMs took over cash-handling activities and reduced branch operating costs, but teller employment did not simply vanish; branch expansion and role redesign increased the relative importance of sales and interpersonal capabilities inside the occupation.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses the ATM and bank-teller case to explain task substitution within an occupation.
- Emphasizes changing skill composition rather than a one-step machine-for-worker replacement.
- Connects automation with customer-relationship and marketing responsibilities.

## Evidence
Bank-teller redesign:
- [[in-a-robot-economy-all-humans-will-be-marketers-bloomberg]] quotes Bessen describing tellers as becoming part of banks’ customer-relationship teams as cash handling declined.

Employment mechanism:
- [[in-a-robot-economy-all-humans-will-be-marketers-bloomberg]] says cheaper branch operation supported more branches and staff, qualifying a simple ATM-causes-teller-loss narrative.

## Qualifications
The wiki currently represents Bessen through Cowen’s short quotation and summary, not Bessen’s complete underlying research. The source supplies no teller-employment series, branch counts, causal design, periodization details, wage outcomes, job-quality measures, or distributional analysis.
