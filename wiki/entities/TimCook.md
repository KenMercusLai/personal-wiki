## Overview
[[TimCook]] appears in the Stratechery sources as Apple's former CEO, associated with extraordinary revenue and profit scaling, the Services narrative, the insistence that Apple's active-device base is the right strategic measurement lens, and the retail framing behind [[TodayAtApple]], but also with questions about product pruning and AI preparedness.

## Current Profile
The Apple middle-age source presents Cook as managing Apple after the Steve Jobs rebirth period, when the premium smartphone market had matured and Apple's largest opportunities increasingly came from its existing installed base. Thompson cites Cook's earnings-call framing around Services and active devices to argue that Apple remained a vertical hardware company: Services mattered because they made Apple devices more valuable, and active devices mattered because Apple could sell users more hardware, services, and ecosystem-specific accessories. The Apple social-network source adds that Cook introduced Today at Apple as part of the retail mission to educate and inspire customers, but also quotes his refusal to release a user count because Apple preferred active devices as the more accurate measure.

The 2026 HomeView source assesses Cook after his retirement. Thompson credits Cook with taking Apple to $436 billion in annual revenue and $118 billion in profit without a post-founder collapse, but questions whether a services focus and the removal of businesses too small to affect total revenue weakened the integrated user experience. AirPort's discontinuation is the concrete case, while Apple's dependence on outside models for Siri raises a Cook Doctrine problem around owning the primary technologies behind Apple products. The judgment remains mixed: Apple Silicon and wearables are material investments associated with Cook's tenure, while HomeView exposes unresolved AI and product-usefulness costs.

## Key Characteristics
- CEO of Apple in the source's 2018 strategy analysis.
- Publicly emphasized Services growth as iPhone unit growth slowed.
- Preferred active devices over user counts as the measurement frame for Apple.
- Represents the mature-Apple operating context after Steve Jobs' return-and-growth era.
- Frames Apple retail stores as education and inspiration venues through Today at Apple.
- Scaled Apple to extraordinary revenue and profit while avoiding an immediate post-founder decline.
- Associated in the newest source with a contested tradeoff between Services-scale focus and smaller products that improve the integrated experience.

## Evidence
- Services narrative: [[apples-middle-age-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] says Cook and [[LucaMaestri]] had been pushing the narrative that Apple was a services company since the 1Q 2016 earnings call.
- Hardware model: [[apples-middle-age-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] quotes Cook's 2016 premium-product answer as evidence that Apple remained focused on differentiated hardware.
- Active devices: [[apples-middle-age-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] cites Cook saying Apple would not release a user number because active devices were the more accurate and proper lens.
- Mature context: [[apples-middle-age-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] contrasts Cook's strategic questions with the different circumstances Steve Jobs faced.
- Retail mission: [[apples-social-network-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] quotes Cook introducing Today at Apple as part of Apple's effort to educate and inspire users in stores.
- Metrics dispute: [[apples-social-network-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] argues Cook's active-device preference was less useful to investors than active-customer and revenue-per-user metrics.
- Scale record: [[do-you-trust-apple]] credits Cook with growing Apple to $436 billion in revenue and $118 billion in annual profit before retiring in 2026.
- Product-pruning critique: [[do-you-trust-apple]] uses AirPort's abandonment to question whether businesses too small to move Apple's totals were undervalued despite ecosystem benefits.
- Core-technology critique: [[do-you-trust-apple]] argues that Apple's reliance on partner models for Siri conflicts with Cook's doctrine that Apple should own the primary technologies behind its products.
- Investment counterweight: [[do-you-trust-apple]] credits Apple Silicon and wearables as meaningful Cook-era investments while noting that the silicon effort began under Jobs.

## Qualifications
This page is source-scoped to two 2018 Stratechery articles and one 2026 retrospective. The revenue and profit figures, retirement framing, Siri-model ownership, and interpretation of Cook's priorities come from Thompson's article rather than a full audited career assessment. AirPort does not by itself establish the effects of every product-pruning decision, and Apple's AI partnerships may still produce compelling experiences. The page does not comprehensively cover Cook's supply-chain record, privacy positioning, regulatory environment, management decisions, or the outcomes of later services and hardware investments.
