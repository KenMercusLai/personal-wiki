## Overview
[[MarcAndreessen]] appears in the sources as a venture-capital voice on change-oriented investing and emotionally detached competition.

## Current Profile
Housel quotes Andreessen saying venture capital bets for change while Buffett bets against it, then rejects a clean binary: venture investments still need timeless customer desires, and value investments still experience changing technologies, channels, and operating methods. Happy Xiao attributes a second formulation to Andreessen: a16z is intensely competitive without centering emotional reward, illustrated by professional gamblers training themselves to make wagers without attachment. Together the essays use Andreessen to separate disciplined competition from both static strategy and result-driven emotion, although neither supplies a complete account of his views or evidence for the gambling analogy.

## Key Characteristics
- Represents the venture-capital emphasis on technological and market change.
- Provides the contrast against Buffett-style value investing.
- Is used as a foil for the source's claim that all successful investments mix change and timelessness.
- Is cited for a competitive culture that does not make celebration or reward the purpose of the work.
- Uses professional gamblers as an analogy for emotionally steady judgment under uncertain outcomes.

## Evidence
- Change quote: [[betting-on-things-that-never-change-collaborative-fund]] quotes Andreessen saying venture investors bet for change and err when change does not arrive.
- Buffett contrast: [[betting-on-things-that-never-change-collaborative-fund]] uses Andreessen's comparison to introduce the value-versus-venture distinction.
- Qualification: [[betting-on-things-that-never-change-collaborative-fund]] argues the contrast is directionally true but too black-and-white.
- Competition without reward: [[happy-xiao-leng-ku-zhi-dao]] attributes to Andreessen the claim that a16z is intensely competitive without competing for emotional payoff.
- Gambling analogy: [[happy-xiao-leng-ku-zhi-dao]] reports Andreessen describing professional gamblers who make side bets to strengthen emotionally detached judgment.

## Qualifications
This page covers only Andreessen's roles in two short secondary essays. Happy Xiao does not link directly to the complete interview passage, and the gambling story is an analogy rather than evidence that emotional detachment improves decisions. The page does not profile Andreessen's full career, firm history, investment record, or broader public views.
