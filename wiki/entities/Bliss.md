## Overview
[[Bliss]] is the startup used in [[BrianYork]]'s 2016 case study of how repository size and technical debt can reflect changes in company priorities.

## Current Profile
The source describes Bliss as bootstrapped in early 2015 by York and Ian Connor. The founders consciously favored feature delivery and paying-customer growth over early debt reduction, shipped less while fundraising, and later hired Jace Patel and Mikey Sive after raising initial capital. York reports that the larger team then expanded the repository while bringing debt to a level its stakeholders considered sustainable.

## Key Characteristics
- Early-stage startup initially optimized for customer validation and feature speed.
- Case study for reading business events through engineering metrics.
- Shifted toward codebase consistency before and during team expansion.
- Reported a stakeholder-accepted technical-debt level of 20% by spring 2016.

## Evidence
- Validation priority: [[using-technical-debt-as-a-time-machine-brian-york-medium]] says the founders prioritized paying customers and strong growth over early debt management.
- Metric narrative: [[using-technical-debt-as-a-time-machine-brian-york-medium]] links the repository plateau to fundraising and the later size increase to engineering hires.
- Team preparation: [[using-technical-debt-as-a-time-machine-brian-york-medium]] says the founders wanted a consistent codebase so new developers could contribute quickly.
- Reported outcome: [[using-technical-debt-as-a-time-machine-brian-york-medium]] states that stakeholders were comfortable with a 20% debt level.
