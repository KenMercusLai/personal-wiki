## Overview
[[HenryWard]] is the eShares chief executive and author of a 2015 employee-course essay and a 2016 management FAQ.

## Current Profile
Ward appears as a founder-operator who personally taught a monthly full-day course to small groups of employees. His operating philosophy combines Lean Startup vocabulary, Dale Carnegie relationship advice, professional-sports-team discipline, software automation, Peter Thiel's competition strategy, patient capital deployment, and a learning-centered employee bargain.

His management FAQ frames hierarchy as increasing obligation rather than privilege. Ward favors reinforcing strengths, diagnosing mistakes with curiosity, treating communication and training gaps as managerial failures, delegating attractive work, retaining hard problems, and teaching employees to evaluate themselves. At larger scope, he says leaders must rely on influence because capable lieutenants and their teams cannot be commanded into commitment. His personnel doctrine asks managers to act quickly when they conclude that someone cannot succeed, apologize for hiring or support failures, listen, and give an honest reference.

## Key Characteristics
- Personally taught company culture and execution strategy to groups of eight to ten employees.
- Favored leverage, automation, and repeated learn-execute cycles over static role definitions.
- Used a sports-team metaphor to justify synchronized routines and explicit performance expectations.
- Treated recurring one-on-ones and negative feedback as listening and diagnostic work.
- Favored delegation of desirable work and upward ownership of difficult work and organizational problems.
- Defined leadership at scale through influence, coaching, problem prevention, and responsibility for role fit.
- Defined company legacy partly through the later accomplishments of its alumni.

## Evidence
- Teaching role: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] says Ward ran the eShares 101 course monthly and supplied the lecture notes and deck excerpts.
- Operating philosophy: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] links Ward's model to MVPs, software leverage, learn-execute cycles, narrow markets, durability, and controlled burn.
- People philosophy: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] describes listening-oriented one-on-ones, market-based compensation, learning as career capital, and the PayPal-inspired alumni ambition.
- Feedback and delegation: [[a-managers-faq-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] argues for strength reinforcement, curiosity before criticism, self-evaluation, and delegating work the manager wants.
- Authority and hierarchy: [[a-managers-faq-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] says managers should take hard problems and use influence rather than command as their organizational responsibility grows.
- Personnel decisions: [[a-managers-faq-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] asks managers to own their contribution to failed hires or support and conduct exits candidly and humanely.

## Qualifications
This profile is limited to Ward's own 2015 and 2016 accounts of intended eShares practices. They do not independently establish how consistently the policies were applied, how employees experienced them, or whether the promised financial, cultural, compensation, performance, and alumni outcomes occurred. His FAQ makes categorical claims about dismissal and performance-improvement plans without addressing documentation, bias, accommodation, legal duties, employee response, or cases in which clear correction succeeds.

## What Changed
- Added Ward's management philosophy around strengths, curiosity, delegation, self-evaluation, influence, and humane exits.
- Qualified the personnel doctrine with fair-process and evidentiary boundaries absent from the essay.
