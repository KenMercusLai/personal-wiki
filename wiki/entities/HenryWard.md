## Overview
[[HenryWard]] is the eShares chief executive and author of the 2015 eShares 101 employee-course essay.

## Current Profile
Ward appears as a founder-operator who personally taught a monthly full-day course to small groups of employees. His stated management philosophy combines Lean Startup vocabulary, Dale Carnegie relationship advice, professional-sports-team discipline, software automation, Peter Thiel's competition strategy, patient capital deployment, and a learning-centered employee bargain.

## Key Characteristics
- Personally taught company culture and execution strategy to groups of eight to ten employees.
- Favored leverage, automation, and repeated learn-execute cycles over static role definitions.
- Used a sports-team metaphor to justify synchronized routines and explicit performance expectations.
- Treated recurring one-on-ones primarily as listening and relationship-strengthening work.
- Defined company legacy partly through the later accomplishments of its alumni.

## Evidence
- Teaching role: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] says Ward ran the eShares 101 course monthly and supplied the lecture notes and deck excerpts.
- Operating philosophy: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] links Ward's model to MVPs, software leverage, learn-execute cycles, narrow markets, durability, and controlled burn.
- People philosophy: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] describes listening-oriented one-on-ones, market-based compensation, learning as career capital, and the PayPal-inspired alumni ambition.

## Qualifications
This profile is limited to Ward's own 2015 account of intended eShares practices. It does not independently establish how consistently the policies were applied, how employees experienced them, or whether the promised financial, cultural, compensation, and alumni outcomes occurred.
