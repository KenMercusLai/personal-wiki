## Overview
[[IndieVC]] is an investment program presented in its 2016 version-two release as a source of capital and peer support for revenue-generating companies pursuing profitability and durable independence rather than optimizing for repeated venture rounds.

## Current Profile
The release describes Indie.vc as an experiment in the space between a lifestyle business and a venture-scale monopoly. Its first cohort included eight companies selected from more than 500 applicants; after about a year, the program reported that three were profitable, two were close, and all remained operating. Version 2 moved from money allocated out of an existing OATV fund to a dedicated pool, replaced batches with rolling decisions, expanded initial checks from a fixed $100,000 to $100,000-$500,000, and aimed to fund 15-20 companies during the following year.

The program combines selection, financing terms, and group support. Applicants were expected to have raised no more than $500,000 and to produce at least $10,000 in monthly revenue. The described instrument capped investor distributions at 5×, converted to equity only if the company later raised capital or was acquired, and halved the agreed conversion percentage when the distribution cap was reached within four years. Quarterly retreats supplied workshops, founder conversations, and peer reporting, while the release explicitly denied investor authority to hire or fire the founder.

## Key Characteristics
- Targets companies with existing revenue and a plausible route to profit rather than pre-revenue ideas seeking accelerator validation.
- Uses capped distributions plus contingent equity conversion to seek returns without requiring every company to raise again or sell.
- Preserves founder operating authority under the described terms while offering advice and peer support.
- Selects on prior capital, current revenue, customer payment, and business-model fundamentals.
- Operates from a dedicated investment pool with rolling admissions rather than an accelerator-like batch.
- Treats long-lived, profitable independent companies as potentially attractive investments between lifestyle and monopoly outcomes.

## Evidence
- Pilot and target segment: [[indie-vc-v-2-release-notes-strong-words-medium]] reports more than 500 applicants, eight investments, three profitable companies, two near profitability, and all eight still operating after about a year.
- Institutional design: [[indie-vc-v-2-release-notes-strong-words-medium]] describes a dedicated pool, rolling investment, a $100,000-$500,000 range, and a goal of 15-20 investments in the next year.
- Eligibility: [[indie-vc-v-2-release-notes-strong-words-medium]] sets ceilings of $500,000 previously raised and a floor of $10,000 in monthly revenue, while preferring paid products over third-party data monetization.
- Return structure: [[indie-vc-v-2-release-notes-strong-words-medium]] describes a 5× distribution cap, equity conversion on financing or acquisition, and a 50% conversion reduction after fast capped repayment.
- Control and support: [[indie-vc-v-2-release-notes-strong-words-medium]] says the investor cannot hire or fire founders and describes quarterly workshops, discussions, and progress reporting.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on a release note written by the program's investor, not legal documents, audited fund results, founder interviews, or comparative research. Its one-year pilot metrics are too early and too small to establish causation, long-run company survival, or fund returns. The source does not identify the cohort's financial figures, margins, later outcomes, distribution performance, investment-by-investment terms, or how disagreement and default worked in practice. Version 2 should therefore be read as a historical design and stated thesis, not evidence that the model ultimately performed as intended.

## What Changed
- Created the entity from the version-two release and its first-year pilot account.
- Captured the shift to dedicated capital, rolling decisions, larger flexible checks, and explicit revenue and fundraising filters.
- Distinguished capped distributions, contingent equity conversion, and founder operating authority from conventional accelerator and pure-equity models.
