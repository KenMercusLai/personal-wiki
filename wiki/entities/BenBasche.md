## Overview
[[BenBasche]] is represented here by a 2016 essay interpreting [[Snapchat]] as an [[AuthenticallyMobile]] social product rather than merely a phone-optimized application.

## Current Profile
Basche’s source joins product design, mobile capabilities, identity pressure, and media temporality into a historical explanation of Snapchat. His central distinction is between persistent feeds organized around presentation and a camera-first environment organized around capturing and briefly sharing the present. The profile is source-bounded: the wiki has no independent biographical or professional evidence beyond authorship of this essay.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses product mechanics and social behavior together to interpret platform strategy.
- Treats camera default, ephemerality, and device integration as a coherent mobile-native system.
- Contrasts archival feeds with temporary interpersonal experience.
- Makes strategic forecasts while acknowledging uncertainty in some reported engagement figures.

## Evidence
- Mobile-native thesis: [[ghost-in-the-machine-snapchat-isnt-mobile-first-its-something-else-entirely]] distinguishes phone adaptation from products dependent on mobile hardware and context.
- Social interpretation: [[ghost-in-the-machine-snapchat-isnt-mobile-first-its-something-else-entirely]] connects persistent identity and archive pressure with lower-stakes ephemeral sharing.
- Strategic forecast: [[ghost-in-the-machine-snapchat-isnt-mobile-first-its-something-else-entirely]] proposes that Snapchat’s active-connection role could carry into whatever followed smartphones.
