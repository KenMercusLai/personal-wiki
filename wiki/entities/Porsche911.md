## Overview
[[Porsche911]] appears as the physical-product comparator for the [[GoogleHomepage]] in the source's argument about design timelessness.

## Current Profile
The source frames the Porsche 911 as a car born around a pure driving experience rather than frivolous luxury or passenger niceties. Its visual evidence shows continuity across generations: a front-view comparison between an older and newer 911 and a side-view comparison labeled 1963, 1993, and 2013. The article uses that continuity to argue that a product can be simple, innovative, unobtrusive, carefully iterated, and still recognizable across decades.

## Key Characteristics
- Used as the physical benchmark for timeless design.
- Defined in the source by focus on the driving experience.
- Shows recognizable design continuity across multiple generations.
- Serves as a contrast case for why physical products may feel more timeless than digital interfaces.

## Evidence
- Focused origin: [[can-digital-products-be-timeless-startup-grind-medium]] says the Porsche 911 was born with the driver in mind.
- Design quality: [[can-digital-products-be-timeless-startup-grind-medium]] describes the 911 and Google Homepage as simple, innovative, unobtrusive, engineered, iconic, beloved, and recognizable.
- Iteration over time: [[can-digital-products-be-timeless-startup-grind-medium]] says both products were carefully iterated and became more robust.
- Visual continuity: [[can-digital-products-be-timeless-startup-grind-medium]] includes inspected images comparing older and newer Porsche 911 forms and showing 1963, 1993, and 2013 side profiles.

## Qualifications
The source uses the Porsche 911 as a design analogy, not as a full automotive history. It does not evaluate model-by-model engineering changes, market performance, safety changes, ownership costs, or dissenting views of the 911's design continuity.
