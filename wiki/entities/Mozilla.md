## Overview
[[Mozilla]] appears in the wiki as an independent browser organization: [[DavidHumphrey]] calls on it to lead integrated web-payment experiments, while the 2017 Firefox campaign source presents it as the nonprofit steward behind [[Firefox]] and a counterweight to [[Google]]-centered browser dominance.

## Current Profile
The payment source presents Mozilla as a possible browser-vendor leader for moving beyond binary ad rendering and ad blocking. Humphrey's argument is not that Mozilla already operates such a system, but that a browser organization could create new revenue and user-choice models by integrating payments into the user agent. The Firefox campaign source adds Mozilla's own strategic posture: it frames Mozilla as a nonprofit with a healthy-web mission, financially healthier and more diversified than before, and willing to challenge Google even though Google remained a revenue source.

## Key Characteristics
- Represents the browser-vendor actor most explicitly named in the payment proposal.
- Is associated with the trusted-user-agent side of the web economy rather than with a specific payment processor.
- Could, in the source's view, treat integrated payments as a browser revenue stream analogous to search defaults.
- Operates [[Firefox]] as an independent browser alternative to [[Chrome]].
- Frames its nonprofit mission as preserving web health and checking corporate power.
- Acknowledges Google revenue dependence while arguing that independence must still be exercised.

## Evidence
- Vendor leadership: [[402-payment-required-david-humphrey-medium]] says Humphrey would especially like Mozilla to lead on browser-integrated payment options.
- Revenue analogy: [[402-payment-required-david-humphrey-medium]] compares browser payment brokerage to search-engine default revenue.
- User-agent role: [[402-payment-required-david-humphrey-medium]] argues that browsers are trusted enough to remember passwords, protect history, and accompany users across sites.
- Firefox campaign: [[browse-against-the-machine-the-official-unofficial-firefox-blog-medium]] presents Mozilla's "Browse Against the Machine" campaign as a renewed external posture for Firefox.
- Nonprofit mission: [[browse-against-the-machine-the-official-unofficial-firefox-blog-medium]] says Firefox is built by nonprofit Mozilla to preserve a healthy web and check corporate power.
- Independence qualification: [[browse-against-the-machine-the-official-unofficial-firefox-blog-medium]] acknowledges Mozilla's Google revenue while saying Mozilla was financially healthier and more diversified.

## Qualifications
The payment source is a proposal, not evidence that Mozilla adopted browser-mediated payments. The Firefox source is a Mozilla-side marketing essay and should be read as campaign positioning rather than independent proof of Firefox performance, Mozilla finances, or current browser-market structure.

## What Changed
- Added Mozilla's Firefox stewardship, nonprofit web-health mission, and qualified Google-revenue independence argument from the 2017 campaign source.
