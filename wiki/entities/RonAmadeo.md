## Overview
[[RonAmadeo]] is represented in the wiki as the Ars Technica author arguing that Google's repeated product shutdowns damaged trust in the company's broader portfolio.

## Current Profile
In the April 2019 source, Amadeo moves from a shutdown chronology to a platform-investment argument. Consumers, enterprise buyers, developers, and hardware partners accept different kinds of dependency, so a dense stream of closures can change the reception of unrelated future products. His [[GoogleStadia]] discussion uses launch journalism to illustrate that spillover rather than claiming measured customer behavior.

## Key Characteristics
- Technology journalist writing critically about Google product strategy.
- Connects individual shutdown decisions to company-wide reputation and adoption risk.
- Uses Stadia's launch reception as a historically situated example of inherited skepticism.

## Evidence
- Shutdown analysis: [[googles-constant-product-shutdowns-are-damaging-its-brand-ars-technica]] counts Google's early-2019 closures and announced wind-downs.
- Stakeholder model: [[googles-constant-product-shutdowns-are-damaging-its-brand-ars-technica]] distinguishes consumer, enterprise, developer, and hardware-partner commitment costs.
- Portfolio spillover: [[googles-constant-product-shutdowns-are-damaging-its-brand-ars-technica]] collects mainstream Stadia coverage asking about Google's long-term commitment.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one opinion analysis dated April 2, 2019. The article does not supply a representative survey, longitudinal brand measure, internal Google decision evidence, or a comparison of shutdown benefits and costs. Its Stadia argument records launch-time expectations rather than later outcomes.
