## Overview
[[DeannaZandt]] is represented as a media technologist and writer who connects depression with the mismatch between lived experience and curated social-media identity.

## Current Profile
Zandt uses photographs from a period of severe depression to show how a smiling public feed can diverge from private distress. She argues that the expectation to present an acceptable version of oneself can worsen the condition and produce alienation from other people and from oneself. Her design prescription is directional rather than concrete: digital products should treat vulnerability, authenticity, connection, and human messiness as core conditions instead of optimizing only for positive reinforcement and polished performance.

## Key Characteristics
- Media technologist and writer addressing mental health through product and digital-culture criticism.
- Uses her own curated photographs as evidence of public-private identity dissonance.
- Frames alienation as both interpersonal and intrapersonal.
- Advocates product space for vulnerability, authenticity, intimacy, and messy human experience.

## Qualifications
The source summarizes one public talk and personal testimony rather than a product experiment, clinical study, or detailed design proposal. It does not establish which interface changes would reduce dissonance, whether more vulnerable sharing is safe in broad audiences, or how platforms should handle privacy, abuse, crisis response, and unwanted exposure.
