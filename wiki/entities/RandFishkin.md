## Overview
[[RandFishkin]] is an entrepreneur and the author of the 2013 essay proposing parallel individual-contributor and people-management career tracks at [[Moz]].

## Current Profile
In the source, Fishkin writes as Moz's first-time CEO while the company is developing a new title, team, and salary structure. He argues that his own leadership style resembles a high-level IC: reports own their teams and details, while he offers product, marketing, communication, and company-context support as a resource. His account presents influence as something earned through useful cross-team contribution rather than direct-report count.

## Key Characteristics
- Advocates equal-status advancement for individual contributors and people managers.
- Treats management as enabling other people's growth rather than as a generic seniority reward.
- Favors substantial contributor ownership over work definition and execution.
- Describes himself as a first-time, IC-style CEO whose reports own their teams and details.

## Evidence
- Career architecture: [[if-management-is-the-only-way-up-were-all-fd-sparktoro]] says Fishkin and colleagues were preparing multiple tracks with roughly equivalent salary levels.
- Leadership stance: [[if-management-is-the-only-way-up-were-all-fd-sparktoro]] says he asks product and marketing teams to use him as a resource for writing, customers, and broader-goal context.
- Organizational judgment: [[if-management-is-the-only-way-up-were-all-fd-sparktoro]] warns that promoting strong ICs without management fit can damage both execution and team quality.

## Qualifications
The profile is based on one self-authored 2013 essay and selected examples from Fishkin's own company. It describes a proposed organizational model and self-conception rather than independently measured leadership behavior or later outcomes.
