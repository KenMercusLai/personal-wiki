## Overview
[[JasonZweig]] appears in the source as the Wall Street Journal investing writer quoted on the danger of learning overly narrow lessons from financial crashes.

## Current Profile
Housel cites Zweig to make the repeatability argument concrete in investing behavior. In the quoted example, some investors interpreted the dot-com crash as a warning against Internet stocks specifically rather than against speculating in overvalued financial assets generally. That narrow lesson left the same speculative behavior available under a new label during the housing boom.
