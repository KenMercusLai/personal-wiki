## Overview
[[AnandSanwal]] is represented in this wiki as the founder/CEO voice behind a public retrospective on early [[CBInsights]] operating mistakes.

## Current Profile
The source presents Sanwal as a self-critical startup CEO who treated CB Insights' early mistakes as evidence for better operating systems. His lessons span culture, hiring, sales, pricing, marketing, product direction, administration, and founder time allocation. The profile is less a biography than an operator pattern: he learns by naming mistakes publicly, turning them into process changes, and asking the team to hold him accountable.

## Key Characteristics
- Founder/CEO of [[CBInsights]] in the source context.
- Publicly reflective about startup mistakes across functions.
- Treats culture, onboarding, communication, feedback, and celebration as active CEO responsibilities.
- Moves from product-first instincts toward stronger sales, customer success, and customer-meeting discipline.
- Reframes pricing, hiring, and focus around value, standards, and scarce founder attention.

## Evidence
- Role and context: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] is written from Sanwal's perspective as CB Insights' CEO during a year when revenue and team growth were improving.
- Self-critical operating style: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] presents 54 mistakes across culture, hiring, pricing, sales, product, administration, and miscellaneous founder behavior.
- Culture ownership: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] says Sanwal stopped assuming culture would happen by itself and began using a Culture Code, onboarding work, feedback loops, and context sharing.
- Sales and customer learning: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] says Sanwal improved by asking more questions, following up after demos, asking for the sale, meeting customers in person, and adding customer success.
- Focus and standards: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] says he learned to avoid fads, non-customer advice, competitor-driven product work, vague integrations, weak references, and pressure hiring.
