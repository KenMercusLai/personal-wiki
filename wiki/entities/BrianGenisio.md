## Overview
[[BrianGenisio]] is represented in the wiki as a Khan Academy engineer and engineering manager who led the endgame of its Python-monolith migration.

## Current Profile
Genisio explains the migration as both a production-safety problem and a coordination problem. His account emphasizes field-level side-by-side comparison, direct behavior ports, fixed scope and timeline, staffing movement across teams, dependency-aware deadlines, and the organizational cost of a 3.5-year infrastructure program.

## Key Characteristics
- Led and coordinated the endgame phase of Khan Academy's monolith rewrite.
- Advocated shadowing, response comparison, canaries, fallback, and gradual traffic transfer.
- Used heuristic estimates first, then refined them as APIs were decomposed into smaller units.
- Treated the known migration scope with a fixed deadline and cross-team staffing flexibility.
- Reflects candidly on language-learning cost, internal-tool scope expansion, and migration fatigue.

## Evidence
- Migration leadership: [[real-world-engineering-challenges-8-breaking-up-a-monolith]] says Genisio moved from contributing engineer to manager of the endgame phase.
- Rollout safety: [[real-world-engineering-challenges-8-breaking-up-a-monolith]] records his field-level, side-by-side approach and staged cutover sequence.
- Planning: [[real-world-engineering-challenges-8-breaking-up-a-monolith]] attributes the fixed deadline, burndown, borderless staffing, and final critical-path coordination to his account.
- Retrospective judgment: [[real-world-engineering-challenges-8-breaking-up-a-monolith]] records his uncertainty about Go's payback and his view that rebuilding rather than directly porting internal tools slowed completion.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one reported interview embedded in a second-party case study. It does not independently measure Genisio's contribution, represent his broader career, or separate his judgment from the collective work of Khan Academy's engineering organization.
