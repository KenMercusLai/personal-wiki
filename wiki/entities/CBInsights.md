## Overview
[[CBInsights]] is represented in this wiki as a startup, venture, and market-intelligence publisher and as the SaaS company whose early operating mistakes [[AnandSanwal]] analyzed publicly.

## Current Profile
The sources show two complementary CB Insights roles. As a research publisher, it turns shutdown narratives into evidence for [[StartupFailurePatterns]], [[ProductMarketFit]], [[StartupRunway]], and adjacent risk concepts, and it assembles company launches, investments, partnerships, metrics, failures, and rumors into a strategic portfolio analysis of Amazon's financial-services activity. In Sanwal's retrospective, CB Insights is itself the operating case: a growing SaaS/data company that learned through mistakes in culture, hiring, pricing, sales, marketing, customer success, product focus, and administrative systems.

## Key Characteristics
- Startup and venture research publisher that curates shutdown narratives across sectors and years.
- SaaS/data company whose early operating lessons cover culture, hiring, pricing, sales, product, marketing, and administration.
- Uses qualitative evidence, both external post-mortems and internal retrospectives, to expose recurring startup failure and scaling mechanisms.
- Combines product, investment, partnership, market, and rumor evidence into company strategy reports that require careful evidence-level separation.
- Moved from low, gimmicky plan packaging toward value-based pricing and clearer institutional positioning.
- Treats customer learning, customer success, and data-driven content as important product and distribution loops.

## Evidence
- Research publishing: [[323-startup-failure-post-mortems]] is a CB Insights compilation of startup failure post-mortems by founders, investors, and journalists.
- Pattern aggregation: [[323-startup-failure-post-mortems]] frames public obituaries as clues to why startups shut down and connects them to broader startup-death and failure-reason work.
- Sector breadth: [[323-startup-failure-post-mortems]] covers consumer hardware, AR/VR, healthcare, autonomous vehicles, agtech, food delivery, ecommerce, social products, marketplaces, fintech, media, and enterprise software.
- Internal operating case: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] describes CB Insights learning from mistakes in culture, hiring, sales, pricing, customer success, product focus, office infrastructure, payroll, advisors, and founder meetings.
- Pricing evolution: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] says CB Insights later raised pricing by more than 10x after realizing cheap pricing was the wrong competitive dimension; the inspected pricing screenshot shows the earlier Limo, Town Car, and Cab plan packaging.
- Distribution and customer learning: [[54-screwups-as-a-startup-ceo-anand-sanwal-medium]] describes data-driven research content, newsletter growth, customer meetings, customer success, journalist outreach, and technology storytelling as important growth and learning mechanisms.
- Market-intelligence synthesis: [[everything-you-need-to-know-about-what-amazon-is-doing-in-financial-services]] organizes Amazon's payments, cash, lending, insurance, international investment, and rumored banking moves around an ecosystem-participation thesis.

## Qualifications
The failure compilation is useful qualitative evidence, but it is not a controlled causal study; cases are selected from public shutdown narratives and investigative reports, which can overrepresent visible, venture-backed, English-language, or media-friendly failures. The Amazon report is a mid-2018 market snapshot that mixes company figures, third-party estimates, strategic interpretation, and explicitly labeled rumors without testing whether the products caused the proposed ecosystem outcomes. Sanwal's retrospective is a first-person operator account, so it is valuable for decision logic and lessons learned but should not be treated as an independent audit of CB Insights' culture, performance, or later trajectory.

## What Changed
- Added company-strategy research spanning products, investments, partnerships, metrics, failures, and rumors.
- Made evidence-level separation an explicit qualification on CB Insights' portfolio reports.
