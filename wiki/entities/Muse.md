## Overview
[[Muse]] is a Meta consumer AI agent represented in the current evidence as a region-limited product that gives users a hosted computer, operates software on their behalf, and can create personal applications through conversation.

## Current Profile
The source frames Muse as more than a chatbot: its appeal comes from delegating computer tasks, while its launch mechanics make access itself part of distribution. Restricted availability, waitlisting, identity and network checks, card-based age verification, and referral-code redemption created a difficult funnel. Successful users could reportedly earn large token allowances by bringing in others, aligning tutorial publication with personal reward.

The same design concentrates risk. A computer-operating agent needs broad authority and costly execution capacity, while the source reports connector failures, private-message access complaints, a macOS vulnerability, and Amazon blocking access. Launch downloads and referral-driven attention therefore describe awareness, not durable retention, safety, trust, or unit economics.

Thompson's recipe experiment supplies the most concrete capability evidence in the current sources. He asked Muse to organize his saved Instagram recipes; it produced a searchable recipe-box interface with 85 items in about five minutes, then continued analyzing captions and videos for ingredients. The retained progress screenshot also shows Instagram rate limiting, so the case demonstrates useful delegated work without establishing completeness, accuracy, or independence from supplier controls.

Thompson also adds a developer-ecosystem dimension through Muse Gadgets. The inspected announcement presents open-source ESP32/Linux firmware and an SDK for making hardware controllable by Muse, plus a small USB-C Muse Home Link gateway that exposes local-network devices to the agent. In his interpretation, the strategic prize is making Muse the interface across software and devices rather than selling hardware. Both claims remain source-reported product direction rather than adoption, security, interoperability, or business-model results.

## Key Characteristics
- Consumer agent positioned to perform computer tasks rather than only answer in conversation.
- Reportedly provisions a two-core, 8 GB RAM, 8 GB storage virtual computer per user.
- Can generate a personal visual application from a conversational request and continue work in the background.
- Uses regional restrictions, waitlisting, verification, account, network, and referral mechanics as access and distribution gates.
- Depends on privileged software access and virtual-computer infrastructure while inheriting supplier rate limits and destination policies.
- Faces source-reported security, privacy, connector, and scaling concerns.
- Extends toward open-source gadgets and a local-network gateway for custom devices.

## Qualifications
The Gadgets announcement shows intended capability, not deployed-device quality, protocol coverage, secure local-network operation, developer adoption, or consumer demand. The recipe case is one author's first-person experiment; it does not measure extraction accuracy, missing items, task success, accessibility, maintenance, retention, or comparative effort, and its five-minute timing is not independently verified.

The profile is based on two Thompson essays and one secondary commentary article rather than Meta documentation, security advisories, retention cohorts, or infrastructure accounts. Product availability, virtual-machine specifications, verification, referral terms, vulnerability status, and destination policies may change. Reported downloads do not identify active or retained users, and an analyst cost estimate without workload, utilization, hardware, pricing, or architecture assumptions cannot establish Muse's actual unit economics. The name also collides with an unrelated cryptocurrency called MUSE; the retained screenshot documents that ambiguity rather than a relationship between the products.
