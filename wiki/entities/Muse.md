## Overview
[[Muse]] is a Meta consumer AI agent represented in the current evidence as a region-limited product that can operate a computer for users and that combined waitlisted access with referral-token rewards.

## Current Profile
The source frames Muse as more than a chatbot: its appeal comes from delegating computer tasks, while its launch mechanics make access itself part of distribution. Restricted availability, waitlisting, identity and network checks, card-based age verification, and referral-code redemption created a difficult funnel. Successful users could reportedly earn large token allowances by bringing in others, aligning tutorial publication with personal reward.

The same design concentrates risk. A computer-operating agent needs broad authority and costly execution capacity, while the source reports connector failures, private-message access complaints, a macOS vulnerability, and Amazon blocking access. Launch downloads and referral-driven attention therefore describe awareness, not durable retention, safety, trust, or unit economics.

## Key Characteristics
- Consumer agent positioned to perform computer tasks rather than only answer in conversation.
- Initially represented as geographically restricted to the United States and Canada.
- Uses waitlisting, age verification, and account or network checks as access gates.
- Couples invitations with large token rewards for participants in the referral loop.
- Depends on privileged device or browser operation and virtual-computer infrastructure.
- Faces source-reported security, privacy, connector, destination-access, and scaling concerns.

## Evidence
- Product and access model: [[muse-qu-mei-cong-wo-zuo-qi-ni-ka-zai-na-er]] describes Muse as a personal agent gated by region, waitlist, network, identity, age-verification, and redemption steps.
- Referral distribution: [[muse-qu-mei-cong-wo-zuo-qi-ni-ka-zai-na-er]] reports large non-expiring token rewards and connects tutorial traffic with inviter benefit.
- Consumer-agent capability: [[muse-qu-mei-cong-wo-zuo-qi-ni-ka-zai-na-er]] presents computer operation as the feature distinguishing Muse from a pure chatbot.
- Trust and safety: [[muse-qu-mei-cong-wo-zuo-qi-ni-ka-zai-na-er]] relays reports of unauthorized message access, a macOS zero-day, connector failures, and Amazon blocking the agent.
- Economics: [[muse-qu-mei-cong-wo-zuo-qi-ni-ka-zai-na-er]] raises the prospective infrastructure cost of supplying virtual computers at mass scale.

## Qualifications
The current profile is based on one commentary article rather than Meta documentation, direct testing, security advisories, retention cohorts, or infrastructure accounts. Product availability, verification, referral terms, vulnerability status, and destination policies may change. Reported downloads do not identify active or retained users, and an analyst cost estimate without workload, utilization, hardware, pricing, or architecture assumptions cannot establish Muse's actual unit economics. The name also collides with an unrelated cryptocurrency called MUSE; the retained screenshot documents that ambiguity rather than a relationship between the products.
