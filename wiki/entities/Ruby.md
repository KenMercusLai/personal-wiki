## Overview
[[Ruby]] is the programming language [[Appcanary]] returned to after discarding its first [[Clojure]] implementation.

## Current Profile
Ruby is presented less as a technically flawless language than as a language with an unusually explicit human-centered design value: programming should be fun and make programmers happy. Mendonca-Vieira acknowledges Ruby's tooling and runtime flaws, but says Appcanary benefited from deep team familiarity and from a culture that treated developer happiness as a legitimate product quality.

## Key Characteristics
- Ruby was the familiar tool Appcanary returned to under startup time pressure.
- Its tooling is described as technically imperfect despite the author's preference for it.
- The language is praised for valuing programmer happiness.
- Ruby is used as a contrast case against tool cultures that dismiss ease of use.
- The source says Ruby's developer-happiness values influenced later communities such as Elixir, Rust, and Ember.

## Evidence
- Familiarity: [[appcanary-simple-aint-easy-but-hard-aint-simple-leaving-clojure-for-ruby]] says Appcanary went back to Ruby because the team knew it well.
- Technical flaws: [[appcanary-simple-aint-easy-but-hard-aint-simple-leaving-clojure-for-ruby]] criticizes Ruby tooling, speed, memory behavior, introspection, metaprogramming, and state bleed.
- Happiness philosophy: [[appcanary-simple-aint-easy-but-hard-aint-simple-leaving-clojure-for-ruby]] says Ruby is designed to be fun and make programmers happy.
- Cultural influence: [[appcanary-simple-aint-easy-but-hard-aint-simple-leaving-clojure-for-ruby]] says ex-Rubyists carried those values into other communities.

## Qualifications
The source is not a broad technical comparison of Ruby and Clojure. Its positive Ruby judgment is primarily about team familiarity, programmer happiness, and startup fit, while the author openly concedes major Ruby tooling shortcomings.
