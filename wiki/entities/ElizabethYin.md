## Overview
[[ElizabethYin]] is represented in the wiki as a startup-advice author arguing that early product scope should fit the founder's available runway.

## Current Profile
The source presents Yin as a practical early-stage startup voice focused on capital discipline, differentiated entry, and honest founder storytelling. Her advice is strongest for first-time founders or teams without easy access to large amounts of capital: build a narrow product quickly, avoid copying mature incumbents, and preserve enough runway for learning and go-to-market work.

## Key Characteristics
- Runway-sensitive product advisor: she ties first-product scope to available capital and time.
- Differentiation advocate: she recommends entering existing markets through a focused slice rather than incumbent feature parity.
- Founder-communication advisor: she warns founders to separate current product reality from future vision when pitching.

## Evidence

Differentiation advocate:

Founder-communication advisor:

## Qualifications
The source is a short practitioner essay rather than a detailed case study. It gives useful early-stage heuristics but does not quantify development scope, sales cycles, engineering complexity, or market-size thresholds for every startup type.
