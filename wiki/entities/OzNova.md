## Overview
[[OzNova]] is the Bradfield author of "You Are Not Google" and the presenter of UNPHAT, a checklist for matching technology choices to the problem, scale, and historical context that matter locally.

## Current Profile
In the available source, Nova writes as an engineering educator challenging prestige-driven architecture. He uses Cassandra, Kafka, service-oriented architecture, GFS, and MapReduce as examples of legitimate solutions that become irrational when adopters ignore the exceptional workload or organizational conditions behind them. His positive contribution is a compact decision process that asks engineers to compare candidates, read primary material, surface tradeoffs, and state what evidence would change their minds.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes about software architecture and engineering decision-making.
- Critiques cargo-cult adoption of technologies associated with Google, Amazon, and LinkedIn.
- Uses order-of-magnitude comparisons to test whether a tool's motivating workload resembles the adopter's workload.
- Emphasizes primary papers and the historical context behind design priorities.
- Treats explicit disadvantages and disconfirming evidence as necessary parts of technical judgment.

## Evidence
- Authorship: [[you-are-not-google-bradfield]] identifies Oz Nova with Bradfield and presents his argument against imitating giant technology companies.
- Framework: [[you-are-not-google-bradfield]] introduces UNPHAT as a sequence for understanding a problem, enumerating candidates, reading papers, recovering history, weighing tradeoffs, and thinking about fit.
- Comparative method: [[you-are-not-google-bradfield]] tests Cassandra, Kafka, service-oriented architecture, and MapReduce against the conditions they were designed to address.
