## Overview
[[ErikDietrich]] is a software-industry author and management consultant who writes at DaedTech about developer careers, organizational systems, and corporate incentives.

## Current Profile
In the source represented here, Dietrich critiques software hiring from experience as an interviewer, hiring-process participant, and process designer rather than as a rejected candidate. He argues that high-volume employers and individual candidates optimize for incompatible things, advises candidates with sufficient leverage to set interview boundaries, and proposes replacing cold stranger selection with longer-lived evidence relationships. His comment replies also acknowledge that this alternative can create access, referral, and discrimination risks.

## Key Characteristics
- Analyzes developer hiring as an organizational-design problem rather than only an interview-technique problem.
- Prefers demonstrated work, code review, and established contribution over algorithm trivia and résumé filtering.
- Treats candidate refusal and explicit boundaries as forms of market agency, while recognizing that not every candidate has equal leverage.
- Uses deliberately strong constraints to challenge growth and staffing assumptions.
- Engages counterarguments about relevance, portability, referrals, geography, and bias without resolving them empirically.

## Evidence
- System critique: [[hiring-is-broken-and-it-isnt-worth-fixing-daedtech]] frames standardized developer interviews as a volume-driven mismatch between individualized evaluation and organizational processing.
- Candidate practice: [[hiring-is-broken-and-it-isnt-worth-fixing-daedtech]] recommends stating deal breakers before interviews, preferring public work or code review, and leaving misaligned processes diplomatically.
- Organizational alternative: [[hiring-is-broken-and-it-isnt-worth-fixing-daedtech]] proposes slower, relationship-led staffing based on known work rather than repeated cold selection.
- Qualification through dialogue: [[hiring-is-broken-and-it-isnt-worth-fixing-daedtech]] records Dietrich's replies to concerns about unequal labor markets, network exclusion, poor referrals, and discriminatory monocultures.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one 2016 opinion article and its preserved comments. It does not establish the outcomes of Dietrich's own hiring designs, the later development of his views, or the viability of replacing cold interviews across different labor markets and roles.
