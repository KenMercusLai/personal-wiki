## Overview
[[ErikDietrich]] is a software-industry author and management consultant who writes at DaedTech about developer careers, organizational systems, professional metaphors, and corporate incentives.

## Current Profile
Dietrich critiques software hiring from experience as an interviewer, hiring-process participant, and process designer rather than as a rejected candidate. He argues that high-volume employers and individual candidates optimize for incompatible things, advises candidates with sufficient leverage to set interview boundaries, and proposes replacing cold stranger selection with longer-lived evidence relationships. His comment replies also acknowledge that this alternative can create access, referral, and discrimination risks.

His programming essay applies the same systems-oriented habit to a professional identity question. Rather than equate creativity with art, he classifies software work by purpose: aesthetic entertainment can be art, while most business software is functional algorithmic delegation. He treats programming as context-dependent work that may resemble art, science, engineering, craft, or routine production, and his replies preserve disagreements about self-expression, code beauty, correctness, and safety-critical rigor.

His leadership retrospective applies that distinction between title and work to his own career. Dietrich says he pursued technical authority partly to escape micromanagement, then discovered that leadership displaced implementation with delegation and final accountability. Moving from CIO work into freelancing, development, coaching, and consulting let him recover hands-on contribution without treating team membership or taking direction as loss of rank.

## Key Characteristics
- Analyzes developer hiring as an organizational-design problem rather than only an interview-technique problem.
- Prefers demonstrated work, code review, and established contribution over algorithm trivia and résumé filtering.
- Treats candidate refusal and explicit boundaries as forms of market agency, while recognizing that not every candidate has equal leverage.
- Distinguishes autonomy over one's work from authority over other people.
- Rejects formal leadership as a universal competence signal or superior career rank.
- Engages counterarguments about relevance, portability, referrals, geography, and bias without resolving them empirically.
- Separates creative method from artistic purpose and uses context-dependent art, science, engineering, craft, and production frames for software work.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on three 2016 opinion articles and their preserved comments. It does not establish the outcomes of Dietrich's own hiring designs, whether his leadership interpretation generalizes across organizations, or the viability of replacing cold interviews across different labor markets and roles. The programming taxonomy relies on a narrow working definition of art and practitioner debate rather than philosophical consensus or comparative evidence. His successful move toward freelancing also does not account for the financial, client, sales, or benefits trade-offs of independent work.

## What Changed
- Added Dietrich's distinction between seeking autonomy and seeking authority over a team.
- Added his account of formal leadership exchanging hands-on implementation for delegation and final accountability.
- Reframed experienced team membership and taking direction as compatible with professional standing.
