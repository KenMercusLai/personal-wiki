## Overview
[[ErikDietrich]] is a software-industry author and management consultant who writes at DaedTech about developer careers, organizational systems, professional metaphors, and corporate incentives.

## Current Profile
Dietrich critiques software hiring from experience as an interviewer, hiring-process participant, and process designer rather than as a rejected candidate. He argues that high-volume employers and individual candidates optimize for incompatible things, advises candidates with sufficient leverage to set interview boundaries, and proposes replacing cold stranger selection with longer-lived evidence relationships. His comment replies also acknowledge that this alternative can create access, referral, and discrimination risks.

His programming essay applies the same systems-oriented habit to a professional identity question. Rather than equate creativity with art, he classifies software work by purpose: aesthetic entertainment can be art, while most business software is functional algorithmic delegation. He treats programming as context-dependent work that may resemble art, science, engineering, craft, or routine production, and his replies preserve disagreements about self-expression, code beauty, correctness, and safety-critical rigor.

## Key Characteristics
- Analyzes developer hiring as an organizational-design problem rather than only an interview-technique problem.
- Prefers demonstrated work, code review, and established contribution over algorithm trivia and résumé filtering.
- Treats candidate refusal and explicit boundaries as forms of market agency, while recognizing that not every candidate has equal leverage.
- Uses deliberately strong constraints to challenge growth and staffing assumptions.
- Engages counterarguments about relevance, portability, referrals, geography, and bias without resolving them empirically.
- Separates creative method from artistic purpose when evaluating programming as art.
- Uses multiple professional frames rather than claiming that all software work belongs to one discipline.

- Candidate practice: [[hiring-is-broken-and-it-isnt-worth-fixing-daedtech]] recommends stating deal breakers before interviews, preferring public work or code review, and leaving misaligned processes diplomatically.
- Organizational alternative: [[hiring-is-broken-and-it-isnt-worth-fixing-daedtech]] proposes slower, relationship-led staffing based on known work rather than repeated cold selection.
- Qualification through dialogue: [[hiring-is-broken-and-it-isnt-worth-fixing-daedtech]] records Dietrich's replies to concerns about unequal labor markets, network exclusion, poor referrals, and discriminatory monocultures.
- Purpose-based classification: [[is-programming-art-daedtech]] argues that open-ended choice does not itself make software art and contrasts entertainment with efficiency-seeking business systems.
- Mixed professional identity: [[is-programming-art-daedtech]] presents art, science, engineering, craft, and routine production as context-dependent descriptions while preserving commenters' competing definitions.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on two 2016 opinion articles and their preserved comments. It does not establish the outcomes of Dietrich's own hiring designs, the later development of his views, or the viability of replacing cold interviews across different labor markets and roles. The programming taxonomy relies on a narrow working definition of art and practitioner debate rather than philosophical consensus or comparative evidence.
