## Overview
[[BillGates]] appears in the source as [[Microsoft]]'s co-founder and pricing-conscious IPO-era leader.

## Current Profile
The article presents Gates less as a general technology figure than as a founder managing the transition from profitable private software company to public company. His role is associated with Microsoft's unusually self-funded path: he said the company was cash-positive, did not have a loss-making year, and took a small venture investment mainly to add experienced board advice. The source also portrays him as conservative about IPO valuation, reportedly pushing for a lower price range because he thought a half-billion-dollar market value was too high.

## Key Characteristics
- Co-founded [[Microsoft]] and remained a major shareholder at the IPO.
- Emphasized that Microsoft could grow without needing large external capital.
- Treated venture involvement as advice and governance support rather than necessary operating funding.
- Reportedly preferred lower IPO pricing to avoid what he saw as excessive valuation.
- Represents founder control over financing timing in the source's Microsoft case.

## Evidence
- Founder stake: [[a-look-back-in-ipo-microsoft-the-software-success-crunchbase-news]] cites Fortune's account that Gates retained a 45% stake after the IPO.
- Self-funding account: [[a-look-back-in-ipo-microsoft-the-software-success-crunchbase-news]] quotes Gates saying Microsoft was cash-positive and did not lose money annually.
- Advice capital: [[a-look-back-in-ipo-microsoft-the-software-success-crunchbase-news]] says Gates described the $1 million venture investment as mainly for adding board advice.
- IPO pricing caution: [[a-look-back-in-ipo-microsoft-the-software-success-crunchbase-news]] reports that Gates pushed for a lower price range because he thought a valuation above half a billion dollars was too high.

## Qualifications
The source is a retrospective article that quotes or cites other materials rather than a complete biography of Gates. It does not evaluate his later Microsoft leadership, philanthropy, antitrust role, or technical contributions beyond the IPO-era financing story.
