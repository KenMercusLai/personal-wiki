## Overview
[[BillGates]] appears in the sources as [[Microsoft]]'s co-founder, pricing-conscious IPO-era leader, and later interpreter of software's broader intangible-asset economics.

## Current Profile
The IPO source presents Gates less as a general technology figure than as a founder managing the transition from profitable private software company to public company. His role is associated with Microsoft's unusually self-funded path: he said the company was cash-positive, did not have a loss-making year, and took a small venture investment mainly to add experienced board advice. The Gates Notes source adds a reflective economics role: Gates uses his Harvard economics memory and Microsoft's early investor story to argue that software and other intangible assets require updated economic measurement and policy. Across the two sources, Gates is a founder whose Microsoft experience becomes evidence for both capital-light software growth and the broader shift toward [[IntangibleAssetEconomics]].

## Key Characteristics
- Co-founded [[Microsoft]] and remained a major shareholder at the IPO.
- Emphasized that Microsoft could grow without needing large external capital.
- Treated venture involvement as advice and governance support rather than necessary operating funding.
- Reportedly preferred lower IPO pricing to avoid what he saw as excessive valuation.
- Uses Microsoft's early software business as an example of why intangible assets can be hard for investors to understand.
- Argues that policymakers need better tools for measuring and governing intangible-heavy economies.

## Evidence
- Founder stake: [[a-look-back-in-ipo-microsoft-the-software-success-crunchbase-news]] cites Fortune's account that Gates retained a 45% stake after the IPO.
- Self-funding account: [[a-look-back-in-ipo-microsoft-the-software-success-crunchbase-news]] quotes Gates saying Microsoft was cash-positive and did not lose money annually.
- Advice capital: [[a-look-back-in-ipo-microsoft-the-software-success-crunchbase-news]] says Gates described the $1 million venture investment as mainly for adding board advice.
- IPO pricing caution: [[a-look-back-in-ipo-microsoft-the-software-success-crunchbase-news]] reports that Gates pushed for a lower price range because he thought a valuation above half a billion dollars was too high.
- Software-economics reflection: [[capitalism-without-capital-bill-gates]] says Gates had to explain Microsoft's asset logic to investors who could not imagine its long-term returns.
- Policy argument: [[capitalism-without-capital-bill-gates]] says lawmakers should update economic policymaking for intangible assets, including measurement, IP, competition, taxation, and stimulus.

## Qualifications
The sources are a retrospective IPO article and Gates's own review of an economics book. They do not evaluate his full Microsoft leadership, philanthropy, antitrust role, technical contributions, or later public-health work.
