## Overview
[[JoshuaSchachter]] is represented as the founder of [[Delicious|Del.icio.us]], which grew from his personal link-management practice into an influential social-bookmarking service before its 2005 sale to [[Yahoo]].

## Current Profile
Schachter describes an operator path driven by a concrete personal information problem. He first collected links in a Unix text file with terse hash-marked notes, exposed the collection through single-user Muxway, and then made it multi-user as Del.icio.us. The resulting save-and-tag interaction lowered filing effort and made other users' interests visible.

He also describes the limits of solo technical intensity. While employed at Morgan Stanley, he shipped at night, sometimes broke saving for users, and struggled to keep the service available as traffic grew. A 2005 US$1 million financing and a small team did not remove the scale pressure or produce an attractive next round, helping turn Yahoo's offer into a rational liquidity and infrastructure decision.

His retrospective distinguishes financial correctness from emotional reward. Schachter later became an angel investor with nearly 200 investments, but says founder exits often remove momentum and control. He does not regret selling, while remaining frustrated by Yahoo's handling of the product and uncertain about what a broader independent Del.icio.us might have become.

## Key Characteristics
- Built products by generalizing a personal link-collection workflow into shared infrastructure.
- Associated low-overhead tagging with both retrieval and social discovery.
- Operated Del.icio.us under severe time, reliability, traffic, and fundraising constraints.
- Chose acquisition as a financially stabilizing response to those constraints and Yahoo's apparent Web 2.0 commitment.
- Separates the financial logic of a founder exit from its emotional and product consequences.
- Later applied the founder perspective as an angel investor across nearly 200 reported investments.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one retrospective first-person interview. It does not independently verify the deal, availability, funding, staffing, product-influence, or investment figures; present Yahoo's account; establish priority over every earlier tagging system; or show what would have happened without the sale.
