## Overview
[[SteveWozniak]] appears as Apple's technical co-founder, part of the company's humble hands-on origin story, and one of the early Apple figures associated with the [[HomebrewComputerClub]].

## Current Profile
The advertising article mentions Wozniak mainly to establish Apple's founding myth: a small, hard-working company beginning in a garage and workshop rather than a polished corporate setting. It pairs him with [[SteveJobs]] as someone influenced by Bob Dylan and shows the founders' early workbench context, which supports the source's later argument that Apple's brand could draw on humility, craft, and cultural storytelling. Barnett's meeting article adds that Jobs and Wozniak showed their first Apple computers at the [[HomebrewComputerClub]], making Wozniak part of the wiki's informal technical-exchange thread.

## Key Characteristics
- Co-founder figure in Apple's early garage-origin story.
- Represents the hands-on engineering side of Apple's beginnings.
- Paired with Jobs in the source's claim about Bob Dylan's influence.
- Associated with early Apple demonstrations in an informal computing-club setting.

## Evidence
- Co-founder context: [[40-lessons-from-40-years-of-apple-ads-mission-org-medium]] introduces Apple's first office as Jobs' parents' garage and then describes its first products as humble.
- Workshop image: [[40-lessons-from-40-years-of-apple-ads-mission-org-medium]] includes a black-and-white image of Jobs and Wozniak working around electronics, reinforcing the source's handmade-origin frame.
- Cultural influence: [[40-lessons-from-40-years-of-apple-ads-mission-org-medium]] says Jobs and Wozniak drew inspiration from Bob Dylan.
- Homebrew setting: [[bill-barnett-delete-all-meetings]] says Jobs and Wozniak showed off their first Apple computers at the [[HomebrewComputerClub]].

## Qualifications
The sources do not discuss Wozniak's engineering contributions in detail. They use him chiefly as part of Apple's founding story, brand mythology, and informal early-computing context.
