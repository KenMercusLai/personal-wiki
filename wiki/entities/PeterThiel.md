## Overview
[[PeterThiel]] appears as an example of redirecting away from credential competition, as a [[PayPal]] founder and [[MaxLevchin]] collaborator, as an advocate associated with seasteading, as the source of seven startup-evaluation questions, as the reported backer of a prepared campaign against [[Gawker]], and as a cofounder of [[Palantir]].

## Current Profile
Within this wiki, Thiel is still not profiled as a general business figure. The goal-setting source portrays him as someone who moved through a competitive path of grades, exams, degrees, and elite clerkship ambitions before a setback prompted reassessment and eventually a different entrepreneurial direction. Levchin's commencement address supplies a collaborator's compact founding account: Levchin says he met Thiel almost accidentally after moving to Palo Alto and that they founded the company that became PayPal together. Srinivasan's talk adds a governance angle: Thiel is named as one of two PayPal founders pursuing extreme but peaceful exit experiments, specifically seasteading. Lee's checklist article adds a startup-strategy role: Thiel's seven questions are used as a compact diagnostic for technology, timing, market wedge, team, distribution, durability, and non-obvious opportunity. Holiday adds a different exercise of judgment and power: he reports that Thiel backed an unnamed young man's researched plan to investigate and pursue legal claims against Gawker after an initially skeptical discussion. Alden's investigation adds an operating-company role by identifying Thiel as a Palantir founder and linking the company to his PayPal background, although it does not describe his day-to-day management or decision rights in 2016.

## Key Characteristics
- Used as a cautionary example of credential competition and conformist educational paths.
- Presented as redirecting from elite legal ambition toward entrepreneurship.
- Named by Levchin as the collaborator with whom he founded PayPal.
- Used as an example of moving exit from company formation toward new jurisdictions.
- Associated with a seven-question checklist for evaluating startup theses.
- Reported as funding a long-horizon investigation-and-litigation proposal against Gawker.
- Identified as a Palantir cofounder in an investigation of the company's enterprise model.

- Reassessment: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] describes a setback on the path toward a Supreme Court clerkship as the occasion for rethinking his life.
- Entrepreneurial redirection: [[she-li-mu-biao-ke-yi-gai-bian-ni-de-sheng-huo]] links that reassessment to the later creation of PayPal.
- Seasteading example: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] names Thiel as a PayPal founder interested in seasteading as a peaceful way to start new countries.
- Exit framing: [[balaji-srinivasan-silicon-valleys-ultimate-exit-genius]] uses Thiel's seasteading interest to illustrate maximal physical or jurisdictional exit.
- Startup questions: [[checklists-for-startups-david-lee-medium]] recommends Thiel's seven questions covering engineering, timing, monopoly, people, distribution, durability, and secrets.
- Gawker campaign: [[heres-the-technique-that-ambitious-people-use-to-get-what-they-want-ryanholiday]] reports that Thiel approved a proposed shell company, investigative and legal team, three-to-five-year timeline, and $10 million budget after challenging the proposer's initial pitch.
- Palantir founding: [[inside-palantir-silicon-valleys-most-secretive-company]] identifies Thiel as a former PayPal CEO who founded Palantir with several others in 2004.
- PayPal collaboration: [[uiuc-2018-commencement-address-too-long-to-tweet]] says Levchin met Thiel after moving to Palo Alto and that the two founded the company that became PayPal.

## Qualifications
The profile is narrow and source-scoped. The wiki has not yet ingested an independent biography of Thiel or a comprehensive PayPal history; Levchin's brief collaborator account does not specify roles, ownership, chronology, disputes, or the contributions of other founders and employees. The checklist source points to Thiel's questions but does not independently validate whether using them predicts startup success. Holiday's account is a secondary retelling from the author's own book research, gives no records with which to verify the meeting independently, and uses the episode to teach preparation rather than fully assess the campaign's legal, ethical, or media consequences. The Palantir investigation establishes a founding role but does not specify Thiel's 2016 ownership, board authority, operating involvement, or responsibility for the client, financial, and workforce conditions it reports.
