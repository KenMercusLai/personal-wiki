## Overview
[[LindsayHolmwood]] is a technology leader and writer represented in the wiki through a practitioner essay about the transition from engineering to management.

## Current Profile
Holmwood presents management as a learned profession centered on multiplying group performance rather than preserving an engineer's individual-output identity. Drawing on almost four years in leadership, Holmwood describes early inadequacy, mistakes, incorrect priorities, and accidental harm as reasons to approach the transition with humility, training, mentorship, reflection, and continued practice.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames engineering management as a career change rather than a promotion.
- Defines a leader's purpose as removing friction and improving the team's collective effectiveness.
- Emphasizes listening, trust, conflict resolution, advocacy, difficult communication, and systemic thinking.
- Treats management competence as deliberately learnable through training, mentorship, reading, critique, and reflection.
- Uses personal mistakes and changed success criteria to argue for humility during the transition.

## Evidence
- Career distinction and multiplier model: [[its-not-a-promotion-its-a-career-change-fractional-by-lindsay-holmwood]] separates engineering output from a manager's responsibility for enabling people and teams.
- Development practice: [[its-not-a-promotion-its-a-career-change-fractional-by-lindsay-holmwood]] recommends professional training, diverse mentors, transition peers, self-education, and reflective critique.
- Experience boundary: [[its-not-a-promotion-its-a-career-change-fractional-by-lindsay-holmwood]] reports almost four years in the new career and acknowledges mistakes, misplaced priorities, and accidental interpersonal harm.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one first-person practitioner essay. It does not independently establish Holmwood's broader career history, the effectiveness of the recommended development methods, or the prevalence of the described technology-industry culture.
