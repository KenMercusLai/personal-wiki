## Overview
[[JonathanCourtney]] is an [[AJSmart]] founding partner and UX director represented here by prototype-first product arguments and a detailed remote redesign of [[Oak]].

## Current Profile
Courtney describes changing his consultancy's process after personas, empathy maps, interviews, and documentation repeatedly contributed less actionable evidence than the first user tests. He adopted the [[GV]] [[DesignSprint]] model to make assumptions tangible quickly, while preserving deeper discovery for unclear problems. In the Oak account, he extends that practice to remote client work through explicit shared notes, inspiration references, votes, narrated feedback, prototype alternatives, and alignment check-ins; however, the case records client approval rather than the behavioral user evidence central to his broader argument.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats lengthy up-front research as a possible form of product procrastination.
- Prioritizes realistic prototype behavior over confidence derived from documentation.
- Uses AJ&Smart's shift from long design packages to Design Sprints as the central case.
- Preserves exploratory research for ambiguous or consequential problem domains.
- Treats explicit artifacts and frequent alignment as essential controls for remote design work.
- Uses staged visual choices to convert subjective preference into decisions before production.

## Evidence
- Process critique: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] says AJ&Smart's personas and research documentation often stopped influencing the work once design and testing began.
- Prototype-first practice: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] reports that one five-day sprint advanced a client product further than the firm's previous six-to-eight-week package.
- Scope qualification: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] names hospital emergency-room improvement as a case where up-front research can make sense.
- Remote workflow: [[from-idea-to-app-store-a-design-sprint-case-study-ux-planet]] describes Courtney's team moving from expert interview and shared mapping through sketches, votes, narrated feedback, and prototype variants.
- Self-qualification: [[from-idea-to-app-store-a-design-sprint-case-study-ux-planet]] warns that expanding one screen into a full redesign is not normal sprint practice without unusual experience and capacity.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from Courtney's own favorable practitioner essays. They provide no independent outcome measure, failed-project comparison, or evidence that Oak's recognition resulted from the design process; the Oak case also omits the end-user testing that grounds Courtney's prototype-first claim elsewhere.

## What Changed
- Added Courtney's remote coordination practices and separated client alignment from user validation.
- Added his explicit warning against exceptional scope expansion as routine sprint practice.
