## Overview
[[JonathanCourtney]] is an [[AJSmart]] founding partner and UX director represented in this wiki by a critique of intensive up-front user research.

## Current Profile
Courtney describes changing his consultancy's process after personas, empathy maps, interviews, and documentation repeatedly contributed less actionable evidence than the first user tests. He adopted the [[GV]] [[DesignSprint]] model to reach a realistic prototype and observed use within about five days, arguing that teams learn more quickly when assumptions become tangible. His position is conditional rather than a rejection of research: deeper discovery remains valuable when the problem itself is unclear.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats lengthy up-front research as a possible form of product procrastination.
- Prioritizes realistic prototype behavior over confidence derived from documentation.
- Uses AJ&Smart's shift from long design packages to Design Sprints as the central case.
- Preserves exploratory research for ambiguous or consequential problem domains.

## Evidence
- Process critique: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] says AJ&Smart's personas and research documentation often stopped influencing the work once design and testing began.
- Prototype-first practice: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] reports that one five-day sprint advanced a client product further than the firm's previous six-to-eight-week package.
- Scope qualification: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] names hospital emergency-room improvement as a case where up-front research can make sense.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from one polemical first-person essay and one selected client comparison. It provides no independent outcome measure, longitudinal evidence, failure cases, or detail about project comparability; claims such as prototypes being "infinitely" more valuable are rhetorical rather than measured.
