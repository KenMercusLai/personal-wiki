## Overview
[[AnthonyWood]] led Netflix's [[ProjectGriffin]] streaming-player team and became CEO of [[Roku]] after the program was spun out of [[Netflix]].

## Current Profile
In the source's retrospective, Wood is both the operating lead who carried a nearly launch-ready player through hardware, software, interface, thermal, supply-chain, and manufacturing work and a later defender of the decision not to release it under the Netflix brand. He argues that first-party Netflix hardware would have created continuing tension between decisions benefiting the device and decisions benefiting licensing across other manufacturers.

## Key Characteristics
- Led the roughly twenty-person Project Griffin team inside Netflix.
- Oversaw work spanning product design, software, manufacturing, and Foxconn production preparation.
- Experienced Hastings' cancellation as a surprising reversal after substantial launch momentum.
- Continued the device effort through the Roku spinout.
- Retrospectively favored Netflix's service-partnership strategy over combined service and hardware ownership.

## Evidence
- Griffin leadership: [[inside-netflixs-project-griffin-the-forgotten-history-of-roku-under]] identifies Wood as the leader of the internal player project.
- Launch preparation: [[inside-netflixs-project-griffin-the-forgotten-history-of-roku-under]] quotes Wood on the working hardware, company demonstrations, and internal enthusiasm.
- Strategic judgment: [[inside-netflixs-project-griffin-the-forgotten-history-of-roku-under]] quotes Wood that hardware would have forced conflicts between licensing and device interests.
- Spinout outcome: [[inside-netflixs-project-griffin-the-forgotten-history-of-roku-under]] identifies Wood as Roku CEO when recounting the project.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from one 2013 retrospective article and selected quotations. It does not reconstruct Wood's full career, quantify his individual contribution, or include a complete contemporaneous record of the spinout and Roku's formation.
