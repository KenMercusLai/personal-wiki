## Overview
[[CYY]] is the authorial identity behind a reflective essay on AI, systems engineering, and the future of hobbyist black-box exploration.

## Current Profile
The source presents CYY as a systems-oriented hobbyist and engineer whose path began with early computer tinkering, programming, hardware modding, Linux desktops, robotics, Arduino, OI, and ICPC. Later experiences include building a Linux-capable five-stage dual-issue CPU in NSCSCC, helping with the one-student-one-chip project, reforming undergraduate CPU and OS labs, contributing to GCC and the Linux kernel through PLCT, and interning at XiangShan and Intel. This background makes the AI transition personally consequential: the concern is not only employment disruption, but the loss of learning and invention that came from opening black boxes directly.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats computing as a lifelong practice of opening black boxes rather than only a profession.
- Connects competitive programming, CPU/OS education, open-source contribution, and hardware/software systems work.
- Evaluates AI coding from personal experience with [[GitHubCopilot]] and current frontier models.
- Worries that AI offloading can separate task success from skill growth and creative idea formation.
- Responds by emphasizing subjective human problem discovery, slow reflection, and fast action on felt opportunities.
