## Overview
[[Path]] was a private mobile social product initially designed for sharing personal photos with close friends and family. The source presents its 2011 redesign as a behavior-led shift toward a broader private journal of everyday life.

## Current Profile
Path's first version combined mobile photography with a privacy thesis: intimate camera-roll photos needed a less public destination than the social networks then expanding around broad sharing. The product reached about 10,000 daily active users before growth plateaued. Primary research later found users stretching photo posts into proxies for other experiences by sharing screenshots of album covers, notes, running maps, and health apps. Path 2 responded by supporting photos, music, locations, thoughts, and health directly, with a Nike+ Running integration. The source reports that daily active users rose from about 10,000 to more than 500,000 within two weeks, making Path a strong first-party example of discovering a wider product identity through observed behavior.

## Key Characteristics
- Mobile social product built around private sharing with close friends and family.
- Began with personal photographs as its intended core content.
- Plateaued near 10,000 daily active users before its 2011 redesign.
- Users repurposed screenshots to journal music, notes, exercise, and health activity.
- Path 2 expanded the product into a multi-format private life journal.
- Reportedly exceeded 500,000 daily active users within two weeks of the Path 2 launch.

## Evidence

- Behavioral discovery: [[get-out-of-the-way-design-philosophy-medium]] says research found screenshots of album covers, notes, Nike+ Running maps, and health apps among user posts.
- Product expansion: [[get-out-of-the-way-design-philosophy-medium]] says Path 2 made photos, music, locations, thoughts, and health core cases and integrated Nike+ Running.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one short, first-person retrospective whose author is not identified in the supplied Markdown. It reports a dramatic active-user increase but provides no cohort, retention, acquisition, revenue, or independent causal evidence, and it does not cover Path's later trajectory. The case supports a discovery mechanism more strongly than it establishes the redesign as the sole cause of growth.
