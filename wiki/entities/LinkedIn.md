## Overview
[[LinkedIn]] appears as a company context for scaling lessons, mission and culture codification, viral product mechanics, the [[EconomicGraph]] vision, and [[SachinRekhi]]'s practitioner account of using [[NetPromoterScore]] in product planning.

## Current Profile
The scaling source uses LinkedIn as an organization that had to formalize hiring and communication as it grew across employee counts and offices. The dedicated Weiner session adds the operating narrative behind that summary: LinkedIn moved from roughly 338 employees into a much larger distributed company by codifying mission, vision, values, objectives, all-hands communication, ownership language, and coaching norms. The virality source adds a product-growth angle: by showing connection counts prominently on profiles, LinkedIn made network size into a visible goal and nudged users to invite more people into the network. The NPS source adds an internal product-operations angle: Rekhi used LinkedIn experience to describe quarterly loyalty surveys, cross-functional analysis, verbatim comment categorization, and behavior correlation as inputs to roadmap planning.

## Key Characteristics
- Illustrates recruiting machinery that grows from simple to specialized.
- Uses recurring all-hands meetings for company-wide alignment.
- Reinforces culture through leadership behavior, hiring, rewards, and evaluation.
- Serves as a context for product-sense claims about Silicon Valley CEOs.
- Uses an economic-graph vision to connect professional identity, jobs, skills, learning, and publishing.
- Balances consumer membership and enterprise sales through a member-first value.
- Provides product-growth and product-planning examples through public connection counts and quarterly NPS analysis.
