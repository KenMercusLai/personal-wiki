## Overview
[[LinkedIn]] appears as both the company context for [[JeffWeiner]]'s scaling lessons and a public-profile product whose connection metrics can motivate user invitations.

## Current Profile
The scaling source uses LinkedIn as an organization that had to formalize hiring and communication as it grew across employee counts and offices. The virality source adds a product-growth angle: by showing connection counts prominently on profiles, LinkedIn made network size into a visible goal and nudged users to invite more people into the network.

## Key Characteristics
- Illustrates recruiting machinery that grows from simple to specialized.
- Uses recurring all-hands meetings for company-wide alignment.
- Reinforces culture through leadership behavior, hiring, rewards, and evaluation.
- Serves as a context for product-sense claims about Silicon Valley CEOs.
- Uses public connection counts as a vanity and network-growth surface.

## Evidence
- Recruiting scale: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] cites Weiner's 15, 150, and 1,500 employee recruiting contrast.
- All-hands: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] says LinkedIn broadcast all-hands meetings to offices in many cities and time zones.
- Culture reinforcement: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] uses Weiner's LinkedIn perspective to explain values in practice.
- Connection-count loop: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] says prominently displayed connection counts encouraged users to invite more contacts, bringing more people into the network.
