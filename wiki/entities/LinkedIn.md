## Overview
[[LinkedIn]] appears as a company context for scaling lessons, mission and culture codification, viral product mechanics, the [[EconomicGraph]] vision, product-planning use of [[NetPromoterScore]], and public headcount as a competitive-estimation signal.

## Current Profile
The scaling source uses LinkedIn as an organization that had to formalize hiring and communication as it grew across employee counts and offices. The dedicated Weiner session adds the operating narrative behind that summary: LinkedIn moved from roughly 338 employees into a much larger distributed company by codifying mission, vision, values, objectives, all-hands communication, ownership language, and coaching norms. The virality source adds a product-growth angle: prominent connection counts made network size into a visible goal and nudged invitations. The NPS source adds an internal product-operations angle through quarterly loyalty surveys, cross-functional analysis, comment categorization, and behavior correlation. Lemkin's revenue-estimation article treats company-profile employee counts as an external signal for estimating private-company scale, but asserts their accuracy without auditing completeness, freshness, contractors, or full-time-equivalent boundaries.

## Key Characteristics
- Illustrates recruiting machinery that grows from simple to specialized.
- Uses recurring all-hands meetings for company-wide alignment.
- Reinforces culture through leadership behavior, hiring, rewards, and evaluation.
- Uses an economic-graph vision to connect professional identity, jobs, skills, learning, and publishing.
- Balances consumer membership and enterprise sales through a member-first value.
- Provides product-growth and product-planning examples through public connection counts and quarterly NPS analysis.
- Supplies public company-profile headcount that outsiders may use as a rough competitive signal.

## Qualifications
The page remains source-bounded: it covers LinkedIn as a scaling-organization example, a mission and economic-graph case, a virality example, a product-metrics context, and a proposed source of public headcount. Lemkin supplies no measurement of profile coverage or headcount error, so the revenue-estimation use should not be read as a validated LinkedIn data-quality claim. The page does not cover LinkedIn's full product, financial, acquisition, Microsoft, or labor-market impact history.
