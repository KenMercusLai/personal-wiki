## Overview
[[BleacherReport]] is a sports publisher presented by Digiday as an early audience-development operator and a case in platform-era distributed publishing.

## Current Profile
The source frames Bleacher Report as moving beyond the older model of attracting all attention back to its own site. Under [[RoryBrown]]'s account, it builds sports content for search, social platforms, apps, Facebook video, and Instant Articles while trying not to become overdependent on any one channel. Its Team Stream app is reported at 15 million downloads, and the company is described as a 300-plus-person cornerstone of Turner's digital media strategy.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses [[DistributedPublishingStrategy]] to reach audiences wherever they already spend time.
- Practices channel hedging across search, social, apps, and external platforms.
- Assigns platform-specific editorial missions and managing-editor responsibility.
- Treats Facebook as a direct distribution and video surface rather than only a referral source.
- Measures success through [[SocialInteractionMetrics]] such as views, likes, and shares as well as owned-audience indicators.

## Evidence
- Platform-era strategy: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says Bleacher Report embraced content that can live both on its own properties and on outside platforms.
- Audience-development history: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says critics focused on aggregation and SEO, while Brown argues the company was matching content supply to search demand.
- App funnel: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says search traffic was converted to email newsletters and then to the Team Stream app.
- Metrics shift: [[bleacher-report-embraces-content-everywhere-strategy]] says social interactions were becoming a more important barometer than destination-site uniques.

## Qualifications
The source is a 2016 Digiday profile built around Brown's perspective, so it does not evaluate later Bleacher Report outcomes, platform policy changes, economics, or whether social interaction metrics converted into durable revenue.
