## Overview
[[Bandcamp]] is represented as a music platform whose tags, collections, buyer activity, catalog, and editorial work support intentional exploration.

## Current Profile
The interviewees use Bandcamp differently: Lauren Rearick searches genre tags and follows users' purchases, Hua Hsu looks at what people with overlapping purchases explore, and senior editor Marcus Moore performs professional online crate digging. Together these accounts present discovery as navigation through explicit cultural and social signals rather than only personalized prediction.

## Key Characteristics
- Organizes music through searchable tags.
- Exposes collection and purchase trails that can guide adjacent discovery.
- Supports editorial browsing and professional catalog review.
- Functions as a complement to streaming recommendation and music publications.

## Evidence
- Tag exploration: [[finding-new-music-in-the-algorithm-age-the-outline]] describes searches across several genre labels.
- Social trails: [[finding-new-music-in-the-algorithm-age-the-outline]] reports following users and overlapping buyers to see what they purchase.
- Editorial use: [[finding-new-music-in-the-algorithm-age-the-outline]] describes Moore's Bandcamp-based crate digging as part of his senior-editor role.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on three interviewees' 2018 discovery habits, not a complete product or company history. It does not measure recommendation quality, artist economics, catalog coverage, or how the platform has changed since publication.
