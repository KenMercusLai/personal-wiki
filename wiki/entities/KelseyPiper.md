## Overview
[[KelseyPiper]] is represented here as the [[Triplebyte]] employee who wrote a 2018 first-person account of producing and improving individualized rejection feedback for engineering candidates.

## Current Profile
Piper reports writing more than 3,000 detailed rejection emails in one year. Her role combined interviewers' structured observations, appropriateness review, decision-relevant explanation, and maintained recommendations for free learning resources. The experience led her to reject a simple story in which companies withhold feedback only because of legal fear: in her account, responsible feedback is expensive to produce, candidates can react badly to judgments that seem broad or inconsistent, and legal risk rises when an improvised process exposes bias or creates its appearance.

## Key Characteristics
- Edited structured interviewer evidence into individualized rejection messages.
- Treated candidate interpretation and emotional response as design inputs rather than assuming detail was inherently helpful.
- Distinguished observed interview performance from claims about a candidate's underlying knowledge.
- Emphasized the weaknesses that actually affected the decision and made mixed evidence more specific.
- Presented detailed feedback as a resource-intensive, iterative organizational capability.

## Evidence
- Work volume and role: [[i-know-why-rejection-emails-suck-i-write-them-triplebyte-blog]] says Piper wrote more than 3,000 individualized rejection emails over a year and periodically checked that recommended resources remained current, available, and free.
- Process diagnosis: [[i-know-why-rejection-emails-suck-i-write-them-triplebyte-blog]] attributes limited feedback mainly to workload and difficult candidate reactions, with legal exposure becoming material when the process is careless or biased.
- Wording revisions: [[i-know-why-rejection-emails-suck-i-write-them-triplebyte-blog]] describes changing claims about what candidates knew into bounded statements about what they communicated or demonstrated.
- Decision clarity: [[i-know-why-rejection-emails-suck-i-write-them-triplebyte-blog]] says later messages emphasized decisive sections and explained apparently mixed strengths and weaknesses more precisely.
