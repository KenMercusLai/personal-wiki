## Overview
[[DavidRobinson]] appears as the Stack Overflow data scientist who authored the analysis of programming-technology differences across wealthy and developing countries.

## Current Profile
In the source, Robinson uses Stack Overflow traffic data to compare technology-tag attention across national income groups. His analysis is careful about scope: it treats question visits as a useful but limited developer signal, emphasizes segmentation by World Bank income category, and warns against causal interpretations of the GDP correlations.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses Stack Overflow traffic as a quantitative lens on the global developer ecosystem.
- Segments countries by World Bank high-income status rather than by region alone.
- Interprets programming-language differences as social and economic signals rather than simple causality.
- Highlights audience-specific implications for recruiters, students, and investors.

## Evidence
- Data method: [[a-tale-of-two-industries-how-programming-languages-differ-between-wealthy-and-developing-countries-stack-overflow-blog]] analyzes 2017 traffic by country, tag, and GDP per capita.
- Segmentation choice: [[a-tale-of-two-industries-how-programming-languages-differ-between-wealthy-and-developing-countries-stack-overflow-blog]] argues that World Bank high-income status is more informative than some geographic splits.
- Causal caution: [[a-tale-of-two-industries-how-programming-languages-differ-between-wealthy-and-developing-countries-stack-overflow-blog]] explicitly says the analysis does not prove causality.
- Practical audience: [[a-tale-of-two-industries-how-programming-languages-differ-between-wealthy-and-developing-countries-stack-overflow-blog]] contrasts the needs of an American recruiter, an Indian student, and an investor studying Kenya.
