## Overview
[[BillGurley]] appears in this wiki as a venture investor referenced for marketplace evaluation and for warning employees about the liquidity consequences of preference-heavy late-stage financing.

## Current Profile
David Lee points marketplace founders to Gurley’s “All Markets Are Not Created Equal” factors as a checklist for evaluating digital marketplaces, but does not reproduce the factors. Andrew Chen supplies a more specific attribution from Gurley's Uber analysis: local growth can shorten pickup times, expand coverage density, and raise driver utilization, with utilization potentially supporting lower fares and more use cases. Scott Belsky cites a separate Gurley post, “On the Road to Recap,” for the warning that employees face very low odds of receiving liquidity near the value they expect when leadership both accepts a “dirty” round and resists an IPO. The evidence therefore represents Gurley as a cited authority on marketplace quality, [[MarketplaceLiquidity]], and financing-dependent employee liquidity, while remaining indirect because the original Gurley articles have not been ingested.

## Key Characteristics
- Used as a marketplace-specific authority in a startup checklist collection.
- Associated with evaluating whether different digital marketplace opportunities are attractive.
- Functions as a supplement to general startup, fundraising, and metrics checklists.
- Cited for connecting preference-heavy late-stage rounds with reduced employee liquidity prospects.
- Treats leadership’s willingness to pursue an IPO as relevant to whether private-company equity becomes realizable.
- Connects local marketplace growth with shorter pickup times and wider geographic supply coverage.
- Treats higher supplier utilization as a possible route from density to lower prices and more use cases.

## Qualifications
This page is intentionally source-scoped. The wiki has not ingested the original Gurley articles, so it should not infer the ten marketplace factors, treat Chen's summary of the Uber mechanisms as Gurley's complete argument, define every structure he would call a dirty round, or generalize the quoted employee-liquidity warning beyond Belsky’s use of it. The marketplace claims are also directional and provide no city-level pickup, coverage, utilization, pricing, or driver-earnings data; the financing claim does not quantify outcomes or account for later recapitalization, tender offers, acquisitions, or changes in IPO strategy.
