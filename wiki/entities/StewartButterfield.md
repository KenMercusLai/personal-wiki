## Overview
[[StewartButterfield]] appears in the wiki as the leader quoted by [[ZachHolman]] to illustrate dissatisfaction with a successful product's current state.

## Current Profile
The source uses Butterfield's deliberately exaggerated criticism of [[Slack]] as a counterexample to product complacency. Holman interprets the remark as respect for product development as a continuing process, not as a literal dismissal of employees' work.

## Key Characteristics
- Associated with Slack's product leadership in the quoted interview.
- Expresses urgency about continued product improvement despite public success.
- Serves as an example of separating confidence in a company from satisfaction with its current product.

## Evidence
- Product posture: [[evaluating-delusional-startups]] quotes Butterfield describing Slack's current product in deliberately harsh terms while discussing future changes.
- Interpretation: [[evaluating-delusional-startups]] reads the remark as facetious pressure for continued refinement rather than contempt for the team.
- Diligence signal: [[evaluating-delusional-startups]] contrasts Butterfield's improvement posture with startups that rely on old reputation while competition advances.

## Qualifications
The corpus evidence here is one quotation embedded in Holman's essay, not a complete account of Butterfield's leadership, Slack's development process, or employee experience. The interpretation belongs to Holman, and rhetorical self-criticism can motivate improvement or create unhealthy pressure depending on how leaders apply it.
