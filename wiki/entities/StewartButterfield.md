## Overview
[[StewartButterfield]] appears in the wiki as a [[Slack]] founder articulating the company's early product, launch, customer-feedback, and measurement practices, and as the leader quoted by [[ZachHolman]] to illustrate dissatisfaction with a successful product's current state.

## Current Profile
The First Round interview presents Butterfield as a founder who connected Slack's early traction to progressively larger beta cohorts, category education, team-level adoption, systematic qualitative-feedback processing, a product-specific activation threshold, and concentrated quality in search, synchronization, and file sharing. The later Holman source uses Butterfield's deliberately exaggerated criticism of [[Slack]] as a counterexample to product complacency, interpreting it as respect for product development as a continuing process rather than a literal dismissal of employees' work. Together, the sources show a consistent improvement posture, but only through favorable practitioner accounts rather than employee, customer, or comparative evidence.

## Key Characteristics
- Associated with Slack's product leadership in the quoted interview.
- Describes staged customer learning and fast response to feedback as core early operating practices.
- Connects category education and bottom-up team adoption to Slack's go-to-market model.
- Advocates product-specific activation and retention definitions rather than generic growth metrics.
- Emphasizes a few unusually strong product capabilities over broad feature completeness.
- Expresses urgency about continued product improvement despite public success.

## Evidence
- Launch and learning system: [[from-0-to-1b-slacks-founder-shares-their-epic-launch-strategy-first-round-review]] attributes Slack's early progress to larger beta cohorts, preview iteration, category teaching, support operations, and focused product quality.
- Feedback operations: [[from-0-to-1b-slacks-founder-shares-their-epic-launch-strategy-first-round-review]] quotes Butterfield on tagging, retaining, routing, and acting on qualitative feedback alongside usage and sales metrics.
- Metric definition: [[from-0-to-1b-slacks-founder-shares-their-epic-launch-strategy-first-round-review]] presents 2,000 team messages as Slack's historical threshold for a meaningful trial and reported retained use.
- Product posture: [[evaluating-delusional-startups]] quotes Butterfield describing Slack's current product in deliberately harsh terms while discussing future changes.
- Interpretation: [[evaluating-delusional-startups]] reads the remark as facetious pressure for continued refinement rather than contempt for the team.
- Diligence signal: [[evaluating-delusional-startups]] contrasts Butterfield's improvement posture with startups that rely on old reputation while competition advances.

## Qualifications
The corpus evidence consists of a favorable founder interview and one quotation embedded in Holman's essay, not a complete account of Butterfield's leadership, Slack's development process, or employee experience. The launch figures and 2,000-message retention relationship are self-reported without complete cohort or causal detail. Holman's interpretation belongs to Holman, and rhetorical self-criticism can motivate improvement or create unhealthy pressure depending on how leaders apply it.

## What Changed
- Expanded the profile from one product-humility quotation to Butterfield's source-scoped account of Slack's staged learning, launch, category education, feedback system, activation metric, and focused differentiation.
