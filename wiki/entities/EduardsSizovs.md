## Overview
[[EduardsSizovs]] is a software-development writer represented here by a 2019 practitioner essay arguing that companies should build developers through mentoring rather than compete only for already polished candidates.

## Current Profile
In the available source, Sizovs treats technical talent as developable organizational capacity rather than a fixed commodity found through increasingly restrictive interviews. He asks experienced developers to learn mentoring, receive protected teaching time, and grow less-experienced colleagues through pairing, feedback, career advice, and curated learning. His account is strongest as a challenge to confidence- and pedigree-heavy selection; it does not supply comparative hiring, performance, retention, inclusion, cost, or mentoring-capacity data.

## Key Characteristics
- Advocates development-first engineering hiring and workplace apprenticeship.
- Treats mentoring as an organizational capability that needs senior time and communication skill.
- Favors learning attitude and growth capacity over current polish or interview confidence.
- Uses vivid scarcity and raw-diamond metaphors to criticize competition for experienced talent.
- Presents broad practitioner prescriptions without measured outcome evidence.

## Evidence
- Talent-development thesis: [[great-developers-are-raised-not-hired-the-principal-developer-by-eduards-sizovs]] argues that companies perpetuate scarcity by searching for strong engineers instead of helping create them.
- Mentoring mechanism: [[great-developers-are-raised-not-hired-the-principal-developer-by-eduards-sizovs]] recommends pair programming, continuous feedback, career advice, books, videos, meetups, conferences, workshops, and protected mentor time.
- Hiring implications: [[great-developers-are-raised-not-hired-the-principal-developer-by-eduards-sizovs]] recommends relaxing hard requirements, selecting for learning capacity, and considering beginner bootcamps.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one short 2019 essay and should not be read as a complete biography or assessment of Sizovs's wider work. The article gives no definitions or measurements for developer quality, learning capacity, loyalty, interview simplification, mentoring return, or workforce outcomes. Its "broken toys" and "raw diamonds" language can also reduce candidates to damaged or unfinished inputs even while arguing for their potential and dignity.

## What Changed
- Created a source-bounded profile of Sizovs as an advocate for mentoring capacity and development-first engineering hiring.
