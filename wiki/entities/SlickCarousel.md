## Overview
[[SlickCarousel]] is the open-source carousel library [[KenWheeler]] uses as his central example of a successful project that began from agency ecommerce needs and later became a maintenance burden.

## Current Profile
The source frames Slick Carousel as a jQuery-era solution to fashion ecommerce carousel requirements that existing libraries could not handle flexibly enough. Its adoption gave Wheeler early open-source success, but the project also became the example of how popularity can create burnout, criticism, guilt, and long-term maintenance pressure.

## Key Characteristics
- Originated from repeated fashion ecommerce carousel and slider needs.
- Was designed to be flexible, easy to use, and easy to modify compared with prior carousel libraries.
- Became popular quickly after release.
- Became emotionally costly when Wheeler left the problem space, moved on technologically, and experienced maintainer burnout.

## Evidence
- Problem origin: [[a-bitter-guide-to-open-source-codezillas-medium]] says Wheeler built carousels repeatedly for fashion ecommerce projects because existing options did not support the designs he received.
- Product intent: [[a-bitter-guide-to-open-source-codezillas-medium]] says he wanted a carousel plugin flexible enough for designers' demands and easy for developers to use and modify.
- Adoption: [[a-bitter-guide-to-open-source-codezillas-medium]] describes the first months after release as highly popular.
- Maintenance burden: [[a-bitter-guide-to-open-source-codezillas-medium]] uses Slick Carousel to discuss burnout, user entitlement, shifting interests, and guilt about letting users down.
