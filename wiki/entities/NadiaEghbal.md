## Overview
[[NadiaEghbal]] is represented in the wiki as a [[Feast]] cofounder and as an author examining the language, culture, labor, and sustainability of public software development.

## Current Profile
The available startup source names Eghbal as David Spinks's Feast cofounder but supplies no separate account of her role. Her 2016 open-source essay argues that common practice had outgrown a purely license-centered cultural vocabulary: GitHub normalized building and collaborating in public, while authors and communities still lacked clear ways to formalize stewardship, time, compensation, and ownership.

Eghbal proposes [[PublicSoftware]] as an accessible umbrella rather than a replacement legal regime. Her follow-up is essential to the profile: she explicitly defends open-source licensing and access rights, then locates the unresolved problem in the surrounding culture and support system.

## Key Characteristics
- Cofounded [[Feast]] with [[DavidSpinks]].
- Distinguishes the legal definition of open source from the broader culture of public development.
- Focuses on author and community stewardship, compensation, sustainability, and participation.
- Proposes “public software” as an inclusive umbrella while preserving open-source rights.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on two 2016 first-person sources. The startup source only names Eghbal as a cofounder, and the essay captures her position at that time rather than a complete biography or current view. Its GitHub licensing statistic is historical, and the proposed terminology is an argument, not evidence that users adopted it or found it clearer.
