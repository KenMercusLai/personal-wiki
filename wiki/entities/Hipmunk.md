## Overview
[[Hipmunk]] appears as the travel-service chatbot example used to argue that early bots could already outperform mobile web for some tasks.

## Current Profile
In Livingston's source, Hipmunk is not analyzed as a full company. It matters as a concrete transactional bot: [[DavidMarcus]] says its chatbot could book airline tickets and hotels in an experience close to native apps and better than mobile web. The example supplies the article's strongest practical evidence for chat as a service interface.

## Key Characteristics
- Travel booking service used as a chatbot example.
- Represents a transactional bot rather than a content or entertainment bot.
- Serves as evidence that some chat experiences could beat mobile web quality by late 2016.

## Evidence
- Travel bot example: [[chat-is-the-new-browser-ted-livingston-medium]] says users could book airline tickets and hotels through Hipmunk's chatbot.
- Quality comparison: [[chat-is-the-new-browser-ted-livingston-medium]] cites Marcus's view that the experience was close to native app quality and better than mobile web.
- Platform role: [[chat-is-the-new-browser-ted-livingston-medium]] uses Hipmunk to support the claim that chatbots were improving quickly.

## Qualifications
The source gives only a brief product example and does not evaluate Hipmunk's business model, adoption, conversion performance, or later trajectory.

## What Changed
- Created Hipmunk as the source's central transactional chatbot example.
