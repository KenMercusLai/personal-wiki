## Overview
[[TimUrban]] is the writer behind [[WaitButWhy]], represented here by an illustrated career framework and a personal travelogue about [[Bhutan]].

## Current Profile
Across the two current sources, Urban uses personal experience, informal humor, and inspected visuals to make unfamiliar systems legible. [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-how-to-pick-a-career]] develops a first-principles self-audit for careers; [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-the-sights-and-sounds-of-bhutan]] turns a guided trip into a compact account of geography, culture, policy, and development tension. In both, he foregrounds a memorable explanatory frame while signaling important limits to his authority.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses illustrated metaphors and named internal characters to make abstract self-reflection easier to inspect.
- Treats career choice as a first-principles reasoning problem rather than a conventional-wisdom problem.
- Emphasizes self-knowledge, motive auditing, and humility about changing future selves.
- Uses travel observation, local testimony, and visual evidence to introduce places to a general audience.
- Flags uncertainty when a visitor's impressions or official stories cannot be independently verified.

## Evidence
- Illustrated framework style: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-how-to-pick-a-career]] uses diagrams such as the Yearning Octopus, yearning hierarchy, Want/Reality overlap, and dot-jump loops as part of the argument.
- First-principles career reasoning: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-how-to-pick-a-career]] revisits the cook-versus-chef distinction and applies it to career-path choices.
- Experimental career view: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-how-to-pick-a-career]] argues that careers are connected dots in a changing world, so the next dot should test a hypothesis.
- Visual travel explanation: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-the-sights-and-sounds-of-bhutan]] combines a regional map, architectural photographs, and a linked video with a short national profile.
- Epistemic qualification: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-the-sights-and-sounds-of-bhutan]] says Urban could not verify the happiest-country claim and explicitly reflects on the risk of accepting favorable ruler narratives.

## Qualifications
Both sources are first-person explanatory essays rather than systematic research. The career framework does not provide empirical career-outcomes evidence, while the Bhutan article comes from a one-week guided visit and relies on local testimony without supplying primary policy, emissions, migration, or wellbeing data.
