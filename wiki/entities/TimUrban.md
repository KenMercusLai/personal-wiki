## Overview
[[TimUrban]] is the writer of the [[WaitButWhy]] essay [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-how-to-pick-a-career]], represented here by his career-decision framework.

## Current Profile
The wiki currently knows Urban through one source: an illustrated long-form essay about career choice. In that source, he positions himself less as a career expert than as a framework builder drawing from his own career-path uncertainty, turns, friend observations, and first-principles habit. His contribution is a synthesis-heavy self-audit method that asks readers to question inherited desires, assess real career game boards, and treat the next career move as an experiment rather than a life sentence.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses illustrated metaphors and named internal characters to make abstract self-reflection easier to inspect.
- Treats career choice as a first-principles reasoning problem rather than a conventional-wisdom problem.
- Emphasizes self-knowledge, motive auditing, and humility about changing future selves.

## Evidence
- Illustrated framework style: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-how-to-pick-a-career]] uses diagrams such as the Yearning Octopus, yearning hierarchy, Want/Reality overlap, and dot-jump loops as part of the argument.
- First-principles career reasoning: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-how-to-pick-a-career]] revisits the cook-versus-chef distinction and applies it to career-path choices.
- Experimental career view: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-how-to-pick-a-career]] argues that careers are connected dots in a changing world, so the next dot should test a hypothesis.

## Qualifications
The source is reflective and framework-driven, not empirical career-outcomes research. Urban explicitly avoids ranking career values or careers and presents the model as a thinking aid rather than authoritative advice.
