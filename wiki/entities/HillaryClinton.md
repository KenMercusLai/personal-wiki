## Overview
[[HillaryClinton]] is the comparison candidate in the POLITICO source's account of 2016 campaign technology.

## Current Profile
The article contrasts Clinton's campaign with [[BernieSanders]]' volunteer-driven technology ecosystem. Clinton's operation is described as professionally staffed and connected to experienced Democratic and technology talent, but critics in the article characterize it as hierarchical, guarded, and slower to ship public-facing innovations.

## Key Characteristics
- Serves as the centralized-campaign comparison case.
- Has experienced digital and technology staff rather than a primarily volunteer coder movement.
- Is portrayed by critics as constrained by approvals and hierarchy.
- Focused public technology claims on tools for donations, organizing, persuasion, and optimization rather than volunteer-built app proliferation.

## Evidence
- Comparison frame: [[2016-bernies-army-of-coders-politico-magazine]] contrasts Clinton's operation with Sanders' bottom-up volunteer ecosystem.
- Staffed operation: [[2016-bernies-army-of-coders-politico-magazine]] says her campaign included experienced digital hands and former Google executive Stephanie Hannon.
- Hierarchy critique: [[2016-bernies-army-of-coders-politico-magazine]] cites Democratic and GOP technology experts who saw management structure as slowing projects.
- Public posture: [[2016-bernies-army-of-coders-politico-magazine]] quotes a campaign spokesperson emphasizing tools for fundraising, organizing, voter contact, and optimization.

## Qualifications
The article gives Clinton's campaign mainly as a contrast case and relies partly on outside critics. It does not provide a full audit of the campaign's internal technology stack or later general-election operation.
