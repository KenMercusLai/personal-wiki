## Overview
[[UniversityOfIllinoisUrbanaChampaign]] is the university where [[MaxLevchin]] studied computer science and delivered his 2018 commencement address, represented in that source as both a computing environment and a durable relationship network.

## Current Profile
Levchin says a guidance counselor directed him toward the University of Illinois after science-fair success made state scholarships and a subsidized loan available. He arrived in 1993 as the National Center for Supercomputing Applications' Mosaic browser was helping make the graphical web broadly consequential, and he found a technical community in the Digital Computer Lab.

The university's role in the address extends beyond instruction. Levchin says two fellow engineering students recruited him into his first startup, many early PayPal engineers were Illinois classmates, and those relationships continued across later companies and projects. The institution is therefore presented as a place where technical access, peer proximity, and repeated collaboration interacted.

## Key Characteristics
- Public university and Levchin's computer-science alma mater.
- Connected in the speech to state financial support that made attendance possible.
- Home to the National Center for Supercomputing Applications during Mosaic's emergence.
- Supplied a concentrated computing community through the Digital Computer Lab.
- Served as the origin of startup and engineering relationships that reportedly persisted for decades.

## Qualifications
This profile reflects one alumnus's celebratory commencement narrative, not a general institutional history or a measured account of Illinois's educational outcomes. It does not isolate the university's contribution from Levchin's prior preparation, scholarship access, timing, immigration experience, individual peers, the wider web boom, or later Silicon Valley capital and opportunity.
