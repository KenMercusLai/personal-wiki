## Overview
The [[InternalRevenueService]] is the United States federal tax agency, represented here as the administrator and government counterparty of the historical [[IRSFreeFile]] industry partnership.

## Current Profile
In the 2019 ProPublica account, the IRS did not provide its own online tax-preparation product. It instead linked eligible taxpayers to participating private companies whose offers carried distinct restrictions. This arrangement created an oversight obligation across eligibility promises, provider discovery, and user routing, yet the National Taxpayer Advocate and an IRS advisory body reportedly found that Free File was failing its objectives and that use had declined by millions from its 2005 peak.

## Key Characteristics
- Administers federal taxation and, in the source period, a directory for private Free File offers.
- Negotiates a public-private arrangement that links free access to provider participation.
- Leaves provider-specific age, income, geography, and return rules inside the shared program.
- Faces an institutional conflict between relying on industry delivery and potentially offering a direct public service.
- Was criticized in the source for inadequate program oversight and declining participation.

This profile covers one historical program controversy, not the IRS’s full responsibilities or current filing services. The source reports competing legal interpretations of proposed legislation and does not independently resolve them. Program design, thresholds, provider participation, and law may have changed after publication.
