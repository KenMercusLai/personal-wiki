## Overview
[[MiaoYi|缪伊]] is a Chinese-language blogger whose saved essay examines social-media abstinence, experience-led posting, algorithmic incentives, online hatred, and trolling.

## Current Profile
In the available source, 缪伊 writes from first-person observation rather than claiming a platform audit or formal psychological study. The essay joins an empathetic account of why people narrate private experience with a sharp critique of the point where visibility, algorithmic reward, and commercial opportunity turn life into content inventory. Its practical stance is selective disengagement: understand the human need beneath expression, but refuse to feed deliberately provoked conflict.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes reflective cultural criticism grounded in personal online experience.
- Treats social posting as capable of both narrative integration and exploitative performance.
- Connects recommendation incentives to anger, self-disclosure, and creator self-exploitation.
- Advocates withholding attention from trolls while preserving formal escalation for serious harm.

## Evidence
- Reflective method: [[himikouchuu-31]] begins from the author's attempt to stop using Douban, Weibo, and Xiaohongshu and explicitly presents the argument as personal observation rather than proof.
- Dual account of expression: [[himikouchuu-31]] describes experience-sharing as connection and self-integration before tracing how it can become audience-facing material production.
- Incentive critique: [[himikouchuu-31]] argues that algorithms reward strong emotion, exposure, and conflict because each produces traffic.
- Practical norm: [[himikouchuu-31]] recommends not replying to or retransmitting provocation and using platform, official, or legal channels when warranted.
