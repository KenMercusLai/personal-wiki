## Overview
[[DannyGlasser]] is represented as the Microsoft engineer who conceived and coded the original [[TypingIndicator]] mechanism for [[MSNMessenger]] and later reflected on the difference between patent credit and useful product contribution.

## Current Profile
Glasser's account centers his work on detecting and communicating typing activity rather than on the final visible interface. He built the mechanism and a rough proof of concept, helped validate it through internal self-hosting, and credits David Auerbach and other collaborators with the polished interface that shipped. His broader view of invention is contribution-based: he takes pride in useful work he substantially shaped, not in prestigious names or patents produced through minimal involvement.

## Key Characteristics
- Conceived and coded the initial typing-activity detection and communication mechanism.
- Used a rough UI to make the mechanism testable before shipping design work.
- Values practical utility and personal contribution more than patent status or reflected prestige.
- Explicitly credits collaborators whose interface work fell outside the patent's claims.
- Treats legal coverage and later infringement as areas of uncertainty rather than making definitive claims.

- Prototype practice: [[danny-glasser-is-typing-danny-glasser]] describes a rough UI used as proof of concept and internal self-hosting before polish.

## Qualifications
The profile is grounded in Glasser's own retrospective and does not independently reconstruct the full MSN Messenger team, patent prosecution, or later patent-family status. It covers one invention and a brief comparison with other patents rather than his complete Microsoft career.
