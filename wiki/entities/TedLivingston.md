## Overview
[[TedLivingston]] appears as the author of the 2016 "Chat is the New Browser" argument and as a [[Kik]] leader advocating for chatbot platforms.

## Current Profile
The source presents Livingston as an operator-advocate for the first chatbot platform wave. He argues that chatbots should be judged like early websites: initially crude, but positioned to improve because they remove major access barriers around app discovery, downloads, account creation, and learning curves. His argument uses Kik's Bot Shop and bot features as evidence that messaging could become a development, discovery, sharing, and payment platform.

## Key Characteristics
- Advocates the thesis that chat can become the new browser and bots the new websites.
- Frames friction reduction as the central reason chatbots could compete with mobile apps and mobile web.
- Uses Kik's platform features and million-user bots as evidence of early ecosystem momentum.
- Treats payments as the economic layer needed for chatbot developers to build richer experiences.

## Evidence
- Chat-as-browser thesis: [[chat-is-the-new-browser-ted-livingston-medium]] says Livingston had been arguing that chat is the new browser and bots are the new websites.
- Friction argument: [[chat-is-the-new-browser-ted-livingston-medium]] contrasts chatbot starts through scans, links, or usernames with app-store search, downloads, account creation, and learning.
- Kik platform evidence: [[chat-is-the-new-browser-ted-livingston-medium]] discusses Kik's Bot Shop, Kik Codes, web bubbles, invites, mentions, and several million-user bots.
- Payments forecast: [[chat-is-the-new-browser-ted-livingston-medium]] says payments would incentivize better bot tooling and richer bot features.

## Qualifications
The source is Livingston's own optimistic 2016 argument and should not be treated as neutral proof that chatbot platforms later achieved an App Store moment.
