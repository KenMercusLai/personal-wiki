## Overview
[[TedLivingston]] appears as a [[Kik]] leader and author who advocated for messaging bots while revising the case away from humanlike conversation.

## Current Profile
The two 2016 sources present Livingston as an operator-advocate whose thesis changed with early platform experience. In August, four months after Kik's Bot Shop launch, he says conversational commerce was overemphasized because NLP could not sustain humanlike dialogue; the durable bot advantages were lower entry friction, discovery and sharing, consolidated service access, social context, and instant structured interactions. In December, he broadens that position into the "chat is the new browser" analogy, arguing that crude early bots could improve as tooling, payments, and distribution matured.

## Key Characteristics
- Advocates messaging as a service platform while rejecting humanlike conversation as its necessary interface.
- Frames friction reduction, discovery, sharing, consolidation, and social context as bots' central advantages.
- Uses Kik's Bot Shop, Kik Codes, Mentions, Invites, bot counts, and message volume as early ecosystem evidence.
- Treats payments as a missing economic layer needed for richer bot ecosystems.

## Evidence
- Chat-as-browser thesis: [[chat-is-the-new-browser-ted-livingston-medium]] says Livingston had been arguing that chat is the new browser and bots are the new websites.
- Friction argument: [[chat-is-the-new-browser-ted-livingston-medium]] contrasts chatbot starts through scans, links, or usernames with app-store search, downloads, account creation, and learning.
- Kik platform evidence: [[chat-is-the-new-browser-ted-livingston-medium]] discusses Kik's Bot Shop, Kik Codes, web bubbles, invites, mentions, and several million-user bots.
- Payments forecast: [[chat-is-the-new-browser-ted-livingston-medium]] says payments would incentivize better bot tooling and richer bot features.
- Conversational correction: [[ted-livingston-bots-are-better-without-conversation]] says 2016 NLP was not ready for humanlike exchanges and reframes WeChat around instant interactions.
- Early operating snapshot: [[ted-livingston-bots-are-better-without-conversation]] reports more than 20,000 Kik bots and almost two billion bot messages while acknowledging that no killer bot had emerged.

## Qualifications
Both sources are Livingston's own optimistic 2016 platform arguments and use Kik's reported figures without independent verification. His rejection of conversation and app replacement narrows the thesis, but neither article proves that the ecosystem later achieved an App Store-like breakthrough.

## What Changed
- Added Livingston's earlier correction from conversational commerce to structured instant interactions.
- Distinguished his stable messaging-platform thesis from changing claims about the interaction model.
