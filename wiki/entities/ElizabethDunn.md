## Overview
[[ElizabethDunn]] is a University of British Columbia psychology professor quoted by NPR as an author of research on whether spending money to buy time improves happiness.

## Current Profile
Within this wiki, Dunn appears as the main research voice behind the claim that people can increase well-being by outsourcing disliked, time-consuming tasks. She explains both the mechanism the researchers expected, buying oneself out of chores people do not like, and the social friction that can make such spending feel guilty even when it improves life satisfaction.

## Key Characteristics
- University of British Columbia psychology professor.
- Author of the study discussed in NPR's article on buying time.
- Frames time-saving services as a possible route to greater positive emotion and life satisfaction.
- Notes guilt as one reason people resist paying for tasks they could technically do themselves.
