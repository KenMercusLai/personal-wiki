## Overview
[[PokemonGo]] is a mobile augmented-reality game used in the source as an example of free download, familiar IP, and public participation driving adoption.

## Current Profile
The growth source says Pokemon Go launched with little conventional advertising and relied on public familiarity with Pokemon plus curiosity about augmented reality. Free download helped build a massive user base that could later support premium additions and related hardware. The usage-moment essay adds a complementary routine explanation: moving around was a recurring state without one dominant mobile incumbent, so leaving home could become a situational cue to open the game. This is a 2016 interpretation, not evidence that movement-state fit caused adoption or durable retention.

## Key Characteristics
- Mobile augmented-reality game built on familiar Pokemon IP.
- Launched with minimal explanation and advertising according to the source.
- Used free download to maximize adoption.
- Reported as reaching 100 million downloads by August 2016.
- Used physical movement and location as part of the product's recurring use context.
- Presented as occupying a relatively open “moving around” state without depending primarily on notification prompts.

## Evidence
- Familiar IP and launch restraint: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says Niantic tweeted availability without conventional advertising.
- Free download and add-ons: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] links free adoption to later premium additions and wearable-device demand.
- Movement-state fit: [[question-exactly-when-is-someone-going-to-use-your-app-service]] argues that leaving home and moving around became a situational trigger to open Pokemon Go.
- Prompt boundary: [[question-exactly-when-is-someone-going-to-use-your-app-service]] contrasts the game's reported adoption with conventional notification-driven growth while also acknowledging the large franchise advantage.

## Qualifications
The two sources provide retrospective practitioner examples rather than acquisition or retention analysis. They do not isolate movement-state fit from app-store featuring, Nintendo and Pokémon brand strength, novelty, press, social participation, Niantic's location-data history, network effects, free entry, or distribution. The claim that moving around had no dominant incumbent is rhetorical and does not measure competing games, maps, messaging, audio, or other mobile uses.

## What Changed
- Added physical movement as the game's proposed recurring use state and situational trigger.
- Qualified state fit against franchise, novelty, distribution, social, and free-entry explanations.
