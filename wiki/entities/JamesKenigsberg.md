## Overview
[[JamesKenigsberg]] is presented in the source as chief technology officer and a founding team member of [[2U]], and as an advocate of senior leaders learning from junior staff.

## Current Profile
Kenigsberg describes leadership as a continuing learning role rather than the endpoint of education. His practical model is to maintain informal contact with passionate junior contributors across different work areas, learn how day-to-day tasks are actually performed, and apply effective local ideas at departmental scale.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats junior and frontline knowledge as a resource for senior-leader learning.
- Favors brief, informal mentoring conversations over mandatory program structure.
- Uses cross-level relationships to refresh skills and observe team operations.
- Emphasizes reciprocity by giving junior contributors visible evidence that their ideas matter.

## Evidence
- Leadership view: [[why-you-should-look-to-your-junior-staff-as-mentors]] argues that senior employees must keep learning even when they have fewer senior colleagues to teach them.
- Informal practice: [[why-you-should-look-to-your-junior-staff-as-mentors]] describes ten-to-fifteen-minute conversations and contact with contributors in several areas.
- Operational learning: [[why-you-should-look-to-your-junior-staff-as-mentors]] says these relationships reveal tasks, methods, and team dynamics.
- Reciprocity: [[why-you-should-look-to-your-junior-staff-as-mentors]] argues that applying a junior employee's idea at wider scale validates that employee's contribution.
