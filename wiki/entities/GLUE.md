## Overview
[[GLUE]]—a Global Language for a Unified Experience—is the [[Spotify]] design language system and dedicated team described in [[StanleyWood]]'s 2016 account of scaling design coherence.

## Current Profile
Spotify created GLUE in 2014 after a visual realignment exposed the difficulty of maintaining consistency. A company-accessible site documented styles, components, and patterns, while matching UI toolkits helped designers begin projects from shared assets and reveal missing components.

In 2015 GLUE became a centralized team of designers and engineers. The team supported distributed designers, facilitated cross-team evolution of the language, and implemented core building blocks across iOS, Android, and desktop. A representative weekly Design Guild supplied local context and conflict resolution so central ownership did not rely only on static guidance.

## Key Characteristics
- Documents shared interface styles, components, and patterns.
- Gives designers and developers common names that connect design specifications with code.
- Pairs written guidance with design-tool kits and platform implementations.
- Uses a dedicated designer-engineer team to maintain and evolve the system.
- Relies on cross-mission guild participation to counter central-team context loss.

## Evidence
- Shared definition: [[design-doesnt-scale-stanley-wood-medium]] says GLUE documented styles, components, and patterns on an internal website.
- Tool and code alignment: [[design-doesnt-scale-stanley-wood-medium]] describes matching UI toolkits and engineering implementations for iOS, Android, and desktop.
- Governance: [[design-doesnt-scale-stanley-wood-medium]] says the dedicated team met weekly with representatives from product missions to exchange consequential context and resolve conflicts.

## Qualifications
The evidence is one first-person 2016 practitioner account. It does not quantify adoption, component coverage, delivery speed, defect reduction, accessibility, or customer outcomes, and it does not show that centralized ownership plus a guild will fit every organization. The article explicitly recognizes that guidelines, toolkits, and central teams require continuing maintenance and can lose relevance.
