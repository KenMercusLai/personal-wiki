## Overview
[[JakeKnapp]] is a product-design practitioner represented in this wiki as the writer through whom [[JonathanCourtney]] encountered [[GV]]'s Design Sprint process.

## Current Profile
The sources credit Knapp with the Design Sprint process that AJ&Smart adopted and later adapted for a cross-continental Oak redesign. His role in this evidence is method attribution and transmission; neither article provides a wider biography or evaluates his complete body of work.

## Key Characteristics
- Associated in the source with the GV Design Sprint process.
- Communicated a rapid path from assumptions to a tangible test.
- Influenced AJ&Smart's move away from long research-first engagements.
- Supplied the process framework that AJ&Smart adapted for remote collaboration.

## Evidence
- Process attribution: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] says Knapp began writing about GV's Design Sprint process.
- Method description: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] characterizes that process as a four-to-five-day route to high-fidelity prototype testing.
- Practitioner influence: [[user-research-is-overrated-muzli-design-inspiration]] says the idea resonated with Courtney and was then tried with a new client.
- Remote adaptation: [[from-idea-to-app-store-a-design-sprint-case-study-ux-planet]] explicitly identifies Knapp's Design Sprint as the main process used for the Oak redesign.

## Qualifications
This profile reflects Courtney's brief attributions rather than a primary text by Knapp. It does not establish authorship details, Knapp's view of the remote variant, or whether the four-week Oak engagement conforms to his canonical process.

## What Changed
- Added the Oak project as evidence that AJ&Smart used Knapp's framework as the base for a remote adaptation.
