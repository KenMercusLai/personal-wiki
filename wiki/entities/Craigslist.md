## Overview
[[Craigslist]] is represented as a broad classifieds incumbent whose category map supports two related startup readings: narrow sections can be entry wedges, but the durable marketplace may need to recombine adjacent sections into a broader category platform.

## Current Profile
Yin uses Craigslist as an example of a large product containing many separable jobs and possible focused first products. Breinlinger revisits the same map five to seven years later and argues that many micro-vertical marketplaces failed, were dying, or pivoted because their transactions were too infrequent or too small to sustain large networks and businesses. His proposed endpoint is not one universal replacement for Craigslist, but broad category platforms spanning community, commerce, services, housing, or jobs, with Airbnb as the exception where one large section can support a major standalone marketplace.

## Key Characteristics
- Broad classifieds incumbent spanning community, housing, jobs, personals, goods, services, gigs, and resumes.
- Category map that reveals focused startup entry wedges.
- Retrospective case showing that a viable wedge does not guarantee a durable narrow marketplace.
- Reference point for consolidation into broader category platforms.

## Qualifications
Neither source evaluates Craigslist's own current business, user behavior, monetization, or market power. The rebundling essay is an investor thesis with undisclosed classification criteria and no marketplace performance dataset; it qualifies rather than disproves the usefulness of narrow entry wedges.

## What Changed
- Reconciled Craigslist's role as a map of narrow entry wedges with later evidence that remaining narrowly vertical may not sustain marketplace economics.
- Added category-level rebundling as a possible outcome between one universal incumbent and dozens of micro-verticals.
