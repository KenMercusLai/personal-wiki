## Overview
[[Craigslist]] is represented as a broad classifieds incumbent whose category map supports two related startup readings: narrow sections can be entry wedges, but the durable marketplace may need to recombine adjacent sections into a broader category platform.

## Current Profile
Yin uses Craigslist as an example of a large product containing many separable jobs and possible focused first products. Breinlinger revisits the same map five to seven years later and argues that many micro-vertical marketplaces failed, were dying, or pivoted because their transactions were too infrequent or too small to sustain large networks and businesses. His proposed endpoint is not one universal replacement for Craigslist, but broad category platforms spanning community, commerce, services, housing, or jobs, with Airbnb as the exception where one large section can support a major standalone marketplace. Chen adds a distribution-platform reading: Craigslist's large audience and relatively unautomated posting flow made it valuable enough for Airbnb to reverse-engineer, even without a public API.

## Key Characteristics
- Broad classifieds incumbent spanning community, housing, jobs, personals, goods, services, gigs, and resumes.
- Category map that reveals focused startup entry wedges.
- Retrospective case showing that a viable wedge does not guarantee a durable narrow marketplace.
- Reference point for consolidation into broader category platforms.
- Borrowed distribution surface whose undocumented posting flow created both reach and integration risk for Airbnb.

## Evidence
Category-level entry wedges:
- [[build-a-product-that-fits-your-runway-elizabeth-yin]] says founders can list every feature or job a bloated incumbent product performs and often find standalone product ideas.

Visual decomposition and retrospective:

- [[a-crowded-space-the-rebundling-of-craigslist]] compares that unbundling map with a version marking many entrants as dead, dying, or pivoted.

Category-level consolidation:
- [[a-crowded-space-the-rebundling-of-craigslist]] argues that limited user attention, transaction frequency, and ticket value favor broad category platforms over dozens of micro-vertical apps.

Distribution surface:
- [[growth-hacker-is-the-new-vp-marketing-at-andrewchen]] describes Airbnb scraping Craigslist forms and regional codes, prefilling listings through unique posting-state URLs, and adapting contact details and HTML to the platform's constraints.

## Qualifications
None of the sources evaluates Craigslist's own current business, user behavior, monetization, or market power. The rebundling essay is an investor thesis with undisclosed classification criteria and no marketplace performance dataset; it qualifies rather than disproves the usefulness of narrow entry wedges. Chen's case gives no Craigslist policy response, channel outcome, maintenance history, or independent confirmation of the reverse-engineered flow.

## What Changed
- Added Craigslist as a high-volume but undocumented distribution surface for Airbnb's early listing integration.
- Reconciled Craigslist's role as a map of narrow entry wedges with later evidence that remaining narrowly vertical may not sustain marketplace economics.
- Added category-level rebundling as a possible outcome between one universal incumbent and dozens of micro-verticals.
