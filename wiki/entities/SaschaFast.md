## Overview
[[SaschaFast]] is a Zettelkasten.de writer represented here by a 2015 practitioner essay on making long-lived notes understandable to one's future self.

## Current Profile
Fast presents note writing as the act of educating a long-term thinking companion. After struggling to interpret his own older Zettel, he adopted a discipline of simple prose, content-specific structure, descriptive titles, and opening summaries. His concern is not stylistic minimalism for its own sake, but lowering the future cost of understanding and retrieving material whose original context has disappeared.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes from retrospective experience repairing unclear older notes.
- Treats a Zettelkasten as a long-lived thinking companion shaped by the quality of material entered into it.
- Recommends short sentences, paragraphs, and chunked prose for later readability.
- Uses different explicit templates for entities, systems, debates, and arguments.
- Front-loads orientation through claim-bearing titles and short opening descriptions.

## Evidence
- Motivation: [[how-to-write-a-note-that-you-will-actually-understand]] recounts the effort required to decode Zettel written several years earlier.
- Writing discipline: [[how-to-write-a-note-that-you-will-actually-understand]] recommends simple prose and explicit form before a note enters the archive.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one 2015 practitioner essay and is not a general biography. Its advice is grounded in Fast's experience rather than measured comparisons, and its lifetime-retention standard may be unnecessary for disposable capture or short-lived project notes.
