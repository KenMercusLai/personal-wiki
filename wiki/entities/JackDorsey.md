## Overview
[[JackDorsey]] appears as Twitter's leader and the addressed recipient of [[AnilDash]]'s public advice, and as a Square executive observed by [[SaumilMehta]].

## Current Profile
The Twitter source frames Dorsey as the person who asked users for ideas about fixing the platform. Dash responds by calling for visible shipping cadence, explicit abuse response, better metrics, differentiated tools for user groups, and a clear decision about developers. Mehta's Square account adds a narrow leadership observation: Dorsey remained engaged with difficult employee questions and screen-level product review and encouraged candid presentations rather than celebrity distance inside the company.

## Key Characteristics
- Publicly solicited ideas for improving Twitter.
- Addressed as the executive accountable for Twitter's product and platform direction.
- Associated in the source with the need to make Twitter's internal efforts legible to outsiders.
- Described at Square as directly engaged with product details, difficult questions, and candid internal communication.

## Evidence
- Public prompt: [[a-billion-dollar-gift-for-twitter-startup-grind-medium]] closes by thanking Dorsey for asking and listening.
- Executive accountability: [[a-billion-dollar-gift-for-twitter-startup-grind-medium]] directs its five-part plan to Dorsey as the person who could guide Twitter's response.
- Legibility problem: [[a-billion-dollar-gift-for-twitter-startup-grind-medium]] says outsiders cannot tell whether Twitter has thought of or acted on many obvious improvements.
- Internal candor: [[confessions-of-a-startup-mole-in-a-dumb-big-company]] says Dorsey answered tough questions without sugarcoating and pushed for more candor in presentations.
- Product engagement: [[confessions-of-a-startup-mole-in-a-dumb-big-company]] describes Dorsey reviewing a product experience screen by screen and offering useful feedback.

## Qualifications
The page captures two contemporaneous practitioner perspectives rather than a complete leadership history. Dash's advice is an external 2016-2017 Twitter critique, while Mehta's description is one employee's favorable observation at Square and does not measure how access or candor affected decisions or outcomes.
