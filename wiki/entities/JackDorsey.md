## Overview
[[JackDorsey]] appears as an executive accountable for [[Twitter]]'s product and platform direction, as an engaged [[Square]] product leader in one employee account, and as an absentee, overly delegated Twitter chief executive in a later retrospective.

## Current Profile
Dash's Twitter critique frames Dorsey as the leader who publicly asked for ideas and therefore owned the need for visible shipping, explicit abuse response, better measures, differentiated user tools, and a clear developer posture. Mehta's Square account is favorable and concrete: Dorsey reportedly answered difficult employee questions, encouraged candid presentations, and reviewed product work screen by screen.

The Twitter takeover investigation supplies a conflicting leadership portrait from Dorsey's earlier tenure. It characterizes him as an absentee leader who simultaneously ran Block, delegated strategy and daily operations to trusted deputies, and left employees frustrated by slow decisions and unrealized potential. That contrast may reflect different companies, periods, responsibilities, and observers, but it weakens any simple claim that Dorsey's hands-on Square behavior generalized to his management of Twitter.

## Key Characteristics
- Publicly solicited ideas for improving Twitter and was addressed as accountable for its product and platform direction.
- Was associated with a need to make Twitter's product, safety, metric, user-tool, and developer choices legible externally.
- Was described at Square as engaged with product details, difficult questions, and candid internal communication.
- Was described by the later Twitter investigation as distant from daily operations and overly reliant on deputies.
- Presents a source-level contrast between direct product engagement and delegated, slow-moving corporate leadership.

## Evidence
- Public accountability: [[a-billion-dollar-gift-for-twitter-startup-grind-medium]] responds to Dorsey's request for ideas with a five-part Twitter turnaround agenda.
- Square engagement: [[confessions-of-a-startup-mole-in-a-dumb-big-company]] describes Dorsey answering hard questions, inviting candor, and reviewing an experience screen by screen.
- Twitter distance: [[extremely-hardcore]] characterizes Dorsey as an absentee leader who divided attention with Block and left strategy and operations to trusted deputies.
- Organizational consequence: [[extremely-hardcore]] reports that some Twitter employees initially welcomed Musk because they associated the prior regime with slow shipping and wasted potential.

## Qualifications
The evidence consists of practitioner and journalistic accounts rather than a complete comparative leadership record. Mehta's favorable observations concern Square, while the negative account concerns Twitter and is partly retrospective framing for a takeover story; differences in company, period, role, and source access may explain some tension. Neither account measures how Dorsey's accessibility, delegation, or divided attention affected product or business outcomes, and the takeover's later failures do not validate every criticism of the preceding regime.

## What Changed
- Added the takeover investigation's contrasting account of absentee, highly delegated leadership at Twitter.
- Preserved the company and source boundary between hands-on Square observations and criticism of Twitter's slow prior regime.
