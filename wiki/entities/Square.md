## Overview
[[Square]] appears as an employee-equity valuation-risk example, a highly visible hardware growth example, and [[SaumilMehta]]'s case of a large technology company that combined accessible leadership with scale-related operating constraints.

## Current Profile
The equity source uses Square to argue that late-stage startup compensation can disappoint when grants are tied to valuations that later reset or prove less favorable than expected. The virality source adds a product-growth lens: a Square terminal in a merchant's store exposes other merchants to a point-of-sale experience in a real purchasing context. Mehta's 2018 retrospective adds an internal operating view after more than two years at the company. He reports direct interaction between senior leaders and contributors, but also describes hiring, strategy, installed-customer, autonomy, dependency, specialization, and abundance constraints that prevent raw headcount from translating into startup speed.

## Key Characteristics
- Serves as a late-stage compensation-risk example.
- Shows that valuation risk is not limited to very early startups.
- Broadens the equity critique from options to RSU-like compensation.
- Uses visible point-of-sale hardware as an offline awareness surface.
- Serves as a first-person case of candid executive access coexisting with slower, more specialized large-company execution.

## Evidence
- Valuation risk: [[4-hard-truths-about-equity-while-west]] refers to people awarded RSUs at Square's pre-IPO valuation.
- Compensation scope: [[4-hard-truths-about-equity-while-west]] includes Square alongside examples of shares that did not reward employees as hoped.
- Employee-risk framing: [[4-hard-truths-about-equity-while-west]] uses Square to support skepticism toward treating equity grants as reliable wealth.
- Visible hardware: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] says Square terminals in bakeries or other stores expose potential merchant customers to a new POS solution.
- Organic signup claim: [[9-ways-to-build-virality-into-your-product-gabor-cselle-medium]] says Square was growing more than 50% year over year and that 80% of signups came at no acquisition cost in the source's period.
- Executive access: [[confessions-of-a-startup-mole-in-a-dumb-big-company]] describes senior leaders reading contributor-produced work, probing details, accepting candid feedback, and engaging directly with product experience.
- Scale constraints: [[confessions-of-a-startup-mole-in-a-dumb-big-company]] says engineering scarcity, hiring, strategy, current customers, reduced autonomy, and peer dependencies kept a well-staffed team from matching a ten-person startup's speed.
- Specialization and abundance: [[confessions-of-a-startup-mole-in-a-dumb-big-company]] presents specialist support and employee amenities as real benefits that can also create dependency and small amounts of compounded optional work.

## Qualifications
The sources do not quantify Square's RSU terms, IPO pricing, lockups, employee-level outcomes, detailed attribution for hardware-driven signups, or comparative organizational speed and waste. Mehta's account reflects one employee's observations during roughly 2015-2018 and explicitly does not claim objective or comprehensive truth.
