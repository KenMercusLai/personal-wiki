## Overview
[[StacyMitchell]] is identified in the source as co-director of the Institute for Local Self-Reliance and an advocate for local economies.

## Current Profile
Mitchell interprets [[Grubhub]]'s restaurant-linked domains and ordering surfaces as a gatekeeping pattern in which a platform inserts itself into an existing customer-business relationship and captures a share of revenue. She compares the mechanism with ride-hailing platforms and argues that policymakers have been slow to respond to corporate control over web access.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames platform power through control of access between local businesses and their customers.
- Evaluates platform value relative to the revenue share captured, not only whether the platform produces transactions.
- Treats ownership of branded discovery surfaces as a threat to a small business's ability to compete for direct demand.
- Advocates policy intervention when platform concentration weakens the open-web ideal.

- Direct-relationship concern: [[grubhub-is-buying-up-thousands-of-restaurant-web-addresses-that-means-mom-and-pop-cant-own-their-slice-of-the-internet-new-food-economy]] quotes her describing domain acquisition and search positioning as predatory when customers intend to reach a restaurant directly.
- Policy stance: [[grubhub-is-buying-up-thousands-of-restaurant-web-addresses-that-means-mom-and-pop-cant-own-their-slice-of-the-internet-new-food-economy]] reports her call for regulation of monopoly-minded platform control over web access.

## Qualifications
The source provides Mitchell's advocacy interpretation rather than a legal finding, market definition, or measured comparison of platform costs and benefits. It does not establish how representative the cited restaurants are or whether all domain registrations had the same contractual and competitive context.
