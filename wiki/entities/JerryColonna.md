## Overview
[[JerryColonna]] is represented as a former operator who co-founded [[FlatironPartners]] with [[FredWilson]] and had to learn the boundary between helping a portfolio company and taking over management's work.

## Current Profile
Wilson uses Colonna's experience on an early Flatiron board to illustrate a transition cost for operator-turned investors. When the company struggled, Colonna repeatedly moved toward direct problem-solving until an experienced investor reminded him that management had to run the company. The episode frames restraint as part of useful governance, not as withdrawal: an investor can advise and support operators without displacing their authority or accountability.

## Key Characteristics
- Had operating experience before becoming an investor.
- Co-founded Flatiron Partners with Wilson.
- Learned through an early board role that investor support differs from company operation.
- Illustrates both the practical value and the role-confusion risk of operator experience in venture capital.

## Evidence
- Career and firm context: [[investor-vcs-and-operator-vcs-avc]] identifies Colonna as a former operator who left to form Flatiron Partners with Wilson.
- Board-role lesson: [[investor-vcs-and-operator-vcs-avc]] recounts an experienced VC telling Colonna to let management run the company after he repeatedly jumped into operating problems.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one anecdote retold by Wilson. It does not identify the company or experienced investor, provide Colonna's full career chronology, compare accounts from other participants, or establish how he changed his later board practice. The episode illustrates a plausible role conflict rather than proving that former operators generally overreach.
