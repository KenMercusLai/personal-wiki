## Overview
[[CelestineOmin]] is the author of “No, you don't need ML/AI. You need SQL,” represented here through a first-person account of database-driven e-commerce operations.

## Current Profile
Omin describes having spent his career in technology and a substantial period in e-commerce, including work associated with [[Konga]]. In this source he advocates using explicit SQL queries and lightweight scheduling to solve legible business problems before investing in scarce ML/AI expertise. The wiki has no independent biographical or employment evidence beyond the article.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames technology choice around the simplest mechanism that can produce a useful business action.
- Uses concrete customer-retention, marketing, fraud, payment, and service examples from e-commerce operations.
- Treats customer recognition and relevant communication as alternatives or complements to paid acquisition.
- Accepts a role for ML/AI while arguing that small stores often have simpler unmet analytical needs.

## Evidence
SQL-first operating position:
- [[no-you-dont-need-ml-ai-you-need-sql]] argues that stores with roughly 1,000-10,000 customers can address many routine cases through SQL rather than trained models.

E-commerce practice:
- [[no-you-dont-need-ml-ai-you-need-sql]] recounts queries for weekly rewards, inactive-customer outreach, product-informed newsletters, abandoned carts, payment-on-delivery controls, overdue orders, and failed-card attempts.

Business outcome orientation:
- [[no-you-dont-need-ml-ai-you-need-sql]] reports repeat purchase, conversion, email-open, social-response, and NPS effects, although it does not provide the underlying measurements.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one self-authored article. The reported outcomes, employer practices, and causal interpretations are not independently verified, and the article does not establish Omin's current role or whether the described processes remain in use. Its compact examples also omit production engineering, privacy, fairness, and campaign-measurement requirements.
