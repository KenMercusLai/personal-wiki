## Overview
[[AndrewMason]] is represented as the founder who used his experience of concentrated employee outcomes at [[Groupon]] to propose [[ProgressiveEquity]] for [[Detour]].

## Current Profile
Mason argues that equity policy should be designed before extraordinary success makes redistribution politically, legally, and financially difficult. He reports considering a large new employee grant at Groupon, expecting resistance from the board, and attempting to transfer some of his own equity to high-impact employees before adverse tax consequences made that approach impractical. At Detour he converted that retrospective concern into a precommitted RSU structure, published the legal template, and invited others to improve it.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats extreme startup-equity concentration as a design problem that should be addressed before an exit.
- Connects the proposal to founder experience at Groupon rather than presenting it as abstract compensation theory.
- Uses a threshold-and-redistribution mechanism to distinguish financial independence from dynastic wealth.
- Publishes implementation paperwork for reuse and modification.
- Acknowledges that the program is only one step within a larger, unresolved equity-distribution problem.

## Evidence
- Motivation: [[introducing-progressive-equity-detour-blog-medium]] says Groupon produced sharply unequal employee outcomes and that later redistribution through a new grant or personal transfers was impractical.
- Program design: [[introducing-progressive-equity-detour-blog-medium]] attributes the Progressive Equity threshold, capped RSU pool, kicker pool, and one-time trigger to Mason and Detour.
- Openness: [[introducing-progressive-equity-detour-blog-medium]] links reusable legal paperwork and asks others to adopt and improve the program.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one short first-person 2015 post. It does not independently verify Groupon's employee outcome distribution, the attempted transfers, Detour's final legal terms, employee support, external adoption, or a completed payout. The post's fictional arithmetic and moralizing treatment of objections should not be mistaken for a representative employee-preference study or legal and financial analysis.
