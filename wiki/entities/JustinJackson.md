## Overview
[[JustinJackson]] is a creator and entrepreneur represented in the wiki by essays about directing founder attention toward customers, craft, product improvement, and demonstrated demand.

## Current Profile
Jackson’s comparison essay uses his own morning habit of checking product launches and peer successes to show how external monitoring can become jealousy and stress. His practical response is to move attention back to understanding and helping customers while improving craft and product. The demand essay extends that customer orientation: identifying a need or beneficial outcome does not establish willingness to adopt, so builders should connect long-term value to something people want now and give new users a quick, meaningful success.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses a first-person creator and startup experience to make an attention problem concrete.
- Treats competitor and peer monitoring as harmful when it produces rumination rather than better decisions.
- Prioritizes customer understanding, relationship, and trust.
- Pairs outward service with inward improvement of craft and product.
- Distinguishes a beneficial need from demonstrated customer demand.
- Connects product positioning and onboarding to immediate reward and continuing value.

## Evidence
Comparison and attention:
- [[justin-jackson-focus-on-your-own-shit]] describes checking Product Hunt, Twitter, and Medium immediately after waking and entering a jealousy-and-stress loop.

Customer and craft orientation:
- [[justin-jackson-focus-on-your-own-shit]] reduces the actionable response to closer attention to users and sustained improvement of skill and product.

Demand and activation:
- [[people-need-my-product-but-do-they-want-it]] argues that health, environmental, and relationship needs do not automatically produce purchase or signup behavior.
- [[people-need-my-product-but-do-they-want-it]] uses weight loss, Tesla, Dating Ring, and software-onboarding examples to connect immediate desirability with longer-term value.

## Qualifications
The profile is based on two short practitioner essays and should not be treated as a comprehensive biography. The comparison essay does not distinguish unstructured monitoring from bounded competitive research, and its physiological claims are not clinically sourced. The demand essay relies on selected company and marketing examples without controlled comparisons, activation or retention data, or evidence for its broad emotional-purchase claim.
