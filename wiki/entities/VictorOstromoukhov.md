## Overview
[[VictorOstromoukhov]] is a computer-graphics researcher represented here through a 2000 MIT-affiliated survey of his earlier EPFL work on artistic screening, multicolor dithering, and digital facial engraving.

## Current Profile
Ostromoukhov's program treats printing algorithms as expressive media without discarding their reproduction role. Across three related systems, he replaces passive artifacts with authored structure: contours turn halftone dots into recognizable forms, cumulative thresholds coordinate nonstandard inks, and warped line fields turn photographic tone into engraving-like strokes.

His recurring technical move is to expose and design the threshold structure beneath dithering. The source also presents a hybrid workflow: artists author contours, mappings, patches, masks, and styles, while algorithms interpolate, rasterize, merge, equilibrate, and render them.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames halftoning artifacts as a possible artistic and informational layer.
- Uses designed threshold structures as a common abstraction across monochrome, color, and engraving methods.
- Combines artist-authored geometry with deterministic raster and print-model operations.
- Targets both expressive graphics and practical printing applications such as posters and valuable documents.
- Preserves print physics and visual perception as constraints on aesthetic construction.

## Evidence

- Application range: [[spie2000-techart]] demonstrates grayscale motifs, microletter security screens, special-ink color fields, sculpture portraits, mezzotint, and enhanced real faces.
