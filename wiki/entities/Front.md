## Overview
[[Front]] is a collaborative email SaaS company used as both a retention-led growth example and the company context for [[MathildeCollin]]'s founder-operator advice.

## Current Profile
Product Habits presents Front as a sticky product in a competitive space. Rather than maximizing logos alone, Front focused on retained and expanding accounts: users added teammates, used collaborative email features, sent more email, upgraded plans, and produced enough expansion revenue for net negative churn. Collin's advice essay adds the earlier founder-operating view: Front began as a collaborative email client, used customer conversations and growth evidence to choose direction, and later had to preserve founder context as executives joined.

## Key Characteristics
- Built around collaborative email workflows.
- Encourages team-wide usage through assignment, sharing, transparency, and collaboration tools.
- Treats onboarding as the beginning of account expansion rather than a separate acquisition step.
- Used retention and upsell to grow existing accounts.
- Reported net negative churn in the source's case narrative.
- Used customer and growth evidence to refine product direction.
- Required founder-context transmission as the company added executives.

## Evidence
- Product stickiness: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] says 50% of Front's monthly active users were also daily active users.
- Collaboration features: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] highlights assigning and sharing emails, team transparency, and built-in collaborative tools.
- Account expansion: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] says Front grew existing accounts by 50% on average.
- Net negative churn: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] says Front could grow month to month without acquisitions because retention and expansion were high.
- Onboarding link: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] says Front encouraged adding team members, using more features, and sending more emails from day one.
- Product origin: [[9-most-useful-pieces-of-advice-ive-received-mathilde-collin-medium]] says Front started as a collaborative email client.
- Customer evidence: [[9-most-useful-pieces-of-advice-ive-received-mathilde-collin-medium]] says Front's path depended on talking to users, refining the product, and following growth.
- Executive onboarding: [[9-most-useful-pieces-of-advice-ive-received-mathilde-collin-medium]] says Collin later spent two months closely onboarding executives to transmit founder instinct before giving autonomy.

## Qualifications
The retention source summarizes Front through a fundraising-deck narrative, so the metrics are source-scoped and date-specific. The advice source is Collin's own retrospective, so it is strongest as evidence of her operating lessons and weaker as independent proof of later performance, pricing, or market position.
