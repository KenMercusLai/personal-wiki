## Overview
[[JeremyABoyd]] is a software practitioner who wrote about experimentation, onboarding, pricing, and additional revenue streams for side projects.

## Current Profile
Boyd presents small-product monetization as a measurable operating system: attribute visitors to offer variants, guide trial users toward activation, structure plans to make tradeoffs visible, and add recurring or transactional revenue around the core product. His evidence is a first-person historical account rather than a disclosed experiment dataset.

## Key Characteristics
- Advocates measuring conversions even when a project cannot justify specialized experimentation software.
- Treats onboarding as behavior-conditioned customer success and sales work, not a single transactional email.
- Uses tiered pricing, anchoring, discounts, licensing, hosting, payments, add-ons, and integrations as monetization levers.
- Reports a 6% to 18% free-to-paid conversion increase and $80 customer lifetime value from an onboarding sequence.

## Evidence
- Experimentation practice: [[jeremy-a-boyd-tricks-to-monetize-your-side-project]] describes a simple ASP.NET MVC variant-assignment system tied to cookies, analytics, and payment records.
- Onboarding practice: [[jeremy-a-boyd-tricks-to-monetize-your-side-project]] gives a staged sequence from signup through usage prompts, personal contact, and a targeted Pro coupon.
- Monetization scope: [[jeremy-a-boyd-tricks-to-monetize-your-side-project]] combines pricing tiers with payment processing, annual licensing, hosted SaaS, add-ons, and integrations.
- Reported outcome: [[jeremy-a-boyd-tricks-to-monetize-your-side-project]] attributes a rise from 6% to 18% conversion and $80 lifetime value to trial-user onboarding.

## Qualifications
The sole source is Boyd's own 2016 practitioner essay. It does not disclose sample sizes, allocation, statistical uncertainty, cohort duration, churn, or the individual contribution of each email and pricing tactic. Its payment-fee figures and vendor examples are historical and should not be treated as current terms.
