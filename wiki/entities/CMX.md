## Overview
[[CMX]] is a company founded by [[DavidSpinks]] to serve community professionals, initially through CMX Summit and later through publishing, training, research, an online community, and planned software.

## Current Profile
CMX began when Spinks identified the absence of a dedicated conference after speaking with hundreds of people who built communities for businesses. Max Altschuler helped launch the first summit, which reportedly sold out in five weeks and generated positive cash flow as soon as ticket sales opened. Two years later, Spinks described CMX as profitable, fully bootstrapped, slowly hiring, and projected to approach US$1 million in annual revenue while deliberately prioritizing durable impact, team meaning, work balance, and strategic freedom over unicorn status.

## Key Characteristics
- Serves people who build communities for businesses.
- Began with a demand-validated professional conference.
- Used customer revenue rather than outside investment to fund operations.
- Expanded from events into media, training, research, and an online community.
- Grew with a very small team and slow, high-conviction hiring.
- Defined ambition through useful impact, profitability, durability, and work quality rather than valuation alone.

## Evidence
- Market origin: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] says hundreds of practitioner conversations revealed the need for a dedicated conference.
- Early demand: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] reports that the first CMX Summit sold out in five weeks with international attendance.
- Customer funding: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] says ticket sales made the company cash-flow positive from launch and that it took no outside money.
- Product expansion: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] lists a publication, professional training, research, an online community, and future software plans.
- Operating constraint: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] links bootstrapping to focus, one-at-a-time hiring, community-aligned revenue, and freedom of direction.
- Historical economics: [[david-spinks-bootstrapping-in-unicorn-land]] reports profitability and a 2016 projection near US$1 million in revenue.

## Qualifications
All performance claims come from Spinks's 2016 first-person account. A projection is not realized revenue, cash-flow positivity from initial ticket sales does not establish continuous profitability, and the source provides no margins, customer retention, founder-capital accounting, or current company status. Bootstrapping also relied on two years of forgone salary and meaningful founder financial risk.

## What Changed
- Created CMX as a customer-funded professional-community business rather than a software-only bootstrapping example.
- Recorded the distinction between historical projections and verified realized performance.
