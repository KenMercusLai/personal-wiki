## Overview
[[LG]] is represented in this wiki as a Korean consumer-electronics manufacturer whose CES 2018 presence joined OLED displays, WebOS televisions, home appliances, connected-home strategy, and the Cloi assistant and hub effort.

## Current Profile
Sinofsky presents LG, alongside [[Samsung]], as evidence that Korean manufacturers had taken leadership in the connected home from Japanese and American incumbents. LG's product breadth let it connect televisions, kitchens, laundry, displays, and a proprietary Cloi assistant direction while also supporting Alexa and Google integrations. The opportunity was a coherent household system; the risk was duplicated controls and rapidly aging software embedded in appliances that owners expect to keep for many years.

The report treats LG's OLED work as substantial display progress rather than just ecosystem positioning. It describes OLED moving toward the mainstream, extremely thin panels creating new speaker-design constraints, and LG demonstrating both large displays and a compact vertical 4K projector.

## Key Characteristics
- Combined display, television, appliance, and connected-home businesses in a broad consumer-electronics portfolio.
- Promoted the Cloi assistant and hub direction while also supporting Alexa and Google integrations.
- Used WebOS and television control-center features as one proposed household interaction surface.
- Contributed to OLED's movement toward mainstream premium television products and related form-factor innovation.
- Exposed lifecycle and interoperability risks when long-lived appliances depend on embedded screens, microphones, and runtimes.

## Evidence
- Portfolio breadth: [[ces-2018-real-advances-real-progress-real-questions]] describes LG across televisions, OLED, projectors, kitchens, laundry, connected-home products, and voice control.
- Assistant strategy: [[ces-2018-real-advances-real-progress-real-questions]] says LG promoted Cloi while product lines also supported Alexa and Google to varying degrees.
- Television control: [[ces-2018-real-advances-real-progress-real-questions]] describes LG televisions as hubs with WebOS, voice control, and smart-center features.
- Display progress: [[ces-2018-real-advances-real-progress-real-questions]] describes OLED moving toward the mainstream and notes LG's large thin displays and portable vertical 4K projector.
- Lifecycle risk: [[ces-2018-real-advances-real-progress-real-questions]] questions putting short-lived software, screens, and microphones into devices expected to last a decade.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one observer's CES 2018 report, not LG filings, market-share data, product teardowns, adoption studies, or later ecosystem outcomes. Several items were demonstrations or newly announced products, so show-floor visibility does not establish durable demand, reliability, support quality, or strategic success.
