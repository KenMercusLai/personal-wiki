## Overview
[[ViniciusBrasil]] is a software-development author represented in the wiki by an essay about programming-language choice, business value, and workplace conduct.

## Current Profile
Brasil presents programming languages as communication tools with different areas of applicability rather than as universal markers of technical superiority. His essay asks developers to connect code to the business problem being solved and to keep technical criticism from becoming a source of hostility toward colleagues.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats programming languages as tools whose suitability depends on context.
- Connects software development with understanding and delivering business value.
- Criticizes blanket language hostility as harmful to workplace relationships.

## Evidence
- Tool framing: [[do-not-be-this-kind-of-developer-by-vinicius-brasil]] compares spoken and programming languages through their communicative purpose.
- Contextual judgment: [[do-not-be-this-kind-of-developer-by-vinicius-brasil]] says Python, Java, and JavaScript have distinct strengths and limitations rather than universal superiority.
- Workplace conduct: [[do-not-be-this-kind-of-developer-by-vinicius-brasil]] argues that repeated contempt for programming languages spreads negativity through a team.

## Qualifications
The wiki has one short opinion essay by Brasil and no independent biographical or employment information. Its language examples are illustrative rather than comparative technical evaluations, and it does not provide a decision method or evidence measuring workplace effects.
