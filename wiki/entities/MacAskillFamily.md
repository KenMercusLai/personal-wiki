## Overview
The [[MacAskillFamily]] is the family group behind [[SmugMug]] in the Los Angeles Times profile, with relatives working across founding, executive, finance, support, testing, analytics, and customer-service roles.

## Current Profile
The source portrays the MacAskills as a close-knit Mormon family that turned its internal trust, shared history, and eccentric rituals into a business identity. [[DonMacAskill]] led SmugMug as chief executive and "chief geek"; [[ChrisMacAskill]] joined early as father, co-founder, and serial entrepreneur; Toni MacAskill handled finances and helped customers; and Don's siblings and relatives took product, analytics, printing support, and customer-service roles. The article treats that family density as a competitive advantage but also notes the risk that family problems can spill into work.

## Key Characteristics
- Close-knit family operating a Silicon Valley software company.
- Multiple generations and relatives worked directly in [[SmugMug]].
- Family identity was made visible to customers through photos, videos, support style, and company rituals.
- Shared family commitment supported self-funded growth and refusal of outside control.
- The same family bond created potential workplace-friction and outsider-alienation risks.

## Evidence
- Family composition: [[a-focused-family-business-los-angeles-times]] describes seven family members involved in SmugMug, ranging from age 23 to 63.
- Operating roles: [[a-focused-family-business-los-angeles-times]] names Don, Chris, Toni, Ben, Mark, Anne, and Robin in leadership, finance, support, testing, analytics, and customer-service work.
- Visible identity: [[a-focused-family-business-los-angeles-times]] says the family posted its own photos and videos, used informal customer emails, and held costume-filled events and playful company photos.
- Self-funded control: [[a-focused-family-business-los-angeles-times]] says the MacAskills turned down outside investment and acquisition offers to retain control.
- Friction risk: [[a-focused-family-business-los-angeles-times]] includes David Russell's warning about family bonds as both advantage and disadvantage and Don's concern that new hires might feel alienated or steamrolled.

## Qualifications
The source presents the MacAskills through a positive profile of SmugMug, so it gives limited independent evidence about internal conflict, non-family employee experience, or how the family model changed as the company scaled.
