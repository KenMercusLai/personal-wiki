## Overview
[[Lyrebird]] is represented in this wiki as a publicly available 2018 service for creating a text-to-speech vocal avatar from prompted recordings.

## Current Profile
In [[CharlieWarzel]]'s experiment, Lyrebird asks a user to record and validate mundane prompted sentences, analyzes pronunciation and cadence, and then renders arbitrary typed text in the learned voice. Roughly sixty sentences and an hour of recording produced recognizable but robotic output; further examples improved cadence while grain, ringing, and buzz remained. The system nevertheless supported a successful short impersonation call when its defects were hidden behind a poor-reception story.

## Key Characteristics
- Collects supervised voice examples through a prompt, record, listen, validate, and retry workflow.
- Converts typed text into speech modeled on the enrolled speaker's vocal patterns.
- Improves with additional recordings but, in the reported 2018 test, retained audible artifacts.
- Was accessible enough for a journalist to create a workable clone without specialist production infrastructure.
- Framed broad release as a way for society to adapt while acknowledging harmful uses.

## Qualifications
The profile reflects one user's 2018 experience and the company's own ethics rationale. It does not establish Lyrebird's typical fidelity, training requirements, safeguards, misuse rate, or performance in unscripted dialogue, and it should not be read as a description of any later product or corporate status.
