## Overview
[[AlbertWenger]] is an investor and Continuations author represented here through essays on customer-aligned internet business models and the human needs for purpose and recognition.

## Current Profile
Wenger's 2018 business-model essay traces how the web inherited advertising from publishing, argues that fragmented attention turned scale and targeting into conflicts with users and advertisers, and presents consumer subscriptions as a more promising basis for trust because providers must keep delivering enough value to earn renewal. His examples range from [[Netflix]] and [[Spotify]] to a cross-category USV portfolio.

His 2016 management essay applies a similarly incentive- and system-oriented lens to motivation. It argues that people need to understand why their work matters and to have their effort noticed, while founders commonly retain too much vision and strategy as private context. Wenger extends the claim beyond companies: if society equates work with purpose, job displacement removes a source of meaning and social acknowledgment as well as income.

## Key Characteristics
- Connects brand trust to the incentives created by a company's revenue model.
- Analyzes advertising as a two-sided platform conflict affecting both end users and advertisers.
- Advocates consumer subscriptions as a conditional alignment mechanism based on continuing value and zero marginal price for additional use.
- Uses USV's investment thesis and portfolio as practical examples of the argument.
- Treats purpose and recognition as paired needs that management systems can support or erode.
- Connects organizational growth and movement away from traditional work to the loss of context, visibility, and meaning.

## Evidence
- Trusted-brand interpretation: [[continuations-by-albert-wenger-from-advertising-to-subscriptions-and-the]] defines trust through incentives aligned with customers over the long term.
- Advertising diagnosis: [[continuations-by-albert-wenger-from-advertising-to-subscriptions-and-the]] links fragmented attention to reach, targeting, user conflict, and platforms' need to mediate advertiser relationships.
- Subscription thesis: [[continuations-by-albert-wenger-from-advertising-to-subscriptions-and-the]] uses renewal value, unrestricted additional use, media breakouts, and USV portfolio examples to argue for consumer subscriptions.
- Purpose and management: [[continuations-by-albert-wenger-our-need-for-purpose-and-recognition]] argues that employees need task-to-mission context and acknowledgment rather than unexplained authority or praise alone.
- Scaling and social transition: [[continuations-by-albert-wenger-our-need-for-purpose-and-recognition]] says added management layers can reduce employee visibility and that societies moving away from traditional work need other sources of purpose and recognition.

## Qualifications
This profile is intentionally bounded to two short essays. It does not establish Wenger's complete biography, investment record, later views, whether the named subscription companies achieved durable alignment or performance, or the empirical strength of the purpose-and-recognition framework. The 2016 essay refers generally to research but provides no citations and relies on personal and television anecdotes for its management examples.
