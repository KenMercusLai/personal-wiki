## Overview
[[Reframed]] was a startup described by one of its cofounders in a 2016 shutdown retrospective about a failed financing round, an unreliable advertising partnership, and the discovery that its remaining runway was shorter than expected.

## Current Profile
The available source presents Reframed as a venture-backed media or advertising-dependent business that had raised about £500,000 over several years and was preparing a £350,000 round at a £4 million valuation. Its immediate failure was compound: the company relied on one operator as client, advertising supplier, commercial-model authority, and prospective lead investor; the author later alleged that the investment, direct advertising deals, invoices, credentials, and some identity representations were unreliable. Once the founders removed that dependency, rebuilt technical control, and excluded unpaid invoices, they concluded that the business lacked both the forecast and cash needed to continue.

The source also resists a pure fraud explanation. Its author says Reframed did not yet have a commercially viable business independent of the disputed advertising model and that earlier viability would have made financing easier. The durable profile is therefore a startup whose weak underlying model and concentrated counterparty exposure interacted with inadequate diligence and limited runway.

## Key Characteristics
- Had reportedly raised about £500,000 before pursuing a further £350,000 round at a £4 million valuation.
- Depended on advertising revenue whose relationships, pricing, and forecast were supplied by an outside operator.
- Concentrated client, supplier, technical-access, commercial-model, and prospective-investor roles in one counterparty.
- Lost its team after the proposed financing collapsed and doubtful receivables were removed from the cash calculation.
- Was wound down solvently, according to the author, through founders covering debts and writing off unpaid wages.

## Evidence
- Financing position: [[from-4-million-to-broke-this-is-our-startup-horror-story]] reports about £500,000 in prior investment and £350,000 offered by a prospective lead investor and incumbents at a £4 million valuation.
- Commercial dependency: [[from-4-million-to-broke-this-is-our-startup-horror-story]] says one counterparty supplied the advertising system, advertiser claims, forecast inputs, client revenue, and most of the proposed new capital.
- Model invalidation: [[from-4-million-to-broke-this-is-our-startup-horror-story]] says the founders later found affiliate links rather than direct advertiser relationships and concluded that the advertising forecast was unusable.
- Runway and shutdown: [[from-4-million-to-broke-this-is-our-startup-horror-story]] reports that removing unpaid invoices shortened runway to month-end, after which employees were told to seek new jobs and founders covered remaining obligations.
- Founder interpretation: [[from-4-million-to-broke-this-is-our-startup-horror-story]] explicitly says the alleged deception was harmful but did not excuse Reframed's failure to establish an independently commercial business.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one cofounder's retrospective. It contains serious allegations about named counterparties but no attached contracts, court records, banking evidence, invoices, advertiser confirmations, employee testimony, investor testimony, or response from the accused parties. The reported financing, valuation, revenue, debt, and solvency figures are unaudited, and the source does not describe Reframed's product, customers, ownership, full financial statements, or later corporate disposition in enough detail for a broader company history.

## What Changed
- Created a bounded company profile that keeps alleged counterparty misconduct separate from independently demonstrated fact.
- Identified concentrated counterparty roles and cash-measurement error as interacting failure mechanisms.
- Preserved the founder's own qualification that the underlying business was not yet commercially viable.
