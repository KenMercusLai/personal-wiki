## Overview
[[BenWeber]] is represented as a StarCraft AI researcher and the author of a 2016 assessment of the obstacles facing expert-level game agents.

## Current Profile
Weber founded the AIIDE StarCraft AI Competition in 2010 to encourage comparative bot evaluation and human-versus-machine exhibitions. Drawing on his dissertation's task-environment analysis, he frames [[StarCraft]] as a research domain joining hidden information, large-scale control, strategic adaptation, adversarial surprises, simulation constraints, and real-time execution.

## Key Characteristics
- Founded the AIIDE StarCraft AI Competition in 2010.
- Evaluates game AI against expert human play rather than bot-only rankings.
- Uses task-environment properties to connect StarCraft research to real-world AI problems.
- Advocates hierarchical abstraction and hybrid learned-and-scripted control.
- Treats his 2016 proposal as a research forecast rather than a demonstrated solution.

## Evidence
- Competition role: [[gamasutra-ben-webers-blog-deepmind-challenges-for-starcraft]] says Weber started the AIIDE competition to compare bots and test them against people.
- Research framing: [[gamasutra-ben-webers-blog-deepmind-challenges-for-starcraft]] grounds the task-environment comparison in Weber's dissertation.
- Technical judgment: [[gamasutra-ben-webers-blog-deepmind-challenges-for-starcraft]] identifies uncertainty, abstraction, adaptation, and real-time control as expected breakthroughs.

## Qualifications
The source is Weber's own short practitioner essay. It does not independently verify bot ratings, competition outcomes, or the proposed correspondence between StarCraft and real-world environments, and it predates later StarCraft AI results.
