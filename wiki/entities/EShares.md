## Overview
[[EShares]] is the company whose 2015 employee course documented a founder-led operating model spanning culture, software leverage, management, capital deployment, and compensation.

## Current Profile
In the source, eShares presents itself as a growth-stage software company run like a professional sports team. Its system combines a shared MVP vocabulary, synchronized daily starts, twice-weekly show-and-tells, recurring one-on-one walks, automation-oriented role evolution, a fixed burn target, and compensation tied to skill-market value. The company also defines success beyond its own survival: it wants employees to develop capabilities and later create important work elsewhere.

## Key Characteristics
- Used a full-day, CEO-led eShares 101 course to teach values and execution strategy in small groups.
- Treated all functions as software work aimed at translating real-world problems and automating repeatable activity.
- Used sports-team language to support synchronized rituals, explicit performance expectations, and collective participation.
- Paired a $600,000 monthly burn target with gradual deployment of its reported cash balance.
- Framed learning, marketability, and future alumni impact as central parts of the employee value proposition.

## Evidence
- Culture system: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] describes the recurring course, 8:30 a.m. start, company show-and-tells, and shared reading and vocabulary.
- Work model: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] says every role should convert real problems into software, create leverage, and cycle from learning through execution to automation or role growth.
- Management and finance: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] reports recurring one-on-one walks, open internal financial updates, a fixed burn target, and staged spending reductions.
- Employee bargain: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] describes market-based pay, a 75th-percentile target, learning opportunities, and an alumni-centered legacy ambition.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from one founder-authored 2015 onboarding essay. It documents intended culture and policy, not employee experience or comparative results, and provides no outcome evidence for retention, performance, compensation fairness, profitability, or long-term durability. The article was later published under Carta branding, but it describes the company as eShares at the time and does not itself explain the later name change.
