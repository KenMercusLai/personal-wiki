## Overview
[[EShares]] is the company whose 2015 employee course and 2016 management FAQ document a founder-led operating model spanning culture, software leverage, feedback, delegation, capital deployment, compensation, and personnel responsibility.

## Current Profile
In the sources, eShares presents itself as a growth-stage software company run like a professional sports team. Its system combines a shared MVP vocabulary, synchronized daily starts, twice-weekly show-and-tells, recurring one-on-one walks, automation-oriented role evolution, a fixed burn target, and compensation tied to skill-market value. The company also defines success beyond its own survival: it wants employees to develop capabilities and later create important work elsewhere.

The management FAQ adds a sharper hierarchy doctrine. Managers should reinforce strengths, diagnose errors as possible communication or training failures, delegate attractive work, keep the undesirable work, resolve present problems, and teach self-evaluation. Executives should prevent larger problems and influence strong lieutenants rather than rely on command. Promotion is therefore framed as additional responsibility without additional privilege, including responsibility for humane exits when the company concludes that an employee cannot succeed.

## Key Characteristics
- Used CEO-led teaching and repeated operating rituals to make culture concrete.
- Treated all functions as software work aimed at translating real-world problems and automating repeatable activity.
- Used sports-team language to support synchronized routines, explicit performance expectations, and collective participation.
- Expected management responsibility and undesirable work to move upward while developmental work moved downward.
- Combined listening, self-evaluation, and advice-seeking with a strong, manager-decided approach to role fit and dismissal.
- Paired a $600,000 monthly burn target with gradual deployment of its reported cash balance.
- Framed learning, marketability, and future alumni impact as central parts of the employee value proposition.

## Evidence
- Culture system: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] describes the recurring course, 8:30 a.m. start, company show-and-tells, and shared reading and vocabulary.
- Work model: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] says every role should convert real problems into software, create leverage, and cycle from learning through execution to automation or role growth.
- Management philosophy: [[a-managers-faq-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] ties performance to strength reinforcement, diagnostic curiosity, developmental delegation, upward problem ownership, self-evaluation, and influence.
- Personnel accountability: [[a-managers-faq-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] asks managers to own communication, training, hiring, support, and humane termination, while presenting promotion as responsibility rather than privilege.
- Management and finance: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] reports recurring one-on-one walks, open internal financial updates, a fixed burn target, and staged spending reductions.
- Employee bargain: [[eshares-101-owners-manual-blog-by-carta-medium]] describes market-based pay, a 75th-percentile target, learning opportunities, and an alumni-centered legacy ambition.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from two founder-authored company essays from 2015 and 2016. They document intended culture and policy, not employee experience or comparative results, and provide no outcome evidence for retention, performance, compensation fairness, profitability, or long-term durability. The FAQ's categorical dismissal advice lacks procedural, legal, anti-bias, accommodation, and employee-response safeguards; its strength-first framing also should not delay correction of harmful or high-impact work. The articles were later published under Carta branding, but describe the company as eShares at the time and do not themselves explain the later name change.

## What Changed
- Added the 2016 management doctrine around strengths, delegation, self-evaluation, influence, problem prevention, and upward responsibility.
- Added qualifications around fair process, evidence, bias, and the limits of founder-authored policy accounts.
