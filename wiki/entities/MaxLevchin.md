## Overview
[[MaxLevchin]] is a Ukrainian-born entrepreneur and engineer whose commencement address links immigration, study at [[UniversityOfIllinoisUrbanaChampaign]], repeated startup failure, the founding of [[PayPal]], and later leadership mistakes to lessons about curiosity, humane recovery, and formative relationships.

## Current Profile
Levchin says his family left the Soviet Union amid political and economic instability, rising antisemitism, and the nearby legacy of Chernobyl, arriving in the United States in 1991 with about $600 for five people. Science-fair results helped him reach the University of Illinois, where the emergence of Mosaic, access to the Digital Computer Lab, and technically ambitious classmates placed him inside an unusually fertile computing environment.

He describes joining his first startup at 19 through curiosity rather than a clear entrepreneurial plan. That company and several successors failed, but the work revealed that company-building was the path he wanted. He later says he met [[PeterThiel]] and co-founded [[PayPal]], whose early team included many Illinois classmates.

The address does not present later success as immunity from failure. Levchin describes nearly exhausting another startup's cash and conducting two rounds of layoffs. A colleague's instruction to act as a person rather than only as a chief executive led him to help departing employees directly; he uses the episode to reject celebratory accounts that erase failure's human cost.

## Key Characteristics
- Immigrated to the United States with his family in 1991 after growing up in Kyiv in the Soviet Union.
- Studied computer science at the University of Illinois and found collaborators through its computing community.
- Discovered an entrepreneurial vocation through a failed student startup and several further failed companies.
- Co-founded PayPal with Peter Thiel according to his address.
- Treats curiosity as a reason to run bounded experiments before certainty is available.
- Rejects romantic accounts of failure and emphasizes direct compassion toward people harmed by leadership mistakes.
- Values teams and relationships that create trust, empathy, respect, and mutual pressure to improve.

- Humane crisis response: [[uiuc-2018-commencement-address-too-long-to-tweet]] says Levchin helped laid-off employees after a colleague urged him to leave the executive office and share the team's pain directly.
- Relationship network: [[uiuc-2018-commencement-address-too-long-to-tweet]] says early PayPal engineers included Illinois classmates and that several relationships continued across later projects.

## Qualifications
The profile is based on Levchin's own motivational commencement address rather than an independent biography. It selectively interprets a path ending in exceptional success, does not name or fully describe the failed companies and later layoff-stage startup, and cannot establish that curiosity, team quality, or Illinois relationships caused the outcomes he recounts. His advice also needs adaptation for people whose financial, family, health, immigration, or employment constraints make experimentation less reversible.
