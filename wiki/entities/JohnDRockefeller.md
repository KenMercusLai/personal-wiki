## Overview
[[JohnDRockefeller]] appears in the source as a historical business example whose distribution-control lesson may transfer, while the legal environment that enabled Standard Oil's competitive conduct should not be treated as repeatable.

## Current Profile
Housel uses Rockefeller as one item in a list of qualified role models. The repeatable lesson is the importance of controlling distribution. The non-repeatable or non-emulable part is the surrounding legal and competitive context that allowed him to destroy competitors. Rockefeller therefore supports the essay's larger claim that learners should separate durable strategic patterns from historically specific permission structures.

## Key Characteristics
- Represents distribution control as a transferable business lesson.
- Represents a legal and competitive environment that should not be copied as if it still exists.
- Functions as a caution against romanticizing historical business tactics.
