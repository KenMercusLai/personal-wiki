## Overview
[[PhillipMendoncaVieira]] is the [[Appcanary]] author reflecting on a failed [[Clojure]] implementation, the return to [[Ruby]], and the developer-experience norms around programming-language communities.

## Current Profile
Mendonca-Vieira appears as a practitioner making a context-specific tool judgment rather than a universal anti-Clojure argument. He says he entered Clojure with goodwill and invested heavily, but later decided that continuing with it was wrong for Appcanary's constraints. He also reflects on how language criticism can land as community criticism when developers treat tools as part of identity.

## Key Characteristics
- He frames tool choice through startup constraints and business focus.
- He argues for avoiding distributed systems until a team can pay their coordination cost.
- He treats developer happiness and ease of use as legitimate tool-design goals.
- He critiques how "simple is not easy" rhetoric can dismiss user-hostile tooling.
- He later recognized that his talk did not sufficiently establish charitable intent.

## Qualifications
The source is a reflective essay about one startup's experience. It includes self-described hyperbole and explicitly asks readers to treat parts of the critique as memorable overstatement rather than a full empirical evaluation of Clojure.
