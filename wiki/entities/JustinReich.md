## Overview
[[JustinReich]] is the author of the EdTech Researcher article critiquing early big-data claims in MOOC and online-learning research.

## Current Profile
In this source, Reich writes as an education-technology researcher using satire to challenge a narrow research pattern: taking massive learner-log datasets and reducing them to obvious measures of effort. His "Reich's Law of Doing Stuff" intentionally exaggerates the triviality of repeatedly showing that students who participate more are more likely to complete or perform well.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses humor and exaggeration to critique shallow interpretations of large education datasets.
- Distinguishes correlation between learner activity and achievement from causal evidence about learning.
- Pushes MOOC researchers toward richer questions about mechanisms, course design, and student motivation.

## Evidence

- Research agenda: [[big-data-mooc-research-breakthrough-learning-activities-lead-to-achievement-edtech-researcher-education-week]] closes by asking why MOOC researchers shrink big data and confirm the obvious.
