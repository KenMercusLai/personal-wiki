## Overview
[[TBH]] was a social polling app and startup represented here through the school-by-school launch method its founders documented after [[Facebook]] acquired the company.

## Current Profile
TBH treated early social-product distribution as a community-density problem. Instead of seeking a broad but fragmented audience, its team reportedly identified students through school references on [[Instagram]], created a private invitation account for each school, accumulated follow requests, and revealed an app link to the group around dismissal time. The memo describes the method as reproducible but not readily scalable and says it was repeated while the team tested products.

Facebook acquired TBH in October 2017 and later shut the app down for low usage. The acquisition nevertheless transferred a documented growth process and polling-product experience. That makes TBH a case of an acquired product yielding operating knowledge even without remaining a durable standalone service.

## Key Characteristics
- Built a polling app that reportedly reached 2.5 million daily users, mostly teenagers, within months of launch.
- Organized launches around individual high schools so peers encountered the product together.
- Combined profile privacy, ambiguous invitation copy, follow-request notifications, and after-school timing to concentrate attention.
- Repeated and increasingly automated launches while testing products before product-market fit.
- Shared its growth process internally after acquisition by Facebook.

## Evidence
- Community launch design: [[internal-facebook-note-here-is-a-psychological-trick-to-target-teens]] reproduces the founders' sequence from identifying school-affiliated accounts to a coordinated public-profile reveal.
- Product iteration: [[internal-facebook-note-here-is-a-psychological-trick-to-target-teens]] says TBH ran launches every few weeks, automated inefficiencies, and reused an app binary for new product updates.
- Acquisition outcome: [[internal-facebook-note-here-is-a-psychological-trick-to-target-teens]] reports Facebook's October 2017 purchase and July 2018 shutdown for low usage.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one reported internal memo and one 2018 news account. User scale, acquisition price, policy compliance, conversion, retention, and the method's causal effect are not independently demonstrated here. Teen identification and curiosity-based targeting raise consent, privacy, and power concerns that are not resolved by the founders' claim of terms-of-service compliance. Facebook's receipt of the memo does not establish its later use.

## What Changed
- Established TBH as a bounded-community social-launch case rather than only a short-lived polling app.
- Separated rapid initial adoption from durable usage, since Facebook later cited low usage when closing the service.
- Added transfer of operating knowledge as one possible acquisition outcome.
