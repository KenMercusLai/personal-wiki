## Overview
[[AbhijitTomar]] is the author of a 2018 first-person account of a telephone-banking scam routed through manipulated Google business-listing information.

## Current Profile
Tomar investigated after his cousin lost INR 9,000 to a caller impersonating a bank employee. He reports finding the same scammer-controlled phone number on several bank branches in one region and uses the case to argue that authoritative interface presentation can transfer platform trust to user-contributed information.

## Key Characteristics
- Documents the victim's path from a failed transaction through Google search to credential disclosure and financial loss.
- Connects an individual social-engineering failure to the verification and correction design of a listing platform.
- Distinguishes the victim's unsafe disclosure from the platform-mediated credibility that made the caller seem legitimate.

## Evidence
- Incident account: [[new-form-of-google-banking-scam]] reports Tomar's cousin calling a listed number, disclosing card details and a CVV, and losing INR 9,000.
- Repeated pattern: [[new-form-of-google-banking-scam]] says Tomar found the same number registered for several bank branches in the region.
- Trust analysis: [[new-form-of-google-banking-scam]] argues that users ordinarily assume information presented by a familiar technology service is accurate.

## Qualifications
The wiki has one source by Tomar. It is a first-person report rather than an independently audited fraud investigation, and the two listing screenshots referenced in the saved Markdown are unavailable for inspection.
