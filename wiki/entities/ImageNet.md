## Overview
[[ImageNet]] is presented as a free collection of more than 14 million labeled images and an annual computer-vision competition that made data scale and model progress publicly comparable.

## Current Profile
Fei-Fei Li launched ImageNet to test the view that larger labeled datasets would change machine learning. The dataset went live in 2009 and gained an annual contest in 2010. Its historical importance in this source comes from the 2012 competition, where a deep neural network built by Geoffrey Hinton's students was reported to identify objects with almost twice the accuracy of the nearest competitor, convincing many skeptics that deep learning had become practically important.

## Key Characteristics
- Combined a very large labeled-image collection with open access for research.
- Supplied supervised training data rather than asking systems to learn solely from unlabeled imagery.
- Used an annual contest to create a common evaluation and publication event.
- Made the 2012 deep-learning performance discontinuity visible to the broader field.
- Became a symbol of the joint importance of data, computation, and trainable model architecture.

## Evidence
- Scale and access: [[from-2016-why-deep-learning-is-suddenly-changing-your-life-fortune]] reports more than 14 million labeled images in a free database.
- Institutional timeline: [[from-2016-why-deep-learning-is-suddenly-changing-your-life-fortune]] dates project initiation to 2007, public availability to 2009, and the contest to 2010.
- 2012 result: [[from-2016-why-deep-learning-is-suddenly-changing-your-life-fortune]] reports that Hinton's students achieved nearly twice the accuracy of the closest competitor.
- Opinion shift: [[from-2016-why-deep-learning-is-suddenly-changing-your-life-fortune]] presents that result as evidence that persuaded previously skeptical researchers.

## Qualifications
The article simplifies the competition result to "accuracy" without naming the error metric or experimental details, and its human-performance comparison refers to a later contest without explaining task scope. ImageNet success measures performance on a curated labeled benchmark, not robust open-world vision, causal understanding, fairness, or safe deployment.
