## Overview
[[AlejandroScrivano]] is an Argentine producer who left game development for marketing after low pay, overtime, difficult team dynamics, and investor theft culminated in studio closure.

## Current Profile
Scrivano presents game production as emotionally conflicted: creating worlds for players is deeply rewarding, while reconciling programmers, artists, and business staff can be exhausting. He describes a small MMORPG team's environment as marked by low salaries, extended overtime, ego, and poor management. When an investor allegedly stole most of the studio's funds and disappeared, the company closed within a week and Scrivano changed industries despite social pressure framing departure as betrayal.

## Key Characteristics
- Values game creation as unusually meaningful collaborative world-building.
- Emphasizes the producer's translation burden across distinct professional cultures.
- Connects bad labor conditions with weak governance and investor risk.
- Chose marketing after studio failure despite identity pressure to remain in games.

## Evidence
- Creative appeal: [[gamasutra-the-great-video-game-exodus]] records Scrivano describing new-world creation as the best feeling in the world.
- Team strain: [[gamasutra-the-great-video-game-exodus]] says producers must reconcile programmers, artists, and business staff with different dispositions.

- Exit pressure: [[gamasutra-the-great-video-game-exodus]] says peers treated his move to marketing as betrayal even though he viewed it as correct.

## Qualifications
The investor-theft account is an uncorroborated allegation in one retrospective interview; the studio and investor are unnamed, and the article supplies no legal or financial records.
