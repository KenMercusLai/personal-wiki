## Overview
[[JasonSnell]] is a Macworld contributor whose 2016 essay compares Google's cross-platform interface choices with Microsoft's treatment of Mac software in the 1990s.

## Current Profile
In the bounded source, Snell writes as a long-time Mac software user who also depends on Google services. He argues that third-party applications should feel at home on their host operating system, using Office 6 for Mac and Google's Material-styled iOS applications as parallel cases where vendor consistency overrode local conventions. His position is not anti-Google or anti-Microsoft; it is a practitioner argument that brand identity and shared code should remain subordinate to user expectations on each platform.

## Key Characteristics
- Technology writer and Macworld contributor focused here on cross-platform interface behavior.
- Uses historical product comparison to connect Microsoft Office for Mac with Google's iOS applications.
- Advocates host-platform conventions while preserving room for genuine interaction innovation.
- Writes from personal use and observation rather than controlled usability or market research.

## Qualifications
The source is a 2016 opinion essay. It does not provide user research, internal product constraints, code-sharing economics, accessibility analysis, or measured outcomes, and its causal explanations for Microsoft and Google's choices are explicitly speculative.
