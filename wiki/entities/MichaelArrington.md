## Overview
[[MichaelArrington]] is presented as the founder of TechCrunch, a former Silicon Valley lawyer, a major rival to [[KaraSwisher]] for technology scoops, and the central ethical counterexample in a 2014 profile of technology journalism.

## Current Profile
The source portrays Arrington as strongly pro-entrepreneur and committed to disclosure rather than conventional recusal. It reports that he held stakes in small companies while writing about them and later started CrunchFund while associated with TechCrunch, a conflict that contributed to his 2011 dismissal after AOL acquired the publication. Swisher attacked these practices as corrupt and described his iterative “process journalism” as tolerance for avoidable inaccuracy, although a former TechCrunch writer also emphasizes similarities between the two reporters' aggressive, unaccountable public personae.

## Key Characteristics
- Built TechCrunch into a powerful technology-news publication and scoop competitor.
- Favored transparent disclosure over broad conflict-based recusal.
- Invested in companies while participating in technology coverage.
- Applied an iterative “process journalism” idea that Swisher rejected as willful inaccuracy.
- Publicly traded personal insults and ethical accusations with Swisher.

## Evidence
- Disclosure standard: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] attributes to Arrington the view that transparency permitted activity traditional ethics would forbid.
- Investing conflict: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] reports company stakes, CrunchFund's creation, and his departure from TechCrunch after the resulting dispute.
- Rivalry and resemblance: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] records the public feud and Paul Carr's claim that both reporters used aggressive access and resisted accountability.

## Qualifications
The evidence comes from a profile organized around Swisher and is openly critical of Arrington. It includes hostile quotations and compressed accounts of his investments, AOL tenure, dismissal, and journalistic philosophy, without a complete response from Arrington or an independent audit of TechCrunch coverage.
