## Overview
[[MichaelArrington]] is presented as the founder of TechCrunch, a former Silicon Valley lawyer, a major rival to [[KaraSwisher]] for technology scoops, and a technology journalist who published investor communications and added reporting context around them.

## Current Profile
The 2014 profile portrays Arrington as strongly pro-entrepreneur and committed to disclosure rather than conventional recusal. It reports that he held stakes in small companies while writing about them and later started CrunchFund while associated with TechCrunch, a conflict that contributed to his 2011 dismissal after AOL acquired the publication. Swisher attacked these practices as corrupt and described his iterative “process journalism” as tolerance for avoidable inaccuracy, although a former TechCrunch writer also emphasizes similarities between the two reporters' aggressive, unaccountable public personae.

His 2008 article on [[RonConway]] shows a narrower reporting mode: Arrington publishes three investor emails, identifies their continuity across the 2000 and 2008 downturns, connects the advice to contemporaneous venture warnings, and reports that Conway remained optimistic about innovation and intended to keep investing. The article supplies useful context around primary communications, but it does not independently test whether the advice improved portfolio outcomes.

## Key Characteristics
- Built TechCrunch into a powerful technology-news publication and scoop competitor.
- Favored transparent disclosure over broad conflict-based recusal.
- Invested in companies while participating in technology coverage.
- Applied an iterative “process journalism” idea that Swisher rejected as willful inaccuracy.
- Publicly traded personal insults and ethical accusations with Swisher.
- Published primary investor communications with brief reporting that distinguished short-term financing danger from long-term technology pessimism.

## Evidence
- Disclosure standard: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] attributes to Arrington the view that transparency permitted activity traditional ethics would forbid.
- Investing conflict: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] reports company stakes, CrunchFund's creation, and his departure from TechCrunch after the resulting dispute.
- Rivalry and resemblance: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] records the public feud and Paul Carr's claim that both reporters used aggressive access and resisted accountability.
- Investor-email reporting: [[michael-arrington-angel-investor-ron-conway-emails-his-portfolio-companies-over-financial-meltdown]] reproduces Conway's 2000 and 2008 emails and adds that Conway planned to continue investing despite his survival warning.

## Qualifications
The critical evidence comes from a profile organized around Swisher and includes hostile quotations and compressed accounts of Arrington's investments, AOL tenure, dismissal, and journalistic philosophy, without a complete response from Arrington or an independent audit of TechCrunch coverage. The 2008 article is Arrington's own brief framing of emails supplied by Conway; it does not independently establish the emails' effects, portfolio representativeness, or the accuracy of the broader market forecast.

## What Changed
- Expanded the profile beyond the Swisher rivalry to include Arrington's publication and contextualization of primary investor communications during the 2008 crisis.
