## Overview
[[GoogleAdWords]] is the source's product case for self-service advertising built through product management across sales, engineering, relevance, and revenue objections.

## Current Profile
The source presents early AdWords as a product that nearly failed to happen because Google's sales team feared cannibalization and engineers feared damage to search quality. The product solution separated self-service ads from salesperson-sold top ads and used an ad-ranking formula that combined price with click-through performance, making the business model more compatible with user relevance.

## Key Characteristics
- Self-service advertising platform at Google.
- Faced internal resistance from both ad sales and engineering.
- Used side placement to distinguish self-service ads from top search-result ads sold by salespeople.
- Ranked ads by a combination of price and performance rather than bid alone.
- Became the source's example of product management overcoming technical and business objections.

## Evidence
- Internal resistance: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says sales feared self-service ads would diminish large-brand sales and engineers feared ads would hurt search relevance.
- Placement design: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says AdWords-generated ads were placed to the side of search results.
- Relevance formula: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says placement multiplied price per impression by click-through performance.
- Spec and launch: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says Jane Manning wrote the first AdWords spec and worked with engineers to build and launch.

## Qualifications
The source is a product-management account of AdWords' origin and does not cover later auction design, quality score changes, advertiser tools, regulatory issues, or the broader evolution of Google's advertising business.

## What Changed
- Created Google AdWords as an internal-objection and relevance-aware advertising product case.
