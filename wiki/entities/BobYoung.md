## Overview
[[BobYoung]] is the Red Hat founder whose retrospective explains how Gerstner-era IBM influenced Red Hat's service-led open-source business model.

## Current Profile
Young says he and Mark Ewing studied why enterprises bought from [[IBM]] even when competitors offered better individual products. The answer—global presence and responsibility for customer success—helped them interpret freely reusable Linux not as an unsellable product but as a foundation for support and service contracts. The account emphasizes customer observation and sustained execution rather than a single business-model trick.

## Key Characteristics
- Cofounded [[RedHat]] with Mark Ewing.
- Learned from customer behavior around Gerstner-era IBM.
- Framed open-source commercialization around services and customer outcomes.
- Treated boxed Linux as an interim route toward enterprise contracts.
- Rejected a “magic bullet” account in favor of customer attention and hard work.

## Evidence
- IBM influence: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] quotes Young describing Red Hat's early attention to Gerstner's turnaround.
- Customer-outcome model: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] quotes him distinguishing service value from exclusive product ownership.
- Transition path: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] says boxed Linux helped Red Hat become large enough to sign enterprise service contracts.

## Qualifications
This is a founder retrospective quoted in a later strategy essay. It does not quantify Red Hat's revenue mechanisms, customer behavior, execution costs, or the relative contribution of licensing, product quality, ecosystem growth, support, and timing.

## What Changed
- Created Young's profile around the IBM-inspired service logic he attributes to Red Hat's early commercialization strategy.
