## Overview
[[EvWilliams]] is a technology founder and [[Medium]] co-founder represented here through essays on publishing incentives and the practical tension between leading growing companies and raising children.

## Current Profile
Williams presents systems through the incentives and constraints that shape behavior. In his Medium strategy essay, attention-funded publishing rewards compulsion and volume, while reader payment can support depth and understanding. In his working-parent essay, unlimited company demand collides with family time; his informal survey finds substantial but varied hours, widespread weekend work, and frequent childcare assistance rather than one universal founder schedule.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames publishing platforms as systems that shape understanding and behavior.
- Diagnoses online-media failure through the link between attention and money.
- Advocates direct reader payment as a way to align product and editorial incentives.
- Questions 100-hour startup mythology through an informal survey of successful working parents.
- Treats work-family allocation as plural and personally situated rather than prescribing one correct schedule.
- Makes hidden household support and work location part of the account of founder workload.

## Evidence
- Mission framing: [[words-still-matter-3-min-read]] revisits Williams's founding claim that publishing tools should deepen understanding and broaden participation.
- Incentive diagnosis: [[words-still-matter-3-min-read]] argues that attention is commodified and rewarded without regard to quality, context, or conscious choice.
- Strategic response: [[words-still-matter-3-min-read]] proposes subscriptions, no ads, an open paywall, and member-value-based partner earnings.
- Work-pattern evidence: [[growing-kids-and-growing-companies-working-parents-medium]] reports 55-60 hours as the largest band and only 2% at 90 or more hours in Williams's selected sample.
- Family-support evidence: [[growing-kids-and-growing-companies-working-parents-medium]] reports that 73% had household childcare assistance and that founders more often worked outside the office.
- Personal stance: [[growing-kids-and-growing-companies-working-parents-medium]] rejects a universal answer and juxtaposes an office dinner with a ski weekend with his children.

## Qualifications
Both sources are first-person essays and document Williams's framing rather than independent outcomes. The Medium essay does not establish the later results of its subscription model. The parent-work survey gives no sample size, response denominator, representative recruitment, demographic detail, or causal analysis; its founder-heavy technology network cannot define normal hours or healthy balance for working parents generally.

## What Changed
- Expanded the profile from publishing incentives to founder work, parenting, and schedule plurality.
- Added survey-method limits and household support as necessary context for his workload claims.
