## Overview
[[ParkerThompson]] is represented in the wiki by a 2017 Pivotal interview on engineering-team productivity and by his role in prompting a discussion about the qualities and development of venture investors.

## Current Profile
The Pivotal interview identifies Thompson as a former Pivot, an AngelList partner advising startups on scaling teams and products, and the person behind the pseudonymous Startup L. Jackson account. His management position is consistently team-centered: leaders should judge net group output, clarify cross-functional responsibilities, prefer problem-oriented craft over technology identity, and address inclusion before a homogeneous culture hardens. In Schlafman's essay, Thompson is the conversation partner who asks what attributes make a successful VC after a discussion of pre-seed investing and building a venture firm, thereby prompting the article's wider selection-and-apprenticeship framework.

## Key Characteristics
- Reframes the "10x engineer" debate around team-level productivity and downstream cost.
- Treats design, engineering, and product management as distinct but communicating responsibilities.
- Prefers collaborative, problem-oriented craftspeople over rigid technology identity.
- Distinguishes tiny product-validation teams from the organizational systems required after demand appears.
- Frames delayed inclusive hiring as compounding cultural debt.
- Uses practitioner conversations to surface a broader question about venture-investor quality and development.

## Qualifications

Schlafman's essay documents Thompson's prompt and professional context, not his answer to the investor-quality question. Neither source independently tests outcomes, documents his broader investment record, or establishes whether his roles and opinions changed later.
