## Overview
[[GregWilson]] is represented in the wiki through a secondary account of his talk “What We Actually Know About Software Development, and Why We Believe It's True,” which argues for stronger standards of evidence in software practice.

## Current Profile
In the source's account, Wilson distinguishes an unsupported software claim from a false one. He reportedly uses Martin Fowler's uncited claims about domain-specific languages as an example: the claimed productivity and communication benefits may be correct, but without data or citations their appropriate status is “not proven.”

The essay adopts Wilson's criticism of anecdotal argument and low standards of proof, then extends it into claims about Agile advocates that go beyond what Wilson is quoted as saying. This page therefore treats Wilson as the source of the epistemic distinction, not as endorsing all of the essay author's judgments about [[MartinFowler]] or [[AgileSoftwareDevelopment]].

## Key Characteristics
- Advocates explicit evidential standards for claims about software-development outcomes.
- Distinguishes absence of proof from proof of falsity.
- Criticizes uncited factual claims even when made by respected industry figures.
- Treats anecdotes and professional authority as insufficient support for broad causal conclusions.

## Evidence
- Standard of proof: [[elegant-coding-the-problem-with-todays-software-thought-leaders]] quotes Wilson classifying uncited DSL benefit claims as “not proven,” rather than true or false.
- Authority and citation: [[elegant-coding-the-problem-with-todays-software-thought-leaders]] reports Wilson criticizing substantive factual claims in an academic publication that supplied no citations or data.
- Anecdotal reasoning: [[elegant-coding-the-problem-with-todays-software-thought-leaders]] quotes Wilson rejecting loosely applicable stories as proof.

## Qualifications
The wiki currently has only a hostile essay's selected quotations from one Wilson talk, not the complete talk or Wilson's broader work. The surrounding source agrees with Wilson's evidence critique but explicitly disagrees with his favorable characterization of Fowler, so its interpretation should not be attributed wholesale to Wilson.
