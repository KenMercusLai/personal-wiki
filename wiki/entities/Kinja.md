## Overview
[[Kinja]] is the Gawker Media content-management and commenting system represented in the wiki through [[JoshLaurito]]'s retrospective on [[Gawker]].

## Current Profile
Within the source, Kinja is both publishing infrastructure and the interface for a large reader community. Laurito describes the platform as sometimes frustrating but treats comments as a major reason staff and readers enjoyed the sites; more than 16 million Gawker.com comments and ranked recommendation signals indicate substantial participation without establishing comment quality, moderation performance, or user retention.

## Key Characteristics
- Combined content-management and commenting functions for Gawker Media sites.
- Supported more than 16 million comments on Gawker.com.
- Used recommendation counts to surface and rank community contributions.
- Was valued for community participation despite acknowledged usability or operational frustration.

## Evidence
- Platform role: [[closing-the-book-on-gawker-com]] identifies Kinja as Gawker Media's CMS and commenting system.
- Participation scale: [[closing-the-book-on-gawker-com]] reports more than 16 million comments on Gawker.com.
- Recommendation layer: [[closing-the-book-on-gawker-com]] refers to prolific and recommended commenter rankings and lists the ten most-recommended comments.
- Mixed experience: [[closing-the-book-on-gawker-com]] describes Kinja as capable of being frustrating while emphasizing pride in and attachment to its comments.

## Qualifications
The source offers an affectionate operational retrospective rather than a technical or comparative account of Kinja. It does not describe the system's architecture, moderation rules, identity model, search and archive behavior, abuse rate, recommendation algorithm, active-user base, or causal effect on loyalty. Total comments and recommendations measure activity, not contribution quality or community health.
