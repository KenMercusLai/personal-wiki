## Overview
[[Odeo]] was a podcasting startup led by [[EvanWilliams]], represented here through Williams's 2006 account of its strategic and operating mistakes.

## Current Profile
Odeo expanded from an early demo and strong publicity into a broadly defined podcasting company without a narrow audience or product boundary. It built creator tools despite Williams not being a podcaster, moved too slowly against Apple, accepted substantial financing before settling its direction, and grew to 14 employees. By the reported conference talk, it had downsized and shifted toward people who listened to podcasts on the web, while keeping [[Twitter]] as a separately staffed experiment and still lacking a settled business model.

## Key Characteristics
- Pursued a broad podcasting ambition instead of a sharply bounded initial product.
- Built recording tools without strong founder identification with podcast creators.
- Relied on a long-term web strategy that did not secure a near-term foothold against Apple.
- Raised and staffed ahead of product and business-model clarity.
- Attempted to recover through downsizing, audience refocus, and small-team project isolation.

## Evidence
- Scope and publicity: [[evan-williams-how-odeo-screwed-up]] links a TED demo and New York Times attention with a company that became unfocused and bloated.
- User mismatch: [[evan-williams-how-odeo-screwed-up]] says Williams did not podcast and the recording tools were too simple.
- Financing and staffing: [[evan-williams-how-odeo-screwed-up]] describes founder seed capital, angel funding, a higher-valued venture term sheet, and a peak team of 14.
- Refocus: [[evan-williams-how-odeo-screwed-up]] says the company downsized, targeted web listeners, and kept Twitter with a dedicated two-person team.

## Qualifications
The evidence is a short journalistic report of Williams's talk rather than a full company history. Funding figures are approximate, the article gives no usage, revenue, retention, or customer-research data, and the reported corrective actions had not yet established a business model.
