## Overview
[[NicoleNguyen]] is a technology reporter represented here by a 2017 BuzzFeed News guide to the privacy and business-model tradeoffs of free apps and online services.

## Current Profile
Nguyen translates the [[UnrollMe]] controversy into a consumer audit method. Her account moves from the economic question—how a zero-price service pays its costs—to the access question—what the app can read or change—and then to practical review of permissions, privacy language, connected accounts, and alternatives. The work is explanatory consumer journalism, not a comprehensive legal or technical privacy audit.

## Key Characteristics
- Reports on consumer technology through concrete product and platform examples.
- Connects company revenue models with the personal data access needed to operate them.
- Turns privacy concerns into user-facing checks around necessity, trust, policies, permissions, and revocation.
- Distinguishes ordinary targeted advertising from more intrusive secondary use of email content.

## Evidence
- Business-model explanation: [[heres-the-thing-with-free-apps-and-services]] explains advertising, profiling, and data transfer as different ways a free service can create value from user information.
- Permission audit: [[heres-the-thing-with-free-apps-and-services]] asks whether Unroll.me needed authority to read, send, delete, and manage email and manage contacts.
- Practical guidance: [[heres-the-thing-with-free-apps-and-services]] recommends reviewing connected apps, phone permissions, privacy policies, and provider revenue sources.
- Consumer interpretation: [[heres-the-thing-with-free-apps-and-services]] points readers to plain-language terms-analysis services while still encouraging review of the underlying policies.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one 2017 article and does not establish Nguyen's wider career, later reporting, or current views. The article's settings paths and named company policies are historical, its privacy assessment is not a legal opinion or security audit, and some examples depend on company statements rather than independent verification.

## What Changed
- Created a profile centered on consumer-facing explanation of app permissions, privacy terms, and data-funded business models.
