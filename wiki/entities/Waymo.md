## Overview
[[Waymo]] is presented in a 2017 strategy essay as a dedicated autonomous-driving technology supplier whose real-world testing, simulation scale, and relationship to Google could create data and computing advantages.

## Current Profile
The source uses Waymo as the clearest example of a possible autonomy-platform vendor rather than as a complete company history. Its reported 25,000 real autonomous miles per week and one billion simulated miles during 2016 illustrate the difference between fleet collection and repeatable software testing. Evans treats Google's compute expertise as a potential scale advantage and asks whether an autonomy supplier such as Waymo could capture the feedback-loop value produced by automakers that deploy its system.

## Key Characteristics
- Develops autonomous-driving technology as a layer that could be supplied to vehicle manufacturers or fleets.
- Combines real-world driving with much larger-scale simulation in the source's 2016 figures.
- Is used to illustrate how compute, data, and institutional expertise might reinforce one another.
- Represents the possibility that a technology vendor, rather than an automaker, captures autonomy's platform value.

## Evidence
- Testing scale: [[winner-takes-all-effects-in-autonomous-cars-benedict-evans]] reports Waymo driving 25,000 real autonomous miles per week and one billion simulated miles in 2016.
- Computing advantage: [[winner-takes-all-effects-in-autonomous-cars-benedict-evans]] argues that being part of Google gave Waymo an advantage in large computing projects.
- Platform role: [[winner-takes-all-effects-in-autonomous-cars-benedict-evans]] uses Waymo as one possible supplier of an autonomy module separable from the car and on-demand service.
- Industry-power scenario: [[winner-takes-all-effects-in-autonomous-cars-benedict-evans]] contrasts a multi-supplier component market with an extreme outcome in which only Waymo succeeds.

## Qualifications
This profile reflects one analyst's speculative 2017 framing and historical figures, not Waymo's current ownership, deployments, safety record, commercial reach, partnerships, or competitive position. The article asks what might happen rather than establishing that Waymo's data advantage became durable or winner-take-all.
