## Overview
[[MartinFowler]] is represented in the wiki as a software-development author, Agile Manifesto signer, and Thoughtworks practitioner arguing for technical depth behind [[AgileSoftwareDevelopment]].

## Current Profile
In the foreword source, Fowler writes from the vantage point of someone involved in the original Agile movement and later practice at [[Thoughtworks]]. His contribution is not a neutral history of agile adoption; it is a correction of shallow mainstream "agile" that keeps ceremonies while losing the technical and collaborative practices that made the movement valuable.

Fowler presents [[ExtremeProgramming]] as a durable foundation for real agile work and uses [[JamesShore]]'s book as a guide to the skills teams need: value focus, customer collaboration, adaptive planning, testing, refactoring, design, DevOps culture, and [[ContinuousDelivery]].

## Key Characteristics
- Signer and early participant in the Agile movement, according to the source.
- Critiques mainstream "agile" when it lacks the original movement's practice depth.
- Treats management practices and technical execution as mutually reinforcing.
- Connects [[ExtremeProgramming]], [[InternalSoftwareQuality]], DevOps, and [[ContinuousDelivery]] to product learning.
- Writes from a [[Thoughtworks]] practice context where XP techniques were applied for client software work.

- Adoption critique: [[blog-martin-fowler-foreword-to-the-art-of-agile-development]] says many teams call themselves agile while their actions diverge from the original vision.
