## Overview
[[AlexisMadrigal]] is represented by a 2016 Fusion essay interpreting [[Snapchat]] Lenses as an early mass-market form of [[AugmentedReality]] and the camera as an interface for social self-presentation.

## Current Profile
Madrigal connects company positioning, product mechanics, acquisition history, and older AR forecasts into a cultural argument: when pictures function as messages, processing the picture becomes part of constructing the transmitted self. His essay is strongest as a reframing of the camera and AR, while its predictions about identity, network ownership, and future usage remain unmeasured.

## Key Characteristics
- Analyzes technology products through their communication and cultural effects.
- Treats camera processing as an interaction model rather than only an imaging improvement.
- Contrasts observed consumer behavior with inherited technical visions of augmented reality.

## Evidence
- Camera-interface thesis: [[forget-drones-and-spaceships-the-snapchat-dogface-filter-is-the-future]] argues that picture-making and processing can generate messages and social connections.
- AR reframing: [[forget-drones-and-spaceships-the-snapchat-dogface-filter-is-the-future]] contrasts Snapchat's face editing with older data-glasses and spatial-annotation models.
- Identity interpretation: [[forget-drones-and-spaceships-the-snapchat-dogface-filter-is-the-future]] presents filters as tools for mood, narrative, and chosen self-presentation.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one interpretive 2016 essay. It does not establish Madrigal's wider career or current views, and the source provides no direct user research or causal product metrics for its cultural and strategic claims.
