## Overview
[[Bird]] is presented as the electric scooter service that pioneered scooters-as-a-service and became a high-growth Los Angeles startup after raising $300 million less than 18 months after formation.

## Current Profile
The source frames Bird as a category-creating [[Micromobility]] company whose early growth was visible in dense Santa Monica street usage before it was legible in financial headlines. Its service lets riders find a nearby scooter in an app, scan a QR code, pay a start fee and per-minute rate, and use the scooter for short urban trips. Suster's bullish view is that Bird's advantage is not a single network effect but a bundle of capital, fleet density, hardware design, data, charger and mechanic networks, battery learning, brand salience, and focused execution.

## Key Characteristics
- Electric scooter service built around app-based discovery, QR-code unlocking, and per-minute rental.
- Associated by the source with unusually rapid consumer demand and revenue growth without paid marketing.
- Depends on dense local scooter availability for convenience.
- Uses local chargers and mechanics as part of the operating model.
- Treats scooters as connected vehicles that generate route, placement, and city-planning data.
- Faces safety, helmet, weather, supply, and competitive constraints.
- Benefits from being early enough that the brand became strongly associated with the category.

## Evidence
- Usage and demand: [[all-the-questions-you-wanted-answered-about-bird-scooters-and-their-recent-300-million-funding]] says Suster watched Bird usage grow from skepticism to repeated scooter traffic near his office and describes zero paid marketing.
- Funding and scale: [[all-the-questions-you-wanted-answered-about-bird-scooters-and-their-recent-300-million-funding]] says Bird announced a $300 million raise less than 18 months after creation and had earlier raised $125 million quickly.
- Service mechanics: [[all-the-questions-you-wanted-answered-about-bird-scooters-and-their-recent-300-million-funding]] describes app discovery, QR-code rental, driver's-license and credit-card setup, and then $1 plus 15 cents per minute pricing.
- Operating network: [[all-the-questions-you-wanted-answered-about-bird-scooters-and-their-recent-300-million-funding]] describes independent local chargers and local mechanics handling charging, placement, and repairs.
- Safety posture: [[all-the-questions-you-wanted-answered-about-bird-scooters-and-their-recent-300-million-funding]] says Bird reminds riders to wear helmets, had distributed more than 30,000 free helmets, and limited scooters to 15 mph.
- Image evidence: [[all-the-questions-you-wanted-answered-about-bird-scooters-and-their-recent-300-million-funding]] includes inspected duplicate investor photos showing Mark Suster and another Upfront attendee standing on branded Bird scooters by a Santa Monica beach setting, while the final inspected street photo shows many dockless Bird scooters parked near a bike path.

## Qualifications
The source is written by an investor whose firm backed Bird, so its valuation and defensibility claims are advocacy rather than neutral measurement. It does not provide audited revenue, utilization, accident, retention, unit-economics, vandalism, regulation, or fleet-lifetime data. The article also acknowledges strong competition, likely including [[Uber]], and notes that weather and seasonality can limit scooter usefulness in some markets.
