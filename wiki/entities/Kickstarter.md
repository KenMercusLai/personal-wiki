## Overview
[[Kickstarter]] is a rewards-crowdfunding platform used in the source as an example of community-embedded marketplace growth.

## Current Profile
The article says Kickstarter gained early traction by being close to the New York creative community. Creators needed funding for projects, and people in the same creative ecosystem were willing to support them, allowing successful examples to snowball platform adoption.

## Key Characteristics
- Rewards-crowdfunding platform.
- Grew through proximity to creative communities.
- Serves both project creators and backers.
- Also functions as a market-validation channel for new products.

## Evidence
- Community proximity: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says Kickstarter was embedded in New York's creative community.
- Snowball effect: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says use of the platform snowballed as successful fundraising became visible.
- Product validation: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] uses Ministry of Supply's campaign as an example of crowdfunding testing demand and acquiring customers.
