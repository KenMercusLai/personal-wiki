## Overview
[[Kickstarter]] is a rewards-crowdfunding platform represented through community-embedded growth, early creator support and curation, mission-versus-use governance, and a reported unlimited-vacation reversal.

## Current Profile
The growth article says Kickstarter gained early traction by being close to New York's creative community, where creators needed funding and people in the same ecosystem were willing to back them. [[CassieMarketos]] supplies a firsthand operating account: the core goal, deadline, and all-or-nothing mechanics were present early, while a small team personally supported and selected initial projects whose examples later creators copied. Editorial choices emphasized compelling stories and attainable creative work, and peer advice helped norms scale beyond staff instruction.

The same interview makes platform authority unavoidable. Overfunded projects and delivery problems led to creator resources and reward caps, while hardware growth created tension between user-led evolution and the original artistic mission. The vacation essay separately adds an internal-policy case, citing Kickstarter's decision to leave an unlimited-vacation model as evidence that attractive benefit language can leave employees unsure how much leave is acceptable.

## Key Characteristics
- Rewards-crowdfunding platform built around a goal, timeframe, and all-or-nothing funding mechanic.
- Grew through proximity to creative communities and close support of early creators.
- Used exemplary projects, storytelling-oriented curation, and peer exchange to establish community norms.
- Serves creators and backers while retaining product and policy responsibility for transparency and delivery risk.
- Faced tension between an artistic mission and user demand for hardware and product launches.
- Also functions as a market-validation channel for new products.
- Is cited as having discontinued unlimited vacation.

- Snowball effect: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says use of the platform snowballed as successful fundraising became visible.
- Product validation: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] uses Ministry of Supply's campaign as an example of crowdfunding testing demand and acquiring customers.
- Early operating model: [[employee-1-kickstarter]] says the core mechanics were present before richer creator tools, while project admission and support were initially personal and informal.
- Norm formation: [[employee-1-kickstarter]] says intensive work with early creators produced example projects that later creators copied and supplemented with peer advice, blogs, and workshops.
- Curation: [[employee-1-kickstarter]] says newsletter choices prioritized compelling storytelling and represented both modest projects and large successes.
- Governance: [[employee-1-kickstarter]] connects overfunding and delivery trouble to creator resources and reward caps, and hardware growth to debate about protecting the platform's artistic purpose.
- Vacation-policy reversal: [[unlimited-vacation-and-other-forms-of-guilt-based-management]] cites Kickstarter as having walked away from unlimited vacation.

## Qualifications
Marketos provides a valuable early insider account but not a complete institutional history. The sources do not measure whether early curation caused quality or growth, include rejected creators and unsuccessful backers, or fully analyze fraud, fees, category policy, delivery outcomes, inclusion, later governance, or the distribution of editorial power. The vacation essay reports the policy reversal but provides no internal usage figures, employee perspectives, replacement-policy detail, or causal outcome evidence.
