## Overview
[[Kickstarter]] is a rewards-crowdfunding platform used in the sources as a community-embedded marketplace growth case and as a reported unlimited-vacation reversal.

## Current Profile
The growth article says Kickstarter gained early traction by being close to the New York creative community, where creators needed funding and people in the same ecosystem were willing to back them. The vacation essay adds an internal-policy case, citing Kickstarter's decision to move away from unlimited vacation as evidence that attractive benefit language can leave employees unsure how much leave is acceptable.

## Key Characteristics
- Rewards-crowdfunding platform.
- Grew through proximity to creative communities.
- Serves both project creators and backers.
- Also functions as a market-validation channel for new products.
- Cited as having discontinued unlimited vacation.

## Evidence
- Community proximity: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says Kickstarter was embedded in New York's creative community.
- Snowball effect: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] says use of the platform snowballed as successful fundraising became visible.
- Product validation: [[51-examples-of-growth-hacking-strategies-techniques-from-the-worlds-most-innovative-businesses]] uses Ministry of Supply's campaign as an example of crowdfunding testing demand and acquiring customers.
- Vacation-policy reversal: [[unlimited-vacation-and-other-forms-of-guilt-based-management]] cites Kickstarter as having walked away from unlimited vacation.

## Qualifications
The sources do not analyze Kickstarter's trust and fraud controls, fee model, category policy, or later marketplace governance. The vacation essay reports the policy reversal but provides no internal usage figures, employee perspectives, replacement-policy detail, or causal outcome evidence.
