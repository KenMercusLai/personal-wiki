## Overview
[[TonyStubblebine]] is a productivity writer and coach represented in the wiki by his 2017 article introducing [[InterstitialJournaling]].

## Current Profile
The current source presents Stubblebine as a practitioner who combines existing productivity and reflection ideas into testable work routines. He derives interstitial journaling from Pomodoro breaks, journaling influences, and action decomposition, then uses his own workday and feedback from a 500-person coaching group to refine the method. His emphasis is on making project transitions observable and repeatable rather than relying on willpower to “switch fast.”

## Key Characteristics
- Develops productivity tactics through personal experimentation and coaching-group feedback.
- Treats journaling as an execution and attention-management tool, not only a retrospective diary practice.
- Prefers literal, low-friction starting actions while acknowledging that different people need different motivational granularity.

## Evidence
- Practice development: [[replace-your-to-do-list-with-interstitial-journaling-to-increase-productivity]] traces the method to journaling during Pomodoro breaks and describes testing the wording of its action prompt with a coaching group.
- Execution journal: [[replace-your-to-do-list-with-interstitial-journaling-to-increase-productivity]] asks workers to reflect at every project transition and, optionally, journal throughout the work itself.
- Adaptation to the person: [[replace-your-to-do-list-with-interstitial-journaling-to-increase-productivity]] reports that “First Action” helped some participants choose a literal opening move while others found tiny actions too boring.

## Qualifications
The wiki currently represents Stubblebine through one practitioner essay. Its anecdotes and informal coaching-group feedback explain how he developed the method but do not establish comparative productivity effects, durable habit adoption, or general applicability across occupations.
