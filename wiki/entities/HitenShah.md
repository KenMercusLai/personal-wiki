## Overview
[[HitenShah]] is a product and SaaS practitioner whose Product Habits essays connect growth strategy to founder operating experience.

## Current Profile
The sources present Shah as a product commentator and founder-practitioner. One essay argues that competition, low switching costs, and cheap distribution make retention harder than acquisition; another uses Facebook's response to Snapchat and Shah's KISSmetrics retrospective to argue that customer research must be paired with competitor awareness and fast execution. The feature-creep essay adds product-scope and organization design: breadth is acceptable when it serves segments through one promise, while disconnected work often reflects weak value execution or misaligned incentives. Together they frame durable growth as a repeated obligation to define, deliver, and retain customer value rather than a reward secured by acquisition volume, simplicity, or early innovation.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes about product and SaaS growth strategy.
- Uses both external company cases and his own operating history to extract product lessons.
- Frames acquisition as less defensible when customers can switch easily and competitors can reproduce tactics.
- Emphasizes product strength, customer usefulness, competitive learning, and execution over raw lead volume or originality claims.
- Treats product breadth as conditional on a coherent promise and relevant segment outcomes rather than feature count.
- Uses the failed Vision Web Hosting project to argue against committee-driven development under conflicting incentives.

## Qualifications
The page is limited to three Product Habits essays and does not cover Shah's broader company history, investments, or writing. The KISSmetrics and Vision Web Hosting accounts are retrospective and do not prove that copying the identified competitor moves or imposing a different decision structure would have changed either outcome. The feature-creep argument relies on selected historical comparisons without feature-use, retention, delivery, or organizational data that isolate its proposed causes.

## What Changed
- Added Shah's competition-aware product strategy and KISSmetrics founder retrospective.
- Reframed retention and competitive response as complementary forms of repeated customer-value delivery.
- Added Shah's distinction between coherent product breadth and feature accumulation caused by weak value execution or misaligned incentives.
