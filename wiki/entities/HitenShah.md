## Overview
[[HitenShah]] is a product and SaaS practitioner whose Product Habits essays connect growth strategy to founder operating experience.

## Current Profile
The sources present Shah as a product commentator and founder-practitioner. One essay argues that competition, low switching costs, and cheap distribution make retention harder than acquisition; the other uses Facebook's response to Snapchat and Shah's KISSmetrics retrospective to argue that customer research must be paired with competitor awareness and fast execution. Together they frame durable growth as a repeated obligation to keep delivering customer value, not a reward permanently secured by early innovation or acquisition volume.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes about product and SaaS growth strategy.
- Uses both external company cases and his own operating history to extract product lessons.
- Frames acquisition as less defensible when customers can switch easily and competitors can reproduce tactics.
- Emphasizes product strength, customer usefulness, competitive learning, and execution over raw lead volume or originality claims.

## Evidence
- Retention argument: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] says founders should improve product and customer experience rather than rely on visible acquisition strategies.
- SaaSFest synthesis: [[acquisition-is-easy-retention-is-hard-product-habits]] uses Price Intelligently's SaaSFest points to frame acquisition as less defensible.
- Competition argument: [[facebooks-shameless-copying-of-snapchat-and-what-it-means-for-your-product-strategy-product-habits]] defends selective imitation when it closes a strategically important customer gap.

## Qualifications
The page is limited to two Product Habits essays and does not cover Shah's broader company history, investments, or writing. The KISSmetrics account is retrospective and does not prove that copying the identified competitor moves would have changed the company's outcome.

## What Changed
- Added Shah's competition-aware product strategy and KISSmetrics founder retrospective.
- Reframed retention and competitive response as complementary forms of repeated customer-value delivery.
