## Overview
[[JohnGreathouse]] is represented as a venture capitalist at Rincon Venture Partners who publicly analyzes why he declined to investigate [[Uber]]'s seed round and later rejected its Series A.

## Current Profile
Greathouse's account is an investment-decision retrospective rather than a broad biography. He describes using firm thesis fit, city-by-city market potential, regulation, marketplace liquidity, and valuation as rejection filters, then concludes that he relied too heavily on a trusted friend's secondhand framing. His revised practice is to distinguish taking a meeting from making an investment: repeated referrals from someone with demonstrated business judgment should earn direct diligence even when the initial description appears outside the fund's model.

## Key Characteristics
- Publicly documents a costly missed investment rather than presenting only portfolio successes.
- Initially favored software-sales models over businesses that directly fulfill real-world services.
- Underestimated Uber's marketplace formation, taxi-market expansion, and consumer-backed regulatory strategy.
- Treats direct founder access and trusted referrals as information-quality inputs, not automatic investment endorsements.
- Retains portfolio-loss discipline despite emphasizing the value of investigating unusual upside.

## Evidence
- Missed opportunity: [[confessions-of-a-vc-crap-why-i-passed-on-ubers-seed-round-john-greathouse]] says Greathouse repeatedly declined the chance to hear the seed pitch and later passed on the Series A.
- Original filters: [[confessions-of-a-vc-crap-why-i-passed-on-ubers-seed-round-john-greathouse]] records his concerns about thesis fit, jurisdiction-specific regulation, limited suitable cities, two-sided liquidity, and valuation.
- Revised judgment: [[confessions-of-a-vc-crap-why-i-passed-on-ubers-seed-round-john-greathouse]] says he should have honored a trusted serial entrepreneur's repeated recommendation by learning directly from the founder.
- Risk boundary: [[confessions-of-a-vc-crap-why-i-passed-on-ubers-seed-round-john-greathouse]] explicitly says failed investments that do not return capital hurt a fund more acutely than wistful regret over a missed winner.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one self-authored 2016 retrospective. Its counterfactual return, recollected breakfast conversation, and interpretation of the original decision are shaped by hindsight and are not independently verified here. It does not summarize Greathouse's broader operating, teaching, investment, or writing record.
