## Overview
[[ChrisBailey]] is a productivity author represented in the wiki by an article on how long knowledge-work breaks should be.

## Current Profile
The ingested article presents Bailey as a productivity writer whose advice emphasizes energy, attention, and recovery rather than continuous time-on-task. His break-cadence guidance combines a broad recommendation to take more breaks with several concrete rhythms: 90-minute morning work blocks, Pomodoro-style afternoon intervals, and an approximately hourly break heuristic.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames productivity around accomplishment, focus, and energy rather than raw busyness.
- Treats breaks as active recovery that can restore attention and support creative connection-making.
- Adapts break timing to daily energy rhythms instead of prescribing one fixed cadence for all work.

## Evidence
- Productivity frame: [[chris-bailey-exactly-how-long-your-work-breaks-should-be]] argues that productivity is about what is accomplished, not how much activity is performed.
- Recovery role: [[chris-bailey-exactly-how-long-your-work-breaks-should-be]] says breaks replenish energy, recharge focus, support creative connections, and create space to reconsider goals or approach.
- Cadence advice: [[chris-bailey-exactly-how-long-your-work-breaks-should-be]] recommends 90-minute morning blocks, Pomodoro cycles later in the day, and about 15 minutes of break time per hour as a starting point.

## Qualifications
The current wiki evidence for Bailey comes from one short article. It records his productivity advice but does not establish the full scope of his work, compare his claims with sleep or attention research directly, or independently validate the studies cited in the article.
