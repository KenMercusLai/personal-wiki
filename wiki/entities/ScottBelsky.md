## Overview
[[ScottBelsky]] is a product and business author represented in the wiki by practitioner frameworks for newcomer product experience and low-overhead micro-brand formation.

## Current Profile
Belsky's product-design argument treats welcome flows, defaults, empty states, copy, and initial actions as a [[FirstMileProductExperience]] that should move newcomers into an oriented state before teaching the whole product. He favors proactive assistance, immediate utility, familiar patterns, and continued redesign as new-user cohorts change.

His commerce argument interprets Instagram discovery, targeted advertising, modular services, small-batch manufacturing, and design as a system that lowers the cost of testing consumer brands. He discloses an investment in packaging supplier Lumi and prior angel investments in several larger modern brands, so that source combines operating observation with an investor's ecosystem perspective.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames newcomer orientation as a product-growth responsibility rather than launch-time polish.
- Prefers proactive setup, immediate utility, strong defaults, and familiar patterns to explanation-heavy onboarding.
- Treats changing adopter cohorts as a reason to keep redesigning the first mile after launch.
- Frames consumer-brand competition as aggregate pressure from a long tail of small operators.
- Examines design, social targeting, supply chains, commerce software, packaging, and fulfillment as complementary infrastructure.
- Questions whether venture capital is necessary for brands that can validate demand with little inventory exposure.

## Evidence
- First-use framework: [[crafting-the-first-mile-of-product-positive-slope-medium]] defines "The Zone" through purpose, achievable value, and a clear next action.
- Product mechanisms: [[crafting-the-first-mile-of-product-positive-slope-medium]] recommends templates, defaults, useful empty states, immediate utility or novelty, visible feedback, and familiar terminology.
- Cohort adaptation: [[crafting-the-first-mile-of-product-positive-slope-medium]] argues that mobile context, demographics, countries, and technical fluency change who counts as a new user.
- Market thesis: [[attack-of-the-micro-brands-positive-slope-medium]] argues that thousands of small brands can collectively pressure incumbents.
- Operating ecosystem: [[attack-of-the-micro-brands-positive-slope-medium]] combines targeted advertising, small-batch production, designers, Shopify, packaging suppliers, and fulfillment providers.
- Investor context: [[attack-of-the-micro-brands-positive-slope-medium]] discloses Belsky's Lumi investment and angel investments in modern consumer brands.

## Qualifications
The two sources are opinionated practitioner essays, not controlled product research or comprehensive accounts of Belsky's career. The first-mile examples are selected and historically situated, its characterization of user motives is reductive, and its 30% resource-allocation recommendation is unmeasured. The commerce source's revenue examples are unnamed and second-hand, its market claims lack longitudinal share or profitability data, and investment disclosures may shape which ecosystem participants it emphasizes.

## What Changed
- Expanded the profile from micro-brand commerce into Belsky's first-mile product-design framework.
- Added his emphasis on proactive assistance, immediate value, familiar patterns, and changing newcomer cohorts.
