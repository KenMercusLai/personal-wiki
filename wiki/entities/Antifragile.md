## Overview
[[Antifragile]] is the Nassim Nicholas Taleb book cited by the source to describe things that benefit from stress, disorder, volatility, and turmoil.

## Current Profile
Within this wiki, the book appears as a conceptual reference rather than a reviewed work. Maioli uses its antifragility idea to describe a programmer who becomes stronger in poorly documented, chaotic, or unfamiliar technical environments instead of depending entirely on stable tutorials and known framework recipes. [[AlbertWenger]] applies the same frame financially: avoid borrowing or contract-embedded leverage during a bubble, preserve segregated liquidity for the aftermath, and realize some gains during the rise so volatility does not end one's ability to participate.

## Key Characteristics
- Supplies the "antifragile" frame used in the source's programmer-learning argument.
- Contrasts with fragile reliance on exact examples, familiar tools, or readily searchable answers.
- Supports the source's claim that disorder can be useful when it triggers deeper system exploration.
- In speculative markets, implies structuring exposure so violent price movement does not force ruin or prevent post-crash action.
- Connects resilience to slack and optionality through dry powder, segregated funds, and partial profit-taking rather than prediction alone.

## Qualifications
Both sources use the book's idea as an analogy or practical frame, not as a detailed treatment of Taleb's broader philosophy. The wiki should not infer the book's full argument from these citations. Wenger's measures can reduce exposure to ruin but do not make a volatile investment beneficial, identify bubble timing, or address every market, custody, counterparty, and regulatory risk.
