## Overview
[[Recode]] is presented as the independent technology-news website and conference business launched by [[KaraSwisher]] and [[WaltMossberg]] after leaving Dow Jones and [[AllThingsD]].

## Current Profile
In the 2014 profile, Re/code was a new attempt to preserve the partners' publication-and-live-journalism model outside a large newspaper company. They rejected venture-capital funding in favor of media partners, published extensive conflict disclosures, and carried forward Swisher's deeply sourced reporting. The same design retained difficult independence questions because the conference needed attendance by covered executives, a Pinterest executive sat on the board, one backer was a former Yahoo chief Swisher had attacked, and Swisher's then-wife worked at Google.

## Key Characteristics
- Combined technology reporting with a high-profile executive conference.
- Continued the editorial partnership between Swisher and Mossberg.
- Used media-company backing rather than venture-capital financing.
- Published unusually extensive conflict and recusal disclosures.
- Depended on participation by powerful people and companies it covered.

## Evidence
- Launch and continuity: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] describes the partners leaving Dow Jones and launching the website and conference in January 2014.
- Funding boundary: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] says they rejected venture money and accepted backing from Terry Semel and NBCUniversal News Group.
- Disclosure practice: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] reports extensive disclosures and Swisher's recusal from personal Google coverage.
- Remaining conflicts: [[kara-swisher-is-silicon-valleys-most-feared-and-well-liked-journalist-how-does-that-work]] identifies conference dependence, board ties, backer history, and family relationships as continuing appearance or coverage constraints.

## Qualifications
The profile captures Re/code near launch in 2014, not its later ownership, editorial record, conference evolution, or ultimate outcome. It identifies incentives and perceived constraints but does not demonstrate that a specific story was suppressed or altered.
