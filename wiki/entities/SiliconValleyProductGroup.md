## Overview
[[SiliconValleyProductGroup]] is the product advisory and publishing context for [[MartyCagan]]'s article on the product-management role.

## Current Profile
In the sources, SVPG is introduced through Cagan's founding story after leaving eBay and through his effort to clarify product management for technology teams. The articles present SVPG less as a company profile than as the home for a product philosophy: great products require someone doing integrative, outcome-accountable product work, whether or not their title is formally product manager.

SVPG's product doctrine in these sources is simultaneously ambitious and anti-hierarchical. The "CEO of the product" metaphor is accepted as a way to communicate business breadth, but only if product managers understand that it gives them no boss authority and must be paired with humility, evidence, and earned team trust.

## Key Characteristics
- Publishes and teaches product-management ideas for technology product teams.
- Founded by Marty Cagan after his eBay experience.
- Frames product management as essential and still widely misunderstood.
- Uses real product case studies to explain the product-manager contribution.
- Treats title as less important than the product work being performed.
- Defends CEO-like product accountability while rejecting boss-like PM behavior.

## Evidence
- Founding context: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says Cagan started SVPG after leaving eBay.
- Role-clarification mission: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says one of Cagan's first SVPG acts was writing an essay on product management.
- Case-study method: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] introduces six product people behind iconic products to show the third, strong way of working.
- Title-agnostic role: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says the work may be done by a PM, co-founder, CEO, or other teammate who steps up.
- CEO metaphor boundary: [[ceo-of-the-product-revisited-silicon-valley-product-group]] says the PM role is like a CEO in business breadth and decision responsibility but not in formal authority.
- Training emphasis: [[ceo-of-the-product-revisited-silicon-valley-product-group]] says many product managers have little real training, making the fuller role scope worth teaching directly.

## Qualifications
The sources are self-authored by SVPG's founder and should be read as product-management doctrine and case-study storytelling rather than an independent profile of SVPG as an organization.
