## Overview
[[PaulAppelbaum]] is a psychiatrist, Columbia professor, and medical ethics and law expert cited for the central ethical and legal framing of patient-targeted online search.

## Current Profile
The source presents Appelbaum as the main interpreter of why patient Googling is both useful and risky. He argues that online information can be valuable collateral data in acute psychiatric situations, but he also warns that frequent searching can shape the [[StandardOfCare]], create malpractice expectations, and produce durable chart content when online material is copied into medical records. His view is not categorically anti-search; it asks clinical professions to decide how they will use the new ability before ad hoc practice hardens into obligation.

## Key Characteristics
- Psychiatrist and Columbia professor in the source's description.
- Expert in medical ethics and law.
- Co-authored work on patient digital footprints in mental healthcare.
- Frames online search as useful collateral data in acute care.
- Warns that common search practice can shift standard-of-care and liability expectations.
- Emphasizes the permanence and third-party reach of medical records.
