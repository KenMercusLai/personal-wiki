## Overview
[[AndrewHarmelLaw]] is the author of a practitioner account that reframes the software architect from primary decision-maker to designer and steward of distributed architectural conversations.

## Current Profile
In the source, Harmel-Law advocates [[DecentralizedArchitectureGovernance]] for continuously delivering autonomous teams. His approach gives decision ownership to the person or team with the need, while requiring consultation with affected people and experts through the [[AdviceProcess]].

His proposed architect role is active but non-commanding: maintain the conditions for timely, inclusive conversations; help teams learn through [[ArchitectureDecisionRecords]] and an [[ArchitectureAdvisoryForum]]; and support shared direction through principles and a [[TechnologyRadar]]. He treats trust, participation, learning from small failures, and resistance to shadow architecture as prerequisites rather than cultural extras.

## Key Characteristics
- Advocates distributing architectural decision authority to autonomous teams.
- Treats conversation and recorded advice as the main mechanisms for shared architectural understanding.
- Reframes architects as facilitators, advisors, and stewards of decision conditions rather than default approvers.
- Connects ADRs, advisory forums, principles, and technology radars into one mutually reinforcing practice.
- Explicitly warns that exclusion and covert central control can destroy the approach.

## Evidence
- Decision stance: [[blog-andrew-harmel-law-martinfowler-com-scaling-the-practice-of-architecture-conversationally]] says he stopped taking architectural decisions and instead enabled others to make them through the Advice Process.
- Practice system: [[blog-andrew-harmel-law-martinfowler-com-scaling-the-practice-of-architecture-conversationally]] presents one core and four supporting elements for conversational architecture governance.
- Architect responsibility: [[blog-andrew-harmel-law-martinfowler-com-scaling-the-practice-of-architecture-conversationally]] defines the architect's task as getting the right conversations to happen with the right people at the right time.
- Failure awareness: [[blog-andrew-harmel-law-martinfowler-com-scaling-the-practice-of-architecture-conversationally]] identifies narrow participation, punitive responses, mistrust, and shadow architecture as failure modes.

## Qualifications
This profile reflects one article and the author's own reported client experience. It does not establish how widely the model has been adopted, compare it experimentally with architecture review boards, or provide independent outcome measurements. The source also leaves technical-strategy and cross-functional-requirement design outside its scope.
