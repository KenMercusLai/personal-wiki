## Overview
[[TerribleSoftware]] is the publication and attributed author of the wiki source "What Actually Makes You Senior."

## Current Profile
Within the current wiki evidence, Terrible Software is represented by a concise practitioner essay about software-engineering seniority. Its contribution is a deliberately reductive thesis: the senior engineer's foundational skill is turning fuzzy product or technical requests into clear, lower-risk work before implementation begins.

## Key Characteristics
- Publishes practitioner-oriented software-engineering commentary.
- Frames seniority through observable behavior on ambiguous work rather than tenure, title, or technology checklists.
- Critiques hiring processes that test algorithm performance without testing problem clarification and planning.

## Evidence
- Publication context: [[terrible-software-what-actually-makes-you-senior]] is archived with Terrible Software as its author and source site.
- Seniority thesis: [[terrible-software-what-actually-makes-you-senior]] treats ambiguity reduction as the foundation beneath architecture, communication, ownership, and leadership.
- Hiring critique: [[terrible-software-what-actually-makes-you-senior]] contrasts technology and LeetCode screening with the ability to turn incomplete requirements into shippable plans.

## Qualifications
The wiki currently contains only one Terrible Software source. This page therefore does not establish the publication's ownership, editorial process, broader catalog, or whether the essay's position represents every contributor. The article's claims are practitioner judgments and are not supported by comparative outcome data.
