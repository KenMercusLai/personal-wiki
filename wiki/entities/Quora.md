## Overview
[[Quora]] appears in the corpus as the company whose 10-year employee stock-option exercise periods made it an early mover on extended exercise windows, and as an entrant that started in question-and-answer when [[Yahoo]] already had an audience there.

## Current Profile
The equity source presents Quora as an early private-company mover on extended option exercise periods. Its compensation philosophy is described as market-based: new grants are set by what a role requires in the current talent market, while option-pool expansion is treated as rational when a hire's expected contribution exceeds compensation cost. The competition source uses the company from a different angle, listing Quora among the entrants that started anyway, in its case against a Yahoo question-and-answer product that was already used by many people. That example is counterfactual rather than evaluative: the answer argues only that the funded or established competitor did not stop the company from starting, and it says nothing about how Quora competed or how the category resolved.

## Key Characteristics
- Adopted 10-year stock-option exercise periods before many startups publicly followed.
- Uses market-based compensation as the stated grant-setting framework.
- Treats former employees retaining vested equity as expected dilution rather than a direct wealth transfer.
- Frames employee equity policy as a stakeholder issue involving current employees, former employees, founders, and investors.
- Presents transparency around equity terms as part of fair recruiting.
- Doubles as an entry example, having started a question-and-answer product where a Yahoo version already had many users.

## Evidence
- Early adoption: [[10-year-exercise-periods-make-sense-thoughts-quora]] says Quora switched to 10-year exercise periods roughly two years before the article and influenced later companies.
- Market-based grants: [[10-year-exercise-periods-make-sense-thoughts-quora]] says Quora estimates typical market compensation for each role and grants accordingly.
- Dilution framing: [[10-year-exercise-periods-make-sense-thoughts-quora]] says former employees retaining options means expected dilution rather than lower grants for future employees.
- Stakeholder claim: [[10-year-exercise-periods-make-sense-thoughts-quora]] explicitly argues the policy serves current employees, former employees, founders, and investors.
- Entry against an incumbent: [[yash-gandhi-startup-idea-well-funded-competitor-quora]] cites Quora as a company that started in question-and-answer despite a widely used Yahoo product in the same category.

## Qualifications
The equity source gives Quora's internal reasoning but not cap-table data, employee survey evidence, or investor-side financial modeling. Claims about ecosystem influence are asserted from the author's vantage point. The competition example is a single short Quora answer that names the company as an entrant without describing its strategy, its funding, or the category's outcome.
