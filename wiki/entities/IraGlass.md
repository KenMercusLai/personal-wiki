## Overview
[[IraGlass]] is a radio producer represented here through his account of the gap between a creator's developed taste and the lower quality of their early work.

## Current Profile
Glass argues that disappointment in early creative output is normal because taste can recognize shortcomings before skill can reliably correct them. His remedy is sustained volume under recurring deadlines: finishing work every week or month creates the repetitions through which ability can approach ambition. Within [[JessicaAbel]]'s essay, this account explains how creators can repay [[IdeaDebt]] by making rather than further idealizing the result.

## Key Characteristics
- Distinguishes critical taste from current production ability.
- Normalizes disappointment during early creative development.
- Recommends high-volume work on recurring deadlines.
- Treats accumulated practice as the route toward matching ambition.

## Evidence
- Taste-skill gap: [[imagining-your-future-projects-is-holding-you-back]] quotes Glass describing creators who can recognize that their early work falls short.
- Attrition risk: [[imagining-your-future-projects-is-holding-you-back]] attributes quitting during this gap to many developing creators.
- Practice remedy: [[imagining-your-future-projects-is-holding-you-back]] relays Glass's recommendation to finish a large volume of work on weekly or monthly deadlines.

## Qualifications
The available profile is limited to an excerpt reproduced in Abel's essay. The claim is practitioner guidance rather than causal evidence, and volume alone may not improve work without feedback, reflection, instruction, or appropriately chosen practice.
