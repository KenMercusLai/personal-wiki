## Overview
[[Shyp]] appears as a mobile shipping service used to illustrate how a focused physical-world product might be rebuilt as a platform-hosted micro app.

## Current Profile
The source describes Shyp's user-facing flow as photographing an item, sharing a location, supplying payment, arranging courier pickup, contacting support, and tracking delivery. [[AaronBatalion]] argues that a platform such as [[FacebookMessenger]] could provide many of those surrounding capabilities, leaving Shyp to concentrate on logistics and fulfillment.

## Key Characteristics
- Shipping service centered on courier pickup and delivery rather than user-managed packing and dispatch.
- Mobile flow combines camera input, location, payment, courier communication, and shipment tracking.
- Used as a decomposition example for separating commodity platform capabilities from a startup's core service.
- Investment example in an article written by a disclosed early angel investor.

## Evidence
- Service flow: [[aaron-batalion-bot-is-the-wrong-name]] describes users photographing an item, identifying their location, paying, and reaching support.
- Inspected UI: [[aaron-batalion-bot-is-the-wrong-name]] retains screens for item photography, courier approach and contact, and delivery tracking.
- Platform argument: [[aaron-batalion-bot-is-the-wrong-name]] claims these surrounding interactions could be supplied by Messenger while Shyp handled the physical service.
- Relationship context: [[aaron-batalion-bot-is-the-wrong-name]] discloses Batalion's early angel investment and permission from Shyp's CEO to use the example.

## Qualifications
The source is an investor-authored 2016 product thought experiment, not an operational or financial assessment of Shyp. It does not compare the proposed Messenger version with the native product or report adoption, conversion, retention, service quality, or unit economics.
