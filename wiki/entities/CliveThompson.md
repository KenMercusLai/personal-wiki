## Overview
[[CliveThompson]] is represented in the wiki as the author of a 2016 Mother Jones article about parking, ride-sharing, autonomous vehicles, and urban land use.

## Current Profile
Thompson's article treats transportation technology as a policy-dependent urban system rather than a vehicle feature. It connects parking supply and pricing to housing costs, traffic, emissions, public space, and equity, then presents shared autonomy and private autonomy as divergent scenarios whose outcomes depend on ownership, pricing, transit integration, and public access.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses parking to expose costs and land consumption hidden by ordinary car ownership.
- Connects technology adoption to urban design, housing, traffic, labor, and environmental consequences.
- Builds scenarios around shared fleets and private autonomous ownership rather than asserting one inevitable future.
- Emphasizes municipal policy as a mechanism that can redirect rebound effects toward civic value.

## Evidence
- Hidden infrastructure: [[no-parking-here-mother-jones]] combines national parking estimates, housing-cost studies, cruising emissions, and downtown land-use examples.
- Scenario analysis: [[no-parking-here-mother-jones]] contrasts shared autonomous fleets with privately owned vehicles that travel empty or enable longer commutes.
- Policy orientation: [[no-parking-here-mother-jones]] discusses parking minimums, dynamic curb prices, congestion charges, transit integration, accessibility, and platform data sharing.

## Qualifications
This profile rests on one long-form article and characterizes Thompson's argument in that source, not his full career or current views. The technology and market claims are a 2016 snapshot, and several projected autonomous-vehicle outcomes had not been observed at publication.
