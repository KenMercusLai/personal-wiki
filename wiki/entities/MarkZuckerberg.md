## Overview
[[MarkZuckerberg]] appears as both the Harvard dorm founder example for learning by building before a perfect plan exists and, in a 2016 strategic account, the leader of Facebook's response to declining personal sharing through live video, acquisitions, virtual reality, messaging, and AI assistants.

## Current Profile
The MaiYang source uses Zuckerberg's creation of Thefacebook as an illustration of immediate action: he saw a campus-connection need, implemented the first product quickly, and used scaling and feature problems as live learning material. This is a motivational origin story rather than a full historical account.

The 2016 Facebook article adds later product and portfolio strategy. It associates Zuckerberg with an effort to make live video feel raw and spontaneous, the acquisitions of Instagram, WhatsApp, and Oculus, a claim that virtual reality might become a highly social platform, and Messenger's expansion through chatbots and the M assistant. The article interprets these moves as preparation for a world in which the main Facebook feed became less interpersonal and more media-oriented.

## Key Characteristics
- Represents fast implementation before a complete business plan.
- Is tied to Facebook's early campus-connection need.
- Serves as an example of learning from real product problems after launch.
- Promoted live video as a route back toward spontaneous sharing in the 2016 account.
- Is associated with a portfolio of bets across photo sharing, messaging, virtual reality, chatbots, and virtual assistants.

## Evidence
- First implementation: [[blog-maiyang-naval-insights-taking-action-with-ai]] says Zuckerberg created Thefacebook in a Harvard dorm.
- Anti-perfection frame: [[blog-maiyang-naval-insights-taking-action-with-ai]] says he did not wait for a perfect business plan or patent strategy.
- Learning loop: [[blog-maiyang-naval-insights-taking-action-with-ai]] frames server scaling and feature iteration as immediate learning after the product became popular.
- Spontaneous-sharing response: [[facebook-isnt-the-social-network-anymore-so-what-is-it]] says Zuckerberg promoted live video as a way to recover a raw, visceral mode of connection.
- Portfolio bets: [[facebook-isnt-the-social-network-anymore-so-what-is-it]] links his leadership to the Instagram, WhatsApp, and Oculus acquisitions and to Messenger bots and the M assistant.
- Virtual-reality thesis: [[facebook-isnt-the-social-network-anymore-so-what-is-it]] reports Zuckerberg's claim that virtual reality could become a highly social platform, while the author questions that framing.

## Qualifications
The MaiYang source gives a motivational origin-story version of Zuckerberg and Facebook and does not establish contested historical details. The Facebook article is an outside 2016 interpretation of strategy rather than Zuckerberg's full rationale or evidence of later outcomes; it does not establish whether live video restored personal sharing, whether virtual reality became a social platform, or whether each acquisition and AI bet succeeded. Neither source provides a broader assessment of governance, harms, regulation, or leadership performance.

## What Changed
- Expanded the profile from an origin-story action example to a 2016 product and portfolio strategist.
- Added the source's distinction between attempts to revive spontaneous sharing and hedges beyond the main Facebook product.
