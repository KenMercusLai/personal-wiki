## Overview
[[WashingtonPost|The Washington Post]] is the journalism institution [[JeffBezos]] bought personally in 2013 and used as a setting for patient digital reinvention while leaving news coverage under editorial leadership.

## Current Profile
The 2016 profile describes a publication moving from print assumptions toward internet-native distribution and product development. Its experiments included aggregating and extending other reporting, PostEverything opinion publishing, a network able to assign work rapidly to 800 freelancers, faster pages, simpler subscription signup, and internally built Arc publishing tools. Bezos had no operational or editorial role but joined biweekly management calls and semiannual Seattle reviews, pressed product and technology leaders on customer experience, and funded hiring and experimentation without a next-quarter penalty. Monthly web visitors rose from 30.5 million in October 2013 to 73.4 million in February 2016, though the source disclosed no profitability figures.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats digital publishing as a different operating environment rather than an electronic copy of print.
- Combines serious reporting investment with aggregation, distributed contributors, and product experimentation.
- Separates owner involvement in technology and customer experience from editorial coverage decisions.
- Uses patient private capital to tolerate failed experiments and increase hiring.
- Frames institutional journalism as part of democratic accountability.

## Evidence
- Digital shift: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] quotes [[MartyBaron]] saying Bezos pushed recognition that the internet world differs from print.
- Product portfolio: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] describes PostEverything, the 800-person freelance talent network, subscription and performance work, and the internally developed Arc tools.
- Audience growth: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] reports monthly visitors growing from 30.5 million to 73.4 million between October 2013 and February 2016.
- Governance boundary: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] says Bezos joined regular management reviews but did not suggest coverage, according to both Bezos and Baron.
- Duty of care: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] reports that Bezos personally retrieved correspondent [[JasonRezaian]] after his release from Iranian detention.

## Qualifications
Fortune presents management testimony and traffic growth, not audited financial or editorial-quality outcomes. The Post released no financial data, the source suspected continued losses during investment, and the long-run sustainability of its experiments was unknown. Claims of editorial independence come from the owner and editor in a profile centered on Bezos.
