## Overview
[[DaveMcClure]] is an investor-author represented by contrarian advice on both VC portfolio construction and focused startup market entry.

## Current Profile
McClure's sources connect two levels of early-stage strategy. From [[500Startups]]' experience investing in more than 1,000 companies, he argues that funds should hold enough startup outcomes to have a realistic chance of capturing rare large winners. From the company side, he argues that a startup should initially do the opposite with its market attention: concentrate on a narrow customer segment whose problem, willingness to pay, and alternatives can be understood well enough for a rough but focused product to win an initial foothold. The common thread is stage-appropriate exposure—broad across a fund's independent bets, concentrated within each startup's earliest customer search.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses portfolio outcome data from 500 Startups as the practical basis for the argument.
- Critiques traditional VC concentration as risky when rare unicorn outcomes dominate returns.
- Recommends far larger early-stage portfolios than common industry practice.
- Treats board-seat norms as a possible cause of undersized portfolios.
- Recommends pairing narrow customer segmentation with product and message differentiation before broad market expansion.
- Separates a large fund's need for large exits from a young startup's immediate need to survive, learn, and iterate.

## Evidence
- Portfolio experience: [[99-vc-problems-but-a-batch-aint-one-why-portfolio-size-matters-for-returns]] says the argument follows five years and more than 1,000 investments at 500 Startups.
- Concentration critique: [[99-vc-problems-but-a-batch-aint-one-why-portfolio-size-matters-for-returns]] says the VC industry worships concentrated portfolio strategy despite weak average fund results.
- Size prescription: [[99-vc-problems-but-a-batch-aint-one-why-portfolio-size-matters-for-returns]] recommends 50-100 later-stage investments and at least 100-200 early-stage investments.
- Board-seat qualification: [[99-vc-problems-but-a-batch-aint-one-why-portfolio-size-matters-for-returns]] suggests VCs may overestimate the need to sit on every board.
- Focused entry: [[niche-to-win-baby-500-hats]] argues that a startup can beat broad alternatives for one specific audience before it can serve a large market.
- MVP and expansion sequence: [[niche-to-win-baby-500-hats]] treats a rough but useful first product as a learning foothold, followed by broader segments and offerings only after initial traction.
- Investor-stage distinction: [[niche-to-win-baby-500-hats]] says large-fund economics can explain demands for large outcomes without making broad initial targeting useful to founders.

## Qualifications
This page reflects McClure through two argumentative 2015 practitioner sources. Neither is an independent audit or comparative outcome study, and the profile does not summarize his broader career, later events, or 500 Startups history beyond those articles. The startup examples in the niche essay are compressed retrospectives, its detailed customer segment is hypothetical, and its claim that slow incumbents will never catch a specialist is unsupported.

## What Changed
- Added McClure's niche-to-win argument and its stage-sensitive distinction between fund ambition and startup focus.
- Connected diversification across investments with concentration within an individual startup's initial market search.
