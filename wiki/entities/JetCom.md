## Overview
[[JetCom]] is represented here as an e-commerce startup that used visible salaries, fixed compensation levels, standardized level-based equity, and promotion-only raises in an attempt to make pay more predictable and contestable.

## Current Profile
At the source's 2016 snapshot, Jet reportedly had 266 full-time employees and had been shipping consumer goods since the previous year. Its compensation system contained 13 salary levels from $40,000 to $300,000, assigned the same equity amount to everyone within a level, paid no bonuses, and allowed raises only through promotion.

CEO [[MarcLore]] said the design was intended to preserve early-startup trust and prevent differences driven by negotiation or threatened departure. The article describes the rules clearly but does not show how jobs entered levels, how promotions were governed, whether equity terms were comparable in economic value, or whether employees and outcomes supported Lore's assessment.

## Key Characteristics
- Used 13 disclosed salary levels spanning entry-level employees through executives.
- Standardized salary and equity amounts within each level.
- Offered equity to every full-time employee in the reported system.
- Replaced bonuses and negotiated raises with promotion to a higher level.
- Treated compensation visibility as a cultural mechanism for trust and challenge.

## Evidence
- Workforce and range: [[jet-employees-know-each-others-salaries-business-insider]] reports 266 full-time employees and levels from $40,000 to $300,000.
- Standardization: [[jet-employees-know-each-others-salaries-business-insider]] says employees at the same level received the same equity and that raises required promotion.
- Incentive structure: [[jet-employees-know-each-others-salaries-business-insider]] reports no bonuses and no counteroffer-based raises.
- Intended culture: [[jet-employees-know-each-others-salaries-business-insider]] attributes trust, fairness, ownership, and empowerment claims to Lore.

## Qualifications
The available evidence is a point-in-time leadership account, not an audit or outcome study. It does not report level criteria, job families, geography, demographic composition, promotion rates, employee dissent, equity terms, turnover, productivity, or later policy changes. Equal pay within a level is not sufficient evidence of pay equity if level placement, promotion, or role classification remains discretionary.
