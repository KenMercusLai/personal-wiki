## Overview
[[Anki]] is a spaced-repetition flashcard application used in the source as a random-review environment for orphaned note fragments.

## Current Profile
Within this wiki, Anki appears as a practical tool for turning otherwise isolated excerpts into recurring prompts. The source's use is not conventional exam drilling alone: excerpts are converted into cloze cards, reviewed in random order, and allowed to collide with other fragments from different fields. That makes Anki a bridge between memory practice and creative recombination inside a personal knowledge workflow.

## Key Characteristics
- Provides spaced repetition for recurring review of knowledge fragments.
- Supports cloze-style cards where important words or phrases are hidden and recalled.
- Can randomize review across domains, making unrelated excerpts reappear near one another.
- Functions as a creative engine when repeated exposure helps isolated fragments form new associations.
- Complements rather than replaces a linked note system.

## Evidence
- Review mechanism: [[wei-shen-me-yi-ji-ru-he-chu-li-gu-er-bi-ji]] recommends placing raw excerpts into Anki and reviewing them like vocabulary cards.
- Cloze format: [[wei-shen-me-yi-ji-ru-he-chu-li-gu-er-bi-ji]] says the author usually hides important words or phrases inside excerpts.
- Creative recombination: [[wei-shen-me-yi-ji-ru-he-chu-li-gu-er-bi-ji]] describes fragments from Lem, Papanek, and Wiener becoming meaningfully connected through repeated review.
- Tool plurality: [[wei-shen-me-yi-ji-ru-he-chu-li-gu-er-bi-ji]] notes that other memory tools may work if they support spaced repetition and random combination.

## Qualifications
The source presents Anki through one author's note practice rather than as a general product evaluation. Its value here depends on the user having enough diverse fragments and enough review discipline for unexpected associations to surface; it may be less useful for notes that require diagrams, full context, or project-specific execution.
