## Overview
[[EdParsons]] is presented as Google's geographer-in-chief and Geospatial Technologist, a public-facing advocate for Google's map of the world.

## Current Profile
The source portrays Parsons as both a cartographer and a diplomat for [[Google]]'s geospatial ambitions. His background at Autodesk and Ordnance Survey gives him credibility with cartographic, academic, government, and GIS communities, while his Google role is to explain and defend a system that combines [[GoogleMaps]], [[GoogleEarth]], [[StreetView]], personalization, local search, user contributions, and sensitive location data. He treats digital maps as a new medium rather than merely paper maps moved onto screens, but he also acknowledges the privacy and trust problems created when a platform knows where people are and where they are going.

## Key Characteristics
- Serves as a visible human face for Google's Geo work and a conduit to governments, academics, GIS researchers, and technologists.
- Frames modern maps as personalized and dynamic rather than one shared artifact made by someone else.
- Advocates for reinventing cartography around screens, user-generated data, augmented reality, indoor mapping, and Google Earth storytelling.
- Acknowledges that location information is unusually sensitive and difficult to anonymize reliably.
- Defends Google's practical map conventions while recognizing that cartographers still matter for abstraction and judgment.

## Evidence
- Public geospatial role: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] describes Parsons as Google's geographer-in-chief and Geospatial Technologist, with a job involving evangelism and public trust.
- Cartographic background: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] traces his work from Autodesk to Ordnance Survey and then to Google's London office.
- Digital cartography view: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] quotes Parsons arguing that maps have become "your map" and that screen-based media require different techniques.
- Privacy caution: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] records Parsons saying location is one of the most sensitive kinds of personal information and that anonymizing movement tracks is difficult.
- Practical-map stance: [[a-selfie-for-the-planet]] presents Parsons defending Google's utility-first cartography while warning that map design still requires abstraction.
