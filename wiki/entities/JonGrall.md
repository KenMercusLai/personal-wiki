## Overview
[[JonGrall]] is the independent developer and company founder who wrote the 2016 retrospective explaining why [[JustLanded]] would be withdrawn and shut down.

## Current Profile
Grall describes building Just Landed with a small group of friends, reaching hundreds of thousands of users, receiving favorable reviews and press, and gaining repeated [[AppStore]] featuring. His account centers on the limits of small-team control: essential flight data came through concentrated suppliers, supporting services changed or disappeared, heavy professional users raised variable costs under one-time pricing, and other projects competed for his time. He chose a scheduled wind-down after judging that a redesign and business-model overhaul would not make the opportunity attractive enough.

## Key Characteristics
- Independent iOS developer operating with a small team.
- Frames product success separately from sustainable operating economics.
- Treats time and development attention as opportunity costs.
- Chose advance notice and a defined shutdown window over waiting for a forced outage.

## Evidence
- Product role and reach: [[just-landed-is-shutting-down-jon-grall-medium]] identifies Grall as the developer behind Just Landed and reports hundreds of thousands of users.
- Economic judgment: [[just-landed-is-shutting-down-jon-grall-medium]] links his closure decision to data costs, one-time pricing, professional usage, crowded app markets, and limited resources.
- Shutdown stewardship: [[just-landed-is-shutting-down-jon-grall-medium]] announces removal from sale five months before server closure while preserving service for existing users during the transition.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from one first-person shutdown essay. It provides no audited finances, supplier contracts, team accounts, user research, or measured comparison of alternative pricing and product strategies.
