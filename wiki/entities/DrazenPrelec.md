## Overview
[[DrazenPrelec]] appears in this wiki as the behavioral economist whose work on [[Metaknowledge]] offers a way to improve [[WisdomOfCrowds]].

## Current Profile
The Aeon essay presents Prelec as an MIT researcher developing survey methods that ask people for both their own answer and their prediction of how others will answer. That second signal lets researchers infer who has better awareness of the knowledge environment and when crowd responses are distorted by shared information, concealment, or shallow confidence.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats crowd judgment as richer than simple averaging when surveys elicit metaknowledge.
- Uses predictions about other respondents as a measurable proxy for knowledge quality.
- Applies the method to sensitive self-reporting, factual quizzes, and political or economic forecasting.
- Emphasizes humility about one's own domain expertise when disagreement is more common than expected.

## Evidence
- Richer crowd signal: [[a-mathematical-bs-detector-can-boost-the-wisdom-of-crowds-aeon-essays]] quotes Prelec arguing that crowd research has not extracted enough information from respondents.
- Prediction method: [[a-mathematical-bs-detector-can-boost-the-wisdom-of-crowds-aeon-essays]] describes asking for a response and a prediction of agreement, then trusting respondents whose predictions are more accurate.
- Survey applications: [[a-mathematical-bs-detector-can-boost-the-wisdom-of-crowds-aeon-essays]] connects Prelec's work to research-misconduct surveys, state-capital quizzes, and forecasting examples.
- Expertise humility: [[a-mathematical-bs-detector-can-boost-the-wisdom-of-crowds-aeon-essays]] cites Prelec's advice that surprise at disagreement can signal novice status in a domain.

## Qualifications
The source presents Prelec through Musser's essay and does not independently evaluate the mathematical details or later reception of the method.
