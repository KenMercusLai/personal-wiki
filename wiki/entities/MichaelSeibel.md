## Overview
[[MichaelSeibel]] appears in the wiki as the CEO of [[YCombinator]] Core and a founder-operator: he sold Twitch to [[Amazon]] for $1 billion in 2014 and was previously a founder of SocialCam, acquired for $60 million. His sources present operating judgments about product-market fit, application screening, technical founding teams, persistence, accelerators, and the discipline required to decide which users a startup should serve.

## Current Profile
The sources frame Seibel as an accelerator insider and founder speaking from experience rather than as a disinterested analyst. He describes YC as a university, investor, and software company combined whose goal is to last hundreds of years while putting founders first, and he explains how that stance shapes which companies he wants to back. His advice is consistently stage-aware and evidence-driven: demand that outruns capacity is the real test of fit, applications are read for disqualifying negatives, technical founders are non-negotiable, and persistence is loyalty to the customer and problem rather than the current solution. His user-scope essay adds a complementary boundary: listen to unexpected users, but change direction only when they represent a larger opportunity with viable economics rather than an isolated custom need.

## Key Characteristics
- Is introduced as CEO of YC Core with a founder track record of the Twitch sale to Amazon and the SocialCam acquisition.
- Leads an institution he describes as a university, an investor, and a software company combined, oriented toward founders-first longevity.
- Screens applications for disqualifying negatives, especially unclear communication, no technical founder, and slow accomplishment.
- Distinguishes persistence from stubbornness: keep the customer and the problem, iterate hard on the solution.
- Rejects contract developers outright for technology companies while accepting missing business experience.
- Treats accelerators as optional and uneven, warning that weak programs overpromise and can set founders back.
- Distinguishes strategically useful adjacent users from one-off requests, using Justin.tv's gaming broadcasters to connect behavior, cost compatibility, monetization, and focus.

- Speed and commitment: [[y-combinator-ceo-if-you-are-not-drowning-in-demand-you-dont-have-product-market-fit-capital-growth-blog]] pairs lack of speed with lack of commitment and says YC prefers founders who have jumped off the ledge.
- Technical requirement: [[y-combinator-ceo-if-you-are-not-drowning-in-demand-you-dont-have-product-market-fit-capital-growth-blog]] refuses to tolerate a founding team that cannot build good technology, even while accepting a lack of business experience.
- Persistence framing: [[y-combinator-ceo-if-you-are-not-drowning-in-demand-you-dont-have-product-market-fit-capital-growth-blog]] argues more startups would succeed if founders stayed bull-headed about the customer and problem while changing the solution.
- Accelerator caution: [[y-combinator-ceo-if-you-are-not-drowning-in-demand-you-dont-have-product-market-fit-capital-growth-blog]] says most great companies never joined an accelerator and that the gap between YC and the tenth-best program is huge.
- User-scope test: [[users-you-dont-want]] asks whether an unexpected user represents a larger group, changes the business economics, or offers a better growth opportunity.
- Refusal and focus: [[users-you-dont-want]] argues that courteous refusal can protect service to the intended market and the search for product-market fit.
- Gaming case: [[users-you-dont-want]] says Justin.tv's gaming broadcasters were persistent, cost-compatible, and attractive to advertisers.

## Qualifications
The page rests on two advice pieces, so the characteristics are Seibel's stated positions and retrospective interpretations rather than controlled evidence. His accelerator and application judgments come from the leader of the best-known accelerator, a self-interested vantage point on his program and rivals. The AMA offers no data on how often its screening rules predict failure, and its side opinions are personal positions rather than evidence-backed findings. The user-scope essay likewise supplies no quantitative threshold for a viable adjacent segment, and its Justin.tv success case may benefit from hindsight. Both sources predate later YC and startup-market changes.
