## Overview
[[SubmitHub]] is presented as a music-submission marketplace connecting artists with bloggers, playlist curators, SoundCloud channels, and YouTubers while exposing behavioral information intended to make curator value more legible.

## Current Profile
Jason Grishkoff reportedly created SubmitHub in late 2015 for submissions to his Indie Shuffle blog and then opened it to other music influencers. Artists could submit for free or buy premium credits that guaranteed feedback and at least 20 seconds of listening rather than acceptance.

After finding that playlist follower counts could be inflated, Grishkoff asked artists to upload Spotify dashboard data showing playlist additions and resulting streams. SubmitHub combined that evidence into estimates of a playlist's likely plays and displayed it beside curator response rates and preferences. This addresses information asymmetry more directly than raw follower counts, but the article supplies company-reported listening, rejection, and submission figures rather than an independent validation.

## Key Characteristics
- Aggregated submissions across several music-discovery intermediaries.
- Offered both free submissions and paid credits tied to attention and feedback rather than guaranteed acceptance.
- Used artist-supplied outcome data to estimate playlist-generated streams.
- Exposed curator response rates and interests alongside estimated reach.
- Positioned high rejection as evidence of selective judgment rather than automatic paid placement.

## Evidence
- Origin and scope: [[inside-the-black-market-for-spotify-playlists]] traces the service from Indie Shuffle to bloggers, playlisters, SoundCloud channels, and YouTubers.
- Submission terms: [[inside-the-black-market-for-spotify-playlists]] says premium credits ensured feedback and at least 20 seconds of listening, not playlist addition.
- Measurement method: [[inside-the-black-market-for-spotify-playlists]] describes aggregating artist dashboard data to estimate streams generated by specific playlists.
- Selectivity claims: [[inside-the-black-market-for-spotify-playlists]] attributes 10,000 daily submissions, two-minute average listening, and a rejection rate above 90% to Grishkoff.

## Qualifications
The article does not audit uploaded dashboard data, sampling bias, attribution across overlapping playlists, response-rate calculation, or whether the estimates predicted future performance. Company-reported activity and listening figures should not be treated as platform-wide facts. Paying for guaranteed attention can reduce submission uncertainty without proving that feedback quality, access fairness, or downstream discovery improves.

## What Changed
- Created SubmitHub as an attention-and-feedback marketplace for music submissions.
- Added its use of observed stream outcomes as an alternative to nominal playlist follower counts.
- Qualified the model with self-reported metrics, selection effects, and unresolved attribution.

## Relationships
- [[Spotify]] - artist dashboard data supplies the reported playlist-outcome signal.
- [[SpotLister]] - comparable submission service differentiated by SubmitHub's observed-performance estimates and free option.
- [[PlaylistManipulation]] - attempts to detect inflated reach while remaining part of the paid-access ecosystem.
- [[MusicDiscovery]] - routes new music through bloggers, playlist curators, and other audience intermediaries.
- [[SoundCloud]] - one of the channel types represented among SubmitHub influencers.
