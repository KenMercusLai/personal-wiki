## Overview
[[DavidKadavy]] is an author, bootstrapped solopreneur, and podcast creator represented through first-person analyses of email experimentation and audience distribution.

## Current Profile
Kadavy's two accounts share a concern with persuasive but incomplete metrics. In email, he describes using [[Mailchimp]] to run identical variants across 56 campaigns and more than 750,000 sends, then uses apparent winners to challenge casual reliance on relative lift and nominal significance. In podcasting, he treats an omitted Product Hunt submission, a delayed submission, and the later shutdown of Product Hunt Podcasts as evidence that the platform materially affected his downloads, while separately warning that direct-MP3 bot requests can inflate the same metric. His position is resource-sensitive rather than anti-measurement: examine consequential changes, test alternative mechanisms where possible, and preserve uncertainty when the observed metric is not the final human or business outcome.

## Key Characteristics
- Bootstrapped solo operator evaluating experimentation under small-organization constraints.
- Uses a long-running first-person email exercise to demonstrate sampling noise.
- Emphasizes absolute business value and qualified prospects over isolated click or open lifts.
- Treats attention spent designing tests as an opportunity cost against creative and product work.
- Uses his podcast's download history to assess a distribution channel and its removal.
- Recommends calibration, mechanism checks, and outcome-aware caution rather than automatic deference to dashboards.

## Evidence
- Experiment scale: [[david-kadavy-a-a-testing-how-i-increased-conversions-300-percent-by-doing-absolutely-nothing]] reports 56 campaigns, more than 750,000 total sends, and eight months of identical-variant tests.
- Statistical caution: [[david-kadavy-a-a-testing-how-i-increased-conversions-300-percent-by-doing-absolutely-nothing]] discusses baseline rates, sample size, power, significance, false positives, and false negatives.
- Business judgment: [[david-kadavy-a-a-testing-how-i-increased-conversions-300-percent-by-doing-absolutely-nothing]] argues that clicks can differ in prospect quality and that test value must exceed the effort required.
- Operating context: [[david-kadavy-a-a-testing-how-i-increased-conversions-300-percent-by-doing-absolutely-nothing]] describes the business as a fully bootstrapped solopreneur operation with a six-figure year.
- Podcast distribution: [[just-how-big-is-the-podcast-discovery-gap-startup-grind-medium]] reports a download drop after an omitted Product Hunt submission, a rebound after delayed submission, and a 20–30% decline when Product Hunt Podcasts shut down.
- Download integrity: [[just-how-big-is-the-podcast-discovery-gap-startup-grind-medium]] distinguishes genuine listening from bot-triggered MP3 requests and reports checking that Product Hunt page views did not automatically load the audio file.

## Qualifications
The profile is bounded to two first-person essays from 2015 and 2017. Their campaign, business, and download claims are self-reported, and neither supplies complete underlying event data. The email case does not establish that the same tradeoffs apply to organizations with larger samples and specialist experiment teams. The podcast case supplies natural before-and-after comparisons but no referral export, episode controls, unique-listener measure, completion data, or counterfactual sufficient to isolate Product Hunt or generalize from one show. Neither source establishes Kadavy's broader career or later views.
