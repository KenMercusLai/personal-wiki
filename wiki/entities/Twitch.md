## Overview
[[Twitch]] appears in the wiki through a 2016 essay that treats it as a possible internet-native medium combining gameplay, viewing, and live audience participation.

## Current Profile
[[AlexDanco]] uses Twitch to distinguish merely fleeting media from media that creates a shared “there.” In his interpretation, active gameplay, passive consumption, and audience interaction jointly produce unpredictable experiences in which viewers are present rather than simply receiving a live version of prerecorded content. He further speculates that digitally native markets can form inside that participatory setting. This is a useful product hypothesis, but Danco explicitly says he did not use Twitch and provides no audience, creator, retention, revenue, or transaction evidence.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines active play, passive viewing, and audience interaction in the source's description.
- Is framed as a native participatory medium rather than old content with a live audience attached.
- Creates a stronger shared-place interpretation than Snapchat's fleeting glimpses, according to Danco.
- May support spontaneous digital goods and services inside the experience, though the source does not measure them.

## Evidence
- Hybrid format: [[from-pull-and-push-to-here-and-now-the-grand-bargain-of-facebook-and-the-feed-is-unraveling-what-comes-next]] describes the experience as roughly equal parts gameplay, media consumption, and audience participation.
- Presence thesis: [[from-pull-and-push-to-here-and-now-the-grand-bargain-of-facebook-and-the-feed-is-unraveling-what-comes-next]] contrasts being fully present in Twitch with Snapchat's less clearly located experience.
- Native-market hypothesis: [[from-pull-and-push-to-here-and-now-the-grand-bargain-of-facebook-and-the-feed-is-unraveling-what-comes-next]] compares possible in-stream markets with economies that emerge inside games.

## Qualifications
The profile is bounded to one speculative 2016 essay by an author who disclaims firsthand use. It does not establish Twitch's broader history, product mechanics, governance, moderation, creator economics, later development, or whether presence caused commercial success. The CS:GO gambling example is an ambiguous warning about blurred boundaries, not proof that permissionless market formation is beneficial.
