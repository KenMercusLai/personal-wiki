## Overview
[[NiklasLuhmann]] is cited in the source as the historical reference point for Zettelkasten-style small notes.

## Current Profile
Within this wiki, Luhmann appears as a sociologist whose paper slip-box practice illustrates atomic notes, sequences, indexes, and skepticism toward rigid hierarchy. The sources describe separate literature and main slip-box functions, with reading notes later processed into durable idea notes. Logical predecessor/successor links, topic entry points, local overviews, and ordinary note-to-note links let complex arguments grow through networked relationships rather than a single predefined outline. Tietze's introductory essay also attributes to Luhmann the communication model in which surprise, recognizable information, and growing relational complexity make a slip-box more than a passive store.

## Key Characteristics
- Used small paper slips as a practical constraint for compact note units.
- Used literature and main slip-box functions to separate source capture from durable idea development.
- Developed or popularized Folgezettel and several index forms for sequences, topic entry points, local overviews, and cross-links.
- Resisted rigid hierarchy because premature ordering can constrain future associations.
- Treated note placement and linking as part of thinking rather than merely storing completed thoughts.
- Serves as a benchmark for modern Zettelkasten, small-note, and externalized-writing debates.
- Provides the communication-with-slip-boxes framing that later practitioners use to explain serendipitous retrieval.

## Evidence
- Slip size: [[zhong-kou-nan-tiao-de-bi-ji-ge-qu-suo-xu-de-gong-ju]] says Luhmann's Zettels were A6-sized paper slips.
- Sequencing: [[zhong-kou-nan-tiao-de-bi-ji-ge-qu-suo-xu-de-gong-ju]] describes Folgezettel as Luhmann's technique for representing connections among thoughts.
- Anti-hierarchy: [[zhong-kou-nan-tiao-de-bi-ji-ge-qu-suo-xu-de-gong-ju]] cites Luhmann's concern that hierarchical systems could bind thinking too early.
- Method role: [[zhong-kou-nan-tiao-de-bi-ji-ge-qu-suo-xu-de-gong-ju]] uses Luhmann as the key historical model for small-note practice.
- Two-box workflow: [[ka-pian-bi-ji-xie-zuo-fa-bi-ji]] describes a literature slip-box and a main slip-box for ideas produced from reading and thought.
- Index and link repertoire: [[ka-pian-bi-ji-xie-zuo-fa-bi-ji]] attributes topic indexes, local overviews, logical predecessor/successor links, and ordinary note links to Luhmann's practice.
- Thinking through writing: [[ka-pian-bi-ji-xie-zuo-fa-bi-ji]] presents the slip-box as an external environment in which written distinctions and connections continue the thinking process.
- Communication model: [[create-a-zettelkasten-for-your-notes-to-improve-thinking-and-writing-zettelkasten-method]] credits Luhmann for the surprise, information, and complexity criteria used to frame a slip-box as a communication partner.

## Qualifications
This profile only reflects Luhmann's role in three secondary note-taking discussions. It does not verify the historical details, publication counts, note counts, or productivity attribution against his archive or scholarship and does not summarize his broader sociology or systems-theory work. One source is itself a reader's notes on a book, while Tietze explicitly adapts concepts from Luhmann's essay; neither should be treated as a complete account of his practice or direct causal evidence about the method.
