## Overview
[[TylerCowen]] is the economist and author of the 2017 Bloomberg opinion essay “In a Robot Economy, All Humans Will Be Marketers.”

## Current Profile
In the source represented here, Cowen rejects mass technological unemployment as the most likely near-term outcome and focuses instead on the composition and social value of work created or expanded after automation. He argues that routine production and well-defined service tasks can give way to sales, branding, client cultivation, and customer-experience work, while warning that some of this [[PersuasionWork]] merely reallocates demand or creates incentives for fraud.

## Key Characteristics
- Analyzes automation through task and role reallocation rather than occupation counts alone.
- Distinguishes employment preservation from productivity growth and social value.
- Treats marketing as heterogeneous: it can inform, match, and add enjoyment, or become wasteful competitive persuasion.
- Uses bank tellers, legal work, medical assistants, warehouses, restaurants, and consumer brands as illustrative cases.

## Evidence
Labor reallocation:
- [[in-a-robot-economy-all-humans-will-be-marketers-bloomberg]] argues that ATM adoption changed teller work toward customer relationships and uses this as a model for broader role redesign.

Social-value boundary:
- [[in-a-robot-economy-all-humans-will-be-marketers-bloomberg]] separates informative and experience-enhancing marketing from costly brand-switching competition and fraudulent sales pressure.

Productivity interpretation:
- [[in-a-robot-economy-all-humans-will-be-marketers-bloomberg]] proposes that visible automation can coexist with weak measured productivity when labor shifts into low-output persuasion work.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one 2017 opinion essay. Its cross-sector examples are illustrative rather than a measured labor-market decomposition, and its prediction that persuasion would remain a durable human advantage predates later generative-AI capabilities in advertising, sales, and customer interaction.
