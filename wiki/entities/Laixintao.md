## Overview
[[Laixintao]] is a blogger and software engineer represented in the wiki by an essay on why personally authored writing remains valuable when AI-generated articles become common.

## Current Profile
In [[wei-he-xie-zuo]], Laixintao presents writing as a long-running personal practice rather than merely a publishing channel. He uses changes in his own views, memories of life in Germany, and the development of his technical explanation skills to argue that writing preserves experience, forces thought into inspectable form, satisfies expression, and helps people recognize one another directly.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats writing as a durable dialogue between past, present, and future selves.
- Connects repeated technical blogging with clearer explanation and faster recovery of forgotten knowledge.
- Values low-pressure blogging as a flexible medium for intrinsically motivated expression.
- Prefers direct human authorship as the basis for reader connection and rejects generated prose as a substitute for the writer's memory, thinking, or voice.

## Evidence
- Personal record: [[wei-he-xie-zuo]] compares old writing with a time capsule that recovers changed beliefs and the emotional texture of earlier life.
- Technical practice: [[wei-he-xie-zuo]] reports that years of writing improved the author's ability to explain opinions and technical problems clearly.
- Expression and connection: [[wei-he-xie-zuo]] describes unconstrained blogging as enjoyable self-expression and a way for like-minded people to discover one another.
- AI boundary: [[wei-he-xie-zuo]] argues that generated diaries and articles do not perform the human functions for which he values writing.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one first-person essay. Its account establishes Laixintao's stated practice and position, not general causal effects of blogging or a measured boundary between AI assistance and delegated authorship.
