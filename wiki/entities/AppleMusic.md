## Overview
[[AppleMusic]] is Apple's paid music-streaming service, used in the source as the clearest example of Apple making a content platform available on non-Apple hardware to defend user relationships and gain scale.

## Current Profile
The article presents Apple Music as a strategic successor to the iTunes download era. Music consumption had shifted from ownership to streaming access, and Cybart argues that Apple needed Apple Music to be a legitimate alternative to [[Spotify]] and Amazon Music. Support on [[AmazonEcho]] is therefore framed as a competitive move: many Echo owners also use iPhones, and Apple can reduce the chance that those users adopt a rival music service simply because their speaker is not made by Apple.

## Key Characteristics
- Paid subscription music service inside Apple's content distribution arm.
- Competes with Spotify and Amazon Music for streaming scale.
- Became available on Amazon Echo as part of Apple's selective non-Apple hardware strategy.
- Strengthens Apple's relationship with music labels by offering an alternative to Spotify.
- Gains strategic value from playlists and A&R as music discovery shifts away from owned downloads.

## Evidence
- Subscriber scale: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] says Apple Music had approximately 60 million paying subscribers.
- Echo support: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] says Apple brought Apple Music to [[AmazonEcho]] to reduce switching pressure among iPhone users who also owned Echo speakers.
- Spotify alternative: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] frames Apple Music scale as a way to become a legitimate alternative to [[Spotify]] for both users and labels.
- Playlist discovery: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] says playlists were gaining power in talent discovery, making A&R capabilities more important.

## Qualifications
The source reflects a 2019 Apple strategy argument and does not provide later subscriber counts, product changes, royalty terms, artist outcomes, or comparative service quality. It also does not measure how much Echo support changed Apple Music adoption.

## What Changed
- Created Apple Music as an Apple content-distribution entity.
- Added Echo support as an example of selective availability on non-Apple hardware.
