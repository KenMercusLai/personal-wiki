## Overview
[[Acxiom]] is presented in the 2018 source as a large data broker whose consumer records and marketing systems combine many data sources into profiles for cross-channel activation.

## Current Profile
The article uses Acxiom to make commercial profiling concrete. A retained Cracked Labs infographic attributes broad demographic, financial, household, property, health-interest, media-use, purchase, and propensity fields to Acxiom profiles. A retained Acxiom marketing diagram shows signals from phone, email, messaging, video, web, commerce, analytics, and social channels being unified around a person, validated and enhanced with first-, second-, and third-party data, activated across channels, and returned through a continuous improvement loop. The article also reports Acxiom's historical marketing claim of insight into roughly 700 million consumers and more than 5,000 data elements, and names it in GDPR complaints filed by Privacy International.

## Key Characteristics
- Aggregates data from multiple sources into person-centered consumer profiles.
- Uses first-, second-, and third-party data to validate and enhance records.
- Activates enriched profiles across communication, advertising, commerce, and digital channels.
- Is attributed with demographic, household, financial, property, behavior, interest, and propensity information.
- Illustrates how parent activity may contribute to a child's profile before the child can consent.
- Appeared among data companies challenged through GDPR complaints in the article's 2018 context.

## Evidence
- Profile breadth: [[dont-post-your-kid-online]] retains a Cracked Labs infographic attributing up to 3,000 attributes and scores across 700 million people to Acxiom's profile system.
- Operating cycle: [[dont-post-your-kid-online]] retains Acxiom's own unify–validate/enhance–activate diagram, with response signals feeding a continuous improvement loop.
- Reported scale: [[dont-post-your-kid-online]] quotes a historical company claim of approximately 700 million consumers and more than 5,000 data elements from hundreds of sources.
- Regulatory scrutiny: [[dont-post-your-kid-online]] reports that Privacy International included Acxiom in formal GDPR complaints over alleged data-protection infringements.

## Qualifications
This is a source-bounded 2018 profile based on a journalistic article, a critical third-party infographic, Acxiom marketing material, and a reported company scale claim. The page does not independently audit the attributed fields, validate the 700-million-consumer or 5,000-element figures, adjudicate the GDPR complaint, or describe Acxiom's later ownership, products, practices, controls, or legal status. The Cracked Labs image itself says the companies keep many activities secret and that its illustration interprets publicly available information.
