## Overview
[[DiomidisSpinellis]] is represented in this wiki as the author of a personal forecast about AI, cognitive work, and life beyond economically necessary thought.

## Current Profile
In [[thinking-will-become-a-hobby]], Spinellis writes from the perspective of someone whose livelihood depends on thinking and who now expects AI systems to perform an increasing share of cognitive labor. He attributes that trajectory to improving capability, falling cost, continuous availability, and expanding compute capacity, while treating regulatory resistance as unlikely to reverse the larger transition.

His account is also existential rather than only economic. Spinellis compares thought's possible future with formerly necessary activities that became hobbies, then frames adaptation as a move from earning a living through thought toward making thought part of a purposeful life. Within the available evidence, this is an authored position and self-description rather than a complete biography or an independently validated labor forecast.

## Key Characteristics
- Presents AI displacement of cognitive work as a likely structural transition rather than a narrow task-specific possibility.
- Organizes the forecast around performance, cost, and availability.
- Uses historical technological substitutions to imagine thinking becoming elective rather than economically necessary.
- Treats knowledge-worker resistance and institutional power as important features of the transition.
- Links economic adaptation with resilience, purpose, and a rewarding life beyond work.

## Evidence
Forecasting frame:
- [[thinking-will-become-a-hobby]] argues that AI's improving performance, declining cost, and round-the-clock availability will expand both augmentation and replacement of cognitive labor.

Historical and institutional interpretation:
- [[thinking-will-become-a-hobby]] compares cognitive automation with earlier transformations of hunting, calligraphy, woodworking, and horse riding, and predicts especially strong resistance from knowledge workers.

Personal adaptation:
- [[thinking-will-become-a-hobby]] describes the challenge as moving from making a living by thinking to including thought within a life worth living.

## Qualifications
This profile is derived from one short first-person essay. It does not establish Spinellis's broader biography, research program, professional history, or the representativeness of his view. The article cites capability, education, and investment examples but does not provide a task-level adoption model, timetable, comparative policy analysis, or direct evidence that human thought as a whole will lose economic value. Its analogies and claims about regulation are interpretive forecasts rather than measured findings.
