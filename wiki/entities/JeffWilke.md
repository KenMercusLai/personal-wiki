## Overview
[[JeffWilke]] appears as a longtime Amazon lieutenant running the consumer business and as an internal witness to Bezos's shift from prescription toward teaching and refinement.

## Current Profile
The Forbes source uses Wilke as an internal witness to Amazon's experimentation culture and capability learning. He says Amazon wants reversible experiments and failures to happen throughout the company without senior review, and he recalls objecting to Kindle because Amazon lacked hardware capability. Bezos accepted those risks because learning hardware was strategically important, making Wilke's account evidence for Amazon's patient capability-building logic.

Fortune's earlier profile adds the organizational complement. Having joined Amazon in 1999, Wilke ran what he called “Amazon Classic.” He says narrative-based annual planning let Bezos audit the company's efforts while Bezos's day-to-day style changed from prescribing answers to teaching and refining the judgment of senior operators.

## Key Characteristics
- Runs Amazon's consumer and retail operations in the source.
- Describes Amazon's tolerance for widespread experimental failure.
- Provides the Kindle anecdote showing how Amazon intentionally learned hard new capabilities.
- Describes written annual planning as an audit mechanism for delegated leadership.
- Characterizes Bezos's evolution from prescription toward teaching and refinement.
