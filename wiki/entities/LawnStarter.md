## Overview
[[LawnStarter]] is the startup operating context for a practitioner account of choosing and developing customer-acquisition channels after entering Techstars with a small validating customer base.

## Current Profile
The source presents LawnStarter as a local-services company that had early demand but had not yet found scalable acquisition. Its early traction came from scrappy distribution, including ranking on Yelp and running daily Groupon deals. The company later treated paid acquisition as an iterative search problem: it reportedly spent about $75,000 on Facebook advertising before finding a creative-and-audience combination that worked.

## Key Characteristics
- Used small, accessible third-party channels to bridge the period before a scalable channel worked.
- Treated channel development as sustained optimization rather than a sequence of unrelated one-off tests.
- Operated in transactional consumer and SMB markets, which explicitly shapes the source's recommendations.
- Supplies first-person examples for the costs and uncertainty of paid-channel discovery.

## Evidence
- Early stage: [[finding-your-startups-customer-acquisition-channels]] says LawnStarter entered Techstars with enough customers to validate the product but no established path to scale acquisition.
- Scrappy acquisition: [[finding-your-startups-customer-acquisition-channels]] says the company reached the top of Yelp and ran daily Groupon deals to acquire early users.
- Paid iteration: [[finding-your-startups-customer-acquisition-channels]] reports spending roughly $75,000 on Facebook ads before finding a workable combination of creative and audience targeting.
- Scope: [[finding-your-startups-customer-acquisition-channels]] says the author's experience is concentrated in transactional consumer and SMB businesses.

## Qualifications
The source is a retrospective practitioner essay, not a LawnStarter growth audit. It does not provide campaign dates, funnel data, cohort quality, attribution methods, revenue, margins, or enough evidence to isolate how much Yelp, Groupon, Facebook, Techstars, product quality, or other channels contributed to company outcomes.
