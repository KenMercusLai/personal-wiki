## Overview
[[AndrewBosworth]] is represented in this wiki as Boz, an author on power-aware product-review meetings and time-respectful meeting ownership.

## Current Profile
The sources present Bosworth as a product and engineering leader who treats meetings as high-cost social systems. In product reviews, he learned that senior participants could make meetings feared by over-contributing and under-appreciating presenting teams; his response was to codify facilitation norms that protect presenter agency, team ownership, and trust while still surfacing important problems. In the time-scarcity essay, he broadens that discipline: anyone who interrupts or schedules colleagues should bear the burden of proving that the collective time cost is justified.

## Key Characteristics
- Reflective leadership: he uses negative feedback about disliked reviews as the trigger for changing meeting norms.
- Review facilitation discipline: he asks participants to avoid piling on, pedantry, pontificating, prescription, permission capture, pessimism, and interruptions.
- Power awareness: he emphasizes that a leader's mild interjection can feel much stronger to presenters because of the power gradient.
- Time stewardship: he treats meetings and interruptions as costly claims on other people's scarcest resource.
- People-centered product leadership: he treats people feeling seen and heard as part of producing better team results.

## Evidence
- Reflective leadership: [[boz-the-p-rules]] says Bosworth and his leadership team changed course after learning that people hated and feared their reviews.
- Review facilitation discipline: [[boz-the-p-rules]] lists the P Rules as decorum for product reviews.
- Power awareness: [[boz-the-p-rules]] says leaders must remember that mild interjections can feel amplified to presenters.
- Time stewardship: [[boz-time-isnt-money]] says people who create meetings or interrupt colleagues must ensure the request is worth the cost.
- People-centered product leadership: [[boz-the-p-rules]] says Bosworth's biggest responsibility is staying mindful of the people in the room.

## Qualifications
The sources are first-person leadership reflections, so they report Bosworth's meeting norms, self-assessment, and advice rather than independent evidence of the practices' later effectiveness. The time-scarcity source is most direct about knowledge-work meetings and interruptions; it does not cover every operational setting where synchronous coordination is unavoidable.
