## Overview
[[AndrewBosworth]] is represented in this wiki as Boz, an author on power-aware product-review meetings, time-respectful meeting ownership, and deliberate workplace information seeking.

## Current Profile
The sources present Bosworth as a product and engineering leader who treats attention and information flow as systems that require personal ownership. In product reviews, he learned that senior participants could make meetings feared by over-contributing and under-appreciating presenting teams; his response was to codify facilitation norms that protect presenter agency, team ownership, and trust while still surfacing important problems. In the time-scarcity essay, he broadens that discipline: anyone who interrupts or schedules colleagues should bear the burden of proving that the collective time cost is justified.

The listening essay adds the inbound side of that responsibility. Bosworth recommends a designed channel portfolio, aggressive removal of low-signal inputs, feedback to communicators, dependency maps that reveal missing flows, notes on surprising information, outbound context that attracts relevant updates, and explicit cross-team callbacks. His final listening rule is interpretive as well as logistical: inspect defensive reactions and look for useful content even when a message arrives without ideal tone, evidence, or context.

## Key Characteristics
- Reflective leadership: he uses negative feedback about disliked reviews as the trigger for changing meeting norms.
- Review facilitation discipline: he asks participants to avoid piling on, pedantry, pontificating, prescription, permission capture, pessimism, and interruptions.
- Power awareness: he emphasizes that a leader's mild interjection can feel much stronger to presenters because of the power gradient.
- Time stewardship: he treats meetings and interruptions as costly claims on other people's scarcest resource.
- People-centered product leadership: he treats people feeling seen and heard as part of producing better team results.
- Information-flow ownership: he treats obtaining necessary context as part of the recipient's job rather than only the sender's duty.
- Systematic listening: he combines channel design, gap mapping, follow-up notes, outbound signaling, callbacks, and non-defensive interpretation.

## Evidence
- Reflective leadership: [[boz-the-p-rules]] says Bosworth and his leadership team changed course after learning that people hated and feared their reviews.
- Review facilitation discipline: [[boz-the-p-rules]] lists the P Rules as decorum for product reviews.
- Power awareness: [[boz-the-p-rules]] says leaders must remember that mild interjections can feel amplified to presenters.
- Time stewardship: [[boz-time-isnt-money]] says people who create meetings or interrupt colleagues must ensure the request is worth the cost.
- People-centered product leadership: [[boz-the-p-rules]] says Bosworth's biggest responsibility is staying mindful of the people in the room.
- Information-flow ownership: [[boz-listening-is-the-job]] says workers should define and maintain the channels through which they obtain necessary context.
- Systematic listening: [[boz-listening-is-the-job]] links channel pruning, dependency mapping, surprise-driven follow-up, outbound communication, callbacks, and receptive interpretation.

## Qualifications
The sources are first-person leadership reflections, so they report Bosworth's norms, self-assessment, and advice rather than independent evidence of the practices' later effectiveness. The time-scarcity source is most direct about knowledge-work meetings and interruptions; it does not cover every operational setting where synchronous coordination is unavoidable. The listening source presents a senior Facebook leader's unusually comprehensive channel practice and does not establish that exhaustive daily reading, frequent follow-up notes, or equivalent access is feasible or beneficial in other roles. Receptivity to imperfect delivery should not be generalized into tolerance for abusive, discriminatory, coercive, or unsafe communication.
