## Overview
[[FrankDAngelo]] is a former game-audio professional whose move into accounting and finance anchors the article's account of unstable game careers.

## Current Profile
D'Angelo entered games through music transcription, audio engineering, and a Volition internship, then advanced to lead sound-design work. Five years of short contracts, four interstate moves, a rapid post-relocation layoff, prolonged unemployment, depleted savings, perceived nepotism, and a blocked permanent role led him to leave games, earn an MBA, and seek stability in corporate finance. He reports less love for the new work but better health, employment, housing, family, and retirement security.

## Key Characteristics
- Combined classical music, game fandom, and audio engineering into a game-audio career.
- Advanced into leadership without obtaining durable employment security.
- Experienced relocation and layoff as direct household financial shocks.
- Interprets routine staffing churn as a systemic studio-design choice.
- Chose stability over vocational attachment while retaining regret about the tradeoff.

## Evidence
- Entry and advancement: [[gamasutra-the-great-video-game-exodus]] traces D'Angelo from game-music transcription to Volition and lead sound design.
- Employment instability: [[gamasutra-the-great-video-game-exodus]] says every role lasted no more than twelve months and required four state moves in five years.
- Household cost: [[gamasutra-the-great-video-game-exodus]] says a layoff two months after relocation led to depleted savings and warehouse work during a year-long search.
- Exit outcome: [[gamasutra-the-great-video-game-exodus]] says accounting and finance brought steady work, home ownership, family formation, and retirement saving.
