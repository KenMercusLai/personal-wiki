## Overview
[[SuzanneImes]] is a clinical psychologist represented here as a co-originator, with [[PaulineRoseClance]], of the impostor-phenomenon construct.

## Current Profile
Imes explains impostor feelings through achievement-contingent self-worth, family messages that alternate praise and criticism, fear of disclosure, and the tendency to mistake anxiety and extreme effort for the cause of success. Her proposed responses combine supportive mentoring, realistic assessment of strengths and limitations, gradual experiments that reduce overpreparation, and therapy when needed.

## Key Characteristics
- Co-originated the impostor-phenomenon construct in the 1970s.
- Emphasizes how secrecy and fear of exposure keep the experience socially hidden.
- Connects family and social achievement pressure to conditional self-worth.
- Favors gradual cognitive and behavioral change rather than abrupt abandonment of entrenched effort rituals.
- Identifies individual therapy as potentially helpful for breaking persistent impostor thinking.

## Evidence
- Social mechanism: [[feel-like-a-fraud]] reports Imes's account of silence, mixed family messages, achievement pressure, and conditional worth.
- Reinforcement cycle: [[feel-like-a-fraud]] presents her view that people may credit success to anxiety-driven self-torture rather than ability.
- Corrective practices: [[feel-like-a-fraud]] reports her recommendations for mentoring, balanced self-assessment, gradual reductions in excessive preparation, and therapy.

## Qualifications
The profile relies on one secondary 2013 magazine article and does not represent Imes's complete clinical work. The source offers plausible mechanisms and practical advice but does not provide intervention-effect estimates or enough evidence to separate family, social, identity, and personality causes.

## What Changed
- Created a profile connecting Imes's account of achievement pressure and secrecy with gradual corrective practices.
