## Overview
[[Momo]] is a Chinese social and dating app used in the source to show livestreaming as both an app feature and a revenue stream.

## Current Profile
The source presents Momo as evidence that livestreaming can strengthen an existing stranger-social product rather than only exist as a standalone platform. After adding livestreaming, Momo reportedly made it the company's largest revenue stream within three quarters, while also using live video and gifts as social proof and sincerity signals in matchmaking.

## Key Characteristics
- Shows livestreaming as an embedded feature inside a social/dating app.
- Uses livestreaming to verify or qualify profile presentation.
- Turns digital gifts into sincerity signals in a dating context.
- Demonstrates rapid revenue impact from adding livestreaming.

## Evidence
- Revenue impact: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] says Momo's livestreaming feature contributed $15.6 million in Q1 2016 and became its largest revenue stream within three quarters.
- Social proof: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] says livestreaming helps users tell whether profile photos were overly edited.
- Matchmaking signal: [[16-observations-on-livestreaming-in-china-andreessen-horowitz]] says recipients viewed digital gifts as sincerity signals.

## Qualifications
The source does not evaluate Momo's broader dating-market dynamics, later product history, or whether livestreaming improved long-term retention or match quality.
