## Overview
[[SteveBlank]] appears in this wiki as an entrepreneurship educator who frames customer development as disciplined learning toward a repeatable and scalable business model.

## Current Profile
In the available article, Blank corrects an overly literal interpretation of “getting out of the building.” Talking to customers, fulfilling requests, surveying users, and running A/B tests are useful only when the evidence changes a coherent model of who is served, what value is delivered, how customers are reached, who pays, what they pay for, and whether the resulting economics can repeat and scale.

His account distinguishes revenue strategy from pricing tactics and single-sided freemium from multi-sided models with separate users and payers. It also treats declining off-model customers as legitimate strategy rather than a failure of customer service.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats startups as searches for repeatable and scalable business models rather than feature-delivery projects.
- Defines customer development as the pursuit of customer understanding, not universal customer satisfaction.
- Connects qualitative feedback and experiments to retention, payment, segment, and market-structure hypotheses.
- Separates a revenue model from the pricing tactics used to realize it.
- Accepts refusing or “firing” customers when their demands undermine the intended model.

## Evidence
- Customer-development purpose: [[steve-blank-your-job-is-not-to-make-every-possible-customer-happy]] says conversations and tests should produce insight about a viable business.
- Business-model scope: [[steve-blank-your-job-is-not-to-make-every-possible-customer-happy]] connects value propositions and customer segments with acquisition, retention, costs, revenue, partners, and resources.
- Monetization distinction: [[steve-blank-your-job-is-not-to-make-every-possible-customer-happy]] contrasts user-to-payer freemium with a multi-sided model in which another segment pays.
- Strategic refusal: [[steve-blank-your-job-is-not-to-make-every-possible-customer-happy]] argues that serving every requester can be less important than selecting segments that sustain the company.

## Qualifications
The current profile rests on one practitioner essay built around an unnamed former student's experience. It does not provide the company's data, rejected alternatives, or outcome after Blank's advice, and it should not be treated as a complete account of Blank's broader work or of customer-development methodology.

## What Changed
- Created a profile of Blank's distinction between listening to customers and obeying every request.
- Recorded his system-level connection among experiments, segment choice, revenue models, pricing, retention, and scale.
