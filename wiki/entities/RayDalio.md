## Overview
[[RayDalio]] is represented here through the All Weather portfolio attributed to him and through his research connecting debt, inequality, populism, internal conflict, and geopolitical risk.

## Current Profile
The available sources show two related sides of Dalio's investment thinking. A course summary presents the All Weather allocation as a diversified portfolio designed for different growth and inflation regimes. Carter's article presents Dalio as a macro-historical analyst who defined populism through economic, cultural, elite-legitimacy, and government-performance grievances and judged late-2010s divisions broadly analogous to 1937. Together they connect portfolio construction with regime uncertainty, but neither source supplies Dalio's complete method or validates the reported historical relationships independently.

## Key Characteristics
- Associates portfolio resilience with diversification across economic regimes.
- Uses long-run economic and political history to interpret current conditions.
- Connects wealth and opportunity gaps, cultural threat, elite distrust, and ineffective government with populism.
- Treats internal and external conflict as risks that can intensify when economic and social divisions deepen.

## Evidence
- Regime diversification: [[blog-wulc-zhang-xiao-yu-de-ge-ren-tou-zi-ke-3-tou-zi-zu-he-gou-jian]] describes a Ray Dalio-style All Weather allocation alongside other portfolio templates.
- Populism framework: [[its-beginning-to-look-a-lot-like-1937-spdr-s-p-500-trust-etf-nysearca-spy-seeking-alpha]] quotes Dalio's four-part definition of populist grievance.
- Historical comparison: [[its-beginning-to-look-a-lot-like-1937-spdr-s-p-500-trust-etf-nysearca-spy-seeking-alpha]] reproduces a developed-world populism chart and Dalio's analogy between the late 2010s and 1937.
- Conflict risk: [[its-beginning-to-look-a-lot-like-1937-spdr-s-p-500-trust-etf-nysearca-spy-seeking-alpha]] quotes Dalio warning that division can accompany rising conflict and threats to democracy.

## Qualifications
The portfolio evidence is a secondary course summary, and the political evidence is Carter's selection from Dalio's report and essay rather than the complete primary works. The sources do not establish the All Weather portfolio's realized performance, define the populism index construction, prove that the compared periods are structurally equivalent, or show that elevated populism necessarily causes war or market decline.
