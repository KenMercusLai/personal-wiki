## Overview
[[ChrisBrycki]] is the author of the wiki's source on smartphone notifications, attention capture, and responsibility across users, app developers, and mobile platform owners.

## Current Profile
In the source, Brycki writes as a product practitioner with experience at [[Secret]]. He combines notification and interruption research with his own account of product incentives and engineering prioritization, arguing that engagement metrics reward aggressive notification use while operating-system platforms are better positioned than individual apps to supply context-aware delivery tools. His personal response is to keep do-not-disturb enabled and review accumulated notifications deliberately.

## Key Characteristics
- Author concerned with notification overload and control of attention.
- Former Secret practitioner using product-development constraints as evidence.
- Critic of engagement metrics that reward short-term attention capture.
- Advocate for platform-assisted, context-aware notification prioritization.
- Uses always-on do-not-disturb as a personal batching practice.

## Evidence
- Authorship and thesis: [[why-were-stuck-in-an-abusive-relationship-with-our-phones]] attributes the article to Brycki and distributes responsibility among users, developers, and platforms.
- Product experience: [[why-were-stuck-in-an-abusive-relationship-with-our-phones]] says his Secret team wanted better notification prioritization but repeatedly faced more urgent engineering work.
- Platform position: [[why-were-stuck-in-an-abusive-relationship-with-our-phones]] argues that Apple and Android possess context and behavior data that individual developers cannot easily reproduce.
- Personal practice: [[why-were-stuck-in-an-abusive-relationship-with-our-phones]] reports that he keeps do-not-disturb enabled nearly all the time and catches up on alerts when he chooses.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one 2015 opinion essay and captures Brycki's role and claims in that source, not a full biography or an assessment of his later work. The article mixes cited studies, practitioner experience, rhetoric, and extrapolated notification-volume estimates.
