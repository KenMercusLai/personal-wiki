## Overview
[[Lego]] is a toy company represented through contrasting accounts of early child testing for physical products and delayed external feedback for the Lego Universe game.

## Current Profile
The source presents Lego as routinely using prototypes and children to test product ideas. In the Nexo Knights example, paper prototypes quickly exposed that children could not distinguish heroes from villains, allowing designers to iterate before manufacturing. Lego Universe provides the counterexample: the team reportedly iterated internally for years but withheld the game from real users while pursuing a polished experience, then received a lukewarm response after launch.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses paper and other prototypes with children during physical-product design.
- Treats recognizable play meaning, such as good-versus-evil roles, as something to test rather than assume.
- Has a quality culture summarized by "Only the best is good enough."
- Shows how a quality principle can help mature products yet delay essential feedback when applied as pre-release perfectionism.
- Provides both positive and negative user-testing cases within one organization.

## Evidence
- Nexo Knights: [[crisps-blog-making-sense-of-mvp-minimum-viable-product-and-why-i-prefer-earliest-testable-usable-lovable]] says child tests of paper prototypes exposed unclear good-and-evil character design.
- Prototype culture: [[crisps-blog-making-sense-of-mvp-minimum-viable-product-and-why-i-prefer-earliest-testable-usable-lovable]] reports collaboration with schools, kindergartens, and families for field testing.
- Lego Universe: [[crisps-blog-making-sense-of-mvp-minimum-viable-product-and-why-i-prefer-earliest-testable-usable-lovable]] says roughly 250 people worked for four to five years before a lukewarm release and later shutdown.
- Feedback boundary: [[crisps-blog-making-sense-of-mvp-minimum-viable-product-and-why-i-prefer-earliest-testable-usable-lovable]] notes that Lego Universe used Scrum and internal iteration but did not expose those increments to real users.

## Qualifications
The profile is based on one practitioner's anecdotal account and does not establish company-wide policy or independently verify the project figures. The contrast does not show that prototyping alone caused Nexo Knights' outcome or that delayed release alone caused Lego Universe's failure. Safety, brand, manufacturing, licensing, and child-research constraints may legitimately limit what can be released and when.
