## Overview
[[BenjaminJones]] is the economist cited for the burden-of-knowledge argument that modern innovation is becoming harder for lone individuals and more dependent on complementary teams.

## Current Profile
The sources use Jones to explain why [[InnovationAtIntersection]] has an organizational dimension. As knowledge expands and specialization deepens, even PhDs master only a slice of a field, so teams need complementary expertise to work at the intersection of their knowledge rather than assuming one person can know enough alone.

## Key Characteristics
- Economist cited through a 2005 paper on whether innovation is getting harder.
- Associated in the source with the decline of the Renaissance-person model.
- Provides the specialization argument behind team-based innovation.

## Evidence
- Paper citation: [[all-new-ideas-are-combinations-of-old-ideas]] cites Jones's "The Burden of Knowledge and the Death of the Renaissance Man: Is Innovation Getting Harder?"
- Specialization claim: [[all-new-ideas-are-combinations-of-old-ideas]] says modern PhDs specialize in niches and cannot master whole broad fields.
- Specialization restatement: [[all-new-ideas-are-combinations-of-old-ideas-at-the-intersection-of-fields]] summarizes the same claim with the example of machine-learning PhDs mastering only a subfield after years of study.
- Team implication: [[all-new-ideas-are-combinations-of-old-ideas]] uses Jones to argue for teams whose expertise complements other members.
- Team confirmation: [[all-new-ideas-are-combinations-of-old-ideas-at-the-intersection-of-fields]] explicitly says innovation requires teams with complementary expertise.
