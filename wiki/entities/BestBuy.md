## Overview
[[BestBuy]] is a consumer-electronics retailer represented in the source through a historical ecommerce product-page bundling case.

## Current Profile
The article presents Best Buy's “Our Experts Recommend” widget as a way to raise basket value while keeping the core product's advertised price competitive. The widget places a selectable package beneath the main product-page content, lets a shopper remove unwanted items, and offers one action to add the remaining set to the cart. Its examples span complementary electronics and media, multiple variants of the same toy, and a hair straightener paired with a same-value Sephora gift card.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses product-page recommendations to assemble selectable multi-item offers.
- Matches bundle composition to complementary, repeat-purchase, or gift contexts.
- Reduces the navigation and cart work needed to buy several related items.
- Preserves the core item's listed price while attempting to increase total basket value.

## Evidence
- Widget design: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] describes a selectable “Our Experts Recommend” module with a single add-multiple-items action.
- Complementary bundle: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] pairs Beats headphones with a speaker, portable charger, and iTunes gift card.
- Same-product bundle: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] offers four Fingerlings Baby Monkey variants together.
- Gift bundle: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] pairs a hair straightener with a same-value Sephora gift card.
- Basket arithmetic: [[ecommerce-product-bundling-strategy-hint-baby-monkey]] calculates potential increases of $190.98 and $44.97 for two displayed offers.

## Qualifications
The source is a promotional Sumo article, not Best Buy documentation or an independent outcome study. It shows offer composition and arithmetic but provides no impressions, take rate, controlled comparison, realized AOV, gross margin, return behavior, customer satisfaction, or profit contribution. The claim that this tactic helped explain Best Buy's broader profit is therefore unsupported by the evidence supplied.
