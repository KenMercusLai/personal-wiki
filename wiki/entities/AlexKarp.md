## Overview
[[AlexKarp]] is the [[Palantir]] cofounder and CEO discussed in William Alden's 2016 investigation of the company's growth, client economics, and employee-retention problems.

## Current Profile
The source presents Karp as the chief executive of a highly valued but unprofitable enterprise-software company whose strategy depended on large, complex customer relationships. He publicly acknowledged that some important clients paid little or nothing, while internal reporting tied him to the company's largest reported commitment through discretionary BP bonus terms.

Karp also appears as the executive responding to workforce pressure. In April 2016 he announced a 20% raise for employees with at least 18 months of service, ended the existing annual performance-review system, and promised to pursue another employee liquidity event. Those actions show material intervention, but the article does not establish their later effects on retention, performance, or employee wealth.

## Key Characteristics
- Cofounder and CEO of Palantir during the source's reporting period.
- Pursued transformational, complex enterprise relationships rather than a uniform self-service product model.
- Acknowledged that some large client appointments generated little or no payment.
- Responded to rising departures with a broad tenure-based raise and performance-review reset.
- Recognized employee demand for liquidity in a stock-heavy compensation system.

The raise, review cancellation, and proposed liquidity event occurred amid departures but cannot be assumed to have solved or caused the underlying retention issues.
