## Overview
[[JasonCosta]] is the author of a 2016 essay comparing Twitter and Slack's API-platform strategies, written while he was an entrepreneur in residence at [[GGVCapital]].

## Current Profile
Costa presents API publication as a durable strategic commitment rather than a technical release. His comparison emphasizes business-model fit, ownership of the core experience, reciprocal ecosystem benefits, and active developer guidance.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes from a product and platform-strategy perspective.
- Uses Twitter and Slack as contrasting historical API-governance cases.
- Treats developer trust, incentives, and ecosystem investment as management responsibilities.

## Evidence
- Role and context: [[a-tale-of-2-api-platforms-ggv-capital-medium]] identifies Costa as the author and an entrepreneur in residence at GGV Capital.
- Platform analysis: [[a-tale-of-2-api-platforms-ggv-capital-medium]] compares Twitter's API restrictions with Slack's complementary app strategy.
- Governance prescription: [[a-tale-of-2-api-platforms-ggv-capital-medium]] argues for explicit product boundaries, aligned incentives, communication, distribution, and long-term support.
