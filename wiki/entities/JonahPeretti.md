## Overview
[[JonahPeretti]] is BuzzFeed's founder and strategic leader, represented by an external 2017 interview and his later "9 Boxes" memo to employees.

## Current Profile
The sources present Peretti as a long-horizon digital-media operator who expects internet-native, personalized, shareable, and global delivery to displace print and broadcast assumptions. In the interview he blames incumbent publishers' short-term earnings focus for their weak digital position and treats Facebook and Google as partners; in the later memo he argues that those same platforms capture too much advertising value. Across both accounts, his response is to invest in quality content, distribute across platforms, combine news with entertainment, diversify revenue, and organize BuzzFeed around brands and business opportunities.

## Key Characteristics
- Takes a long-term view of internet-driven change and criticizes quarter-to-quarter media management.
- Defines BuzzFeed around digitally native audiences rather than a permanently young demographic.
- Treats news and entertainment as complementary sources of repeat use, reach, and institutional purpose.
- Uses audience behavior to shape platform-specific formats and commercial creative.
- Moves from partnership-oriented language about platforms to a sharper demand for fair publisher payment.
- Treats diversified revenue and brand portfolios as the next stage of BuzzFeed's maturation.

## Evidence
- Long-horizon diagnosis: [[buzzfeeds-jonah-peretti-news-publishers-only-have-themselves-to-blame-for-losing-out-to-google-and-facebook-the-drum]] quotes Peretti blaming legacy publishers for stockpiling cash and maximizing near-term earnings instead of investing in digital.
- Audience and portfolio stance: [[buzzfeeds-jonah-peretti-news-publishers-only-have-themselves-to-blame-for-losing-out-to-google-and-facebook-the-drum]] presents BuzzFeed as a modern media company for digitally native audiences, with global news, entertainment, Tasty, and other service brands.
- Format and data practice: [[buzzfeeds-jonah-peretti-news-publishers-only-have-themselves-to-blame-for-losing-out-to-google-and-facebook-the-drum]] describes short video for reach, longer video for watch time, and the Audience Engine for data-informed advertising ideas.
- Platform critique: [[9-boxes]] says big technology platforms capture much of the advertising revenue while content creators are underpaid.
- Long-term content stance: [[9-boxes]] argues that quality content is BuzzFeed's best weapon in the fight for fair platform economics.
- Organizational design: [[9-boxes]] introduces the nine-box meeting plan and operating matrix.

## Qualifications
Both sources convey Peretti's and BuzzFeed's own strategic narrative. The interview repeats company-reported audience, revenue, engagement, and completion figures without independent methods, while the memo states intentions and targets. Neither establishes profitability or evaluates later public-company performance, layoffs, acquisitions, platform-policy shifts, or whether the predicted investment in news materialized.

## What Changed
- Expanded Peretti from an internal-memo author into a founder with a long-term, data-informed, distributed-media strategy.
- Added the change in emphasis from defending platform partners and blaming publisher short-termism to criticizing platform value capture.
