## Overview
[[Lob]] appears in the wiki through its engineering blog, which published the [[UnderstandDesignBuild]] framework for problem-solving. The source is written from inside the company: it describes how Lob coaches engineers, what it hands to intern mentors and first-time engineering managers, and how it was thinking about scale as its engineering and data teams grew.

## Current Profile
Within this wiki, Lob is a company that treats engineering practice itself as publishable material, writing up its own coaching model for a public blog. The operating stance in the article is that engineers own problems rather than code volume: the job is to find and solve problems that move the business, and the framework is how the company teaches newcomers to slow down and do that deliberately. Lob also presents the framework as a scaling mechanism. With engineering and data teams poised to double in size, a teachable mental model keeps problem-solving and decision-making with the engineers doing the work instead of concentrating it in managers.

## Key Characteristics
- Coaches engineers to become problem solvers rather than code producers.
- Uses the three-step Understand, Design, Build framework as its mentoring model for interns and entry-level engineers.
- Offers the framework to intern mentors and first-time engineering managers so that check-ins after each step reveal a mentee's reasoning.
- Reports less backtracking, fewer interruptions, simpler projects, less toil and operational burden, and higher success odds for projects led with the framework.
- Treats shared mental frameworks as a way to decentralize decision-making while its engineering and data teams double in size.
- Draws on outside influences - Nathan Yergler on the cost of interrupts and a First Round Review decision matrix - rather than presenting the framework as wholly original.

## Evidence
- Engineering coaching: [[understand-design-build-a-framework-for-problem-solving-lob-blog]] says Lob coaches engineers to become great problem solvers by decomposing the process into Understand, Design, and Build.
- Framework outcomes: [[understand-design-build-a-framework-for-problem-solving-lob-blog]] reports that projects led by engineers who follow the framework involve less backtracking, fewer interruptions, simpler solutions, less toil and operational burden, and a higher chance of success.
- Mentoring use: [[understand-design-build-a-framework-for-problem-solving-lob-blog]] says the framework is offered to intern mentors and first-time engineering managers because checking in after each step gives x-ray vision into new engineers' reasoning.
- Scaling motive: [[understand-design-build-a-framework-for-problem-solving-lob-blog]] ties publishing the framework to engineering and data teams poised to double in size in 2019 and to keeping decisions with the engineers solving problems autonomously.
- Outside influences: [[understand-design-build-a-framework-for-problem-solving-lob-blog]] links Nathan Yergler's writing on interrupt cost and a First Round Review benefits, risks, and mitigations article as the framework's inputs.

## Qualifications
Everything on this page comes from one company blog post published in 2019, so it is self-description rather than independent reporting. The outcome claims are the publisher's framing and are not measured against comparable projects, and the article gives no baseline for what "less backtracking" or "less toil" means. The page also cannot say what Lob's business was, how large the teams actually were, whether the coaching practice persisted, or how engineers who were not being mentored experienced the framework, because the source does not cover those questions.
