## Overview
[[Stratechery]] is the technology-strategy publication context for [[BenThompson]]'s analyses of [[Adobe]]'s subscription model, [[Apple]]'s mature hardware-and-services strategy, Apple's engagement-driven reporting problem, and the unbundling of editorial brands from scalable publishing operations.

## Current Profile
In the Adobe source, Stratechery appears as a strategy-analysis venue that treats a product announcement as evidence of a deeper business-model shift. The article links Adobe's move away from packaged software to SaaS economics, mobile-device fragmentation, app-store policy, and the strategic incentives of [[Apple]], [[Google]], and [[Microsoft]] as platform owners. In the Apple middle-age source, Stratechery uses earnings-call framing, product history, and business-model reasoning to argue that Apple was not becoming a services company; Services, Apple Music, and HomePod made more sense as tools for differentiating and selling hardware to an already-large installed base. In the Apple social-network source, Stratechery uses Apple's unit-reporting change and Today at Apple presentation to argue that Apple's mature growth story needs customer-level metrics such as active customers and revenue per user. In the faceless-publisher source, it uses The Ringer's Vox Media partnership to argue that digital publishing can separate creator-owned identity from reusable sales, technology, and business operations.

## Key Characteristics
- Publishes technology strategy analysis centered on business models and platforms.
- Uses company announcements as entry points into broader economic and ecosystem questions.
- Connects software pricing models to app-store policy and platform competition.
- Distinguishes financial narratives from underlying business-model incentives.
- Uses company history and market maturity to explain why strategies can change over time.
- Connects public reporting choices to whether a company's strategic narrative can be evaluated.
- Uses organizational boundaries and scale economics to distinguish differentiated editorial work from reusable publishing infrastructure.

## Qualifications
This page is based on four articles and does not describe Stratechery's full publication model, subscription business, audience, or later analytical frameworks. The faceless-publisher argument is a 2017 forecast grounded chiefly in one announced partnership, not a measured account of the model's later prevalence or performance.
