## Overview
[[DisneyInfinity]] was Disney's internally developed toys-to-life game franchise and the central 2016 example in the source of a successful product being closed before an expected decline.

## Current Profile
Business Insider reports that Disney Infinity, together with studio closures, helped Disney's game business become profitable after years of losses and reached a reported billion-dollar franchise milestone. Disney nevertheless stopped production and closed developer Avalanche Software because management expected weaker toys-to-life growth and judged development costs too high. The case distinguishes cumulative franchise revenue from confidence in future category economics.

## Key Characteristics
- Combined video games with the toys-to-life category.
- Became Disney's strongest cited internal-game success after earlier large-game investments disappointed.
- Reached a reported billion-dollar franchise milestone.
- Helped the broader game division turn profitable alongside cost reduction from studio closures.
- Was discontinued when Disney forecast weaker category growth and high continuing development costs.

## Evidence
- Turnaround role: [[inside-disneys-messy-video-game-business-business-insider]] says Disney Infinity and studio closures helped end years of game-business losses.
- Scale: [[inside-disneys-messy-video-game-business-business-insider]] reports that the franchise reached one billion dollars after an earlier executive prediction.
- Closure rationale: [[inside-disneys-messy-video-game-business-business-insider]] reproduces Disney's explanation that toys-to-life lacked growth and development costs made the model challenging.
- Organizational effect: [[inside-disneys-messy-video-game-business-business-insider]] says stopping the franchise also meant closing Avalanche Software.

## Qualifications
The source does not define whether the billion-dollar figure is retail sales, recognized revenue, or another franchise measure, and it provides no product-level profit, inventory, forecast, or cost data. The game division's return to profit also coincided with studio closures, so the article cannot isolate Disney Infinity's contribution. Ending the product before forecast decline may reflect rational capital allocation as well as risk aversion.
