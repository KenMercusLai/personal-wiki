## Overview
[[JordanStaniscia]] is represented in this wiki as the author of a practitioner essay on hover, touch-enabled laptops, and modality-independent interface design.

## Current Profile
Staniscia turns a failed user-research session into a design rule for hybrid-device interaction. After a Surface Pro user could not reveal hover-only commenting controls, he argues that laptop layout no longer implies pointer capability. His resulting position is not that hover should disappear, but that it should provide feedback or optional shortcuts while essential actions retain a touch-operable route.

## Key Characteristics
- Grounds an interaction-design prescription in direct observation of a failed task.
- Challenges mouse-centered assumptions about desktop web interfaces and laptop users.
- Retains hover as progressive feedback while rejecting it as the exclusive trigger for essential actions.

## Evidence
Observed failure:
- [[hover-is-dead-long-live-hover]] recounts a Surface Pro session in which touch scrolling never exposed an inline-commenting interface.

Design prescription:
- [[hover-is-dead-long-live-hover]] recommends hover for clickability feedback and shortcuts only when a finger-operable primary action remains available.

Organizational lesson:
- [[hover-is-dead-long-live-hover]] describes the author's team dismissing the first failure as an anomaly and later urges teams to investigate similar user reports.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one short 2016 essay and describes only the design stance evidenced there, not Staniscia's broader biography or body of work. The article's device-market claims and forecast are historical and do not independently establish how widespread each failure mode became.
