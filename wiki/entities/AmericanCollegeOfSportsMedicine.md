## Overview
The [[AmericanCollegeOfSportsMedicine]] (ACSM) is presented as a professional organization that translates exercise science through certifications, programs, publishing, and practitioner guidelines.

## Current Profile
Two 2013 sources place ACSM between exercise research and frontline practice. An affiliated editorial credits its certifications, programs, and [[ACSMHealthAndFitnessJournal]] with translating science for professionals. A HICT article uses ACSM resistance, aerobic, and high-intensity exercise guidance as the conventional baseline against which a compact body-weight circuit is designed and qualified. This evidence shows how practitioners invoke ACSM, but it remains affiliated and historical rather than an independent current audit of the organization's standards or effects.

## Key Characteristics
- Operates in the health, fitness, exercise, and sports-medicine professional domain.
- Offers certifications and other professional programs.
- Supports a practitioner-facing journal intended to translate science into practical guidance.
- Is presented as helping professionals distinguish evidence from commercial fads and gimmicks.
- Supplies exercise-volume, intensity, repetition, set, and recovery guidance used as a programming baseline.

## Evidence
- Programs and certifications: [[from-the-editor-acsm-s-health-fitness-journal]] credits ACSM with decades of work translating research through certifications and other programs.
- Publishing role: [[from-the-editor-acsm-s-health-fitness-journal]] identifies its health and fitness journal as a channel for translating science for practitioners.
- Practice orientation: [[from-the-editor-acsm-s-health-fitness-journal]] frames the issue around trend interpretation and applied health topics.
- Conventional programming baseline: [[high-intensity-circuit-training-using-body-weight]] cites ACSM guidance for aerobic duration and intensity and for resistance-training repetitions, load, sets, and recovery.
- HICT dose context: [[high-intensity-circuit-training-using-body-weight]] invokes ACSM high-intensity exercise guidance when recommending repeated circuits rather than assuming one seven-minute round is sufficient for most people.

## Qualifications
Both sources are affiliated practitioner publications from 2013. The editorial does not inventory programs, describe certification standards, or measure outcomes, while the HICT article quotes selected recommendations without reproducing the guideline-development methods or full context. This page should not be read as a current organizational audit or as independent validation of every exercise claim in the article.

## What Changed
- Added ACSM guidance as a concrete baseline for aerobic, resistance, and high-intensity exercise programming.
