## Overview
[[LeaHickman]] is the Adobe product leader in the source's [[CreativeCloud]] transformation case.

## Current Profile
The source presents Hickman as the product leader for Adobe Creative Suite who helped drive the shift from desktop-centric license upgrades to Creative Cloud subscriptions. Her case is framed as large-company product leadership: she had to move a successful company away from a major revenue model, align many functions, create a compelling product vision, use prototypes to make the future concrete, and communicate continuously through resistance.

## Key Characteristics
- Led product for Adobe Creative Suite around 2011.
- Recognized that desktop-centric upgrade economics were becoming strategically weak for customers and Adobe.
- Helped move Adobe toward Creative Cloud as a subscription model across devices and applications.
- Worked with Kevin Lynch on prototypes that made the new foundation compelling.
- Used continuous communication with executives, product teams, and stakeholders to sustain the transition.
- Later moved to InVision according to the source.

## Evidence
- Creative Suite role: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says Hickman led product for Adobe's Creative Suite in 2011.
- Strategic concern: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says she knew the market was changing and the upgrade model was bad for customers and Adobe long term.
- Scale of risk: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says Creative Suite produced about $2B of annual license revenue and roughly half of Adobe's annual revenue.
- Prototypes: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says Hickman worked with Kevin Lynch on prototypes showing the new foundation.
- Communication: [[behind-every-great-product-silicon-valley-product-group]] says she ran a sustained communication campaign with leaders and stakeholders.

## Qualifications
The source treats Hickman's leadership as critical but does not independently measure the relative contribution of executives, engineering, pricing, market pressure, or customer adoption patterns.
