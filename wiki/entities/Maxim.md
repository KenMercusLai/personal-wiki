## Overview
[[Maxim]] is the semiconductor company [[BrettFox]] uses to show both sides of focused competition: a young challenger defeating larger companies and, after growing, a diversified entrant failing to displace smaller specialists.

## Current Profile
Fox's account presents Maxim's competitive position as changing with the market. Early in his career, the company attacked National Semiconductor, Analog Devices, and Texas Instruments with greater focus and won. Later, Fox ran a Maxim division entering communications against AMCC and Vitesse. Although he regarded Maxim's products as superior and cheaper, those specialists held their position because communications was their whole business while it was only another Maxim product line. Maxim therefore functions in the wiki as a reversible case of [[IncumbentAttentionAsymmetry]], not as evidence that company size alone predicts outcomes.

## Key Characteristics
- Semiconductor-company setting for Fox's first-hand competition examples.
- Won as a focused challenger against larger established companies.
- Later became the larger entrant in communications against smaller specialists.
- Could not convert claimed product and price advantages into displacement when the target market was strategically secondary.

## Evidence
- Challenger phase: [[brett-fox-when-vcs-say-giants-will-enter-your-market]] says young Maxim attacked established semiconductor companies and won because it cared more and executed at a high level.
- Communications entry: [[brett-fox-when-vcs-say-giants-will-enter-your-market]] says Fox led a Maxim division competing with AMCC and Vitesse in the late 1990s.
- Failed displacement: [[brett-fox-when-vcs-say-giants-will-enter-your-market]] reports that Maxim's claimed product and price advantages did not unseat the smaller specialists.
- Attention mechanism: [[brett-fox-when-vcs-say-giants-will-enter-your-market]] contrasts AMCC and Vitesse's dependence on communications with Maxim treating it as another product line.

## Qualifications
The profile is deliberately limited to Fox's retrospective account. The source provides no independent product comparison, pricing data, market-share series, customer evidence, or complete company history, and it does not isolate focus from switching costs, distribution, relationships, timing, or execution. "Won" and "couldn't unseat" are Fox's summaries rather than audited outcome measures.
