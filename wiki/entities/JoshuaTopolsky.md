## Overview
[[JoshuaTopolsky]] is a media editor, writer, and entrepreneur represented in the wiki through his critique of novelty-driven, scale-dependent digital publishing.

## Current Profile
Topolsky argues from his experience speaking with media investors, executives, editors, writers, and technologists that the industry repeatedly mistakes a new format or channel for a business-model solution. His proposed alternative combines distinctive editorial work, a meaningful brand, a finite intended audience, product thinking, and a long planning horizon.

## Key Characteristics
- Critic of recurring media-industry bets on fashionable formats and distribution channels.
- Treats quality and audience relevance as upstream of format, reach, and advertising tactics.
- Diagnoses digital abundance and surrendered distribution control as structural changes to publishing.
- Advocates building for a specific committed audience rather than maximizing undifferentiated reach.
- Frames media companies as product-and-storytelling businesses rather than containers for headlines and body copy.

## Evidence
- Novelty critique: [[your-media-business-will-not-be-saved-joshua-topolsky-medium]] rejects video, bots, newsletters, apps, and platform partnerships as standalone rescue strategies.
- Economic diagnosis: [[your-media-business-will-not-be-saved-joshua-topolsky-medium]] connects the loss of physical scarcity, platform-controlled distribution, and programmatic scale to declining audience value.
- Strategic alternative: [[your-media-business-will-not-be-saved-joshua-topolsky-medium]] prioritizes compelling voices, stories, talent, ideas, meaningful brands, finite audiences, and ten-year planning.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one polemical 2016 essay. Its account of industry conversations is not independently documented, its broad claims do not distinguish among media categories, and it does not supply financial results for the alternative business he was then developing.
