## Overview
[[FarnamStreet]] is a publication that translates investing, judgment, decision-making, and learning ideas into reusable mental models, represented here by essays on the [[CircleOfCompetence]], [[ReversibleDecisionMaking]], and writing as a tool for clear thought.

## Current Profile
The circle-of-competence essay is a curated synthesis rather than original research. It assembles Buffett, Munger, and Tom Watson Sr. into a boundary discipline: define what you understand, operate inside it, expand it slowly, and admit ignorance. The reversible-decisions essay uses a similar editorial method, combining Bezos's shareholder-letter distinction with startup velocity, learning loops, sunk costs, a Patton maxim, and a second-hand Gladwell medical example. The writing essay compresses a similar learning model into a short argument: composition reveals ignorance, develops understanding, forces insight-preserving compression, and must be oriented toward the reader. Together they show Farnam Street's recurring form: extract a portable heuristic from attributed examples, widen its application, and add practical guidance.

That portability is useful but can hide classification problems. Knowing whether a domain is inside one's competence, whether a decision is truly reversible, and whether AI support has preserved rather than replaced thought all require judgment the essays do not operationalize. The publication's advice is therefore best treated as a framing aid whose source claims and consequences still need independent scrutiny.

## Key Characteristics
- Publishes essays that translate investing, decision-making, and learning concepts for a general audience, often by curating primary and secondary quotations rather than reporting new research.
- Curates a "latticework of mental models" collection that the circle of competence belongs to.
- Treats an investing rule as a general thinking tool applicable to business and personal life.
- Keeps the practical instruction narrow even while widening the concept's scope: define the perimeter, stay inside it, expand slowly, admit when you do not know.
- Attributes the concept to Buffett while using Munger to extend it beyond markets.
- Uses Bezos's one-way-door/two-way-door distinction to argue for faster learning on reversible choices.
- Treats writing as a method for exposing ignorance, learning, compressing insight, and communicating from the reader's desired destination.

## Qualifications
This page is source-scoped to three essays and does not establish the quality of the publication's wider catalog. The pieces curate attributed material or practitioner arguments rather than report original comparative research. The circle-of-competence essay reproduces Buffett and Munger second-hand; the reversible-decisions essay's medical example is also second-hand and should not be treated as clinical guidance; and the writing essay quotes Graham without linking the original context. Its 70% threshold, startup-advantage claims, and predictions about AI-era fluency and information quality are heuristics rather than measured universal rules.
