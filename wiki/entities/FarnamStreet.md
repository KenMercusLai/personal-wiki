## Overview
[[FarnamStreet]] is the blog that published the 2013 essay carrying the wiki's account of the [[CircleOfCompetence]], and it presents that model as one piece of a wider collection it calls the latticework of mental models.

## Current Profile
The essay is a curated synthesis rather than original research. It assembles material from several external sources - Buffett's 1996 Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letter, a talk in which Buffett describes the competence of a business manager he bought a company from, a line from IBM founder Tom Watson Sr., and Munger's prescription for where to spend a life - and adds an editorial argument that the same boundary discipline applies to capital allocation, to business, and to life generally. Its practical instruction stays deliberately narrow: define the perimeter of what you understand, operate inside it, expand it slowly, and admit ignorance rather than extending a conclusion past the evidence. The essay closes by placing the circle of competence inside Farnam Street's latticework of mental models, which is how the publication positions its material: not as domain-specific advice but as one reusable thinking tool among many.

## Key Characteristics
- Publishes essays that translate investing and decision-making concepts for a general audience.
- Builds the essay mainly by quoting primary and secondary sources rather than reporting new research.
- Curates a "latticework of mental models" collection that the circle of competence belongs to.
- Treats an investing rule as a general thinking tool applicable to business and personal life.
- Keeps the practical instruction narrow even while widening the concept's scope: define the perimeter, stay inside it, expand slowly, admit when you do not know.
- Attributes the concept to Buffett while using Munger to extend it beyond markets.
