## Overview
[[ZachHolman]] is the author of a 2016 practitioner essay about evaluating startup employers through interview behavior, compensation promises, product posture, and attitudes toward coworkers and competitors.

## Current Profile
The available source presents Holman as an experienced startup practitioner using humorous first-person interview stories to help candidates recognize organizational warning signs. His central distinction is between the conviction required to build something new and delusion expressed as guaranteed-riches recruiting, public blame, product complacency, or contempt for competitors.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses candidate-side interview observations as evidence about startup culture.
- Treats realistic caution and optimistic humility as healthier leadership signals than certainty about wealth or success.
- Favors blameless recovery, continued product improvement, and nuanced competitive analysis.

## Evidence
- Candidate diligence: [[evaluating-delusional-startups]] turns recruiting promises, missed interviews, blame, colleague talk, product claims, and competitor descriptions into questions for prospective employees.
- Leadership posture: [[evaluating-delusional-startups]] contrasts promises of employee riches with leaders who remain cautious, realistic, and optimistic.
- Learning culture: [[evaluating-delusional-startups]] argues for blameless treatment of mistakes and dissatisfaction with the current product as conditions for improvement.

## Qualifications
The corpus contains one opinionated essay by Holman rather than a broad profile of his work or a comparative study. Its examples are anonymized anecdotes, its compensation comparison comes from friends, and its cultural signals are plausible screening prompts rather than validated predictors.
