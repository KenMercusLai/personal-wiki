## Overview
[[ZachHolman]] is a startup practitioner and former early [[GitHub]] employee represented through essays about evaluating employers and about the personal and organizational consequences of firing.

## Current Profile
Holman's candidate-side framework distinguishes the conviction required to build something new from delusion expressed as guaranteed-riches recruiting, public blame, product complacency, or contempt for competitors. His firing essay extends that concern with organizational truth into employment termination: after being dismissed from GitHub in 2015, he argues for clear expectations, time to evaluate separation terms, secure but considerate offboarding, honest internal communication, and durable alumni relationships.

The later account is deliberately personal. Holman describes burnout, uncertainty about the rationale for his dismissal, depression, equity pressure, and the value of coworker and alumni support. He uses that experience and conversations with other former employees to reject both stigma around being fired and managerial advice that reduces the problem to speed.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses candidate-side interview observations as evidence about startup culture.
- Treats realistic caution and optimistic humility as healthier leadership signals than certainty about wealth or success.
- Favors blameless recovery, continued product improvement, and nuanced competitive analysis.
- Treats termination as an end-to-end human, operational, financial, and cultural process.
- Emphasizes truthful communication and continuing relationships with former employees.

## Evidence
- Candidate diligence: [[evaluating-delusional-startups]] turns recruiting promises, missed interviews, blame, colleague talk, product claims, and competitor descriptions into questions for prospective employees.
- Leadership posture: [[evaluating-delusional-startups]] contrasts promises of employee riches with leaders who remain cautious, realistic, and optimistic.
- Learning culture: [[evaluating-delusional-startups]] argues for blameless treatment of mistakes and dissatisfaction with the current product as conditions for improvement.
- Termination process: [[firing-people]] connects warning, decision-making under stress, access removal, written separation terms, benefits, equity, coworker support, and alumni relations.
- Personal scope: [[firing-people]] grounds the framework in Holman's own dismissal, uncertain rationale, post-employment depression, and conversations with other former employees.

## Qualifications
The corpus contains two opinionated practitioner essays rather than a broad profile or comparative research. The interview examples and termination stories are anecdotal, and Holman remained uncertain about why GitHub dismissed him. His employment, HR, tax, benefits, and equity claims reflect a historical U.S. startup context and should not be treated as current professional advice.

## What Changed
- Expanded Holman's profile from candidate diligence to employee termination, offboarding, recovery, and alumni relationships.
