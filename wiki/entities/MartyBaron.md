## Overview
[[MartyBaron]] is the Washington Post editor who, in the 2016 Fortune profile, explains the publication's internet adaptation and confirms that owner [[JeffBezos]] did not direct coverage.

## Current Profile
Baron treats digital survival as an active redesign problem rather than a graceful decline. He supports aggregation augmented with original reporting, expert opinion through PostEverything, and an automated freelance network as alternatives to rebuilding a traditional bureau system. At the same time, he describes a governance boundary: Bezos presses on technology, page speed, subscriptions, and experimentation but leaves editorial decisions to the newsroom.

## Key Characteristics
- Advocates adapting newsroom production and distribution to internet economics.
- Accepts aggregation and distributed freelance assignment when paired with Post reporting and judgment.
- Maintains editorial control under an engaged private owner.

## Evidence
- Digital operating model: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] quotes Baron distinguishing the internet environment from print and describing the Post's aggregation and talent-network changes.
- Adaptation stance: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] quotes him saying he has no interest in dying gracefully.
- Editorial independence: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] says Baron confirms Bezos did not suggest coverage.

## Qualifications
The page reflects one Fortune interview during an early period of Bezos ownership. It does not assess Baron's full editorial record, the quality effects of the new production model, or later owner-newsroom tensions.
