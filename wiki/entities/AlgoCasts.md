## Overview
[[AlgoCasts]] is Hawstein's algorithm teaching video product and the first independent product described in the five-year reflection.

## Current Profile
The source frames AlgoCasts as a successful but limited entry point into independent work. It gave [[Hawstein]] his first post-employment income and let him work on a fixed content-production routine for about two years, but its domestic market, one-time payment model, ongoing content demands, and piracy pushed him toward overseas subscription SaaS.

## Key Characteristics
- Teaches algorithms through scripted, recorded, edited, and published videos.
- Functioned as an information product rather than software-as-a-service.
- Served domestic users with one-time payment.
- Created Hawstein's first independent income after leaving employment.
- Exposed structural weaknesses of small content products, including unstable acquisition and piracy.

## Evidence
- Work routine: [[yi-ge-du-li-chuang-zao-zhe-de-wu-nian]] says the author spent roughly two years researching algorithm problems, writing scripts, recording, editing, and publishing videos.
- Entry product: [[yi-ge-du-li-chuang-zao-zhe-de-wu-nian]] calls AlgoCasts the best starting point available to the author at the time.
- Business-model limits: [[yi-ge-du-li-chuang-zao-zhe-de-wu-nian]] identifies domestic users, one-time fees, and content production as the three problem-bearing keywords.
- Piracy pressure: [[yi-ge-du-li-chuang-zao-zhe-de-wu-nian]] says the product was pirated by multiple sellers, influencing the author's decision not to build domestic-facing products again.

## Qualifications
The source does not say content products are universally poor businesses. It explicitly notes that creators with massive audiences can earn large sums from low-priced books or courses.
