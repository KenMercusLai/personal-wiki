## Overview
[[HQTrivia]] appears as a live mobile trivia product whose rapid concurrent-user growth exposed infrastructure strain, video lag, and the "lag daddy" meme.

## Current Profile
The source presents HQ Trivia as an extreme early-scale consumer product. Within months of launch, it reached peak concurrent usage comparable to top online games, but unlike text-based services it had to deliver live mobile video at that scale. The resulting lag and pixelated host experience were disruptive, yet the article argues that user love kept people returning while the company worked through the kinks.

## Key Characteristics
- Live mobile trivia product.
- Grew quickly to more than one million peak concurrent users, then doubled that scale within another three months according to the source.
- Faced live-video infrastructure strain rather than only static or text-service scaling issues.
- Turned the pixelated-host failure mode into the "lag daddy" meme.

## Evidence
- Launch and scale: [[broken-is-beautiful-lightspeed-venture-partners-medium]] says HQ launched publicly in October 2017 and reached over one million peak concurrent users within three months.
- Live-video difficulty: [[broken-is-beautiful-lightspeed-venture-partners-medium]] contrasts HQ's live mobile video challenge with Twitter's text service.
- Meme evidence: [[broken-is-beautiful-lightspeed-venture-partners-medium]] describes "lag daddy" as pixelated host Scott Rogowsky and says the disruptions became a meme.

## Qualifications
The source uses HQ Trivia as an example of user love overcoming early infrastructure problems. It does not provide a full business history, retention curve, monetization analysis, operational postmortem, or later-company outcome.
