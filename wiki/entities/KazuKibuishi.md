## Overview
[[KazuKibuishi]] is a comics creator represented here as the source from whom [[JessicaAbel]] learned the term [[IdeaDebt]].

## Current Profile
Kibuishi describes the danger of making a future project mentally grand before attempting it. His snowboarding analogy treats delay as compounding pressure: a person waiting above a jump becomes colder, more afraid, and more invested in an impressive landing. His operating rule is to take the jump in the moment or pass it, allowing action or release instead of prolonged anticipatory debt.

## Key Characteristics
- Names the accumulation of project expectation as idea debt.
- Uses a physical-action analogy to explain how delay compounds fear.
- Recommends immediate commitment or deliberate release.
- Avoids framing future work as a single grand achievement.

## Evidence
- Term attribution: [[imagining-your-future-projects-is-holding-you-back]] says Abel learned “idea debt” from Kibuishi during an interview.
- Snowboarding analogy: [[imagining-your-future-projects-is-holding-you-back]] quotes Kibuishi comparing delayed creative action with waiting fearfully above a jump.
- Practical rule: [[imagining-your-future-projects-is-holding-you-back]] attributes “take the jump or skip it” to Kibuishi.

## Qualifications
The profile relies on excerpts selected and contextualized by Abel rather than Kibuishi's complete interview. The analogy is motivational and does not distinguish low-risk creative experiments from work requiring extensive preparation.
