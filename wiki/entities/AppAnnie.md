## Overview
[[AppAnnie]] appears in the wiki as the mobile analytics firm behind comparative [[GooglePlay]] and [[AppStore]] estimates for 2015 and Q4 2017.

## Current Profile
Across the VentureBeat and AppleInsider reports, App Annie is the evidence provider for a repeated platform-economics pattern: Google Play had far more downloads, while Apple's App Store generated much more direct consumer spend. The 2015 retrospective supplies relative annual figures, geography, and game revenue mix; the Q4 2017 report supplies quarterly absolute estimates and contrasting growth rates. App Annie's role is measurement rather than strategy, and both articles summarize its findings without reproducing the full methodology.

## Key Characteristics
- Provides app-store market estimates.
- Supplies comparative data for Google Play and Apple's App Store.
- Frames mobile platform performance through downloads, revenue, geography, and category mix.
- Supplies historical comparisons that can reveal whether a platform split persists across periods.

## Evidence
- Platform comparison: [[app-annie-2015-google-play-saw-100-percent-more-downloads-than-ios-app-store-venturebeat]] says the 2015 download and revenue estimates came from App Annie's retrospective report.
- Download metric: [[app-annie-2015-google-play-saw-100-percent-more-downloads-than-ios-app-store-venturebeat]] reports Google Play at 100% more downloads than Apple's App Store.
- Revenue metric: [[app-annie-2015-google-play-saw-100-percent-more-downloads-than-ios-app-store-venturebeat]] reports Apple's App Store at 75% more revenue than Google Play.
- Q4 2017 scale: [[apples-ios-app-store-users-spent-11-5-billion-in-q4-95-more-than-google-play]] attributes more than 19 billion Google Play downloads, roughly 8 billion App Store downloads, and $11.5 billion App Store consumer spend to App Annie.
- Growth comparison: [[apples-ios-app-store-users-spent-11-5-billion-in-q4-95-more-than-google-play]] reports 7% year-over-year download growth and 20% consumer-spend growth.

## Qualifications
Both sources are secondary reports on App Annie estimates rather than the complete original research and do not disclose the full methodology. The 2015 source omits absolute totals, while the Q4 2017 source uses rounded figures that do not reconcile exactly, excludes much of China's Android distribution through its Google Play framing, and adds causal commentary not established by the reported data.

- Added App Annie's Q4 2017 estimates as a second historical observation of the download-versus-spending split.
