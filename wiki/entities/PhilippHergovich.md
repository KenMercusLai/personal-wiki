## Overview
[[PhilippHergovich]] is an economist represented in the wiki as a coauthor, with [[JosueOrtega]], of a model connecting online dating to social integration.

## Current Profile
The 2017 article identifies Hergovich with the University of Vienna and summarizes his work on how digitally created links between previously unconnected people may alter marriage patterns. His contribution is presented through [[OnlineDatingSocialIntegration]]: a network model predicts that a small number of absent ties can sharply increase interracial matching and improve modeled marriage stability, while U.S. observations are offered as consistency evidence rather than causal proof.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses economic and social-network modeling to study partner formation and integration.
- Treats online dating as a mechanism for creating links beyond existing family, friend, work, school, and neighborhood networks.
- Separates model predictions from the weaker causal force of historical timing correlations.

## Evidence
- Research role: [[first-evidence-that-online-dating-is-changing-the-nature-of-society-mit-technology-review]] identifies Hergovich as a coauthor of *The Strength of Absent Ties*.
- Model contribution: [[first-evidence-that-online-dating-is-changing-the-nature-of-society-mit-technology-review]] attributes predictions about interracial marriage and modeled marriage strength to Hergovich and Ortega.
- Empirical restraint: [[first-evidence-that-online-dating-is-changing-the-nature-of-society-mit-technology-review]] says observed U.S. changes are consistent with the hypothesis but do not prove causation.

## Qualifications
This profile is limited to one popular 2017 account of one paper. It does not establish Hergovich's broader research record, and the article's stylized assumptions and observational comparisons do not identify online dating as the sole or main causal driver of changing marriage outcomes.

## What Changed
