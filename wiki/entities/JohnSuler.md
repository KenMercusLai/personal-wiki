## Overview
[[JohnSuler]] is represented in this wiki as the psychologist whose 2004 article “The Online Disinhibition Effect” supplied a multi-factor framework for understanding loosened restraint in online communication.

## Current Profile
Suler's framework distinguishes constructive disclosure from harmful conduct and resists reducing online behavior to anonymity alone. The source attributes to him six mechanisms involving identity, visibility, timing, imagined interaction, dissociation, and authority, along with questions about personality and shifting self-boundaries across media.

## Key Characteristics
- Distinguishes benign from toxic forms of online disinhibition.
- Treats anonymity as one contributor among several interacting online conditions.
- Connects communication affordances with personality and self-presentation.
- Supplies a more granular counterpart to the folk model of [[GreaterInternetFuckwadTheory]].
