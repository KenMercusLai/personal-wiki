## Overview
[[MicheleGilman]] is presented as a University of Baltimore law professor and poverty-law scholar who studies privacy harms experienced by low-income and marginalized people.

## Current Profile
The source uses Gilman as its primary legal interpreter for the [[PrivacyPovertyDivide]]. She connects welfare home visits, benefits verification, employer monitoring, database errors, data brokers, and opaque algorithms into a pattern where poor people are asked to surrender dignity and autonomy as the price of survival. Her perspective is grounded in supervising law students who represent low-income clients in Baltimore, so her privacy argument is not framed as a luxury concern but as part of access to housing, food, healthcare, employment, and legal status.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames privacy as a dignity and autonomy issue for poor people, not only a consumer-data issue for affluent people.
- Argues that surveillance techniques are often tested first on poor and marginalized communities.
- Criticizes legal reasoning that compares welfare recipients to criminal probationers for privacy purposes.
- Describes data-broker and database errors as durable barriers to housing, work, and public benefits.
- Supports legal protections such as explanation rights and clean-slate mechanisms when algorithms or records deny opportunities.

## Evidence
- Privacy as dignity: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] quotes Gilman saying immediate needs matter, but dignity and autonomy are also important.
- Poor-first surveillance: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] cites her warning that novel surveillance techniques are typically used first on the poor.
- Welfare privacy critique: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] uses her response to Wyman v. James and Sanchez v. San Diego to challenge probation-like treatment of welfare mothers.
- Database harms: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] reports her students expunging arrest records that can still persist through commercial data brokers.
- Legal reform stance: [[another-tax-on-the-poor-surrendering-privacy-for-survival]] presents her support for stronger explanation rights and attention to privacy have-nots in U.S. legislation.
