## Overview
[[JasonRoberts]] is represented here as the writer who coined “Luck Surface Area” during a TechZing discussion and later developed it into a short framework for increasing exposure to serendipitous opportunities.

## Current Profile
Roberts connects opportunity creation to two complementary practices: doing substantive work driven by genuine interest and telling other people about it effectively. He argues that doing builds expertise and communicates enthusiasm, while telling makes those qualities discoverable to people who may respond with jobs, partnerships, investment, or other unanticipated offers.

His formula `L = D * T` is best read as a rhetorical model of interaction rather than measured mathematics. The source provides no outcome data, comparison group, or account of unequal access to time, audiences, platforms, or safe public visibility.

## Key Characteristics
- Originates the “Luck Surface Area” phrase in the supplied account.
- Treats sustained interest as a route to expertise and socially contagious enthusiasm.
- Frames communication as a multiplier of the opportunity value created by doing.
- Uses a compact equation to make a career and serendipity heuristic memorable.
