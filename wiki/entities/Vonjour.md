## Overview
[[Vonjour]] is the subscription software company used in [[DanielTawfik]]'s retrospective about excessive feature investment, paid acquisition, signup friction, and startup survival.

## Current Profile
The source portrays Vonjour as technically stable and scalable but commercially stagnant after seed capital was directed toward engineering. With feature development curtailed, the company reportedly reinvested revenue into Google Ads, increased signups, and found that acquisition economics and signup conversion mattered more than the marginal features the team had expected to unlock growth.

## Key Characteristics
- Had a stable product whose expanding feature list reportedly failed to produce corresponding revenue growth.
- Reached customers through paid search at a reported average CAC of about $70 against about $35 in monthly subscription revenue.
- Used increased traffic to identify signup friction and reportedly lower CAC from about $130 to $70.
- Operated on a reduced product-development budget after seed funding and payroll pressure constrained further feature work.

## Evidence
- Resource trap: [[its-not-a-feature-problem-avoiding-startup-tarpits-by]] says Vonjour exhausted seed funding on product development without enough growth to make follow-on fundraising easy.
- Customer response: [[its-not-a-feature-problem-avoiding-startup-tarpits-by]] says paid acquisition increased signups and that most users were satisfied despite flaws the team had feared would drive churn.
- Funnel and economics: [[its-not-a-feature-problem-avoiding-startup-tarpits-by]] reports the company's CAC, subscription revenue, and signup-driven CAC improvement.

## Qualifications
Vonjour's profile is based on a single founder-authored retrospective. The source supplies no product description, audited financials, campaign window, customer count, gross margin, churn rate, cohort retention, attribution method, or later company outcome. Its growth projections should therefore be read as illustrative internal planning rather than demonstrated scale economics.

## What Changed
- Created a source-bounded company profile centered on its product-allocation and distribution case.
- Qualified the reported CAC, revenue, conversion, and customer-satisfaction claims as unverified.
