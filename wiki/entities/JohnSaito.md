## Overview
[[JohnSaito]] is a product writer and designer represented here through a 2016 essay about grammatical perspective in interfaces.

## Current Profile
Saito treats interface wording as relationship design. His account distinguishes the user-owned, extension-like feeling of “my” from the conversational, assistant-like feeling of “your,” while treating neutral labels and collective “we” as contextual alternatives rather than defaults. His practical rule is to let clarity and speaker role determine the pronoun: first person can represent the user's action or possession, second person can represent product-authored guidance, and collective language should correspond to meaningful human participation.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames interface copy as a design choice that shapes the perceived relationship between user and product.
- Associates first-person labels with ownership, privacy, personalization, and control.
- Associates second-person labels with product-authored conversation, instruction, and task guidance.
- Prefers omitting possessive language when a neutral label remains unambiguous.
- Warns that “we” can humanize people-powered services but misrepresent automated systems.

## Evidence
Perspective as relationship design:
- [[is-this-my-interface-or-yours-john-saito-medium]] compares “My Channel,” “My Drive,” “Your Music,” and “Your Account” and interprets their implied speaker-user relationships.

Contextual writing guidance:
- [[is-this-my-interface-or-yours-john-saito-medium]] recommends first person for user actions, second person for product questions or instructions, neutral wording where clear, and collective wording where actual people provide the service.

## Qualifications
The profile rests on one practitioner essay. Its interface examples establish real wording variation, but the claimed feelings and behavioral effects are interpretive and are not supported by user-research samples, experiments, accessibility analysis, localization evidence, or outcome measures. The examples also reflect interfaces available in 2016 rather than current product language.

## What Changed
- Established Saito's contextual framework for first-, second-, neutral-, and collective-person interface language.
- Preserved the distinction between observed product wording and unmeasured claims about how that wording feels.
