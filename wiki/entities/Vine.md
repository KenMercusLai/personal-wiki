## Overview
[[Vine]] was a six-second looping-video platform acquired by [[Twitter]] for a reported $30 million in 2012 and launched in 2013. The source presents it as a creator platform whose emergent comedy, personality, and sports culture diverged from its founders' and owner's early artistic vision.

## Current Profile
Vine began with editorial emphasis on stop-motion, visual technique, and art, then became strongly associated with tightly scripted comedy and creator personalities such as King Bach, Lele Pons, and Logan Paul. That change created large audiences and sponsorship income for creators, but the source says it did not create corresponding revenue or strategic clarity for Twitter. Instagram video removed one broad early use case, while Vine's relatively independent operation, weak creator support, abandoned original programming, and inability to settle on an identity contributed to decline. Twitter announced Vine's shutdown in October 2016, then reportedly received acquisition interest after the public response.

## Key Characteristics
- Six-second looping-video format that encouraged compressed visual performance.
- Shifted from an art-oriented editorial identity toward comedy, personality, entertainment, and sports clips.
- Produced creators with millions of followers and meaningful brand-sponsorship income.
- Operated relatively independently after Twitter's acquisition without effective strategic integration or monetization.
- Lost its general-purpose short-video differentiation after Instagram introduced video.
- Damaged creator and user trust through reduced promotion, abandoned projects, and abrupt shutdown communication.

## Evidence
- Format and ownership: [[vine-insiders-say-twitter-never-liked-what-vine-became]] describes a six-second looping-video service acquired by Twitter for $30 million in 2012.
- Identity shift: [[vine-insiders-say-twitter-never-liked-what-vine-became]] contrasts early artistic Editor's Picks with the later dominance of scripted comedy, personality, and sports clips.
- Creator economics: [[vine-insiders-say-twitter-never-liked-what-vine-became]] says top creators earned hundreds of thousands of dollars from sponsored posts while Twitter struggled to monetize the activity.
- Competition: [[vine-insiders-say-twitter-never-liked-what-vine-became]] says Instagram video displaced Vine's early role as an Instagram-like service for video.
- Governance: [[vine-insiders-say-twitter-never-liked-what-vine-became]] describes a hands-off relationship that preserved independence but did not produce faster growth, monetization, or strategic alignment.
- Creator trust: [[vine-insiders-say-twitter-never-liked-what-vine-became]] reports a rejected creator-retention proposal, a cancelled original series, withdrawn promotion, and an abrupt closure announcement.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from a November 2016 report based heavily on two anonymous former employees and one anonymous prominent creator, supplemented by contemporaneous reporting. It captures perceptions around the shutdown rather than a complete internal record, audited business analysis, or proof that paying top creators, deeper integration, or a comedy-first strategy would have made Vine sustainable. Reported monthly-active-user, sponsorship, and creator-payment figures are not independently validated in the source.

## What Changed
- Created Vine as a distinct entity whose emergent creator culture, business-model gap, and acquisition governance shaped its shutdown.
