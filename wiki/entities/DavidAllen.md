## Overview
[[DavidAllen]] is the productivity author whose [[GettingThingsDone]] framework treats the mind as a focusing tool rather than a reliable store for unresolved commitments.

## Current Profile
In [[write-things-down]], Allen is represented through two excerpts from *Getting Things Done*. The first compares overloaded short-term memory to full computer RAM and recommends placing incomplete commitments in a trusted system that will be reviewed. The second argues that unaided memory often surfaces a need when action is impossible rather than at the useful time and place. [[BenThompson]] uses these ideas to interpret both human task management and file-backed AI-agent workflows.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames personal organization as protection of attention rather than maximal scheduling.
- Treats trusted external capture and reliable review as a response to unresolved mental loops.
- Connects reminders to actionable context instead of relying on spontaneous recall.
- Distinguishes a productivity system from the action required to finish work.

## Evidence
- External capture: [[write-things-down]] quotes Allen comparing incomplete commitments in short-term memory to overloaded RAM.
- Trusted review: [[write-things-down]] says writing an open loop down only helps when it enters a bucket that will be reviewed appropriately.
- Contextual reminders: [[write-things-down]] quotes Allen's dead-battery example to show that unaided recall often arrives when action is unavailable.
- Practical influence: [[write-things-down]] traces Thompson's use of inboxes, next actions, ticklers, assistants, and agent status boards back to Allen's method.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one secondary article quoting and interpreting one book. It does not summarize Allen's full career, the complete GTD method, comparative productivity evidence, or whether the method transfers equally across jobs, resources, neurotypes, and levels of autonomy.
