## Overview
[[SethGodin]] is an author and blogger represented in the wiki by a short essay that redefines hard work around leverage, preparation, emotional effort, and connection.

## Current Profile
In [[seth-godin-hard-work]], Godin uses loading-dock and keyboard-work comparisons to challenge visible strain as the default measure of effort. His practical frame gives cognitive and relational actions—finding a better method, asking to borrow equipment, and preparing before work arrives—the same status as direct execution because they can determine the result.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses compact workplace parables to question familiar measures of effort.
- Treats insight, emotional effort, and human connection as economically important work.
- Values preparation and tool choice when they reduce avoidable physical or repetitive labor.
- Frames initiative and the risk of rejection as part of resourceful execution.

## Evidence
Approach and argument:
- [[seth-godin-hard-work]] contrasts an eight-hour manual unload with a one-hour unload enabled by a borrowed hand truck and ramp.
- [[seth-godin-hard-work]] extends the comparison to advance preparation and to a keyboard worker who finds a plug-in or macro for repetitive work.

View of effort:
- [[seth-godin-hard-work]] explicitly locates modern hard work in insight, emotional effort, and connection rather than brawn alone.

## Qualifications
The profile is based on one brief 2019 essay and describes only the position represented there, not Godin's broader career or body of work. The examples are thought experiments without measured output quality, safety, access, or transferability across jobs.

## What Changed
- Created the profile around Godin's distinction between visible exertion and leveraged effort.
