## Overview
[[DaveBrailsford]] is a cycling performance director represented here through his account of British Cycling and Team Sky's marginal-gains method.

## Current Profile
Brailsford describes marginal gains as a way to map performance into components, measure small controllable changes, and give riders and support staff a shared method for building momentum. His examples span aerodynamics, equipment setup, nutrition, recovery, hygiene, and consistent hotel-room and sleep arrangements. He also narrows the popular account: marginal gains are not mainly novel technology, cannot replace the core factors responsible for performance, and cannot by themselves establish why a dominant team won.

## Key Characteristics
- Combines sport science, psychology, measurement, and team operations in performance design.
- Uses decomposition to turn a daunting ideal performance into controllable next improvements.
- Treats shared mindset and collective buy-in as central to marginal gains.
- Places foundational talent, willingness, and barrier removal ahead of peripheral optimization.
- Acknowledges that cycling's doping history creates a legitimate burden of transparency for dominant teams.

## Evidence
- Measurement and adjustment: [[in-praise-of-incrementalism-ep-264-freakonomics-freakonomics]] describes data feedback and examples such as head position, tire alignment, nutrition, and recovery.
- Operating environment: [[in-praise-of-incrementalism-ep-264-freakonomics-freakonomics]] reports hygiene protocols and a traveling hotel team that standardized cleaning, mattresses, pillows, and sleeping posture.
- Culture and foundations: [[in-praise-of-incrementalism-ep-264-freakonomics-freakonomics]] says the method empowered a group and created momentum, while warning that peripheral gains are pointless without the major drivers of performance.
- Trust boundary: [[in-praise-of-incrementalism-ep-264-freakonomics-freakonomics]] records Brailsford's acceptance of public suspicion after earlier cycling scandals and the episode's subsequent discussion of Team Sky therapeutic-use exemptions.

## Qualifications
The profile is bounded to one 2016 interview and is not a complete biography or current assessment. Team and medal outcomes do not isolate the effect of marginal gains from athlete selection, funding, coaching, equipment, competition, or luck. The episode states that therapeutic-use exemptions were permitted and that wrongdoing was not established, while also recording unresolved ethical and transparency criticism; later findings are outside this source's scope.
