## Overview
[[Optimizely]] is represented here as an experimentation and personalization company applying its own products and methods to a 2016 homepage redesign.

## Current Profile
The source presents Optimizely moving from a single, conversion-focused homepage toward modular experiences tailored to named accounts, industries, geographies, customers, and previously engaged visitors. The company combined its Dynamic Customer Profiles and internal customer or prospect data with Demandbase-supported data, while treating the redesign itself as an A/B test. This is a first-party account of strategy and implementation intent, not evidence of the redesign's eventual business impact.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses controlled experimentation to evaluate changes to digital experiences.
- Connects personalization with target-account, firmographic, geographic, customer-status, and behavioral signals.
- Treats page architecture as infrastructure for changing messages, creative, proof, content, and calls to action by audience.
- Evaluates acquisition quality beyond raw account creation or form completion.
- Presents its own homepage as both a marketing surface and a demonstration of product capability.

## Evidence
- Experimentation practice: [[designing-for-personalization-the-story-of-optimizelys-homepage-optimizely-blog]] says the old and new homepages were being tested against each other before full rollout.
- Audience data: [[designing-for-personalization-the-story-of-optimizelys-homepage-optimizely-blog]] describes combining internal customer and prospect data, Dynamic Customer Profiles, and Demandbase-supported data.
- Modular design: [[designing-for-personalization-the-story-of-optimizelys-homepage-optimizely-blog]] identifies a flexible hero, personalization modules, customer validation, and promotion cards as variable components.
- Outcome breadth: [[designing-for-personalization-the-story-of-optimizelys-homepage-optimizely-blog]] lists engagement, lead conversion, accounts created, and lead qualification as test metrics while also discussing sales velocity.

## Qualifications
The only source is Optimizely's own pre-results blog post. It does not publish audience-assignment accuracy, sample sizes, experimental outcomes, privacy analysis, implementation cost, maintenance burden, or evidence that visitors valued the tailored experiences.
