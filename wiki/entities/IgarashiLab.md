## Overview
[[IgarashiLab]] is the University of Tokyo research lab where [[AmyWibowo]] led a creative interface project for designing clothing patterns with a tracked pen and mannequin.

## Current Profile
The source presents Igarashi Lab as a Media-Lab-like environment that funded Wibowo's own research idea rather than only assigning her to someone else's project. Her interface let a person draw garment lines around a life-size mannequin, generate a 3D dress model, flatten the surface into 2D pattern pieces, and then sew the resulting design.

## Key Characteristics
- Supported a self-directed art-and-technology research project.
- Treated clothing-pattern design as a legitimate computational-interface topic.
- Connected physical drawing, 3D modeling, geometric flattening, and sewing.
- Also became the setting for harassment and later institutional policy changes described in the source.

## Evidence
- Research opportunity: [[a-career-retrospective-10-years-working-in-tech-sailor-mercury-medium]] says the lab asked what Wibowo would work on with her own funding and then funded the clothing-pattern idea.
- Interface workflow: [[a-career-retrospective-10-years-working-in-tech-sailor-mercury-medium]] describes motion-tracked drawing on a mannequin, 3D dress generation, and 2D pattern flattening.
- Research output: [[a-career-retrospective-10-years-working-in-tech-sailor-mercury-medium]] says she built the system, ran user studies, and published a paper.
- Workplace harm: [[a-career-retrospective-10-years-working-in-tech-sailor-mercury-medium]] says a research advisor withheld advice outside private dinners and escalated into harassment.
