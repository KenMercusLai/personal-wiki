## Overview
[[Homebrew]] is the venture firm presenting the startup compensation and offer-process guidance in [[quip]].

## Current Profile
In this source, Homebrew acts as an investor and talent adviser to portfolio companies. Its stated compensation philosophy favors market-average cash for the startup's stage and comparatively strong equity for early employees, treating ownership as partial compensation for joining an unproven management team, mission, product, and business. The firm also describes helping companies with backchannel references, candidate-closing calls, network access, and compensation review.

The profile is source-bounded: the article does not establish how consistently portfolio companies followed the guidance, whether the practices improved hiring or retention, or whether its 2015 market examples remain applicable.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames compensation policy as an early company-design responsibility connected to mission and values.
- Recommends median-market cash and upper-quartile equity as a general early-stage target.
- Treats substantial early employee ownership as risk sharing and mission alignment.
- Supports portfolio recruiting through references, candidate conversations, network access, and operating advice.
- Presents formulaic bands, explicit exceptions, and consistent offer handling as protections against arbitrary outcomes.

## Evidence
- Compensation philosophy: [[quip]] says Homebrew favors market-average salaries for the startup's phase and “over-equitizing” early employees.
- Risk and alignment: [[quip]] argues that early employees are betting on an uncertain team, mission, idea, and business and should receive meaningful ownership.
- Portfolio support: [[quip]] describes Homebrew helping with references, investor closing calls, compensation review, and candidate or family networks.
- Process discipline: [[quip]] recommends preset salary and equity bands, defined negotiation boundaries, and a repeatable exception-approval process.

## Qualifications
The evidence is one Homebrew-authored guide containing historical compensation examples and practitioner recommendations. It supplies no portfolio adoption rate, candidate or employee perspectives, demographic analysis, controlled comparison, retention result, or realized equity outcome. Some proposed recruiting tactics—especially informal references and candidate-closing pressure—require stronger consent, fairness, privacy, and legal safeguards than the source specifies.
