## Overview
[[TrevorMcKendrick]] is an essayist represented in the wiki by a critique of simplified founder histories.

## Current Profile
McKendrick uses [[SamWalton]]'s pre-Walmart retail career to argue that entrepreneurs learn more from the long period of mistakes, experiments, recovery, and capability development than from a polished company-origin anecdote. His framing opposes idea-first mythology and emphasizes [[AdaptivePersistence]].

## Key Characteristics
- Writes about entrepreneurship through historical case reconstruction.
- Treats narrative selection as a source of distorted business lessons.
- Emphasizes operating psychology, persistence, and correction over the isolated startup idea.

## Evidence
- Founder-story critique: [[why-you-should-ignore-every-founders-story-about-how-they-started-their-company-trevor-mckendrick]] calls company founding stories non-malicious distortions that can mislead learners.
- Walton reconstruction: [[why-you-should-ignore-every-founders-story-about-how-they-started-their-company-trevor-mckendrick]] traces Walton's career from his first franchise through the first Walmart.
- Practical lesson: [[why-you-should-ignore-every-founders-story-about-how-they-started-their-company-trevor-mckendrick]] tells readers to study entrepreneurs' actual beginnings rather than their famous endpoints.

## Qualifications
This profile covers one essay and should not be treated as a complete account of McKendrick's career, companies, investments, or broader writing.
