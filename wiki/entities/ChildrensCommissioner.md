## Overview
The [[ChildrensCommissioner|Children’s Commissioner for England]] is the public institution whose 2018 report *Who knows what about me?* anchors the source's account of children's data collection, sharing, profiling, and practical protections.

## Current Profile
In this source, the Commissioner acts as a child-rights interpreter of the data ecosystem. The report, introduced by then-commissioner Anne Longfield, is cited for the claim that parents had posted about 1,300 photos and videos by a child's thirteenth birthday; it distinguishes data given deliberately from data given off or inferred, warns that profiling may shape later opportunities, and addresses risks extending from social posts to connected toys and baby monitors. Its retained parent/carer guidance combines disclosure minimization with device security and institutional accountability.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames children's data collection and sharing as a long-term rights and welfare issue.
- Distinguishes explicit disclosure from observed, given-off, and inferred data.
- Connects family posts with commercial profiling, identity risk, connected devices, and consequential decisions.
- Recognizes potentially beneficial uses of children's data while warning about durable records and abuse.
- Gives parents and carers concrete privacy, device-security, update, authenticity, and accountability guidance.

## Qualifications
The page represents the institution only through a 2018 article's account of one report and its screenshot. It does not independently inspect the complete report, underlying studies, statutory remit, later publications, policy outcomes, or current officeholder. The 1,300-item statistic is therefore retained as a reported figure rather than treated as a universally measured rate.
