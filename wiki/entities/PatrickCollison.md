## Overview
[[PatrickCollison]] is a [[Stripe]] co-founder represented through the company's origin, developer-infrastructure strategy, hiring discipline, product judgment, and leadership transition under growth.

## Current Profile
Collison describes Stripe as a response to the gap between increasingly easy software creation and the difficulty of accepting payments online. He and [[JohnCollison]] tested a private prototype through friends and [[YCombinator]], then expanded it into infrastructure for increasingly complex internet money flows. His management account favors long, persistent early recruiting; real-work engineering interviews; conventional organization structures unless evidence warrants deviation; CEO attention to long-horizon product choices; and a gradual shift from direct decisions toward strategy, culture, senior leadership selection, writing, and delegation.

## Key Characteristics
- Frames Stripe as enabling infrastructure for internet businesses rather than a simple payment transfer product.
- Values historical reading and detailed software work as defenses against shallow reinvention.
- Prefers slow, high-conviction early hiring and work samples that resemble the job.
- Treats initial product-market pull as the start of further product, partnership, and operational work.
- Favors stable organizational defaults while questioning copied hiring proxies that lack predictive evidence.
- Uses durable writing and explicit broadcast communication to replace lost shared context at scale.
- Reduces the CEO role to strategy, culture, senior management selection, and optional ownership of one function.

## Evidence
- Founding and market thesis: [[cs183c-session-11-patrick-collison-stripe-blitzscaling-class-notes-and-essays-medium]] traces the `/dev/payments` prototype, early YC users, low internet share of consumer spending, and demand for tools supporting complex money flows.
- Hiring method: both [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] and [[cs183c-session-11-patrick-collison-stripe-blitzscaling-class-notes-and-essays-medium]] describe a six-month search for Stripe's first two hires and week-long trials.
- Candidate leverage: [[cs183c-session-11-patrick-collison-stripe-blitzscaling-class-notes-and-essays-medium]] says an early hire should be evaluated partly through the many later people they may recruit or attract.
- Product and organization: [[cs183c-session-11-patrick-collison-stripe-blitzscaling-class-notes-and-essays-medium]] describes CEO involvement in long-horizon product choices, engineering-led product teams, and skepticism toward needless organizational novelty.
- Communication threshold: [[16-lessons-on-scaling-from-eric-schmidt-reid-hoffman-marissa-mayer-brian-chesky-diane-greene-jeff-weiner-and-more]] and [[cs183c-session-11-patrick-collison-stripe-blitzscaling-class-notes-and-essays-medium]] say growth past roughly 150 employees required more formal broadcast communication because newer employees lacked earlier decision context.
- Writing and CEO scope: [[cs183c-session-11-patrick-collison-stripe-blitzscaling-class-notes-and-essays-medium]] argues that writing persists, can be corrected, and adds rigor, then cites strategy, culture, senior management, and optional functional involvement as the CEO's core remit.

## Qualifications
The account is a 2015 founder interview and a later multi-speaker class synthesis, not an independent evaluation of Stripe's performance or a current company profile. Claims about founder tenure, interviews, organization defaults, and executive roles are practitioner judgments whose applicability varies by team, labor context, and company stage.
