## Overview
[[BrieCode]] is a game designer and studio founder represented in this wiki by her argument that the game industry mistakes a narrow established audience for the limits of interactive entertainment.

## Current Profile
Code draws on fourteen years of work including *Company of Heroes*, *Assassin's Creed*, and *Child of Light*, then subjects her own assumptions to informal testing with friends who do not identify as gamers. Their reactions lead her from recommending established titles toward making games with underserved players, centered on care, identity, relationships, compassion, and difficult questions from everyday life.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats non-participation as a possible design failure rather than proof that an audience cannot value interactive experiences.
- Uses conversations and observed play to challenge expert and enthusiast assumptions.
- Connects workforce homogeneity to constrained references, theories, and product possibilities.
- Advocates games built around care, social connection, identity, and meaning rather than default combat and stimulation.
- Frames audience care as both an artistic responsibility and a practical market-development method.

## Evidence
- Industry experience: [[video-games-are-boring-gamesindustry-biz]] identifies Code's work across several major game franchises and her role on *Child of Light*.
- Assumption revision: [[video-games-are-boring-gamesindustry-biz]] describes how her cousin's attachment to Lydia in *Skyrim* exposed value that neither person had initially articulated.
- Design diagnosis: [[video-games-are-boring-gamesindustry-biz]] groups non-gaming friends' objections around depth, repellent representation, missing cultural references, inaccessible controls, and unwanted action.
- Studio direction: [[video-games-are-boring-gamesindustry-biz]] says Code founded a studio to make games with people who do not like existing games and to escape established paradigms.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from one first-person 2016 essay rather than an independent biography or evidence about the studio's later products and outcomes. Its audience observations are qualitative and centered on Code's friends, especially her cousin, so they generate design hypotheses rather than population-level conclusions.
