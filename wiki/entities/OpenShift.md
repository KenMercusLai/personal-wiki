## Overview
[[OpenShift]] is the Red Hat software suite that [[BenThompson]] identifies as the strategic prize in IBM's announced 2018 acquisition of [[RedHat]].

## Current Profile
The essay presents OpenShift as a layer for building and managing [[Kubernetes]] containers across on-premises infrastructure and competing public clouds. Its strategic promise was portability: IBM could offer a consistent hybrid-management layer without first matching the hyperscale infrastructure of AWS, Microsoft, or Google. The source does not evaluate OpenShift's implementation, adoption, or later outcomes.

## Key Characteristics
- Builds on Kubernetes and container portability.
- Targets application operation across private and public infrastructure.
- Supplies the product center of IBM's proposed hybrid-cloud strategy.
- Lets IBM operate above infrastructure providers rather than duplicate them.
- Remains a strategic proposition rather than an outcome in this source.

## Evidence
- Acquisition value: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] calls OpenShift the real prize beyond Red Hat's RHEL subscription cash flow.
- Portability role: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] connects Kubernetes portability to running across AWS, Azure, Google Cloud, and on-premises systems.
- Hybrid role: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] says IBM expected Red Hat products to bridge private data centers and multiple public clouds.

## Qualifications
The article offers a 2018 analyst interpretation of acquisition logic, not technical benchmarking or evidence of later commercial success. Portability at the orchestration layer does not remove every provider-specific service, data, operational, or commercial dependency.

## What Changed
- Created OpenShift's profile as the portable orchestration layer in IBM's 2018 hybrid-cloud thesis.

## Relationships
- [[RedHat]] - company that developed and sold OpenShift.
