## Overview
[[Basecamp]] is the collaboration and project-management product that [[37signals]] uses as the central system for its own internal communication.

## Current Profile
In the source, Basecamp is not merely a set of messaging features. It is the shared location for company-wide discussions, social exchanges, project work, ideas, debates, status and policy updates, decisions, and announcements. Automatic prompts create recurring reflection and planning, while project-local comments attach communication to the task or document it concerns. The resulting operating claim is that one durable workspace can reduce fragmented context, internal email, separate chat tools, meetings, and search ambiguity.

## Key Characteristics
- Centralizes nearly all reported 37signals internal communication in one workspace.
- Supports automatic daily, weekly, and occasional social check-in questions.
- Keeps discussions attached to projects, tasks, and documents through contextual comments.
- Holds durable announcements, policies, decisions, and historical records for current and future employees.
- Supports broad awareness through Heartbeats and Kickoffs without exposing everyone to every project detail.

## Evidence
- Communication hub: [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] says 98% of 37signals' internal communication occurs inside Basecamp.
- Recurring prompts: [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] describes automatic daily work reflections, Monday planning prompts, and optional social questions.
- Six-week cadence: [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] describes retrospective Heartbeats and prospective Kickoffs written for company-wide reading.
- Spatial context: [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] says task and document discussions remain attached to those objects inside separate project spaces.
- Durable record: [[guide-37signals-how-we-communicate]] contrasts centralized announcements and policy comments with decentralized internal email.

## Qualifications
The profile is based on a first-party guide written by the company's co-founder. It describes intended practice rather than independently observed usage, gives no product comparison or employee survey, and does not show that Basecamp's fit transfers to organizations with different size, regulation, workflows, accessibility needs, or incident-response requirements.
