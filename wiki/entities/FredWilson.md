## Overview
[[FredWilson]] is an investor and AVC author whose ingested essays distinguish active from passive investing, analyze changes in early-stage financing through [[UnionSquareVentures]]' portfolio experience, and recall [[JeffLawson]]'s seed pitch for [[Twilio]].

## Current Profile
Wilson prefers active investing because it lets capital be amplified by time, energy, judgment, and influence, but he accepts that active and passive models can both work when their portfolio structures fit their demands. His valuation analysis adds an empirical side to that framework: he uses 55 USV-led or co-led rounds to argue that seed and Series A prices rose between 2012 and 2017, while preserving limits around geography, thin Series B evidence, and the long delay before entry prices can be connected to realized returns. His Twilio retrospective adds an experiential evaluation style: a concise, surprising platform claim became credible when Lawson immediately demonstrated the core capability.

## Key Characteristics
- Distinguishes investing modes by the investor's role, not only by asset class.
- Prefers active investing when pursuing large gains.
- Treats active investing as capacity-constrained because attention and judgment do not scale like capital.
- Warns angel, seed, and venture investors against claiming active support across portfolios too broad to serve actively.
- Uses his firm's historical investments as a directional market sample rather than presenting them as a universal benchmark.
- Treats a fast, claim-aligned product demonstration as unusually persuasive seed-stage evidence.

## Evidence
- Role distinction: [[active-vs-passive-investing-avc]] contrasts buying public shares, REITs, treasury bills, and led angel deals with buying board-level stakes, developing property, hard-money lending, and leading seed rounds.
- Active preference: [[active-vs-passive-investing-avc]] says large gains require investing time, energy, and intellect as well as capital.
- Capacity constraint: [[active-vs-passive-investing-avc]] says active investing limits how many investments one can make.
- Venture-market warning: [[active-vs-passive-investing-avc]] criticizes angel, seed, and VC investors who try to scale their investing while pretending to be active investors.
- Market analysis: [[valuation-inflation-avc]] reports higher seed and Series A pre-money valuations between the 2012 and 2017 timeframes from 55 USV-led or co-led rounds.
- Evidentiary restraint: [[valuation-inflation-avc]] declines to infer a Series B trend from eight rounds and says the later investment cohorts were too immature for return conclusions.
- Seed-pitch judgment: [[best-seed-pitch-ever-avc]] recounts Lawson reducing telephony to five API calls and live-coding an application that made Wilson's phone ring within about 30 seconds.

## Qualifications
The active-investing source is a short opinion essay, the valuation source is a descriptive review of one firm's selected led or co-led investments rather than a market-wide dataset, and the Twilio source is a retrospective anecdote about an exceptional pitch. They do not provide realized return comparisons, causal evidence about pitch effectiveness, or a full account of Wilson's investing career.
