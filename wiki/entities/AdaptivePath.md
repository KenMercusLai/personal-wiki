## Overview
[[AdaptivePath]] appears in the wiki as the design and research organization that worked with [[Twitter]] on a 2009 user-growth research project later cited by [[JoshElman]].

## Current Profile
The source attributes part of Twitter's early adoption learning to a project with Adaptive Path. Interviews reportedly showed that many people first understood Twitter as a broadcasting megaphone and left when they had nothing to say, whereas returners who deliberately found useful accounts built a valuable timeline. Elman says that learning informed later onboarding and messaging centered on finding and following.

## Key Characteristics
- Design and user-research organization associated with Twitter's 2009 growth research.
- Helped investigate why some users abandoned Twitter and later returned.
- Contributed to learning that following relevant accounts was a key activation behavior.

## Evidence
- Project role: [[four-questions-towards-understanding-user-adoption-of-your-product]] says Twitter ran a 2009 user-growth research project with Adaptive Path.

- Product consequence: [[four-questions-towards-understanding-user-adoption-of-your-product]] says Twitter subsequently rebuilt onboarding and revised messaging around finding and following.
