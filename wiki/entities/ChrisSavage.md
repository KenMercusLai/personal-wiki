## Overview
[[ChrisSavage]] is represented in the wiki as a [[Wistia]] co-founder who reversed a growth-at-all-costs strategy, negotiated an investor buyout, and connected restored founder control with profitable product focus and employee profit sharing.

## Current Profile
The Inc. article presents Savage as a founder who internalized pressure to sacrifice profitability for faster growth, then concluded that the resulting sales expansion, advertising, experiments, losses, and cultural strain had distracted Wistia from its core product. After three acquisition offers forced a strategic review, he and co-founder Brendan Schwartz chose to borrow $17.3 million, offer investors a reported 10x return, and state that no later liquidity event was planned. Savage also describes replacing employee options with profit sharing as a way to make operating improvements financially meaningful to the whole company.

## Key Characteristics
- Co-founded Wistia with Brendan Schwartz in 2006.
- Publicly acknowledges his own role in adopting growth-at-all-costs assumptions.
- Preferred renewed product innovation, profitability, customer value, and employee culture to a sale or continued venture-scale pursuit.
- Helped negotiate a debt-financed share repurchase that restored full founder control.
- Set explicit expectations that investors could sell any portion but should not expect another near-term liquidity event.
- Reports unusually strong employee interest in financial performance after Wistia introduced profit sharing.

## Evidence
- Strategic reversal: [[heres-how-and-why-entrepreneurs-are-getting-venture-investors-out-of-their-companies-inc-com]] quotes Savage describing how the growth push distracted Wistia and made the company feel unlike the one employees had valued.
- Control transaction: [[heres-how-and-why-entrepreneurs-are-getting-venture-investors-out-of-their-companies-inc-com]] reports that Savage and Schwartz used $17.3 million in debt and a 10x investor offer to regain full control.
- Liquidity framing: [[heres-how-and-why-entrepreneurs-are-getting-venture-investors-out-of-their-companies-inc-com]] quotes Savage telling investors that they could sell as much or as little as they wanted but should not plan on another liquidity event.
- Incentive redesign: [[heres-how-and-why-entrepreneurs-are-getting-venture-investors-out-of-their-companies-inc-com]] attributes to Savage the comparison between hard-to-value employee options and concrete, recurring profit sharing.

## Qualifications
The profile comes from one journalistic article built largely around founder interviews. It does not include loan documents, investor accounts, employee interviews, audited results, or evidence about Wistia's later repayment, growth, culture, or performance. Savage's interpretation is useful first-person evidence about the decision, not a complete evaluation of the transaction.
