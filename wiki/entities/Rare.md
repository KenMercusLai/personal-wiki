## Overview
[[Rare]] is the game developer credited by the source with legitimizing console first-person shooters through GoldenEye 007 on Nintendo 64.

## Current Profile
GamesRadar+ frames Rare as the studio that showed FPS design could work culturally and mechanically on consoles before Halo. GoldenEye 007 used the James Bond license, console-optimized level design, four-player local multiplayer, slower pacing, and stealth to reach a living-room audience that had not yet adopted FPS games as deeply as PC players.

## Key Characteristics
- Converted a licensed console game into a major FPS milestone.
- Adapted FPS design around controller limits and local multiplayer play.
- Shifted console FPS pacing toward more methodical and stealth-oriented action.
- Helped broaden FPS culture beyond arcades and PC keyboard/mouse play.

## Evidence
- Console legitimacy: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] says GoldenEye 007 changed the status of console shooters in August 1997.
- Social multiplayer: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] highlights GoldenEye's four-player local multiplayer and Bond character roster.
- Design adaptation: [[a-43-year-history-of-first-person-shooters-from-maze-war-to-destiny-2-gamesradar]] says GoldenEye moved away from Doom and Wolfenstein's run-and-gun style toward slower, option-driven, stealth-inflected play.

## Qualifications
The source discusses Rare almost entirely through GoldenEye 007. It does not cover the studio's broader catalog or later history.
