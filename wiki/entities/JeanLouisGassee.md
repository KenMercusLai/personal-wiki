## Overview
[[JeanLouisGassee]] appears in the wiki as the author of a comparative analysis of why major pre-iPhone smartphone incumbents failed after [[Apple]] and [[Google]] shifted the industry into the modern [[MobileEcosystem]].

## Current Profile
In the BlackBerry source, Gassee's analytical role is to separate superficially similar smartphone failures into different causal patterns. He treats [[BlackBerry]] as a case of recognizing the iPhone's software threat but failing to act quickly enough, [[Palm]] as a case of technically plausible but late recovery, [[Nokia]] as a case of understanding ecosystem competition while triggering an [[OsborneEffect]], and [[Microsoft]] as a case where the mobile OS licensing model was undermined by free [[Android]].

## Key Characteristics
- Writes as a technology-strategy observer comparing incumbent responses to platform shifts.
- Distinguishes technical platform weakness from human decision error and business-model collapse.
- Uses BlackBerry, Palm, Nokia, and Microsoft as contrast cases rather than one uniform cautionary tale.

## Evidence
- Comparative frame: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] asks whether the failures came from human error, technological inadequacy, or business-model collapse.
- BlackBerry diagnosis: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] argues BlackBerry saw the software threat but waited years before buying [[QNX]].
- Nokia and Microsoft contrast: [[blackberry-meditation-at-the-grave-jean-louis-gassee-medium]] separates Nokia's [[OsborneEffect]] from Microsoft's [[PlatformBusinessModelObstacle]].

## Qualifications
The page is grounded in one opinion essay from 2016. It captures Gassee's interpretation of smartphone history rather than a full biography or independent measurement of each company's later outcomes.
