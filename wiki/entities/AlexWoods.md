## Overview
[[AlexWoods]] is a software-oriented writer represented in this wiki by an essay arguing that human-owned writing develops understanding and earns trust in ways that finished LLM-generated prose may not.

## Current Profile
Woods treats documents as answers to questions and writing as the work of finding structure inside uncertainty. His position is selective rather than anti-AI: models can support research, checking, transcription, capture, and brainstorming, but the human should perform the central reasoning and composition when a document is meant to demonstrate judgment or support leadership.

## Key Characteristics
- Treats writing as a method for thinking, questioning, and learning rather than a text-production task.
- Connects authorship with social credibility and evidence that the writer has grappled with the ideas.
- Uses deliberate-practice and workout analogies to explain capability loss from delegated writing.
- Endorses bounded LLM assistance where weak candidates can be discarded and human judgment remains decisive.

## Evidence
- Writing purpose: [[dont-let-ai-write-for-you]] says documents answer questions and that drafting can reveal whether the question itself is right.
- Capability development: [[dont-let-ai-write-for-you]] compares effortful writing with exercise near the boundary of current ability.
- Trust: [[dont-let-ai-write-for-you]] argues that model-generated prose does not demonstrate that its sender contended with the ideas or can lead the resulting initiative.
- Selective tool use: [[dont-let-ai-write-for-you]] accepts research, checking, transcription, information capture, and divergent ideation as useful LLM roles.
