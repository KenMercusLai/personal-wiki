## Overview
[[DerekSivers]] is an entrepreneur, programmer, and writer represented through his influence on [[Hawstein]]'s independent-creator practice, his arguments that identity and professed priorities must remain accountable to conduct, and his database-centered approach to application architecture.

## Current Profile
Across the sources, Sivers connects selective commitment with behavioral honesty and structural simplification. [[Hawstein]] cites him for choosing only product ideas that produce a strong yes, treating business as a creative medium, and remaining reachable to interesting people. Sivers applies an action-first standard both to role titles and to stated goals: current conduct tests whether an identity remains earned and which of several wants has operative priority. His PostgreSQL essay applies comparable directness to software by placing shared data rules and JSON-producing operations in the long-lived database and keeping replaceable language-specific interfaces thin.

## Key Characteristics
- Supplies a decision filter for selecting among product ideas.
- Influences the author's view that business can be practiced creatively.
- Models a personal website style that invites email conversation.
- Treats identity titles and stated priorities as claims that require current behavioral support.
- Separates legitimate past accomplishment and weaker desire from present role status and operative priority.
- Warns that flattering self-labels and repeated declarations can displace the action that would test them.
- Favors database-owned invariants and operations when they reduce duplicated logic across changing application layers.

## Evidence
- Decision filter: [[yi-ge-du-li-chuang-zao-zhe-de-wu-nian]] invokes Sivers's book title "Hell Yeah or No" to describe choosing only highly compelling product ideas.
- Business creativity: [[yi-ge-du-li-chuang-zao-zhe-de-wu-nian]] says reading Sivers strengthened the author's sense that business is an art.
- Reachability: [[yi-ge-du-li-chuang-zao-zhe-de-wu-nian]] cites Sivers's website as a model for inviting strangers to say hello by email.
- Identity accountability: [[keep-earning-your-title-or-it-expires-derek-sivers]] says Sivers dropped the entrepreneur title after years without starting a company and extends the rule to social and character roles.
- Action over declaration: [[keep-earning-your-title-or-it-expires-derek-sivers]] argues that an old title can provide satisfaction without work and recommends reserving present-tense claims for what a person is actually doing.
- Priority inference: [[actions-not-words-reveal-our-real-values]] interprets years of not launching a company as evidence that Sivers preferred his simpler life more, while allowing that he still wanted the company to some degree.
- Corrective test: [[actions-not-words-reveal-our-real-values]] offers acceptance of the revealed ranking or action on the professed goal as the two responses to a words-conduct mismatch.
- Architecture simplification: [[simplify-move-code-into-database-functions]] places constraints, normalization, reusable operations, JSON shaping, and a narrow API inside PostgreSQL so surrounding clients remain replaceable.

## Qualifications
The Hawstein source uses Sivers as a motivating reference rather than analyzing his work in depth. Sivers's behavioral essays are concise normative reflections, not empirical evidence about identity, motivation, values, or performance. Their rules need exceptions for durable qualifications, aspirational identities, conflicting duties, constrained inactivity, addiction, fear, and structural limits; conduct reveals an effective priority under present conditions rather than an unconstrained or moral value. The database essay is likewise a first-person architecture proposal rather than comparative evidence; its examples omit production controls and include correctness hazards, while its claimed decoupling shifts dependency toward PostgreSQL and PL/pgSQL.

## What Changed
- Extended Sivers's action-first standard from present identity titles to competing wants and operative priorities.
- Qualified action as conditional evidence rather than infallible proof of a person's values or unconstrained preferences.
