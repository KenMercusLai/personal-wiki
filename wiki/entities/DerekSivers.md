## Overview
[[DerekSivers]] is an entrepreneur and writer represented through both his influence on [[Hawstein]]'s independent-creator practice and his own argument that identity titles must remain accountable to current conduct.

## Current Profile
Across the sources, Sivers connects selective commitment with behavioral honesty. [[Hawstein]] cites him for choosing only product ideas that produce a strong yes, treating business as a creative medium, and remaining reachable to interesting people. Sivers's own essay applies the same action-first standard to identity: he stops calling himself an entrepreneur after years without starting a company and argues that roles such as leader, athlete, risk-taker, success, or good friend should be continually earned through present practice.

## Key Characteristics
- Supplies a decision filter for selecting among product ideas.
- Influences the author's view that business can be practiced creatively.
- Models a personal website style that invites email conversation.
- Treats present-tense identity titles as claims that require current behavioral support.
- Separates legitimate past accomplishment from present role status through explicit tense and self-description.
- Warns that flattering self-labels can create premature satisfaction and displace action.

## Evidence
- Decision filter: [[yi-ge-du-li-chuang-zao-zhe-de-wu-nian]] invokes Sivers's book title "Hell Yeah or No" to describe choosing only highly compelling product ideas.
- Business creativity: [[yi-ge-du-li-chuang-zao-zhe-de-wu-nian]] says reading Sivers strengthened the author's sense that business is an art.
- Reachability: [[yi-ge-du-li-chuang-zao-zhe-de-wu-nian]] cites Sivers's website as a model for inviting strangers to say hello by email.
- Identity accountability: [[keep-earning-your-title-or-it-expires-derek-sivers]] says Sivers dropped the entrepreneur title after years without starting a company and extends the rule to social and character roles.
- Action over declaration: [[keep-earning-your-title-or-it-expires-derek-sivers]] argues that an old title can provide satisfaction without work and recommends reserving present-tense claims for what a person is actually doing.

## Qualifications
The Hawstein source uses Sivers as a motivating reference rather than analyzing his work in depth. Sivers's own title essay is a concise normative reflection, not empirical evidence about identity, motivation, or performance, and its rule needs exceptions for durable qualifications, relationships, constrained inactivity, and identities used as aspirations rather than earned status.

## What Changed
- Added Sivers's action-first identity principle and his decision to retire an outdated entrepreneur title.
- Qualified the principle as motivational advice rather than a universal test for every identity or period of inactivity.
