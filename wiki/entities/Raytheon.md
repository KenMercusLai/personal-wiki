## Overview
[[Raytheon]] is represented in the wiki as the organizational setting for [[TerriPotts]]'s small-team build-automation case.

## Current Profile
The source uses one Raytheon team to illustrate how a bounded automation intervention can produce visible evidence for wider DevOps adoption. It reports a large increase in integration-procedure frequency after build automation, but does not describe Raytheon's broader engineering system or establish organization-wide change.

## Key Characteristics
- Supplies a source-scoped enterprise DevOps pilot case.
- Connects build automation with increased integration frequency.
- Illustrates the use of local results to support wider organizational change.

## Evidence
- Pilot result: [[devops-is-a-culture-not-a-role-irma-kornilova-medium]] reports that one Raytheon team moved from two integration procedures per month to 27 in one night after automating builds.
- Adoption context: [[devops-is-a-culture-not-a-role-irma-kornilova-medium]] uses the result in an argument for starting with specific tasks, projects, and subteams.

## Qualifications
The evidence is a secondhand, single-team anecdote. Integration frequency is not equivalent to production deployment or customer value, and the source gives no failure, recovery, security, cost, or long-term adoption measurements.

## What Changed
- Created the entity from the build-automation pilot reported in Kornilova's article.

## Relationships
- [[TerriPotts]] - Raytheon practitioner associated with the cited example.
- [[DevOpsCulture]] - organizational change the pilot is intended to support.
- [[DeploymentAutomation]] - mechanism behind the reported integration-frequency improvement.
- [[ContinuousDelivery]] - broader delivery capability for which the pilot offers limited supporting evidence.
