## Overview
[[BlueOrigin]] is [[JeffBezos]]'s privately funded aerospace company, presented in the 2016 Fortune profile as a long-horizon effort to make repeated human spaceflight practical and eventually support millions of people living in space.

## Current Profile
The company began as a quiet research project and initially explored alternatives to the polluting, extremely loud launch methods used since the 1960s. When that research failed, Bezos redirected the program toward liquid propulsion so vehicles could fly repeatedly. By the profile's publication, the automated New Shepard system had crossed the Kármán line and returned safely under powered vertical landing, while Blue Origin was developing the BE-4 engine and an orbital launch vehicle. Bezos supplied capital and strategic attention through monthly daylong operational reviews and weekly four-hour vehicle updates but was described as nonoperational.

## Key Characteristics
- Pursues a decades-long vision of large-scale human life in space.
- Treats reusable, frequent flight as the economic and technical path toward that vision.
- Changed technical direction after an early launch-method research program failed.
- Uses written narratives and recurring reviews to expose assumptions and track execution.
- Was funded personally by Bezos and expanding headcount and launch facilities in the source period.

## Evidence
- Mission and redirection: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] says the failed alternative-launch research led Bezos to invest in liquid propulsion so the company could fly often.
- Demonstrated reuse: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] reports an automated New Shepard suborbital flight past roughly 100 kilometers followed by safe landing; its inspected photograph shows the booster descending vertically under power.
- Operating system: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] describes monthly operational reviews, weekly vehicle updates, outside-expert presentations, and six-page narratives.
- Scale and infrastructure: [[bezos-prime-fortune]] reports roughly 600 employees growing toward 800, a Texas launch site, and a planned Cape Canaveral facility.

## Qualifications
This profile is a 2016 snapshot and relies heavily on statements from Bezos and then-president Rob Meyerson. Passenger flights projected for 2017 and 2018, future ticket sales, orbital capability, BE-4 performance, spending estimates, and the ultimate settlement vision were forecasts or incomplete programs rather than outcomes established by the source.
