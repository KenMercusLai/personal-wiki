## Overview
[[ClaytonDArnault]] is a writer and the founding editor of Digital Culturist represented in the wiki by a first-person essay about compulsive information consumption and its perceived effects on attention, memory, and creativity.

## Current Profile
In the available source, d’Arnault acts as both author and case subject. He inventories his reading queues, subscriptions, feeds, platforms, tabs, checking habits, task switching, and creative difficulties, then interprets them through popular psychology and media research. His strongest contribution is experiential: abundance can remain attractive after it stops being useful, and practical recovery begins by deciding what a person wants from information. The profile should not turn that self-observation into a clinical diagnosis or general causal proof.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes about digital culture through personal observation and synthesis of secondary sources.
- Frames compulsive information seeking as a management problem rather than a condition with a permanent technological cure.
- Connects attention, memory, unfinished work, and creativity within one account of digital overload.
- Reports deliberate filtering and temporary disconnection as helpful, while acknowledging continued dependence on technology.

## Evidence
- Personal case method: [[drowning-in-a-sea-of-information-digital-culturist]] uses detailed inventories and daily routines to make the overload problem concrete.
- Management stance: [[drowning-in-a-sea-of-information-digital-culturist]] concludes that filtering and understanding the purpose of disconnection matter more than rejecting technology wholesale.
- Qualified self-report: [[drowning-in-a-sea-of-information-digital-culturist]] reports feeling clearer after Infomagical but also says the compulsive pattern later returned.

## Qualifications
The wiki has one source by d’Arnault, and it is an essay published in 2016 rather than a clinical or longitudinal study. Its quantitative support is dated and uneven, and its causal interpretation of the author's own attention, memory, and creativity cannot exclude other explanations.

## What Changed
- Established d’Arnault as the author and first-person subject of the wiki's information-overload source.
- Recorded his management-oriented conclusion without treating the essay as clinical evidence.
