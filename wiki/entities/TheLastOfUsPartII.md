## Overview
[[TheLastOfUsPartII]] is a narrative action game represented here as -LAN-'s case study of peak-end effects in audience memory and evaluation.

## Current Profile
The essay treats the game as a deliberately confrontational emotional design. Joel's death turns prior attachment into anger; control of Abby forces a perspective shift toward a hated character; and Ellie ultimately spares Abby, returns to an empty farm, and can no longer fully play Joel's guitar. In the author's account, these peaks and the bleak close can outweigh memories of strong levels, performance, stealth, combat, and detail without implying that dissatisfied players failed to understand the work.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses attachment to earlier characters to make Joel's death an unusually intense negative peak.
- Forces a perspective change by making Abby playable and supplying her relationships, fears, commitments, and reasons.
- Ends without conventional revenge catharsis or a clear restoration of Ellie's peace.
- Serves as a case where deliberately difficult emotional design can dominate retrospective evaluation of broader craft.

## Evidence
- Emotional peak: [[peak-end-rule]] identifies Joel's death as a designed source of pain and anger grounded in player attachment from the previous game.
- Perspective reversal: [[peak-end-rule]] describes the playable Abby section as a direct and potentially abrasive attempt to make players inhabit an antagonist's life.
- Ending: [[peak-end-rule]] summarizes Ellie sparing Abby, losing Dina and the farm life, and being unable to fully play Joel's guitar after losing fingers.
- Retrospective effect: [[peak-end-rule]] argues that these moments can become the dominant remembered summary despite extensive strengths elsewhere in the game.

## Qualifications
This profile records one essay's interpretive use of the game, not a comprehensive production history, critical consensus, audience study, or independent assessment of narrative quality. Players can experience the same scenes differently, and the source does not measure how frequently peak-end weighting explains favorable or unfavorable judgments.
