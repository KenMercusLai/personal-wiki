## Overview
[[RedHat]] is represented as the open-source infrastructure company behind [[Ansible]], RHEL subscriptions, and [[OpenShift]], and as the subject of IBM's announced $34 billion acquisition in 2018.

## Current Profile
The Ansible source places Red Hat around an open-source automation product expanding into multivendor networks. The Stratechery source supplies the broader business model: founder Bob Young says Red Hat learned from Gerstner-era IBM that customers pay for services and successful outcomes even when the underlying software is freely reusable. Thompson distinguishes the cash-generating RHEL subscription business from OpenShift, which he sees as the strategic asset in IBM's hybrid-cloud acquisition thesis.

## Key Characteristics
- Commercializes open-source infrastructure through subscriptions, support, and enterprise services.
- Provides the corporate context for Ansible's network-automation expansion.
- Uses RHEL as an established subscription business in the 2018 account.
- Develops OpenShift as a Kubernetes-based application and management layer.
- Became IBM's proposed foundation for hybrid and multi-cloud strategy in the acquisition announcement.

- Service model: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] quotes Bob Young describing enterprise service contracts as Red Hat's intended destination even during its boxed-Linux period.
- Product portfolio: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] separates RHEL subscription cash flow from OpenShift's strategic role.
- Acquisition thesis: [[ibms-old-playbook-stratechery-by-ben-thompson]] says IBM expected Red Hat to support portable operation across private and public clouds.

## Qualifications
The two sources cover narrow historical moments rather than a current company profile. Young's account is retrospective, and Thompson's acquisition analysis is a 2018 forecast. Neither establishes the deal's later outcome, current product economics, or whether hybrid portability reduced customer lock-in.

## What Changed
- Expanded Red Hat from Ansible's corporate context into its open-source services model and the 2018 IBM/OpenShift acquisition thesis.
