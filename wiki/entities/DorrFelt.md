## Overview
[[DorrFelt]] is presented as the inventor of the Comptometer, a key-driven adding machine whose 9-to-1 columns shaped calculator-keypad history.

## Current Profile
The source describes Felt's Comptometer as an 1880s machine with multiple decimal columns, each running from 9 at the top to 1 at the bottom. Its layout was not designed for casual discoverability; it served trained operators who could combine lower-value keys to enter values quickly and avoid inefficient hand movement.

## Key Characteristics
- Invented the Comptometer adding machine.
- Used multi-column digit keys ordered 9-to-1 from top to bottom.
- Connected mechanical leverage and decimal-position layout to numeric input design.
- Designed for trained speed rather than beginner-friendly interaction.

## Evidence
- Comptometer layout: [[a-brief-history-of-the-numeric-keypad]] says Felt's machine had eight columns of keys, with 9 on top and 1 on bottom.
- Mechanical explanation: [[a-brief-history-of-the-numeric-keypad]] links the high-to-low order to possible lever length and drum-rotation constraints.
- Operator method: [[a-brief-history-of-the-numeric-keypad]] says operators were trained to use combinations of lower keys, such as 4 and 5 for 9 cents, to preserve speed.

## Qualifications
The source marks the mechanical explanation as reasonable but not absolutely settled. It also notes that Comptometer-style productivity required trained users and was difficult for one-handed operation.
