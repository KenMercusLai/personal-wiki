## Overview
[[AndreStaltz]] appears in the wiki as the author of a critical essay about the open Web's decline under Google, Facebook, and Amazon concentration.

## Current Profile
The source presents Staltz as a web and internet critic concerned with the difference between the broader internet and the open Web. His argument is not that internet use is shrinking, but that browser-mediated, independent, multilateral publishing is losing practical power as discovery, hosting, commerce, social communication, and user experience shift into large proprietary networks.

## Key Characteristics
- Distinguishes the Web from the broader internet infrastructure.
- Uses traffic concentration, publisher dependence, mobile behavior, and corporate strategy to argue that the open Web is decaying.
- Treats Google, Facebook, and Amazon as orthogonal giants of knowledge, social communication, and commerce.
- Frames the future risk as loss of freedom and independent access rather than immediate disappearance of websites.

## Evidence
- Web-decline thesis: [[andre-staltz-the-web-began-dying-in-2014-heres-how]] argues that the Web can die by becoming less necessary while the internet transport layer persists.
- Platform triad: [[andre-staltz-the-web-began-dying-in-2014-heres-how]] names Google as the knowledge internet company, Facebook as the social internet company, and Amazon as the e-commerce company.
- Freedom concern: [[andre-staltz-the-web-began-dying-in-2014-heres-how]] argues that dependence on private networks weakens anonymity, independent publishing, startup access, and account-level recourse.
