## Overview
[[ProjectGriffin]] was the codename for Netflix's late-stage 2007 streaming-player program, whose team and device work were spun out as [[Roku]] shortly before launch.

## Current Profile
Griffin was intended to move [[Netflix]] from DVD-by-mail delivery toward direct television streaming through a compact black set-top box. A roughly twenty-person team led by [[AnthonyWood]] developed hardware, software, user interface, buffering and compression capabilities, thermal design, and supply-chain execution. The project passed engineering, design, and production validation, reached internal beta, pricing, marketing, advertising, and Foxconn preparation, and was then stopped when [[ReedHastings]] judged that a Netflix-branded device would obstruct partnerships with other hardware companies.

## Key Characteristics
- Combined streaming software with a dedicated television set-top box.
- Reached advanced validation and launch preparation rather than remaining a prototype experiment.
- Drew on years of Netflix work in buffering, compression, and digital delivery.
- Had strong internal visibility and support before the reversal.
- Became the operating and product foundation of the Roku spinout described by the source.

## Qualifications
The account is retrospective and does not provide project budgets, schedules, specifications, validation results, contracts, unit economics, or contemporaneous decision records. It also cannot establish what would have happened had Netflix launched the player itself.

## What Changed

## Relationships
- [[Netflix]] - incubated and nearly launched the project.
- [[AnthonyWood]] - led the project team.
- [[ReedHastings]] - cancelled the Netflix-branded launch and approved the spinout.
- [[Roku]] - continued the hardware effort outside Netflix.
- [[PlatformNeutrality]] - strategic concern that caused the project to leave Netflix.
- [[SunkCostFallacy]] - Griffin illustrates stopping despite extensive unrecoverable investment.
