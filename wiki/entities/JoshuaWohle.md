## Overview
[[JoshuaWohle]] is presented as a [[SuperAwesome]] co-founder and startup CTO whose responsibilities changed repeatedly as the company grew from a small London team into a multi-product kidtech organization.

## Current Profile
Wöhle's source-backed profile is primarily an operating retrospective. From 2013 through 2018, he moved through infrastructure repair, early technical recruiting, product management, product-organization building, customer work, strategic coordination, and senior functional hiring. He interprets successful CTO development as repeatedly giving former responsibilities to better-focused leaders and then finding the company's next scaling constraint.

## Key Characteristics
- Began with hands-on reliability work and inherited-system cleanup.
- Recruited early engineers through personal trust and company-vision selling.
- Learned and led product management as SuperAwesome moved from maintenance into product development.
- Created or hired specialist leadership across architecture, engineering, and product.
- Treats relinquishing control and anticipating future bottlenecks as core startup-CTO work.

## Evidence
- Early operations: [[being-a-start-up-cto-or-how-i-fired-myself-enough-times-to-finally-become-cto]] places Wöhle in repeated incident recovery, hosting migration, and refactoring work in 2013.
- Product transition: [[being-a-start-up-cto-or-how-i-fired-myself-enough-times-to-finally-become-cto]] says he learned product management with help from [[JannaBastow]] and built SuperAwesome's product organization.
- Leadership handoffs: [[being-a-start-up-cto-or-how-i-fired-myself-enough-times-to-finally-become-cto]] describes adding a Chief Architect in 2016, Head of Engineering in 2017, and Chief Product Officer in 2018.

## Qualifications
This profile comes from Wöhle's own 2018 account and does not independently evaluate SuperAwesome's team experience, operating outcomes, or later company history. The source names many helpers but does not fully specify all executives or measure the effect of each role change.
