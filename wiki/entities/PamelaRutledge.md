## Overview
[[PamelaRutledge]] is identified in the source as director of the Media Psychology Research Center and comments on validation, self-presentation, self-worth, and memory in Instagram Stories use.

## Current Profile
Rutledge interprets idealized Stories as a mixture of aspiration and ordinary human desires for acceptance and validation rather than automatically deceptive or pathological behavior. Her boundary is functional: preoccupation becomes concerning when online self-presentation interferes with other goals, dominates personal value, or gives viewers power to define that value. She also preserves a benefit often lost in platform-harm accounts, arguing that recorded events can serve an important purpose when revisiting them restores positive emotion and memory.

The profile is narrow because it rests on quotations in one 2018 journalistic article. The source does not supply her underlying research, a diagnostic framework, participant data, or a full account of her work.

## Key Characteristics
- Interprets online self-presentation through aspiration, acceptance, and validation motives.
- Distinguishes ordinary recognition seeking from preoccupation that displaces other goals.
- Warns against allowing audience response to define personal value.
- Treats positive emotional recall as a possible benefit of recording events.
