## Overview
[[Dreamwidth]] is a community-oriented fork of [[LiveJournal]] co-founded by former LiveJournal contributors Denise Paolucci and Mark Smith.

## Current Profile
The source positions Dreamwidth as an attempt to preserve the smaller, configurable, community-centered social model that former LiveJournal staff valued after concluding that LiveJournal's owners did not understand it. The fork also became a destination for some users after SUP Media moved LiveJournal's servers to Russia and concerns grew about government access to stored data.

## Key Characteristics
- Fork of LiveJournal's code and journal-centered social model.
- Co-founded by former LiveJournal volunteer leader Denise Paolucci and engineer Mark Smith.
- Explicitly associated with the “mom-and-pop” rather than mass-market model of social media.
- Received migrants concerned about LiveJournal's Russian ownership and server location.

## Evidence
- Founding lineage: [[the-linux-of-social-media-how-livejournal-pioneered-then-lost-blogging-ars-technica]] identifies Paolucci and Smith as former LiveJournal contributors who co-founded Dreamwidth.
- Product philosophy: [[the-linux-of-social-media-how-livejournal-pioneered-then-lost-blogging-ars-technica]] connects Dreamwidth to Paolucci's contrast between intimate community social media and the “Walmart” scale of Facebook and Twitter.
- Migration: [[the-linux-of-social-media-how-livejournal-pioneered-then-lost-blogging-ars-technica]] reports an influx of users after LiveJournal moved servers to Russia, partly over subpoena and data-access concerns.

## Qualifications
The article provides only a brief origin and migration account. It does not evaluate Dreamwidth's scale, finances, governance outcomes, technical development, or whether its community model avoided LiveJournal's own tensions.
