## Overview
[[LiliyaGershengoren]] is a psychiatrist and Cornell professor cited for survey evidence that psychiatrists and residents commonly search for patients online.

## Current Profile
The source presents Gershengoren as the clinician-researcher who moved patient Googling from anecdote toward measurement. Her survey at one U.S. academic hospital found that nearly all responding staff doctors and residents had Googled a patient at least once, with emergency-room searching more common than inpatient searching. She also supports constructive social-media use for patients' emotional and psychosocial needs, especially where patients already use those platforms.

## Key Characteristics
- Psychiatrist and Cornell professor in the source's description.
- Conducted an anonymous survey on patient Googling among staff doctors and residents.
- Found patient Googling especially common in emergency settings.
- Supports appropriate social-media use for patient emotional and psychosocial support.

## Evidence
- Professional role: [[cancer-on-nautilus-getting-googled-by-your-doctor-is-the-new-normal]] identifies Gershengoren as a psychiatrist and professor at Cornell University.
- Prevalence survey: [[cancer-on-nautilus-getting-googled-by-your-doctor-is-the-new-normal]] reports that 93% of staff doctors and 94% of residents in her sample had Googled a patient at least once.
- Emergency setting: [[cancer-on-nautilus-getting-googled-by-your-doctor-is-the-new-normal]] reports more frequent or semi-regular searching in ER settings than inpatient settings.
- Supportive use: [[cancer-on-nautilus-getting-googled-by-your-doctor-is-the-new-normal]] quotes Gershengoren favoring social media for emotional and psychosocial needs.

## Qualifications
The survey is described as one academic-hospital sample presented at an American Psychiatric Association meeting, not a national prevalence study. The source does not provide details on survey design beyond respondent counts and headline results.
