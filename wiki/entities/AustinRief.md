## Overview
[[AustinRief]] is represented here as a [[MorningBrew]] co-founder reflecting on the company's early college ambassador experiments.

## Current Profile
Rief's reported approach was iterative and operational. After a selective cohort suffered heavy attrition and an automated large cohort received too little support, Morning Brew combined broad recruiting with escalation into an advanced program for active ambassadors. His summary emphasizes concentrated contribution, the time required to cultivate strong advocates, and the possible counterproductive effect of cash incentives.

## Key Characteristics
- Tested ambassador recruitment and support models across successive semesters.
- Shifted from choosing participants solely in advance toward identifying commitment through observed activity.
- Concentrated coaching and resources on ambassadors who demonstrated effort.
- Treated affection for the product and résumé value as stronger motives than money in this program.

## Evidence
- Iterative program design: [[happy-xiao-ru-he-kuo-san]] attributes the selective, automated, and hybrid sequence to Rief and the Morning Brew team.
- Performance concentration: [[happy-xiao-ru-he-kuo-san]] attributes to Rief the heuristic that the best ambassadors create 80% of the value.
- Incentive view: [[happy-xiao-ru-he-kuo-san]] reports experiments ranging from résumé workshops to cash and says Rief found money often counterproductive.
