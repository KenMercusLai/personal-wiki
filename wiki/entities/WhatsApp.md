## Overview
[[WhatsApp]] appears in the sources as both a small-headcount startup-success counterexample and a Facebook-owned messaging app whose founders and acquirer publicly rejected advertising as the obvious monetization path.

## Current Profile
The hiring source does not analyze WhatsApp's product or acquisition history in depth. It uses one fact pattern rhetorically: a company could reportedly sell for $19 billion while having only 55 employees, which challenges venture-capital talk that treats rapid hiring and large teams as natural evidence of success. The Mobile Dev Memo source adds a monetization qualification: WhatsApp was part of Facebook's app portfolio and seemed like a possible future messaging-ad surface, but both Mark Zuckerberg and Jan Koum had publicly dismissed advertising as a WhatsApp monetization mechanic.

## Key Characteristics
- Serves as a counterexample to headcount-as-success reasoning.
- Represents the possibility of extreme business impact from a small team.
- Appears as a Facebook-owned messaging app in the mobile advertising discussion.
- Public anti-ad statements qualify assumptions that every messaging platform naturally monetizes through ads.

## Evidence
- Small-headcount acquisition: [[a-thing-i-wonder-do-companies-have-too-many-employees]] says a VC asked whether another investor had just sold a company for $19 billion with only 55 employees, identifying the company as WhatsApp.
- Portfolio context: [[advertising-models-in-mobile-messaging-apps-mobile-dev-memo]] lists WhatsApp alongside Messenger, Instagram, and Facebook as part of Facebook's app portfolio.
- Monetization qualification: [[advertising-models-in-mobile-messaging-apps-mobile-dev-memo]] says Mark Zuckerberg and Jan Koum had publicly dismissed ads as a WhatsApp monetization mechanic.
