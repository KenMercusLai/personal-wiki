## Overview
[[WhatsApp]] appears in the sources as both a small-headcount startup-success counterexample and a Facebook-owned messaging app whose founders and acquirer publicly rejected advertising as the obvious monetization path.

## Current Profile
The hiring source does not analyze WhatsApp's product or acquisition history in depth. It uses one fact pattern rhetorically: a company could reportedly sell for $19 billion while having only 55 employees, which challenges venture-capital talk that treats rapid hiring and large teams as natural evidence of success. The Mobile Dev Memo source adds a monetization qualification: WhatsApp was part of Facebook's app portfolio and seemed like a possible future messaging-ad surface, but both Mark Zuckerberg and Jan Koum had publicly dismissed advertising as a WhatsApp monetization mechanic. The Engadget report adds a pre-acquisition information mechanism: [[Onavo]] telemetry reportedly showed [[Facebook]] WhatsApp's dominance in some markets, including a claimed presence on 99% of Android phones in Spain, and influenced the decision to buy it.

## Key Characteristics
- Serves as a counterexample to headcount-as-success reasoning.
- Represents the possibility of extreme business impact from a small team.
- Appears as a Facebook-owned messaging app in the mobile advertising discussion.
- Public anti-ad statements qualify assumptions that every messaging platform naturally monetizes through ads.
- Its geographic strength reportedly became visible to Facebook through Onavo before the acquisition.
