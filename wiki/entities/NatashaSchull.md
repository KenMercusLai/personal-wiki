## Overview
[[NatashaSchull]] is presented as an anthropologist of human-machine interaction whose gambling research shifts analysis from an allegedly defective user toward the deliberately engineered relation between a person and a machine.

## Current Profile
Schüll's *Addiction by Design* supplies the source's account of the “machine zone”: a trance-like state in which repetitive mechanical rhythm suspends time, space, and social identity. This reframes compulsive gambling as more than pursuit of winning and helps Schulson interpret neurotic checking as focused motion and reward rather than simple distraction. Schüll also challenges claims that designers merely give people what they want, emphasizing how machine design accentuates, accelerates, and elaborates the exchange between wanting and receiving.

## Key Characteristics
- Studies the engineered interaction between gambling-machine design and compulsive behavior.
- Defines the machine zone as repetitive absorption rather than straightforward pursuit of reward value.
- Shifts part of the analytical burden from individual pathology to interaction design.
- Argues that design can amplify an existing desire beyond its initial form or intended extent.
- Treats attention capture followed by user blame as an ethically inconsistent allocation of responsibility.

## Qualifications
This page is based on Schulson's short 2015 synthesis and interview, not a complete reading of Schüll's ethnography. A website differs from a gambling machine in stakes, utility, context, and harms, so the comparison transfers an interaction mechanism rather than proving equivalent addiction or damage.
