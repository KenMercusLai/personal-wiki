## Overview
[[PlayHaven]] is represented in the source as a mobile-gaming startup that contracted from roughly ten to six employees, rebuilt most of its product, won a major client, and later reached reported multimillion-dollar revenue and an 85-person team.

## Current Profile
When [[RyanHoover]] joined, PlayHaven had product traction but nearly stagnant revenue and adoption. Dwindling cash led to layoffs, after which the remaining six-person company adopted a new direction and rebuilt almost all existing code. The small team targeted a private launch at Casual Connect in July 2011, delivered a usable product under severe time and staffing constraints, and secured what Hoover calls its first major client.

Hoover reports that PlayHaven subsequently reached several thousand customers, multimillion-dollar revenue, and 85 employees. Growth also changed its operating structure: roles became more specialized, and a UX designer assumed work previously handled by its generalist product manager. The source presents this as a successful company transition while distinguishing organizational success from continuing individual-role fit.

## Key Characteristics
- Entered a cash-constrained period with stagnant adoption and revenue despite some initial traction.
- Reduced its team to six people and adopted a new product direction.
- Rebuilt nearly all existing code through a small, collaborative product and engineering team.
- Reached a usable private launch and secured a first major client under intense time pressure.
- Reportedly expanded to 85 employees, several thousand customers, and multimillion-dollar revenue.
- Shifted from broad generalist responsibilities toward specialized roles as it scaled.

- Product rebuild: [[knowing-when-its-time-to-move-on-ryan-hoover-medium]] describes a new vision, replacement of almost all existing code, five engineers, one product manager, and a July 2011 launch target.
- Early commercial result: [[knowing-when-its-time-to-move-on-ryan-hoover-medium]] says the rebuilt product became usable and won the company's first large client.
- Reported scale: [[knowing-when-its-time-to-move-on-ryan-hoover-medium]] gives the later team, customer, and revenue figures and describes the addition of a UX specialist.

## Qualifications
The evidence comes from one departing employee's 2013 retrospective. It supplies no financial records, customer definitions, retention measures, product-quality data, exact revenue, profitability, financing history, or account from laid-off workers, leaders, engineers, customers, or the named client. The narrative cannot isolate which parts of the new vision, rewrite, launch deadline, team effort, market growth, or luck caused the turnaround, and rushed testing without a QA function should not be generalized as a safe delivery practice.
