## Overview
[[MarcLore]] is represented here as the [[JetCom]] CEO who argued that visible, standardized compensation could preserve early-startup trust and fairness as the company grew.

## Current Profile
In the supplied 2016 Business Insider article, Lore describes Jet's pay system as a cultural and incentive design: employees could see salaries, 13 levels fixed cash pay and equity within each level, bonuses were absent, and raises came through promotion rather than individual bargaining. He presents the system as protection against opaque managerial tradeoffs and negotiation leverage, but the article supplies only his account and does not independently establish employee acceptance, equitable level assignment, retention, performance, or durability.

The article also identifies Lore as the previous founder of Quidsi, parent of Diapers.com, Soap.com, and Wag.com, which Amazon acquired for about $550 million in 2010. That background explains the source's description of him as a repeat e-commerce founder but does not validate the Jet compensation model.

## Key Characteristics
- Advocates transparent salaries as an input to employee trust and challenge.
- Uses fixed compensation levels to replace individual salary and raise negotiation.
- Links level-standardized employee equity with a sense of ownership.
- Treats the policy as specific to Jet rather than suitable for every workplace.

## Evidence
- Trust rationale: [[jet-employees-know-each-others-salaries-business-insider]] quotes Lore arguing that shared information lets employees identify and challenge unfairness.
- Compensation design: [[jet-employees-know-each-others-salaries-business-insider]] attributes Jet's 13 levels, standardized level equity, absent bonuses, and promotion-only raises to Lore.
- Negotiation critique: [[jet-employees-know-each-others-salaries-business-insider]] reports Lore's view that counteroffers and manager discretion can reward dissatisfaction rather than contribution.
- Prior company: [[jet-employees-know-each-others-salaries-business-insider]] identifies Lore as Quidsi's founder and reports its 2010 sale to Amazon for about $550 million.

## Qualifications
This profile is bounded to one executive interview and does not establish Lore's broader career, later policies, or the long-term results of Jet's system. His causal claims about trust, empowerment, effort, and reduced job shopping are not supported here by employee testimony, controlled comparison, workforce data, or measured outcomes. Fixed bands can reduce negotiation differences while leaving discretion in job design, leveling, promotion, market adjustment, and equity valuation.
