## Overview
[[Bump]] was a mobile startup cofoundered by [[DavidLieb]] that began as an app idea, grew through [[YCombinator]], was later acquired by [[Google]], and became a case study in cognitively clear product interaction.

## Current Profile
The sources present Bump less as a final product success story than as a startup path that created a team, discovery process, photo-sharing direction, and product-design lesson. With five people and $16,000 during YC, it reportedly reached 2 million users, then faced operational pressure after an Apple commercial. Later user calls revealed photo sharing as the real behavior to understand, leading toward [[Flock]], Photoroll, and eventually [[GooglePhotos]]-adjacent work after the acquisition. In Lieb's later First Round account, Bump's physical phone-bump gesture was high-friction but cognitively simple because users knew exactly what action caused sharing.

## Key Characteristics
- Early mobile app that reached large usage with a very small team and little capital.
- Benefited from luck, distribution, and the [[YCombinator]] network as well as product execution.
- Became a discovery surface for photo-sharing behavior.
- Led to follow-on products and ideas rather than a simple straight-line product story.
- Produced unusually strong team retention after acquisition by [[Google]].
- Demonstrates that visible user action can make a product easier to understand even when it adds physical friction.

## Evidence
- Early scale: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says Bump reached 2 million users during YC with five people and $16,000.
- Operational shock: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says an Apple international TV commercial melted Bump's servers before YC network help arrived.
- User behavior: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says calls with the top 100 users revealed they were sharing photos.
- Product continuation: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says [[Flock]] and Photoroll followed Bump, with Photoroll ideas later returning in [[GooglePhotos]].
- Acquisition and retention: [[bump-10-years-of-lessons-learned]] says Bump signed a deal to be acquired by [[Google]] in summer 2013 and that 15 of 17 team members remained at Google after five years.
- Cognitive clarity: [[cognitive-overhead-is-your-products-overlord-topple-it-with-these-tips-first-round-review]] says touching two phones together was straightforward, repeatable, and understandable without much explanation.
- Control through action: [[cognitive-overhead-is-your-products-overlord-topple-it-with-these-tips-first-round-review]] contrasts Bump's required physical action with [[Flock]] removing users too far from the sharing process.

## Qualifications
The sources are founder/product-leader interpretations and do not provide independent analytics, acquisition details, or a full causal account of why Bump grew. They also say later products did not all succeed; Bump's value comes partly from team, learning spillovers, and its contrast with less cognitively legible follow-on products.
