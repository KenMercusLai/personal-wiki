## Overview
[[WaitButWhy]] is [[TimUrban]]'s illustrated long-form publication, represented here by a career framework and a travelogue about [[Bhutan]].

## Current Profile
The two current sources show a publication style that combines informal first-person prose, humor, strong visual framing, and explicit explanatory structures. One turns career choice into a named self-audit framework; the other combines a compact national profile, original photographs, a cartoon map, and a companion video. The shared strength is accessibility, while the evidence base varies from introspective reasoning to brief guided observation.

## Key Characteristics
- Publishes extended explanatory essays that turn abstract life decisions into concrete diagrams and named concepts.
- Uses humor and visual metaphors alongside serious self-analysis.
- Connects personal reflection with broader claims about society, conventional wisdom, and changing career landscapes.
- Mixes short travel writing with original photographs, maps, and linked video.
- Allows the author to state uncertainty and distinguish observation from verification.

## Evidence
- Long-form explanatory style: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-how-to-pick-a-career]] is a sustained career-decision essay rather than a short advice post.
- Visual metaphor: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-how-to-pick-a-career]] relies on inspected local images such as the childhood river, Want/Reality overlap, Yearning Octopus, strategy loop, and dot-jump diagrams.
- Personal-development framing: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-how-to-pick-a-career]] asks readers to audit motives, beliefs, identity, inertia, and regret.
- Multimedia travel format: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-the-sights-and-sounds-of-bhutan]] uses a cartoon regional map, landscape and monastery photography, external references, and a companion YouTube video.
- Qualified narration: [[blog-tim-urban-waitbutwhy-the-sights-and-sounds-of-bhutan]] distinguishes reported royal stories and happiness claims from what the author could personally establish.
