## Overview
[[CapitalismWithoutCapital]] appears in the source as [[JonathanHaskel]] and [[StianWestlake]]'s book explaining why intangible assets are reshaping capitalism.

## Current Profile
The Gates source presents the book as a clear, economics-oriented explanation of how intangible investment differs from tangible investment. Gates values it for making the transition from physical capital toward software, data, branding, research, training, and other intangible assets understandable without turning the argument into a moral condemnation or a narrow policy prescription. The book is framed as especially useful for policymakers who need to update measurement, competition, intellectual-property, taxation, and stimulus assumptions.

## Key Characteristics
- Defines intangible assets as assets that cannot be touched.
- Explains why intangible industries behave differently from tangible-goods industries.
- Presents the transition in a textbook-like and non-polemical style.
- Is recommended for readers with some economics background.
- Is treated by Gates as required reading for policymakers.

## Evidence
- Core definition: [[capitalism-without-capital-bill-gates]] says the book starts by defining intangible assets as things one cannot touch.
- Explanatory role: [[capitalism-without-capital-bill-gates]] calls the book one of the best explanations Gates had seen of why the shift matters.
- Style: [[capitalism-without-capital-bill-gates]] says the book is almost textbook-like and avoids treating the trend as evil or prescribing hard policy solutions.
- Audience: [[capitalism-without-capital-bill-gates]] says readers need some economics familiarity, such as a course or regular finance-section reading.
- Policy relevance: [[capitalism-without-capital-bill-gates]] says the book tackles intangible-economy policy questions in depth and should be required reading for policymakers.

## Qualifications
The source is a favorable review by Gates rather than a neutral bibliography or direct full summary of the book. The article mentions the book's four reasons that intangible investment behaves differently, but the markdown excerpt does not preserve those four reasons in detail.
