## Overview
[[AmazonEcho]] is Amazon's smart-speaker platform in the source, used as the main non-Apple hardware surface where [[AppleMusic]] availability can protect Apple's content relationship with iPhone users.

## Current Profile
The article treats Echo speakers as common stationary music devices that many iPhone owners may also own. Because speaker use often centers on music listening, Apple Music's absence could push those users toward Spotify or Amazon Music. Cybart therefore interprets Apple Music on Echo as selective distribution: Apple uses third-party hardware to defend Apple Music adoption without turning [[HomePod]] into a cheap mass-market service conduit.

## Key Characteristics
- Non-Apple smart-speaker platform used for music listening.
- Likely overlaps with part of Apple's iPhone user base in the source's argument.
- Became a distribution endpoint for Apple Music.
- Serves as the contrast case for Apple's premium HomePod positioning.

## Evidence
- User overlap: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] argues a meaningful portion of Echo owners likely also used iPhones.
- Music use case: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] says stationary speakers are predominantly used for listening to music.
- Distribution endpoint: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] presents Apple Music on Echo as a way to limit [[Spotify]] and Amazon Music adoption among Apple users.
- Hardware contrast: [[above-avalon-apples-content-distribution-strategy]] compares cheap Echo devices with the premium [[HomePod]] to reject a pure services-over-hardware reading.

## Qualifications
The source does not provide measured Echo ownership overlap with iPhone users or adoption effects after Apple Music support. It uses Echo primarily as a strategic example rather than a complete analysis of Amazon's smart-speaker business.

## What Changed
- Created Amazon Echo as the source's key third-party hardware distribution example.

- [[Spotify]] - rival music service Apple wants to prevent Echo-owning iPhone users from adopting.
