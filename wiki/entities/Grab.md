## Overview
[[Grab]] is presented as a Southeast Asian mobility company that used ride-hailing as the habitual base for a regionally localized [[SuperApp]].

## Current Profile
In the 2019 Skift account, Grab's acquisition of Uber's Southeast Asian business enabled a pivot from ride-hailing toward delivery, payments, lending, insurance, and other services. Its model is regional rather than uniform: transport modes, grocery delivery, personal assistance, and planned healthcare vary across countries, languages, cultures, and operating conditions. Grab also integrates with [[GoogleMaps]], making the relationship partly complementary rather than simply competitive.

## Key Characteristics
- Uses frequent ride-hailing demand as the beachhead for adjacent service verticals.
- Combines transportation, food, grocery and parcel delivery with payments and other financial services.
- Localizes its service mix by country rather than imposing one identical regional product.
- Expanded strategic scope after acquiring Uber's Southeast Asian operation in 2018.
- Partners with Google Maps for ride booking despite both products' broad platform ambitions.

## Evidence
- **Mobility-led expansion.** [[google-maps-is-ready-to-transform-the-world-of-superapps-a-skift-deep-dive-skift]] says Grab used ride-hailing to grow parcel, food, and grocery delivery plus financial services.
- **Localized portfolio.** [[google-maps-is-ready-to-transform-the-world-of-superapps-a-skift-deep-dive-skift]] describes tuk-tuks, buses, motorcycles, grocery delivery, and queue-standing assistance as country-specific offerings.
- **Strategic inflection.** [[google-maps-is-ready-to-transform-the-world-of-superapps-a-skift-deep-dive-skift]] attributes the explicit superapp pivot to the 2018 Uber transaction.
- **Partnership boundary.** [[google-maps-is-ready-to-transform-the-world-of-superapps-a-skift-deep-dive-skift]] reports that users could book Grab rides through Google Maps and that Grab did not treat Maps as the same customer proposition.

## Qualifications
This profile is based on one 2019 article and largely on a company spokesperson's account. It does not independently measure adoption, unit economics, service quality, market power, regulatory effects, or whether the post-Uber expansion produced sustainable results. Announced healthcare plans are not evidence of a launched capability.

## What Changed
- Created Grab as a localized, mobility-led Southeast Asian superapp case.
- Distinguished regional service integration from a globally uniform product bundle.
- Recorded Google Maps as both a distribution partner and a different platform proposition.
