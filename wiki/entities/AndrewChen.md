## Overview
[[AndrewChen]] appears in the wiki as the author of a startup essay about surviving the [[TroughOfSorrow]] before [[ProductMarketFit]].

## Current Profile
The source presents Chen as a startup writer drawing from personal experience and from time with startups as an investor or advisor. His contribution here is a stage-specific founder model: after initial attention fades, teams need emotional steadiness, root-cause diagnosis, product-risk reduction, enough runway for iteration, and small tactical wins rather than generic business literature or premature scaling advice.

## Key Characteristics
- Writes from a startup-operator and advisor perspective.
- Frames pre-fit startup struggle as both emotional and analytical.
- Treats product-market-fit discovery as hard, common, and often multi-year.
- Emphasizes root-bottleneck diagnosis over generic growth tactics.
- Connects runway, product iteration, and small tactical wins during difficult startup phases.

## Evidence
- Author role: [[after-the-techcrunch-bump-life-in-the-trough-of-sorrow-at-andrewchen]] is published on Andrew Chen's site and written in first person.
- Startup stage model: [[after-the-techcrunch-bump-life-in-the-trough-of-sorrow-at-andrewchen]] centers on what founders should do when they have a team, idea, and investors but the product is not yet working.
- Emotional emphasis: [[after-the-techcrunch-bump-life-in-the-trough-of-sorrow-at-andrewchen]] says much of Chen's investor/advisor time with startups concerns emotional issues around feeling like failure.
- Analytical emphasis: [[after-the-techcrunch-bump-life-in-the-trough-of-sorrow-at-andrewchen]] recommends identifying whether the main bottleneck is product quality, onboarding, marketing, or growth execution.
- Iteration emphasis: [[after-the-techcrunch-bump-life-in-the-trough-of-sorrow-at-andrewchen]] links capital and small wins to more chances to iterate toward fit.
