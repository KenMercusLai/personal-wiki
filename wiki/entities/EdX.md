## Overview
[[EdX]] is a MOOC platform represented in the source through HarvardX course analytics and public claims about large-scale learning data.

## Current Profile
The article treats edX as both a symbol of big-data ambition in education and a source of HarvardX analytics examples. Reich cites the idea of edX as a "particle accelerator for learning," then critiques his own reports for reducing rich course logs to event counts and showing that certificate earners clicked more than non-earners.

## Key Characteristics
- Symbolizes the promise that massive online-course platforms would accelerate learning research.
- Supplies HarvardX course-log examples in which event counts were compared with certificate earning.
- Appears as a self-critical case where even sophisticated research teams can reduce rich behavior to simple activity totals.

## Evidence
- Big-data promise: [[big-data-mooc-research-breakthrough-learning-activities-lead-to-achievement-edtech-researcher-education-week]] cites edX's particle-accelerator metaphor.
- Event-count analysis: [[big-data-mooc-research-breakthrough-learning-activities-lead-to-achievement-edtech-researcher-education-week]] says HarvardX reports counted log events per student.
- Certificate pattern: [[big-data-mooc-research-breakthrough-learning-activities-lead-to-achievement-edtech-researcher-education-week]] reports that certificate earners took more actions than non-earners.
