## Overview
[[McKinsey|McKinsey & Company]] is represented in this wiki as the management-consulting and research organization that published a preliminary 2015 analysis of workplace automation through its McKinsey Global Institute authors.

## Current Profile
In the source, McKinsey frames automation as an activity- and capability-level transformation rather than a binary prediction about occupations. Its research combines US labor-market activity data, capability requirements, technology assessments, wage exposure, and modeled process cases to derive implications for job design, business processes, leadership, and public risk. The page reflects this one historical research contribution, not McKinsey's current organization, clients, full research program, or present position on automation.

## Key Characteristics
- Publisher of management and economic research on automation and work.
- Uses activity and capability decomposition to analyze technical automation potential.
- Connects technology assessment to job, process, leadership, and competitive implications.
- Distinguishes productivity and quality benefits from labor-cost reduction alone.
- Acknowledges displacement, inequality, privacy, safety, legal, and regulatory risks alongside opportunity.

## Evidence
Research method:
- [[four-fundamentals-of-workplace-automation-mckinsey]] describes a McKinsey analysis of roughly 2,000 US work activities against 18 potentially automatable capabilities.

Organizational framing:
- [[four-fundamentals-of-workplace-automation-mckinsey]] argues that leaders must redesign jobs and processes because automation potential is distributed across occupations.

Qualified implications:
- [[four-fundamentals-of-workplace-automation-mckinsey]] presents modeled business benefits while explicitly raising displacement, inequality, privacy, quality, safety, legal, and regulatory concerns.

## Qualifications
The source is a McKinsey-authored account of its own preliminary model, not an independent replication or a record of realized outcomes. Its findings are historically bounded to demonstrated technology and US labor data available in 2015. This page does not infer McKinsey's current views, later evidence, commercial interests, or broader institutional record.
